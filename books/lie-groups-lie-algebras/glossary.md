
# Glossary and Proof Finder

**Closed matrix group:** a subgroup closed in the relative topology of
GL. Chapter one distinguishes this from closedness in all matrices;
chapter two proves its embedded manifold structure.

**One-parameter subgroup:** a continuous homomorphism from the additive real
line. Chapter three proves it is exp(tX), including automatic differentiability.

**Lie bracket:** the commutator of matrices or of left-invariant fields,
with the corresponding right-invariant sign explained in chapter four.

**Lie correspondence:** differentiation and integration relate connected groups
and their infinitesimal homomorphisms; chapter five supplies the simply-connected
integration hypothesis and the possibility of immersed nonclosed subgroups.

**Solvable / nilpotent:** termination of the derived series / lower central
series. Chapter six proves Engel and Lie theorems with their field conditions.

**Killing form:** trace of two adjoint operators. Chapter seven proves
the Cartan criteria and the decomposition into simple ideals. Its radical
is not, for an arbitrary Lie algebra, automatically the solvable radical.

**Highest weight:** the Cartan weight of a vector annihilated by positive
root operators. Chapter eight proves the rank-one classification and chapter
twelve proves the sl(3) classification and character formula.

**Root system:** a finite spanning reduced set of nonzero Euclidean vectors
stable under its reflections and with integral coroot pairings. Chapter nine
derives simple roots and chamber uniqueness; chapter ten constructs the
system from a complex semisimple algebra; chapter eleven proves its classification.

**Dynkin diagram:** the integral angle and length data of a simple
basis. Chapter eleven proves the A, B, C, D, E, F, G
list, its existence and uniqueness up to similarity. The theorem is
about root systems, not a complete classification of real forms or
global groups.

**Weyl character formula:** an alternating quotient giving the irreducible
character. Chapter twelve proves it for sl(3) by a Casimir differential
identity and states the general semisimple formula as a further theorem.

**Haar probability:** the unique left-invariant probability on a compact matrix
group, proved bi-invariant in chapter thirteen. The construction uses normalized
smooth volume; it is not available on every noncompact group.

**Peter–Weyl:** completeness and orthogonality of irreducible matrix coefficients.
Chapter thirteen proves the compact matrix-group version using the named
Stone–Weierstrass and measure inputs. Uniform density is distinct from arbitrary
Fourier-series uniform convergence.

**Descent:** factoring a representation through a quotient. Chapter fourteen
checks it by trivial action of the covering kernel, with SU(2)/SO(3)
and SU(3)/PSU(3) examples.
