
# References, Provenance and Proof Boundaries {#section-1-references-provenance-and-proof-boundaries}

The following works provide further reading for the fourteen-chapter route.

- Brian C. Hall, *Lie Groups, Lie Algebras, and Representations*, and
  the author's [An Elementary Introduction to Groups and Representations](https://arxiv.org/abs/math-ph/0005032).
  The freely available author notes are a primary reference for the
  matrix-first approach and compact examples.
- James E. Humphreys, *Introduction to Lie Algebras and Representation Theory*.
  This is the structural and root-theoretic scope benchmark.
- Theodor Bröcker and Tammo tom Dieck, *Representations of Compact Lie Groups*.
  This is the compact representation and harmonic-analysis scope benchmark.
- Alexander Kirillov Jr., [institution-hosted Lie groups and Lie algebras notes](https://www.math.stonybrook.edu/~kirillov/liegroups/liegroups.pdf).
  These are a primary comparison for structural theorem statements.
- MIT OpenCourseWare, [18.755 Lie Groups and Lie Algebras II, official lecture notes](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-755-lie-groups-and-lie-algebras-ii-spring-2024/mit18_755_s24_lec_full.pdf).
  These supply official further reading on highest weights and characters.

Linear algebra, the inverse-function theorem, Frobenius, manifold integration
and Stone–Weierstrass are explicit preparation inputs, linked at their point
of use. Jordan decomposition is used for finite-dimensional operators, not
assumed as an unproved structural theorem about arbitrary Lie algebras.

Chapter five imports Ado's theorem only to pass from an abstract real
finite-dimensional Lie algebra to a faithful matrix realization in the
abstract existence discussion. It proves the matrix and simply-connected
integration correspondence. It does not infer a complex holomorphic structure
from a real Frobenius construction alone.

Chapter eleven proves classification of finite reduced crystallographic root
systems. Lie-algebra reconstruction from a diagram and classification of real
forms are separate further results. Chapter twelve proves the highest-weight
and Weyl character theorems for sl(3), while stating the arbitrary semisimple
formula with exact hypotheses. Its general denominator and existence theorems
are not represented as proved here.

Chapter thirteen proves Peter–Weyl for compact matrix groups. Smooth-volume
change of variables, finite-product Fubini, regularity of finite Borel measures
on compact metric spaces, simple-function approximation and Stone–Weierstrass
are the named analytic inputs. A faithful matrix embedding supplies the
separating coefficients; no general theorem making arbitrary compact groups
linear is assumed.
