
# Glossary

**Boundary orientation.** The curve direction matched to an oriented surface by the right-hand convention.

**Constitutive relation.** Additional information linking macroscopic fields and material response, with its own frequency, length-scale and amplitude regime.

**Field tensor.** The antisymmetric spacetime tensor whose electric and magnetic components depend on an observer's inertial frame.

**Gauge transformation.** A change of potentials that leaves electric and magnetic fields unchanged within its stated domain and regularity.

**Lorenz condition.** The potential condition $\nabla\cdot A+c^{-2}\partial_t\phi=0$, distinct from Lorentz transformations.

**Proper time.** The time accumulated along a timelike worldline, invariant under inertial coordinate changes.

**Retarded solution.** A solution selected using source data at earlier times linked by the light-travel delay, together with the stated absence or specification of incoming homogeneous fields.

**Singular source.** An idealized point, line or sheet charge or current requiring integral or distributional equations at its support.

**Stress-energy tensor.** A tensor organizing energy density, momentum density and their spatial fluxes under one spacetime conservation equation.


**Bound sources.** Charge and current represented by polarization divergence, polarization time derivative and magnetization curl, including their surface distributions.

**Canonical momentum.** The derivative of a Lagrangian with respect to velocity or field time derivative; in a gauge coupling it differs from mechanical momentum by a potential term.

**Constitutive memory.** Dependence of material response on its earlier state or earlier fields, requiring more information than one instantaneous field value.

**Constraint propagation.** Preservation of compatible divergence data by Maxwell evolution; it does not correct an initially nonzero constraint residual.

**Dipole approximation.** A leading source-moment description whose wavelength and source-size assumptions must be separate from its observation-distance assumptions.

**Displacement current.** The electric-displacement time derivative in the macroscopic magnetic curl equation, or its vacuum electric-field counterpart, required with source continuity.

**Impedance.** The electric-to-magnetic-intensity amplitude ratio of the stated wave mode and material regime, distinct from its propagation speed.

**Invariant mass.** The positive timelike norm of a system's complete four-momentum divided by light speed, including internal energy in its rest frame.

**Lorentz factor.** The positive normalization of a subluminal boost or timelike velocity, diverging at the excluded light-speed endpoint.

**Mass shell.** The invariant relation between positive energy, mechanical momentum and rest mass; canonical momentum requires its potential correction first.

**Null field.** A nonzero electromagnetic tensor with both contracted field invariants zero; this is a local algebraic classification rather than a complete wave specification.

**Passivity.** Nonnegative net material absorption in the specified response regime, with signs tied to the declared harmonic convention.

**Phase and group velocity.** Motion of a fixed harmonic phase and the frequency derivative along a dispersion branch. Neither is an unconditional definition of signal speed in every material.

**Proper acceleration.** The magnitude of four-acceleration in the instantaneous rest frame, distinct from coordinate acceleration at high speed.

**Radiation zone.** Observation regime where inverse-distance radiative terms dominate relevant faster-decaying source fields; angular zeros require special care.

**Retarded factor.** One minus observation direction dotted with dimensionless source velocity at the retarded event, positive for the stated uniformly subluminal source class.

**Skin depth.** The amplitude-decay depth in a specified good-conductor harmonic approximation; its value depends on frequency and valid material parameters.

**Transverse mode.** A field or potential component orthogonal to a stated propagation direction or Fourier wave vector, with physical constraints distinguished from gauge description.
