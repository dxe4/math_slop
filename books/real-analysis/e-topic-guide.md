# Topic guide

This guide is an index-style map rather than a page-number index, so it remains
usable in both HTML and PDF editions.

## Definitions by topic

- **Approximation:** epsilon control (Chapters 1, 3, 6); Taylor approximation
  (Chapter 7); Bernstein polynomials (Chapter 9); Fourier projection (Chapter 12).
- **Completeness:** least upper bounds (Chapter 2); Cauchy criterion (Chapter 3);
  complete metric and Banach spaces (Chapters 10 and 13).
- **Convergence modes:** sequences (Chapter 3); series (Chapter 4); pointwise and
  uniform (Chapter 9); almost everywhere and $L^p$ (Chapter 11); mean-square
  (Chapter 12).
- **Set structure:** open, closed, compact, connected (Chapter 5); sigma-algebras
  and measurable sets (Chapter 11).
- **Function regularity:** continuity (Chapter 6); differentiability and convexity
  (Chapter 7); equicontinuity (Chapter 9); measurability (Chapter 11).
- **Function spaces:** supremum metric (Chapters 9–10); $L^p$ (Chapter 11); Banach,
  Hilbert, and dual spaces (Chapter 13).
- **Multivariable analysis:** Fréchet derivatives and Hessians (Chapter 16);
  inverse and implicit functions (Chapter 17); Jacobians and coordinate
  transformations (Chapter 18).
- **Advanced measure:** outer measure and Carathéodory construction (Chapter 19);
  signed measures and densities (Chapter 20); product measures (Chapter 22).
- **Generalized differentiation:** bounded variation (Chapter 15); absolute
  continuity (Chapter 20); weak derivatives and Sobolev spaces (Chapter 23).
- **Differential equations:** integral reformulation, contraction, Grönwall,
  continuation, and dependence on data (Chapter 24).
- **Probability:** elementary finite models (the concise primer); distribution functions and
  Stieltjes expectations (Chapter 15); random variables, expectation, independence,
  and convolution (Chapter 22).
- **Category and compact families:** meagre/residual sets, Baire, equicontinuity,
  Arzelà–Ascoli, and Stone–Weierstrass (Chapter 25).
- **Operator structure:** Hahn–Banach, uniform boundedness, open mapping, closed
  graph, weak-star compactness, and reflexivity (Chapter 26).
- **Hilbert and spectral methods:** projection, Riesz representation, orthonormal
  bases, compact operators, and regularisation (Chapter 27).
- **Harmonic analysis:** maximal functions and differentiation (Chapter 28);
  convolution, transforms, sampling, and distributions (Chapter 29).
- **Stochastic analysis:** conditioning, filtrations, martingales, stopping, and
  upcrossings (Chapter 30).
- **Variational and PDE analysis:** first variations and direct method
  (Chapter 31); weak elliptic and evolution equations (Chapter 32).
- **Polish probability:** complete separable spaces, standard Borel structure,
  probability kernels, regular conditional laws, and disintegration (Chapter 39).
- **Optimal transport:** couplings, Kantorovich duality, Wasserstein metrics,
  monotone rearrangement, and displacement interpolation (Chapter 40).
- **Dynamics:** recurrence, Koopman operators, mean and pointwise ergodic
  theorems, mixing, and equidistribution (Chapter 41).
- **Continuous-time probability:** Brownian motion, path regularity, Markov and
  martingale structure, quadratic variation, Itô integration, and Itô's formula
  (Chapter 42).
- **Interpolation and singular integrals:** weak spaces, Marcinkiewicz and
  Riesz–Thorin interpolation, principal values, and Calderón–Zygmund
  decomposition (Chapter 43).
- **Unbounded spectral theory:** closed operators, adjoint domains, resolvents,
  PVMs, Borel functional calculus, forms, and unitary groups (Chapter 44).
- **Vector-valued integration:** strong and weak measurability, Bochner and
  Pettis integrals, vector-valued Fubini, and the Radon–Nikodym property
  (Chapter 45).
- **Fredholm theory:** closed range, kernel and cokernel, index, compact
  perturbations, solvability alternatives, and integral equations (Chapter 46).
- **Convex representation:** extreme and exposed points, Carathéodory,
  Krein–Milman, Choquet representation, and uniform convexity (Chapter 47).
  Its [support clinic](47-extreme-points.md#sec-choquet-support) distinguishes
  full measure on the extreme set from ambient closed support; the
  [changed task](47-extreme-points.md#sec-choquet-support-task) classifies both
  for a compact Hilbert-space example.

## Major theorems by hypothesis

- **Bounded monotone sequence:** monotone convergence, Chapter 2.
- **Bounded sequence:** Bolzano–Weierstrass, Chapter 3.
- **Closed and bounded real set:** Heine–Borel, Chapter 5.
- **Continuous function on a compact set:** extreme values and uniform continuity,
  Chapter 6.
- **Continuous function on an interval:** intermediate values, Chapter 6.
- **Continuous on a closed interval and differentiable inside:** mean value theorem,
  Chapter 7.
- **Continuous on a closed interval:** Riemann integrability and FTC accumulation
  form, Chapter 8.
- **Uniformly convergent continuous functions:** continuous limit, Chapter 9.
- **Uniformly bounded and equicontinuous sequence:** Arzelà–Ascoli subsequence,
  Chapter 9.
- **Contraction on a complete metric space:** unique fixed point and iteration,
  Chapter 10.
- **Pointwise convergence with integrable domination:** dominated convergence,
  Chapter 11.
- **Continuous periodic function with Fejér means:** uniform Fourier approximation,
  Chapter 12.
- **Increasing continuous functions on compact space with continuous limit:** Dini's
  uniform convergence theorem, Chapter 14.
- **Continuous integrand and monotone integrator:** Riemann–Stieltjes existence,
  Chapter 15.
- **Continuously differentiable map with invertible derivative:** local inverse,
  Chapter 17.
- **Invertible partial derivative in dependent variables:** implicit function,
  Chapter 17.
- **Nonnegative product-space function:** Tonelli order exchange, Chapter 22.
- **Absolutely integrable product-space function:** Fubini order exchange,
  Chapter 22.
- **Absolute continuity of measures under sigma-finiteness:** Radon–Nikodym
  density, Chapter 20.
- **Convergence in measure plus uniform integrability:** $L^1$ convergence,
  Chapter 21.
- **Locally Lipschitz ODE vector field:** local existence and uniqueness,
  Chapter 24.
- **Bounded Sobolev family on a regular bounded domain:** compact embedding into a
  lower target norm, Chapter 23.
- **Complete metric space:** Baire category theorem, Chapter 25.
- **Equicontinuous pointwise-bounded family on compact domain:** relatively
  compact closure in the supremum norm, Chapter 25.
- **Pointwise-bounded family of bounded operators on a Banach domain:** uniform
  operator-norm bound, Chapter 26.
- **Bounded surjective linear map between Banach spaces:** open mapping and stable
  inverse consequences, Chapter 26.
- **Closed convex subset of a Hilbert space:** unique nearest-point projection,
  Chapter 27.
- **Compact self-adjoint operator:** orthogonal eigen-expansion, Chapter 27.
- **Locally integrable function:** almost-everywhere recovery by ball averages,
  Chapter 28.
- **Uniformly integrable martingale:** almost-sure and $L^1$ terminal
  representation, Chapter 30.
- **Coercive weakly lower-semicontinuous functional on a weakly closed class:**
  direct-method minimiser, Chapter 31.
- **Bounded coercive bilinear form on a Hilbert space:** unique Lax–Milgram
  solution, Chapter 32.
- **Standard Borel joint law with a declared marginal:** regular conditional
  kernel and disintegration, Chapter 39.
- **Finite-moment probability measures on a Polish space:** optimal couplings
  under the chapter's lower-semicontinuity hypotheses and a Wasserstein metric,
  Chapter 40.
- **Measure-preserving map and $L^2$ observable:** norm convergence of Cesàro
  orbit averages to the invariant projection, Chapter 41.
- **Measure-preserving map and $L^1$ observable:** almost-everywhere Birkhoff
  convergence to invariant conditional expectation, Chapter 41.
- **Adapted square-integrable step process:** Itô isometry and completion to a
  stochastic integral, Chapter 42.
- **Sublinear weak $(1,1)$ and strong $(\infty,\infty)$ operator:** strong
  $L^p$ bounds for $1<p<\infty$, Chapter 43.
- **Self-adjoint densely defined operator:** real spectrum, a unique PVM, and
  measurable functional calculus, Chapter 44.
- **Strongly measurable Banach-valued function with integrable norm:** Bochner
  integrability and the integral norm inequality, Chapter 45.
- **Bochner convergence with one scalar integrable majorant:** norm convergence
  of integrals, Chapter 45.
- **Identity minus a compact operator:** Fredholm index zero and the
  injective–surjective alternative, Chapter 46.
- **Nonempty compact convex set in a locally convex space:** closed convex
  generation by extreme points, Chapter 47.
- **Uniformly convex Banach space:** reflexivity and weak-plus-norm upgrade,
  Chapter 47.

## Counterexamples by failure mode

- **Oscillation:** $(-1)^n$ for bounded nonconvergence (Chapter 3);
  $\sin(1/x)$ for absent function limit (Chapter 6).
- **Terms tend to zero but series diverges:** harmonic series (Chapter 4).
- **Missing endpoint:** $(0,1)$ for noncompactness and unattained extrema
  (Chapters 5–6).
- **Dense discontinuities:** $1_{\mathbb Q}$ for Riemann failure (Chapter 8).
- **Moving boundary layer:** $x^n$ for pointwise but nonuniform convergence
  (Chapter 9).
- **Rapid oscillation:** $\sin(nx)$ for bounded but nonequicontinuous families
  (Chapter 9).
- **Infinite-dimensional separation:** coordinate vectors in $\ell^2$ for failure
  of closed-and-bounded compactness (Chapter 10).
- **Moving mass:** $n1_{(0,1/n)}$ for failure of integral interchange
  (Chapter 11).
- **Persistent overshoot:** Fourier sums near a jump (Chapter 12).
- **Smooth but nonanalytic:** $e^{-1/x^2}$ extended at zero (Chapter 7).
- **Directional but not Fréchet differentiability:**
  $x^3/(x^2+y^2)$ at the origin (Chapter 16).
- **Locally but not globally invertible:** exponential-polar coordinates
  (Chapter 17).
- **Nonmeasurable representative set:** the Vitali construction (Chapter 19).
- **Singular continuous accumulation:** Cantor measure and Cantor function
  (Chapters 19–20).
- **Convergence in measure without pointwise convergence:** the typewriter sequence
  (Chapter 21).
- **Order-dependent iterated sum:** the diagonal/off-diagonal array (Chapter 22).
- **Distributional but not Sobolev derivative:** a step function with a Dirac
  derivative (Chapter 23).
- **Existence without uniqueness:** $y'=2\sqrt{|y|}$ at zero (Chapter 24).
- **Finite-time blow-up:** $y'=y^2$ (Chapter 24).
- **Incomplete Baire domain:** $\mathbb Q$ as a union of nowhere-dense
  singletons (Chapter 25).
- **Bounded without equicontinuity:** $x^n$ on $[0,1]$ (Chapter 25).
- **Weak but not norm convergence:** the basis of $\ell^2$ (Chapter 27).
- **Classical derivative misses singular mass:** the Cantor function
  (Chapter 28).
- **Optional stopping without uniform control:** the doubling strategy
  (Chapter 30).
- **Stationary but not minimizing:** a saddle critical point (Chapter 31).
- **Ill-posed reverse evolution:** backward heat flow (Chapter 32).
- **Finite-dimensional laws without pathwise identity:** the random spike
  modification of the zero process (Chapters 39 and 42).
- **Weak convergence without Wasserstein convergence:** vanishing escaping mass
  with a persistent moment cost (Chapter 40).
- **Ergodic without mixing:** irrational circle rotation (Chapter 41).
- **Weak endpoint without strong endpoint:** $1/x$ and the interval Hilbert
  transform (Chapter 43).
- **Symmetric without self-adjoint:** momentum on $H_0^1(0,1)$ (Chapter 44).
- **Continuous spectrum without eigenvectors:** position multiplication on
  $L^2(\mathbb R)$ (Chapter 44).
- **Weak without strong measurability:** a nonseparable range can defeat the
  Bochner construction (Chapter 45).
- **Bounded perturbation without compactness:** $I-I=0$ is not Fredholm on an
  infinite-dimensional space (Chapter 46).
- **No extreme points without weak compactness:** the nonatomic $L^1$ unit ball
  (Chapter 47).

## Advanced theorem-dependency map

| destination | indispensable earlier engines | principal bridge |
|---|---|---|
| disintegration | Radon–Nikodym, product measures, Polish topology | kernels turn conditional identities into measurable families |
| Wasserstein geometry | weak convergence, tightness, lower semicontinuity | compactness of couplings plus cost control |
| ergodic limits | Hilbert projection, conditional expectation, measure preservation | Koopman fixed spaces and maximal inequalities |
| Brownian/Itô theory | Gaussian laws, martingales, $L^2$ completion | consistent laws, path regularity, and the Itô isometry |
| singular integrals | distribution functions, maximal estimates, Fourier multipliers | level splitting and cancellation of mean-zero pieces |
| unbounded spectral calculus | Hilbert adjoints, closed graph, Fourier multipliers, semigroups | domains plus projection-valued integration |
| Banach-valued integration | scalar measure, Banach completeness, bounded operators | integral-norm approximation by simple functions |
| Fredholm solvability | compact operators, adjoints, elliptic weak formulations | invertibility modulo compact errors |
| convex representation | separation, weak-star compactness, Radon measures | extreme points and barycentric limits |

The map is directional rather than linear. Chapter 42 uses the kernel language
of Chapter 39 and martingales from Chapter 30. Chapter 40 uses weak convergence
from Chapter 36. Chapter 43 closes a proof deferred in Chapter 28 and then feeds
back into differentiation theory. Chapter 44 unifies the compact spectral model
of Chapter 27, Fourier multipliers of Chapter 29, and generator language of
Chapter 35. Chapter 45 supplies the vector integral used by that semigroup
language. Chapter 46 continues compact spectral theory into solvability and
index, while Chapter 47 joins weak-star compactness, Radon representation, and
convex duality.

## Common proof engines

- **Triangle inequality:** uniqueness, limit algebra, Cauchy estimates, norm control.
- **Supremum construction:** roots, monotone limits, intermediate values.
- **Subsequence contradiction:** compactness and uniform continuity.
- **Finite subcover:** global bounds from local neighbourhoods.
- **Comparison with a geometric series:** numerical series, contraction errors,
  power-series uniformity.
- **Three-term approximation split:** uniform limit theorem and density arguments.
- **Near/far integral split:** approximate identities and Fejér convergence.
- **Positivity:** monotone convergence, Fatou, and measure interchange.
- **Linearization plus remainder:** Fréchet derivatives, inverse functions, and
  Jacobian change of variables.
- **Outer measure plus splitting criterion:** construction of Lebesgue measure.
- **Positive/negative decomposition:** BV functions and signed measures.
- **Rapid Cauchy subsequence:** completeness of $L^p$.
- **Test-function integration by parts:** weak derivatives and weak PDE solutions.
- **Fixed point plus invariant ball:** inverse functions and ODE existence.
- **Grönwall comparison:** uniqueness, continuation, and dependence on data.
- **Baire localisation:** pointwise coverage becomes uniform control on one ball.
- **Diagonal extraction plus equicontinuity:** compactness in function spaces.
- **Dual separation:** Hahn–Banach turns geometry into a functional witness.
- **Projection and orthogonality:** best approximation and spectral coefficients.
- **Covering selection plus weak type:** maximal estimates and differentiation.
- **Upcrossing control:** martingale convergence without pointwise monotonicity.
- **Coercivity plus weak lower semicontinuity:** variational existence.
- **Weak formulation plus energy estimate:** PDE existence and stability.
- **Inner/outer regularity plus compact exhaustion:** Radon measure approximation.
- **Convex separation plus conjugacy:** subgradients, dual problems, and monotone
  operators.
- **Resolvent estimates plus semigroup identities:** continuous-time evolution.
- **Tightness plus test functions:** weak convergence of probability laws.
- **Covering and blow-up arguments:** Hausdorff measure and rectifiability.
- **Homotopy invariance plus boundary avoidance:** nonlinear degree and existence.
- **Kernel identities plus monotone classes:** disintegration and conditional
  laws on standard Borel spaces.
- **Gluing plus Minkowski:** the Wasserstein triangle inequality.
- **Koopman projection plus maximal control:** norm and pointwise ergodic limits.
- **Gaussian moments plus $L^2$ completion:** Brownian regularity and Itô
  integration.
- **Layer cake plus level-dependent splitting:** interpolation from weak
  endpoints.
- **Mean-zero decomposition plus kernel smoothness:** singular-integral weak
  type.
- **Graph closure plus spectral integration:** unbounded functional calculus.
- **Simple approximation plus integral-norm completion:** Bochner integration.
- **Parametrix modulo compact errors:** Fredholm stability and index.
- **Separation plus compact faces:** Krein–Milman extreme-point generation.

Chapter 14, [sec-synthesis](14-synthesis.md#sec-synthesis), closes the first-course arc. Chapters 15–24 form a
second arc through measure, Sobolev, and ODE analysis. Chapters 25–32 form a
third arc in which category, duality, spectral decompositions, maximal estimates,
and weak compactness become reusable engines. The hints appendix provides entry
points without removing the responsibility to build the proof.

Chapters 33–38 form a fourth arc from locally compact measure theory to
nonlinear existence. A measure route follows Chapters 15–22, 28, 33, 36, and
37. An evolution route follows Chapters 13, 24, 27, 32, 34, and 35. A nonlinear
functional-analysis route follows Chapters 25–27, 31–32, 34, and 38. Each new
chapter opens with a repair kit for the topology, probability, geometry, or
operator language it needs, so these routes introduce no hidden prerequisite.

Chapters 39–44 form a fifth arc through modern probability, transport,
dynamics, harmonic analysis, and operator theory. A probability route follows
Chapters 22, 28, 30, 33, 36, 39, and 42. A geometry-of-measures route follows
Chapters 20, 31, 34, 36–37, and 40. An operator route follows Chapters 26–29,
35, 43, and 44. The explicit “Preparation from this book” heading in every
Part XII chapter states the shortest repair path for readers entering at that
point.

Chapters 45–47 form a sixth arc that repairs three standard graduate-analysis
dependencies: vector-valued integration for evolution, Fredholm theory for
compact perturbations, and extreme-point representation for weakly compact
convex sets. The short evolution route is Chapters 35, 45, and 46. The convex
representation route is Chapters 26, 33–34, and 47.

## Executable Python route

The [analysis Python laboratory](h-python-laboratory.md) connects finite code
to epsilon witnesses, Cauchy tails, completeness, uniform convergence, Darboux
enclosures, monotone convergence, mollification, contractions, probability,
Brownian quadratic variation, epsilon–delta witnesses, concentration without
domination, Fourier overshoot, $L^p$ norms, and equicontinuity. Each of the
twenty examples names the sampled norm, grid, tail, or precision and then states
the theorem still needed beyond computation. The final five laboratories make
rational bisection, Bernstein approximation, the Cantor function, dominated
convergence, and weak convergence directly inspectable formula by formula.
