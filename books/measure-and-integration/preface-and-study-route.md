
# Preface and Study Route {#section-1-preface-and-study-route}

Measure theory is a theory of reliable approximation. The first part constructs a size for sets and an integral for functions. The middle part proves which limiting operations preserve that integral. Later chapters use products, densities, differentiation and representation to connect the abstract theory with analysis and probability.

This L4 book has sixteen chapters. Chapters 1–8 build the measurable/integral language and convergence machinery; chapters 9–14 develop extension, products, densities, differentiation, representation and convolution; the last two chapters connect probability and synthesis. The earlier Real Analysis measure chapters have moved here with their semantic IDs and eighty original exercises preserved. Their old addresses have declared compatibility destinations.

The required preparation is the L3 Real Analysis primary through Riemann integration, uniform convergence and power series. Do not interpret an unfamiliar symbol as the only obstacle: constructing a simple approximation, separating positive and negative parts, or justifying a limit often requires a distinct proof technique.

For each theorem write its hypotheses before using it. Increasing nonnegative approximation, domination by one integrable function, convergence in measure and convergence in norm are different promises. Keep a counterexample beside each attempted weakening. The exercises provide staged help and full arguments; personal reading marks do not certify those arguments.
