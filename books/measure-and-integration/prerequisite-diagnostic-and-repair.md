
# Prerequisite Diagnostic and Repair

Before beginning, use [Real Analysis](#/books/proof-safe-real-analysis), especially its chapters on sequences and series, Riemann integration and uniform convergence. You should be able to negate a quantified convergence definition, prove a countable union countable, and compare a nonnegative series with a geometric majorant.

Three short diagnostics identify the relevant habits. Explain why the indicator of the rationals is not Riemann integrable on an interval. State exactly which norm makes uniform convergence a single schedule for all inputs. Construct a pointwise convergent sequence whose integrals do not converge to the integral of the limit. If the last question is unfamiliar, study shrinking triangular spikes of fixed area; the convergence-theorem chapters will later explain the missing domination.

You should also distinguish an arbitrary set function from a measure and distinguish finite addition from countable addition. The opening chapter develops those definitions; it does not require you to know them in advance. Later chapters state new prerequisites at the point of use, particularly topology for Radon representation and linear functional language for duality.
