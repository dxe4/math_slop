
# Glossary and Theorem Map

**Sigma algebra:** a collection admitting complements and countable unions. The opening chapter proves closure under countable intersections and differences.

**Measure:** a nonnegative countably additive size on a sigma algebra, with empty set of size zero. Infinity is allowed.

**Counting measure:** cardinality, with every infinite set assigned infinity. A singleton has size one.

**Dirac measure:** one unit of mass at a fixed point. It contrasts atomic size with length.

**Null set:** a measurable set of measure zero. Countable unions of such sets are null; arbitrary unions need not be.

**Almost everywhere:** outside a measurable null set. A countable family of almost-everywhere assertions can share one exceptional null set by taking their union.

**Continuity from below:** measures of increasing sets converge to the measure of their union, without a finite-mass hypothesis.

**Continuity from above:** measures of decreasing sets converge to the measure of their intersection when some member has finite measure. Counting-measure tails show why that condition matters.

**Carathéodory measurability and extension:** chapters 2 and 9 prove the splitting criterion, outer-measure restriction and premeasure extension, with sigma-finite uniqueness distinguished from existence.

**Lebesgue measure and regularity:** chapter 3 proves Euclidean normalization, completion, compact/open approximation, affine scaling and Lipschitz null preservation.

**Integral and convergence:** chapters 4–6 build measurable/simple approximation and the integral, then prove monotone, Fatou and dominated convergence. Positivity and absolute integrability have different roles.

**Lp completeness and modes:** chapters 7–8 prove norm completeness, Egorov, the subsequence principle, finite-measure Vitali, complete convergence-in-measure metrics and Lusin approximation.

**Product measure:** chapter 10 constructs the sigma-finite product from section integrals and proves Tonelli/Fubini, with explicit completion qualifications.

**Hahn/Jordan and Radon–Nikodym:** chapter 11 isolates signed variation, proves density representation and Lebesgue decomposition, and derives conditional expectation from restricted integral information.

**Lebesgue point and absolute continuity:** chapter 12 proves the maximal estimate, differentiation, singular zero density and the exact absolutely continuous fundamental theorem. A general BV function has an integrable derivative but may retain singular change.

**Radon/Riesz representation:** chapter 13 proves the positive Cc and real signed C0 representation theorems on locally compact second-countable metric spaces. It states this topology explicitly.

**Convolution:** chapter 14 proves Young's exponents, finite-Lp approximate identities, smooth density, pointwise Lebesgue-point recovery and weak-derivative smoothing.

**Probability and synthesis:** chapter 15 constructs countably independent inputs and quantile laws, then proves conditional projection, Borel–Cantelli, tail zero–one and the finite-first-moment weak law. Chapter 16 separates concentration, escape, cancellation, singular change and representative effects and proves a global Vitali criterion.
