
# References and Further Reading {#section-1-references-and-further-reading}

This bibliography identifies useful comparison texts, historical sources, and modern treatments. The book is written under CC BY-SA 4.0; standard definitions and geometric conventions belong to shared practice. References are included for further reading, historical context, alternative treatments, and broader coverage.

A named result or study is a retrieval convention, not a complete history. Geometric ideas are often the product of long development across languages, institutions, and communities; a modern statement may differ sharply from an early source.

<a id="section-5-axiomatic-geometry-and-foundations"></a>
## Axiomatic geometry and foundations {#section-7-axiomatic-geometry-and-foundations}

- Hilbert, D. (1899). *Grundlagen der Geometrie*. Teubner. Authorized English translation by E. J. Townsend: *The Foundations of Geometry* (Open Court, 1902). The classical modern axiomatization of Euclidean geometry. Later editions include additions by Bernays. Read with awareness that Hilbert's continuity axioms evolved across editions.
- Hartshorne, R. (2000). *Geometry: Euclid and Beyond*. Springer. A rigorous undergraduate treatment of Euclidean and non-Euclidean geometry from an axiomatic point of view. Includes careful treatments of constructibility, area, and hyperbolic models.
- Borsuk, K., & Szmielew, W. (1960). *Foundations of Geometry: Euclidean and Bolyai–Lobachevskian Geometry, Projective Geometry*. Translated by Erwin Marquit. North-Holland. A formal development of Euclidean and hyperbolic geometry with detailed metamathematical discussion.
- Greenberg, M. J. (2008). *Euclidean and Non-Euclidean Geometries: Development and History* (4th ed.). W. H. Freeman. A historically informed textbook with extensive discussion of the parallel postulate and model construction.

These works use different conventions for betweenness, continuity, and the parallel axiom. Comparison is most useful when those conventions are recorded rather than blended.

<a id="section-14-projective-and-finite-geometry"></a>
## Projective and finite geometry {#section-16-projective-and-finite-geometry}

- Coxeter, H. S. M. (1961). *Introduction to Geometry*. Wiley. A broad survey of geometry from classical to modern, with careful attention to projective and finite geometries. Coxeter's notation and diagram conventions differ from modern homogeneous-coordinate treatments.
- Coxeter, H. S. M. (1987). *Projective Geometry* (2nd ed.). Springer. https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4612-4176-3. A concise introduction to projective planes, conics, and quadrics.
- Dembowski, P. (1968). *Finite Geometries*. Springer. The standard reference for finite projective planes, block designs, and combinatorial properties.
- Hughes, D. R., & Piper, F. C. (1973). *Projective Planes*. Springer. A comprehensive graduate-level treatment of projective planes, coordinatization, and configurational theorems.
- Moorhouse, G. E., & Williford, J. (2009). "Embedding Finite Partial Linear Spaces in Finite Translation Nets." *Journal of Geometry*, 91(1–2), 73–83. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00022-008-2066-4. The introduction records Hall's free-closure embedding into a possibly infinite projective plane and separates it from the open finite-host problem; the paper's own theorem concerns finite translation nets.

The finite-geometry literature varies sharply in notation for block designs and in the treatment of non-Desarguesian planes. A reader moving between texts should verify the exact definition of "projective plane" and "order" in each source.

<a id="section-24-hyperbolic-and-elliptic-geometry"></a>
## Hyperbolic and elliptic geometry {#section-26-hyperbolic-and-elliptic-geometry}

- Ratcliffe, J. G. (2006). *Foundations of Hyperbolic Manifolds* (2nd ed.). Springer. A graduate treatment of hyperbolic geometry in dimensions two and three, with thorough discussion of models and isometry groups.
- Beardon, A. F. (1983). *The Geometry of Discrete Groups*. Springer. A standard reference for Fuchsian groups, hyperbolic tessellations, and discrete transformation groups.
- Berger, M. (1987). *Geometry I*. Translated by M. Cole and S. Levy. Universitext, Springer. https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-540-93815-6. The first volume of a comprehensive encyclopedic treatment with extensive coverage of spherical and hyperbolic geometry in the broader context of Riemannian geometry.
- Berger, M. (1987). *Geometry II*. Translated by M. Cole and S. Levy. Universitext, Springer. https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-540-93816-3. The second volume continues that treatment.

<a id="section-31-convexity-and-geometric-measure-theory"></a>
## Convexity and geometric measure theory {#section-33-convexity-and-geometric-measure-theory}

- Schneider, R. (2014). *Convex Bodies: The Brunn–Minkowski Theory* (2nd ed.). Cambridge University Press. The standard reference for convex geometry, including mixed volumes, surface area measures, and related inequalities.
- Federer, H. (1969). *Geometric Measure Theory*. Springer. The classical comprehensive treatise. The coarea formula, Hausdorff measure, and rectifiability are treated in full generality.
- Evans, L. C., & Gariepy, R. F. (2015). *Measure Theory and Fine Properties of Functions* (rev. ed.). CRC Press. A more accessible introduction to Lebesgue measure, Hausdorff measure, and the coarea formula.
- Morgan, F. (2016). *Geometric Measure Theory: A Beginner's Guide* (5th ed.). Academic Press. An introductory survey with intuitive explanations and illustrations.

<a id="section-38-transformation-geometry-and-discrete-groups"></a>
## Transformation geometry and discrete groups {#section-40-transformation-geometry-and-discrete-groups}

- Artin, E. (1957). *Geometric Algebra*. Interscience Publishers. A concise development of affine and projective geometry from the group-theoretic viewpoint.
- Armstrong, M. A. (1988). *Groups and Symmetry*. Springer. An undergraduate introduction to group actions, wallpaper groups, and tessellations.
- Conway, J. H., Burgiel, H., & Goodman-Strauss, C. (2008). *The Symmetries of Things*. A K Peters. A visually rich treatment of discrete symmetry groups in the plane, sphere, and hyperbolic plane.

<a id="section-44-modern-directions-and-status-boundaries"></a>
## Modern directions and status boundaries {#section-46-modern-directions-and-status-boundaries}

- Milnor, J. (1956). "On Manifolds Homeomorphic to the 7-Sphere." *Annals of Mathematics*, 64(2), 399–405. A primary source for exotic smooth structures in dimension seven; it rules out the claim that smooth Poincaré uniqueness holds in every dimension above four.
- Matschke, B. (2014). "A Survey on the Square Peg Problem." *Notices of the American Mathematical Society*, 61(4), 346–352. https://doi.org/10.1090/noti1100. A status survey for the arbitrary-Jordan-curve problem and its regular special cases. The distinct research manuscript *On the Square Peg Problem and Its Relatives* is arXiv:1001.0186.
- Stromquist, W. (1989). "Inscribed Squares and Square-Like Quadrilaterals in Closed Curves." *Mathematika*, 36(2), 187–197. https://doi.org/10.1112/S0025579300013061. A primary source for the smooth, continuously-turning-tangent case; it explicitly leaves broader curves outside that theorem.
- Chambers, G. R. (2025). "On the Square Peg Problem." *Discrete & Computational Geometry*, 73, 1144–1153. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00454-025-00720-x. A primary recent partial result for curves sufficiently close to a $C^2$ Jordan curve; it does not settle the arbitrary continuous case.

<a id="section-51-historical-attribution-checklist"></a>
## Historical attribution checklist {#section-53-historical-attribution-checklist}

Every historical or priority claim should record:

1. a classification as contemporary exposition, secondary history, translated primary source, or primary source;
2. verified author, title, edition, publisher or archive, and stable locator;
3. a note explaining whether the citation supports provenance, formulation, evidence, example, or broader context;
4. a check that the modern terminology is not projected unchanged backward;
5. careful comparison of primary sources for culturally broad claims and disputed priority.

<a id="section-61-reading-route-recommendations"></a>
## Reading-route recommendations {#section-63-reading-route-recommendations}

For a deeper treatment of axiomatic foundations, proceed to Hartshorne's *Geometry: Euclid and Beyond*. For projective and finite geometry, continue into Coxeter's *Introduction to Geometry* or Dembowski's *Finite Geometries*. For convexity and measure theory, continue into Schneider's *Convex Bodies* or Federer's *Geometric Measure Theory*. For transformation groups and tessellations, continue into Beardon's *The Geometry of Discrete Groups* or Conway et al.'s *The Symmetries of Things*.

<a id="section-65-citation-policy-for-contributors"></a>
## Citation policy for contributors {#section-67-citation-policy-for-contributors}

Paraphrase sources and cite the job they perform. Do not copy exercise sequences, distinctive examples, diagrams, or prose merely because a theorem is standard. Direct quotations require exact locators and license or fair-use review. When sources disagree, record the disagreement. When provenance is unknown, label it unknown rather than inventing a clean lineage.

These sources are cited for the mathematics rather than copied at length.

The earlier Hartshorne and Greenberg bibliographic records remain the roadmap's book-scale comparison targets. The explicit line-order/ruler package is not silently identified with a minimal Hilbert list by an unproved equivalence argument. Standard neutral SSS and the full rectangle/zero-defect/similarity equivalence remain named imported foundations results where complete proofs are not supplied. The finite incidence inequality is now proved; its deeper equality classification is distinguished from that argument. Advanced finite-plane obstruction, general group classification and measure-theory claims retain their stated scope.

The earlier transformation, measure, tessellation and modern-directions readings remain available through their current chapter destinations, with their original exercises and complete solutions.
