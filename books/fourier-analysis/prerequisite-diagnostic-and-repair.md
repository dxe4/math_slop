
# Prerequisite Diagnostic and Repair

Begin with [Real Analysis](#/books/proof-safe-real-analysis): Riemann integration, geometric sums, uniform convergence and integration by parts. Complex exponentials use the elementary complex-number and trigonometric identities developed in Complex Analysis. The periodic core treats continuous and finite-piece C1 functions, whose integrals can be Riemann integrals.

Before coefficient calculation, integrate one integer exponential over a whole period and explain why the zero frequency is exceptional. Before convergence, distinguish a fixed point from one that approaches a jump as the degree grows. Before FFT, compute a two-entry transform with its inverse scaling and explain why frequencies differing by the sample count agree on the grid.

General measure-based L2 spaces, null equivalence, arbitrary integrable functions and distributional extensions need the measure route. The current finite projection chapter does not assume completeness of its Riemann function class. The mean-square and transform chapters establish their own Hilbert completions and density/extension results, while the Measure route identifies those completions with the usual Lebesgue spaces.

Before distributions, derive the test-function derivative sign and the transform of a point mass. Before the Dirichlet chapter, review the Complex Analysis Taylor theorem on a disk and locally uniform holomorphic limits. Before equidistribution, bound a finite geometric sum and state which continuous density theorem extends that calculation beyond finite polynomials.
