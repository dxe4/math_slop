## 15.1 Local cohomology

Let $R$ be a commutative Noetherian ring and $I \subseteq R$ an ideal. The **local cohomology** functors $H^{i}_{I}$ are the right derived functors of the **$I$-torsion functor**

$$\Gamma_{I}(M) = \{m \in M : I^{n}m = 0 \text{ for some } n\}.$$

Equivalently, $H^{i}_{I}(M)$ can be computed as the colimit

$$H^{i}_{I}(M) = \varinjlim_{n} \operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}(R/I^{n}, M).$$

**Example 15.1.** Let $R = k[x_{1}, \dots, x_{n}]$ and $I = (x_{1}, \dots, x_{n})$. The top Cech quotient identifies $H^{n}_{I}(R)$ with the $k$-span of monomials

$$x_1^{-a_1}\cdots x_n^{-a_n}\qquad(a_i\ge1\text{ for every }i).$$

This is not the ordinary polynomial ring $k[x_1^{-1},\ldots,x_n^{-1}]$: multiplication by $x_i$ is truncated to zero when it would remove the last negative power of $x_i$. Equivalently,

$$H_I^n(R)\cong
\frac{R_{x_1\cdots x_n}}
{\sum_iR_{x_1\cdots\widehat{x_i}\cdots x_n}}.$$

**Worked example 15.2.** Compute $H^{0}_{(x)}(k[x])$.

The $I$-torsion submodule of $k[x]$ consists of polynomials annihilated by some power of $x$. Only the zero polynomial satisfies this, so $H^{0}_{(x)}(k[x]) = 0$. However, $H^{1}_{(x)}(k[x]) \cong k[x, x^{-1}] / k[x]$, which is nonzero.


### Derived torsion and the Ext colimit, proved directly

For any ideal I, elements killed by I to some power form a submodule, and taking that submodule is left exact by restriction of an exact sequence's kernel. Maps from R/Iⁿ to M identify with elements killed by Iⁿ by evaluating at one. The transition maps come from R/I^(n+1)→R/Iⁿ, so their direct limit is exactly Γ_I(M).

Filtered colimits of modules are exact: any equation making a represented element zero holds at a later finite stage, where exactness supplies the required preimage; the finitely many representatives in an addition can likewise be moved to a common stage. Thus they commute with kernels, images and cohomology of complexes. Apply the preceding Hom identity termwise to an injective resolution of M. Its direct-limit complex is Γ_I applied to that resolution, and its stage cohomology is Ext. This proves the displayed Ext-colimit formula, including its natural maps, rather than merely matching its degree-zero values.

### Principal support, including zero divisors

For a Noetherian ring and f∈R, local cohomology is computed by the two-term cochain complex M→M_f. To prove this, first let J be injective. Its localisation map J→J_f is onto. Given y/fⁿ, choose k large enough that the annihilators of f^k and f^(n+k) agree, using ascending-chain termination. The map on the ideal generated by f^(n+k) sending r f^(n+k) to r f^k y is well-defined by that annihilator equality. Injectivity extends it to R, giving z with f^(n+k)z=f^k y. The fraction relation says z maps to y/fⁿ.

The kernel is precisely f-power torsion, so 0→Γ_(f)J→J→J_f→0 is exact. Apply this termwise to an injective resolution of M. Localisation is exact, so the two latter complexes have cohomology M and M_f only in degree zero. Their long exact sequence gives H⁰_(f)(M)=ker(M→M_f), H¹_(f)(M)=coker(M→M_f), and zero in every higher degree. This proof did not cancel f or assume it a nonzerodivisor.

For R=k[x], M=R and f=x, the kernel is zero and the cokernel has basis x raised to negative positive powers modulo ordinary polynomials. Multiplication by x removes a negative power until it becomes an ordinary polynomial and hence zero in the quotient. For a module killed by some power of f, localisation is zero and the result is instead all of M in degree zero. These are different support behaviors with the same two-term construction.

### Coordinate support from Koszul resolutions

The earlier multivariable formula can be proved without assuming a general Čech comparison theorem. In R=k[x₁,…,x_d], the ideals J_n=(x₁ⁿ,…,x_dⁿ) are cofinal with powers of the coordinate maximal ideal: J_n lies in its n-th power and its power d(n−1)+1 lies in J_n by counting exponents. Thus the same torsion and Ext-colimit construction can use these J_n.

The powers are a regular sequence: after quotienting previous powers, the remaining ring is free over the polynomial ring in the remaining variables on the bounded monomials of the earlier variables. Multiplication by the next variable power is injective coefficientwise. The proved Koszul resolution therefore applies. Its dual cochain complex has cohomology only in degree d, equal to R/J_n. This also follows inductively from its multiplication cone: the previous quotient's next power is injective, leaving only its cokernel in the next degree.

The map to the next Ext group multiplies by x₁⋯x_d. A comparison of the two Koszul resolutions sends each generator for x_i^(n+1) to x_i times the generator for x_iⁿ, so the top comparison gives exactly that product. Identify a monomial h modulo J_n with h divided by (x₁⋯x_d)ⁿ in the Laurent quotient. The transition leaves this fraction unchanged. Bounded numerator exponents give exactly monomials negative in every variable, and every such monomial eventually occurs. The other Laurent monomials lie in a localisation omitting at least one variable and vanish in the quotient. This proves the asserted top module and vanishing in other degrees. The geometric Čech formalism is not needed for this polynomial calculation.

## 15.2 Local duality

Let $(R, \mathfrak{m})$ be a Noetherian local ring of dimension $d$ with residue field $k = R/\mathfrak{m}$. The **Matlis dual** of an $R$-module $M$ is $M^{\vee} = \operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M, E)$, where $E = E_{R}(k)$ is the injective hull of the residue field.

**Theorem 15.3 (Local duality; imported).** If $R$ is a Cohen--Macaulay local ring admitting a canonical module $\omega_{R}$, then for every finitely generated $R$-module $M$ and every $i$,

$$H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M) \cong \operatorname{Ext}^{d-i}_{R}(M, \omega_{R})^{\vee}.$$

If $R$ is Gorenstein, $\omega_{R} = R$, and local duality simplifies to

$$H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M) \cong \operatorname{Ext}^{d-i}_{R}(M, R)^{\vee}.$$

The exact tagged source first states the result for a normalized dualizing complex: Stacks Project Theorem 47.18.3 ([Tag 0A84](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0A84)). Lemma 47.18.4 ([Tag 0AAK](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0AAK)) identifies the $\mathfrak m$-adic completion of the relevant Ext module with the Matlis dual of local cohomology. In the Cohen--Macaulay specialization, the normalized complex is $\omega_R[d]$; applying Matlis duality gives the displayed formula. Completion remains visible in the reverse-dual statement, while $\operatorname{Hom}_R(-,E)$ does not distinguish a finite module from its $\mathfrak m$-adic completion. These steps, including existence of $\omega_R$, are imported.

## 15.3 Grothendieck duality

Here is a safe absolute form. Let $f:X\to\operatorname{Spec}k$ be a proper finite-type scheme over a field, use the normalization $\omega_X^\bullet:=f^!k$, and let $\mathcal F^\bullet\in D^b_{\mathrm{coh}}(X)$. Stacks Project Lemma 48.27.1 ([Tag 0FVV](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0FVV)) supplies this normalized complex, the duality, and the smooth identification used below; Remark 48.27.2 records the corresponding trace

$$\operatorname{Tr}:R\Gamma(X,\omega_X^\bullet)\longrightarrow k$$

and an isomorphism

$$R\Gamma\!\left(X,R\mathcal Hom_X(\mathcal F^\bullet,\omega_X^\bullet)\right)
\simeq R\operatorname{Hom}_k\!\left(R\Gamma(X,\mathcal F^\bullet),k\right).$$

In a relative version, under the standard finiteness and dualizability hypotheses for a proper morphism $f:X\to Y$, the right adjoint $f^!$ to $Rf_*$ satisfies

$$Rf_*R\mathcal Hom_X(\mathcal F^\bullet,f^!K_Y)
\simeq R\mathcal Hom_Y(Rf_*\mathcal F^\bullet,K_Y)$$

for an appropriate dualizing complex $K_Y$. Properness alone does not imply that $f^!\mathcal O_Y$ is an absolute dualizing complex. These existence and boundedness statements are imported.

For a finite-dimensional commutative $k$-algebra $R$, put $\omega_R=\operatorname{Hom}_k(R,k)$. Tensor--Hom adjunction gives, for every finite $R$-module $M$,

$$\operatorname{Hom}_R(M,\omega_R)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_k(M,k).$$

The functor on the right is exact, so $\omega_R$ is injective and $\operatorname{Ext}^i_R(M,\omega_R)=0$ for $i>0$. This is the finite affine duality statement; it does not involve an undefined dimension or maximal ideal.


### Finite affine duality with an actual evaluation map

For a finite-dimensional commutative k-algebra R, the earlier adjunction with ω=Hom_k(R,k) sends a module map u:M→ω to m↦u(m)(1). Its inverse sends a k-linear functional λ to the map m↦(r↦λ(rm)). These formulas are inverse and R-linear under the coinduced action. The k-dual is exact because vector-space exact sequences split, so ω is injective as an R-module by the same extension correspondence.

For finite M, the evaluation M→Hom_R(Hom_R(M,ω),ω) corresponds to ordinary k-linear bidual evaluation, hence is an isomorphism by finite dimension. This gives a proved contravariant duality on finite modules with its natural maps, rather than a duality on all infinite modules.

For R=k[t]/(tʳ), the functional selecting the coefficient of t^(r−1) makes the multiplication pairing nondegenerate: the monomial bases pair in opposite order. Thus R≅ω as R-modules and R is injective. This finite local example illustrates a genuine dualising module without requiring scheme-theoretic duality. For k[x,y]/(x,y)², a map from its maximal ideal sending x to zero and y to x is linear but cannot extend to multiplication by an element of R. Baer's criterion proves R not injective there, while ω is still injective. Thus finite dimension alone does not identify a ring with its k-dual.

## 15.4 Serre duality

For a smooth projective variety $f:X\to\operatorname{Spec}k$ of dimension $n$, the imported smooth-duality theorem identifies this normalized complex as $f^!k\simeq\omega_{X}[n]$, where $\omega_{X} = \Lambda^{n}\Omega_{X/k}$ is the canonical bundle. Grothendieck duality then becomes **Serre duality**:

$$H^{i}(X, \mathcal{F})^{*} \cong \operatorname{Ext}^{n-i}_{X}(\mathcal{F}, \omega_{X})$$

for every coherent sheaf $\mathcal{F}$. When $\mathcal{F}$ is locally free, $\operatorname{Ext}^{n-i}_{X}(\mathcal{F}, \omega_{X}) \cong H^{n-i}(X, \mathcal{F}^{\vee} \otimes \omega_{X})$.

**Worked example 15.4.** Let $X = \mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}$ and $\mathcal{F} = \mathcal{O}(m)$. Then $H^{0}(X, \mathcal{O}(m)) = k^{m+1}$ for $m \geq 0$ and $0$ for $m < 0$. By Serre duality,

$$H^{1}(X, \mathcal{O}(m)) \cong H^{0}(X, \mathcal{O}(-m-2))^{*}.$$

Hence $H^{1}(X, \mathcal{O}(m)) = k^{-m-1}$ for $m \leq -2$ and $0$ for $m \geq -1$.

**Retained illustrative arithmetic (not executed).**

```python
# Verify Euler characteristic of P^1 with O(m) via Serre duality
# chi(O(m)) = h^0 - h^1 = (m+1) - 0 = m+1 for m >= 0
# chi(O(m)) = 0 - (-m-1) = m+1 for m <= -2
# chi(O(-1)) = 0 - 0 = 0
# Riemann-Roch for P^1: chi(O(m)) = m + 1
for m in range(-5, 6):
    if m >= 0:
        h0 = m + 1
        h1 = 0
    elif m <= -2:
        h0 = 0
        h1 = -m - 1
    else:
        h0 = 0
        h1 = 0
    chi = h0 - h1
    print(f"m={m}: h0={h0}, h1={h1}, chi={chi}")
```

The displayed formulas give this Euler identity algebraically. No execution or independent review is claimed.


### The two-chart polynomial calculation on the projective line

The numerical twist example also has an explicit Čech model. On the two standard affine charts of the projective line, its degree-zero terms are k[t] and t^m k[t inverse], viewed inside the Laurent ring. The kernel of their difference map is their intersection, with basis t^j for zero≤j≤m when m is nonnegative, and zero otherwise. The cokernel is the Laurent ring modulo their sum. For m≤−2 its basis consists of powers with m<j<0, giving dimension minus m minus one; for m≥−1 it is zero.

These calculations prove the dimensions of the two-chart complex directly. Identifying that complex with derived sheaf cohomology uses the standard affine-cover acyclicity theorem for quasi-coherent sheaves, an explicitly imported geometric input here. The Serre-duality theorem supplied earlier gives the same dimensions through the normalized canonical bundle; agreement of the two formulas is not a proof of either general comparison theorem.

## 15.5 Common errors

- **Confusing local and global duality.** Local duality is about modules over a local ring and Matlis duality. Global duality (Serre, Grothendieck) is about sheaves on a scheme and involves the dualizing complex.
- **Forgetting the Cohen-Macaulay hypothesis.** Local duality in its simplest form requires Cohen-Macaulayness. For general rings, one needs a dualizing complex.
- **Using the proper trace formula without properness.** The displayed coherent-cohomology duality uses a proper map. The broader $f^!$ formalism also exists for important nonproper morphisms, but its adjunction, support conditions, and boundedness hypotheses differ.
- **Ignoring the derived category.** Duality theorems are naturally stated in the derived category. The naive statement $H^{i}(X, \mathcal{F})^{*} \cong H^{n-i}(X, \mathcal{F}^{\vee} \otimes \omega_{X})$ only holds for locally free sheaves; for general coherent sheaves, one needs Ext in the derived category.

### HA-13-06 — Nilpotent principal support

If f is nilpotent in a Noetherian ring, compute all local cohomology of an arbitrary module with support in (f).

<details><summary>Hint 1</summary>
One ring power of f kills the whole module.
</details>
<details><summary>Hint 2</summary>
The torsion functor is therefore identity and localisation at f is zero.
</details>
<details><summary>Solution</summary>
Every element is killed by the fixed nilpotent power, so Γ_(f)(M)=M for every M. This is an exact identity functor, with no positive derived functors. Equivalently the proved two-term complex is M→0 because inverting a nilpotent makes the zero ring and zero localised module. Thus H⁰ is M and all positive groups vanish. No cancellation of f is permissible or needed in this example.
</details>

### HA-13-07 — Cofinal coordinate powers

Prove (x₁,…,x_d) raised to d(n−1)+1 is contained in (x₁ⁿ,…,x_dⁿ), and explain its use in the local-cohomology calculation.

<details><summary>Hint 1</summary>
A monomial with all exponents below n has total degree at most d(n−1).
</details>
<details><summary>Hint 2</summary>
The reverse cofinal containment uses the n-th ideal power.
</details>
<details><summary>Solution</summary>
Every monomial of the stated larger degree has some exponent at least n by the bound, and is divisible by the corresponding generator x_iⁿ. This proves containment of the whole power ideal. Conversely each x_iⁿ lies in the n-th maximal-ideal power. Thus elements killed by powers of one family are exactly those killed by some member of the other. Their torsion functors and the termwise Hom unions agree, allowing the Ext computation to use the explicitly resolved coordinate-power ideals.
</details>

### HA-13-08 — Local cohomology over the integers

For a prime p, identify H¹_(p)(Z) and prove its underlying abelian group is divisible.

<details><summary>Hint 1</summary>
Use the principal-support quotient Z[1/p]/Z.
</details>
<details><summary>Hint 2</summary>
Division by p raises the denominator; division by a number prime to p uses an inverse modulo a denominator power.
</details>
<details><summary>Solution</summary>
The group consists of classes a/p^r modulo integers. A p-th root is obtained by replacing the denominator with p^(r+1). For q coprime to p, choose b with qb congruent to a modulo p^r; then b/p^r is a q-th root of the class. Factoring any positive integer into its p-power and coprime parts proves divisibility by that integer. Hence the group is injective as a Z-module by Baer. The quotient description also shows every element is p-power torsion, consistent with the specified support.
</details>

### HA-13-09 — A finite dual basis

For R=k[t]/(t³), show its k-dual is isomorphic to R as an R-module by the coefficient-of-t² pairing.

<details><summary>Hint 1</summary>
Pair the ordered basis one,t,t² against t²,t,one.
</details>
<details><summary>Hint 2</summary>
Use the action (rφ)(s)=φ(sr) on the dual.
</details>
<details><summary>Solution</summary>
The coefficient-of-t² product pairing has one on each opposite basis position and zero elsewhere, so it is nondegenerate. The map a↦(s↦coefficient of t² in sa) is therefore a vector-space isomorphism. Multiplication of a by r changes its value to the functional s↦the coefficient in sra, exactly the coinduced R-action. Thus it is a module isomorphism. Since the dual is injective by the proved adjunction, R is self-injective in this finite example.
</details>

### HA-13-10 — Finite dimension does not force self-injectivity

For R=k[x,y]/(x,y)², prove the ideal map x↦0,y↦x is linear and has no extension to R.

<details><summary>Hint 1</summary>
Both the ideal and its image are killed by the maximal ideal.
</details>
<details><summary>Hint 2</summary>
A map R→R is multiplication by its value at one.
</details>
<details><summary>Solution</summary>
The ideal has k-basis x,y and the ring action on it factors through the scalar residue field, so the specified k-linear map is R-linear. An extending map would multiply by an element with constant coefficient a. Its values on x and y would be ax and ay. The first required value forces a=0, making the second value zero rather than x. Hence no extension exists, and Baer's criterion proves R not injective. Its k-dual remains injective, so those two finite modules are not isomorphic despite having the same vector-space dimension.
</details>
