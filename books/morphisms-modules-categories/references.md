
# References and Further Reading

The current reorganisation retains all earlier Euler sources and identities. Semantic spectral-sequence, homological-dimension and duality readings copy the existing numbered mathematical source without retiring it; group cohomology adapts the retained Computational Algebra source. The exactness reading retains the relevant earlier adjunction sections. The fourteen-chapter primary manuscript is now authored, including full module-class, tensor–Hom, comparison, balanced derived-functor and extension-classification proofs. Its derived, spectral-sequence and duality chapters distinguish proofs developed here from named imported theorems; the latter retain their exact hypotheses and reference boundaries. No independent reviewer of this new manuscript is named or claimed; current native release/review flags are false. The bibliography below remains available for its precise imported-theorem references.

## How to read these records

This bibliography separates comparison texts from sources for imported theorems. A named theorem is a retrieval label, not a complete historical attribution. Page ranges refer to the editions named below. Online works are tied to stable tags or a dated author version.

## Algebra, modules, and homological algebra

- M. F. Atiyah and I. G. Macdonald, *Introduction to Commutative Algebra*, Addison-Wesley, 1969. [Successor-publisher record](https://www.routledge.com/Introduction-to-Commutative-Algebra/Atiyah-Macdonald/p/book/9780367317289). A concise comparison route for modules, localization, and primary decomposition; it is not used as a source for the book's duality theorems.
- David S. Dummit and Richard M. Foote, *Abstract Algebra*, 3rd ed., Wiley, 2004. [Publisher record](https://uat.store.wiley.com/en-us/abstract-algebra-3rd-edition-p-9780471433347). Comparison for algebra and module theory.
- Paolo Aluffi, *Algebra: Chapter 0*, Graduate Studies in Mathematics 104, American Mathematical Society, 2009. [AMS record](https://bookstore.ams.org/gsm-104). Category-first comparison for algebra and introductory homological algebra.
- Charles A. Weibel, *An Introduction to Homological Algebra*, Cambridge University Press, 1994. [Publisher record](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/an-introduction-to-homological-algebra/AAA3F16482097015CD12D4376D505282). Comparison and verification for derived functors, spectral sequences, and homological dimension; Chapter 5 is the spectral-sequence route and Chapter 10 is the derived-category route.
- Sergei I. Gelfand and Yuri I. Manin, *Methods of Homological Algebra*, 2nd ed., Springer, 2003. [Springer record](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-662-12492-5). Comparison for derived and triangulated categories and spectral sequences.
- Masaki Kashiwara and Pierre Schapira, *Categories and Sheaves*, Springer, 2006. [Springer record](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/3-540-27950-4). Advanced comparison for categories, derived categories, and sheaves; it does not by itself verify scheme-theoretic Grothendieck duality.

## Ordinary, monoidal, and enriched categories

- Saunders Mac Lane, *Categories for the Working Mathematician*, 2nd ed., Springer, 1998. [Springer record](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-4757-4721-8). Exact content routes used here are Adjoints, pp. 79–108; the General Adjoint Functor Theorem in Chapter V, Section 6, Theorem 2, pp. 121–122; Monads and Algebras, pp. 137–159; Beck's strict monadicity form in Chapter VI, Section 7, Theorem 1, pp. 151–154, and its up-to-equivalence form in Exercise 6, p. 155; Mac Lane coherence in Chapter VII, Section 2, Theorem 1 and its corollary, pp. 166--170; other monoidal material, pp. 161–190 and 251–266; and Abelian Categories, pp. 191–209. Enriched categories are left to Kelly rather than attributed to this contents record.
- Steve Awodey, *Category Theory*, 2nd ed., Oxford University Press, 2010. [Publisher record](https://global.oup.com/academic/product/category-theory-9780199237180). Introductory comparison.
- Emily Riehl, *Category Theory in Context*, Dover, 2016. [Author book page](https://emilyriehl.github.io/books/). Comparison for universal properties, limits, and adjunctions; a theorem locator must retain the author-file version used.
- Tom Leinster, *Basic Category Theory*, Cambridge University Press, 2014. [Publisher record](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/basic-category-theory/A72533879BBC7BD956CC415777B7DA99). Compact introductory comparison.
- Peter Freyd, *Abelian Categories*, Harper & Row, 1964; Theory and Applications of Categories Reprint 3 (2003), 23–164. [TAC reprint](https://www.tac.mta.ca/tac/reprints/articles/3/tr3abs.html). Theorem 7.34, p. 150, is the precise source used for the small-category Freyd–Mitchell embedding theorem.
- Pavel Etingof, Shlomo Gelaki, Dmitri Nikshych, and Victor Ostrik, *Tensor Categories*, Mathematical Surveys and Monographs 205, American Mathematical Society, 2015. [AMS record](https://bookstore.ams.org/surv-205). Continuation for monoidal, braided, and fusion categories.
- G. M. Kelly, *Basic Concepts of Enriched Category Theory*, London Mathematical Society Lecture Note Series 64, Cambridge University Press, 1982; Theory and Applications of Categories Reprint 10 (2005), 1–136. [TAC reprint and corrections](https://www.tac.mta.ca/tac/reprints/articles/10/tr10abs.html). This is a reprint of the 1982 monograph, not a 2005 journal article.

## Model and higher categories

- Daniel G. Quillen, *Homotopical Algebra*, Lecture Notes in Mathematics 43, Springer-Verlag, 1967. [Springer monograph record](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/BFb0097438). Historical and axiomatic source for model categories; it is not the source for the later unbounded chain-complex model structure.
- Mark Hovey, *Model Categories*, Mathematical Surveys and Monographs 63, American Mathematical Society, 1999. [AMS record](https://bookstore.ams.org/SURV/63). Theorem 2.3.11 is the modern source used for the cofibrantly generated projective model structure on unbounded chain complexes over a ring.
- Emily Riehl, *Categorical Homotopy Theory*, Cambridge University Press, 2014. [Publisher record](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/categorical-homotopy-theory/556C7A200B521E61466BB7763C49DDA4). Comparison for model categories, homotopical diagrams, and higher-categorical connections.
- Jacob Lurie, *Higher Topos Theory*, Princeton University Press, 2009. [Publisher record](https://press.princeton.edu/books/hardcover/9780691140483/higher-topos-theory). Verification and continuation for quasicategories and higher topoi. Section 1.1.2, p. 14, records the Boardman–Vogt weak-Kan origin and Joyal's development. This book is not presented here as a comprehensive source for derived algebraic geometry.
- Jacob Lurie, *Higher Algebra*, author version dated 18 September 2017. [Author PDF](https://www.math.ias.edu/~lurie/papers/HA.pdf). Theorem 1.1.2.14, p. 27, proves that the homotopy category of a stable infinity-category is triangulated; Remark 1.1.2.15 records the canonical triangulation.

## Topos theory and categorical logic

- Saunders Mac Lane and Ieke Moerdijk, *Sheaves in Geometry and Logic: A First Introduction to Topos Theory*, Springer, 1992. [Springer record](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-4612-0927-0). The Prologue, pp. 1–8, gives secondary historical context for the independent Grothendieck and Lawvere developments without claiming an exhaustive original-source history. Exact imported-result routes are Kripke–Joyal semantics in Chapter VI, Section 6, Theorem 1, pp. 304–310, with the site form in Chapter VI, Section 7, Theorem 1, pp. 315–317; the classifying topos of a geometric theory in Chapter X, Section 6, Theorem 1, pp. 559–566; and Giraud's theorem in the Appendix, Theorem 1, p. 575, with the sufficiency construction in Appendix Section 3, pp. 578–586.
- Bart Jacobs, *Categorical Logic and Type Theory*, Studies in Logic and the Foundations of Mathematics 141, North-Holland, 1999. [Publisher record](https://shop.elsevier.com/books/categorical-logic-and-type-theory/jacobs/978-0-444-50170-7). Chapter 10, Sections 4--5, is the route used for display maps, comprehension categories, and closed comprehension categories. The manuscript treats its extra finite-sum and inductive-type clauses as orientation whose full extent is not verified by that locator.
- Peter T. Johnstone, *Sketches of an Elephant: A Topos Theory Compendium*, vol. 1, Oxford Logic Guides 43, and vol. 2, Oxford Logic Guides 44, Oxford University Press, 2002. [OUP two-volume record](https://global.oup.com/academic/product/sketches-of-an-elephant-a-topos-theory-compendium-9780198534259). Encyclopedic continuation; a locator must identify its volume.
- Jiří Adámek and Jiří Rosický, *Locally Presentable and Accessible Categories*, London Mathematical Society Lecture Note Series 189, Cambridge University Press, 1994. [Book DOI record](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511600579). Proposition 1.46 is the exact ordinary-category locator used for the accessible-localization characterization of locally presentable categories.

## Claim-to-source map for imported results

- **Freyd–Mitchell:** Freyd, Theorem 7.34, p. 150, with the manuscript's explicit smallness boundary.
- **Derived and triangulated categories:** Stacks Project [Tag 05QK](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/05QK) states the triangulated axioms, including the octahedral axiom; [Tag 014S](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/014S) proves the homotopy category of complexes is triangulated; the derived-category construction and canonical delta functor are developed at [Tag 014Z](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/014Z). Weibel, Chapter 10, is the book-length comparison route.
- **Unbounded projective model structure on chain complexes:** Hovey, Theorem 2.3.11. Quillen's 1967 monograph supplies the historical axiomatic context only.
- **Stable infinity-categories:** Lurie, *Higher Algebra*, Theorem 1.1.2.14 and Remark 1.1.2.15, p. 27. *Higher Topos Theory* supplies the quasicategory and higher-topos background, not this theorem.
- **Adjoint functor and monadicity theorems:** Mac Lane, Chapter V, Section 6, Theorem 2, pp. 121–122, gives the General Adjoint Functor Theorem used in Theorem 12.7. Chapter VI, Section 7, Theorem 1, pp. 151–154, gives Beck's strict isomorphism/created-coequalizer formulation; Exercise 6, p. 155, gives the up-to-equivalence version stated in Theorem 12.9. The manuscript separately states local smallness, completeness, solution-set, conservativity, and split-coequalizer hypotheses.
- **Mac Lane coherence:** Mac Lane, Chapter VII, Section 2, Theorem 1 and its coherence corollary, pp. 166--170. The corollary is the imported source for the statement that formal diagrams assembled from associativity and unit constraints commute.
- **Dependent-type semantic orientation:** Jacobs, Chapter 10, Sections 4--5, supplies the display-map, comprehension-category, and closed-comprehension-category framework used to orient Theorem 8.7. The finite-sum and inductive-type boundary sentences are not presented as theorem-level consequences verified from those sections.
- **Topoi, classifying topoi, and geometric logic:** Mac Lane–Moerdijk, Appendix, Theorem 1, p. 575, states Giraud's exactness conditions, and Appendix Section 3, pp. 578–586, constructs the site in the sufficiency direction. Chapter VI, Section 6, Theorem 1, pp. 304–310, gives Kripke–Joyal semantics, with the site form in Chapter VI, Section 7, Theorem 1, pp. 315–317. Chapter X, Section 6, Theorem 1, pp. 559–566, gives the classifying topos of a geometric theory. For the locally-presentable conclusion, Adámek–Rosický, Proposition 1.46, identifies locally presentable categories with accessible reflective localizations of presheaf categories; left exactness is the additional topos property used in the chapter. Johnstone is an advanced two-volume continuation rather than a claim locator here.
- **Cartan–Eilenberg and Grothendieck spectral sequences:** Stacks Project [Tag 015J](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/015J) gives the two Cartan–Eilenberg spectral sequences and their bounded convergence; [Tag 015N](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/015N) gives the Grothendieck spectral sequence with its acyclicity hypotheses and finite filtrations. The Leray statement is the corresponding sheaf-cohomology instance. The change-of-rings sequence remains explicitly separated from the covariant Grothendieck construction in the chapter.
- **Regular rings, global dimension, and syzygies:** Stacks Project [Tag 00OC](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00OC), Proposition 10.110.5, gives the regular-local/finite-global-dimension equivalence; [Tag 090V](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/090V), Proposition 10.111.1, gives the Auslander–Buchsbaum formula; [Tag 00OQ](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00OQ) gives global dimension $n$ for a polynomial algebra in $n$ variables over a field. For the structural hierarchy, [Tag 07NU](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/07NU), Lemma 15.44.4, preserves regularity under completion; [Tag 0AWX](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0AWX) gives regular $\Rightarrow$ Gorenstein; [Tag 0DWA](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0DWA) gives local complete intersection over Noetherian Gorenstein $\Rightarrow$ Gorenstein; [Tag 0BJL](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0BJL), Lemma 47.21.9, transfers the Gorenstein property across completion; and [Tag 0DW8](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0DW8) gives Gorenstein $\Rightarrow$ Cohen--Macaulay. [Tag 0BJI](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0BJI) is the exact Ext-vanishing characterization used in Exercise 14.4.
- **Local and scheme duality:** Stacks Project [Tag 0A84](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0A84), Theorem 47.18.3, gives local duality for a normalized dualizing complex; [Tag 0AAK](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0AAK), Lemma 47.18.4, identifies the completed Ext module with the Matlis dual of local cohomology. The canonical-module formula in the manuscript is the Cohen--Macaulay specialization after Matlis duality, and the text records where completion enters. [Tag 0FVV](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0FVV), Lemma 48.27.1, gives the normalized dualizing complex and duality for proper schemes over a field, including the smooth identification. The manuscript restricts its displayed scheme formula to bounded coherent complexes and the normalization $f^!k$.

## Reading routes and use boundaries

Atiyah–Macdonald, Aluffi, and Dummit–Foote are entry and comparison routes. Mac Lane, Awodey, Riehl, and Leinster cover ordinary categorical foundations. Weibel and Gelfand–Manin continue into homological algebra. Kelly is the dedicated enriched-category source. Lurie and Riehl cover different parts of higher and homotopical category theory; neither one book nor one terminology records the full history of the subject.

The imported theorem map identifies where this manuscript stops proving and starts citing. It does not certify historical priority, exhaustive originality, permissions, human approval, or release readiness. Contributors should paraphrase, identify the role of a citation, preserve exact hypotheses, and attach a locator before adding a new imported theorem.

The stable Frobenius-category triangulation is explicitly imported from the classical treatment in Dieter Happel, *Triangulated Categories in the Representation Theory of Finite Dimensional Algebras*, Cambridge University Press, 1988. The current Triangulated Categories chapter separately proves the signed homotopy-category cone calculations; that proof is not a claim to have proved the general Frobenius or heart theorem.
