# Selected bibliography

A curated guide to primary papers, research monographs, and source materials cited in the chapter plans and surveys. Entries are deduplicated across chapters. Links point to publisher, institutional, or author-hosted records checked against those source ledgers or primary records; each annotation says what the book draws from the work. Where the source ledger identifies a preprint or an unfinished text, that status is retained.

## Foundations and probability computation

1. **Amir Dembo (2021). _Probability Theory: STAT310/MATH230_.** Stanford lecture notes. [Author-hosted PDF](https://adembo.su.domains/stat-310b/lnotes.pdf). Useful for probability spaces, reference measures, and conditional expectation; the plan records the notes' date.
2. **Luc Devroye (1986). _Non-Uniform Random Variate Generation_.** Springer. [Author's book page](https://luc.devroye.org/rnbookindex.html). A broad reference on inversion, rejection, and other constructions for generating random variates.
3. **Art B. Owen (2013). _Monte Carlo Theory, Methods and Examples_.** [Author-hosted book](https://artowen.su.domains/mc/). A book in progress covering Monte Carlo integration, variance reduction, importance sampling, and related methods. See [Chapter 9: Importance sampling](https://artowen.su.domains/mc/Ch-var-is.pdf).
4. **Cosma Shalizi (2020). _Simulation and the Monte Carlo Method_, Lecture 15.** Carnegie Mellon University. [Lecture notes](https://stat.cmu.edu/~cshalizi/dst/20/lectures/15/lecture-15.html). An accessible introduction to expectation estimation and computational integration.
5. **Art B. Owen (2012). _Tutorial on Monte Carlo_.** MCQMC tutorial. [Author-hosted PDF](https://artowen.su.domains/pubtalks/MCQMC2012-Owen-Tutorial.pdf). A concise teaching route through Monte Carlo error, integration, and variance reduction.
6. **Stan Development Team (Reference Manual 2.30). _Changes of Variables_.** [Versioned manual](https://mc-stan.org/docs/2_30/reference-manual/change-of-variables.html). Supports the density transformation and Jacobian conventions used in the probability chapter.
7. **Andrew Gelman et al. (2020). _Bayesian Workflow_.** [Preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.01808). A research perspective on model building, computation, checking, and iteration; the chapter plan notes that only the record was inspected.
8. **S. K. Au, J. Ching, and J. L. Beck (2007). _Application of subset simulation methods to reliability benchmark problems_.** [Author-hosted paper](https://jimbeck.caltech.edu/papers_pdf/application_of_subset_simulation.pdf). An applied account of subset simulation for estimating small failure probabilities.

## Algorithms and sampler construction

9. **Nicholas Metropolis, Arianna W. Rosenbluth, Marshall N. Rosenbluth, Augusta H. Teller, and Edward Teller (1953). _Equation of State Calculations by Fast Computing Machines_.** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.1699114). The original paper introducing the Metropolis construction in a statistical-physics setting.
10. **W. Keith Hastings (1970). _Monte Carlo sampling methods using Markov chains and their applications_.** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/57.1.97). Generalizes the Metropolis method to asymmetric proposals.
11. **Gareth O. Roberts, Andrew Gelman, and Walter R. Gilks (1997). _Weak Convergence and Optimal Scaling of Random Walk Metropolis Algorithms_.** [University-hosted paper](https://faculty.wcas.northwestern.edu/lchrist/course/Gerzensee_2011/The%20Annals%20of%20Applied%20Probability%201997%20Roberts.pdf). Gives a carefully scoped high-dimensional product-target scaling result.
12. **Luke Tierney (1998). _A Note on Metropolis–Hastings Kernels for General State Spaces_.** [University-hosted paper](https://www2.stat.duke.edu/~scs/Courses/Stat376/Papers/TransdimMCMC/TierneyAAP1998.pdf). Develops general-state-space kernel arguments and comparison results used in the kernel-design chapter.
13. **Radford M. Neal (2003). _Slice Sampling_.** _The Annals of Statistics_ 31, 705–767. [University-hosted paper](https://people.stat.sc.edu/hansont/stat740/Neal2003.pdf). The main reference for slice sampling, including stepping-out, shrinkage, and correctness details.
14. **Paul Damien, Jon Wakefield, and Stephen Walker (1999). _Gibbs sampling for Bayesian non-conjugate and hierarchical models by using auxiliary variables_.** [University-hosted paper](https://users.wpi.edu/~balnan/Damien-etal-JRSSB.pdf). A construction for Gibbs updates using auxiliary variables when direct conditional sampling is difficult.
15. **Iain Murray, Ryan Adams, and David MacKay (2010). _Elliptical slice sampling_.** [PMLR paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v9/murray10a.html). Uses Gaussian prior structure for rejection-free slice updates in latent Gaussian models.
16. **Matthew D. Hoffman and Andrew Gelman (2014). _The No-U-Turn Sampler: Adaptively Setting Path Lengths in Hamiltonian Monte Carlo_.** _Journal of Machine Learning Research_ 15, 1593–1623. [JMLR](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v15/hoffman14a.html). Introduces NUTS and its dynamic trajectory construction.
17. **Michael Betancourt (2017). _A Conceptual Introduction to Hamiltonian Monte Carlo_.** [arXiv preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.02434). A geometric explanation of HMC, typical sets, energy, and common failure modes.
18. **Michael J. Betancourt and Mark Girolami (2013). _Hamiltonian Monte Carlo for Hierarchical Models_.** [arXiv preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.0906). Connects hierarchical parameterization to local posterior geometry and HMC performance.
19. **Radford M. Neal (2001; preprint posted 1998). _Annealed Importance Sampling_.** [arXiv preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/physics/9803008). Combines transitions across a sequence of distributions with importance weights for inference and normalizing-constant estimation.
20. **Peter J. Green (1995). _Reversible jump Markov chain Monte Carlo computation and Bayesian model determination_.** _Biometrika_ 82, 711–732. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/82.4.711). Establishes the dimension-matching framework for MCMC across parameter spaces of different dimensions.
21. **Yanan Fan, Scott A. Sisson, and Laurence Davies (2024 revision). _Reversible jump Markov chain Monte Carlo and multi-model samplers_.** [arXiv version 2](https://arxiv.org/html/1001.2055v2). A handbook chapter overview of dimension-changing and multi-model methods; the plan flags a displayed reciprocal typo to avoid repeating.
22. **Giuseppe Zanella (2020). _Informed proposals for local MCMC in discrete spaces_.** _Journal of the American Statistical Association_. [Institutional record](https://iris.unibocconi.it/handle/11565/4028550). Introduces locally informed proposal design for discrete state spaces.

## Theory, convergence, comparison, and error

23. **Gareth O. Roberts and Jeffrey S. Rosenthal (2007). _Coupling and Ergodicity of Adaptive Markov Chain Monte Carlo Algorithms_.** [Journal record](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-applied-probability/article/coupling-and-ergodicity-of-adaptive-markov-chain-monte-carlo-algorithms/91713CBCA261758088FCDFBF8DB43FE0). Gives conditions for ergodicity under adaptation, including diminishing adaptation and containment.
24. **Gareth O. Roberts and Jeffrey S. Rosenthal (1998). _Optimal scaling of discrete approximations to Langevin diffusions_.** [University-hosted paper](https://www2.stat.duke.edu/~scs/Courses/Stat376/Papers/Langevin/RobertsRosenthalJRSSB1998.pdf). Derives scaling results for a specified class of Langevin proposals and target regimes.
25. **Gareth O. Roberts and Richard L. Tweedie (1996). _Exponential convergence of Langevin distributions and their discrete approximations_.** [University-hosted paper](https://www2.stat.duke.edu/~scs/Courses/Stat376/Papers/Langevin/RobertsTweedieBernoulli1996.pdf). Studies convergence of Langevin diffusions and discretized algorithms, including tail-sensitive behavior.
26. **Charles J. Geyer (1992). _Practical Markov Chain Monte Carlo_.** [Paper](https://www2.stat.duke.edu/homeweb/scs/Courses/Stat376/Papers/GeyerStatSci1992.pdf). A foundational treatment of dependence-aware Monte Carlo error estimation and initial-sequence methods.
27. **Aki Vehtari, Andrew Gelman, Daniel Simpson, Bob Carpenter, and Paul-Christian Bürkner (2021). _Rank-normalization, folding, and localization: An improved $\hat R$ for assessing convergence of MCMC_.** [Institutional record](https://aaltodoc.aalto.fi/items/332dd844-1b47-4876-a7ca-f2b54a33e4b1) and [author PDF](https://sites.stat.columbia.edu/gelman/research/published/Vehtari_etal_2020_rhat_ess.pdf). Develops modern rank-based convergence diagnostics and complementary bulk/tail efficiency measures.
28. **James M. Flegal and Lei Gong (2015). _Relative fixed-width stopping rules for Markov chain Monte Carlo simulations_.** [Journal paper](https://www3.stat.sinica.edu.tw/statistica/oldpdf/A25n213.pdf). Studies stopping based on estimated Monte Carlo precision under explicit limit assumptions.
29. **Chris Sherlock and Gareth O. Roberts (2009). _Optimal scaling of the random walk Metropolis on elliptically symmetric unimodal targets_.** [arXiv preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/0909.0856). Shows how random-walk scaling conclusions vary beyond a narrow Gaussian product example.
30. **Petros Dellaportas and Ioannis Kontoyiannis (2010 preprint). _Control Variates for Reversible MCMC Samplers_.** [arXiv preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1008.1355). Develops control variates for reversible chains, including links to Poisson equations.
31. **Chris J. Oates, Mark Girolami, and Nicolas Chopin (2014 preprint). _Control functionals for Monte Carlo integration_.** [arXiv manuscript](https://arxiv.org/html/1410.2392). Uses Stein operators and function approximation for variance reduction under stated regularity and sampling assumptions.
32. **Jun S. Liu, Wing Hung Wong, and Augustine Kong (1994). _Covariance structure of the Gibbs sampler with applications to the comparisons of estimators and augmentation schemes_.** [University-hosted paper](https://www2.stat.duke.edu/homeweb/scs/Courses/Stat376/Papers/ConvergeRates/GibbsSampling/LiuWongKongBiometrika1994.pdf). Analyzes dependence and covariance in data-augmentation and Gibbs settings.
33. **David A. Levin and Yuval Peres, with contributions by Elizabeth L. Wilmer (2017). _Markov Chains and Mixing Times_, second edition.** [Author-hosted book](https://pages.uoregon.edu/dlevin/MARKOV/markovmixing.pdf). A detailed reference on finite-chain convergence, spectral bounds, coupling, and conductance.
34. **David Aldous and James Allen Fill (unfinished monograph; online text). _Reversible Markov Chains and Random Walks on Graphs_.** [Author-hosted text](https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~aldous/RWG/Book_Ralph/Ch2.S1.html). Sections on finite chains, reversibility, and weighted graphs support several foundational chapters; the source ledger identifies this as unfinished.

## Workflow, diagnostics, and implementation

35. **Sean Talts, Michael Betancourt, Daniel Simpson, Aki Vehtari, and Andrew Gelman (2018 preprint). _Validating Bayesian Inference Algorithms with Simulation-Based Calibration_.** [arXiv preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.06788). Presents simulation-based calibration as a repeated computational validation method, distinct from posterior predictive model checking.
36. **Christophe Andrieu and Johannes Thoms (2008). _A tutorial on adaptive MCMC_.** [University-hosted paper](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~jordan/sail/readings/andrieu-thoms.pdf). Reviews stochastic approximation, adaptation schemes, and the need for convergence arguments.
37. **Haario, Saksman, and Tamminen (2001). _An adaptive Metropolis algorithm_.** [University record](https://researchportal.helsinki.fi/en/publications/an-adaptive-metropolis-algorithm/). A foundational adaptive covariance proposal; the chapter plan uses the record for bibliographic attribution and flags the limits of generalization.
38. **NumPy developers (current documentation). _Parallel random number generation_.** [Documentation](https://numpy.org/doc/stable/reference/random/parallel.html). Practical guidance on spawning streams and organizing reproducible parallel random-number generation.
39. **Stan Development Team (Reference Manual 2.40, as recorded in the plan). _Reproducibility_.** [Manual](https://mc-stan.org/docs/reference-manual/reproducibility.html). Documents practical conditions for reproducible computation and why a seed alone may not determine identical results across environments.
40. **Geweke (2004). _Getting It Right: Joint Distribution Tests of Posterior Simulators_.** [University-hosted PDF](https://qed.econ.queensu.ca/pub/faculty/ferrall/quant/papers/04_04_29_geweke.pdf). A diagnostic perspective based on testing implications of the joint distribution; the chapter ledger notes that direct PDF inspection was unavailable.

## Applications and specialized models

41. **Andrieu, Doucet, and Holenstein (2010). _Particle Markov chain Monte Carlo methods_.** _Journal of the Royal Statistical Society: Series B_ 72, 269–342. [Author-hosted paper](https://www.stats.ox.ac.uk/~doucet/andrieu_doucet_holenstein_PMCMC.pdf). Introduces particle MCMC, including particle marginal MH and particle Gibbs.
42. **Arnaud Doucet and Adam M. Johansen (2011; linked manuscript dated 2008, corrected 2012). _A Tutorial on Particle Filtering and Smoothing: Fifteen Years Later_.** [University-hosted manuscript](https://warwick.ac.uk/fac/sci/statistics/staff/academic-research/johansen/publications/DJ11.pdf). A detailed tutorial on sequential importance weighting, resampling, filtering, and smoothing.
43. **Fredrik Lindsten, Michael I. Jordan, and Thomas B. Schön (2014). _Particle Gibbs with Ancestor Sampling_.** _Journal of Machine Learning Research_ 15, 2145–2184. [JMLR paper](https://jmlr.org/papers/volume15/lindsten14a/lindsten14a.pdf). Develops ancestor sampling for particle Gibbs in state-space models.
44. **Simo Särkkä (2013). _Bayesian Filtering and Smoothing_.** Cambridge University Press. [Author-hosted book](https://users.aalto.fi/~ssarkka/pub/cup_book_online_20131111.pdf). Covers Kalman filtering, smoothing, and Bayesian state-space methods used as analytic benchmarks.
45. **S. Kim, N. Shephard, and S. Chib (1998). _Stochastic Volatility: Likelihood Inference and Comparison with ARCH Models_.** [Author-hosted working paper](https://www.nuff.ox.ac.uk/economics/papers/1996/w26/chib8.pdf). A key stochastic-volatility reference on latent-state inference and likelihood evaluation.
46. **Sylvia Frühwirth-Schnatter (1994). _Data Augmentation and Dynamic Linear Models_.** [Publisher record](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1467-9892.1994.tb00184.x). Connects augmentation with inference in dynamic linear models and forward-filtering backward-sampling ideas.
47. **Christian P. Robert and George Casella (2004). _Monte Carlo Statistical Methods_, second edition.** Springer. [Publisher record](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-4757-4145-2). A broad reference on Monte Carlo methods and their statistical foundations; the book survey uses its foundations-to-algorithms progression.
48. **Swendsen and Wang (1987). _Nonuniversal critical dynamics in Monte Carlo simulations_.** [Publisher record](https://journals.aps.org/prl/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevLett.58.86). Introduces cluster updates that address slow local dynamics in lattice models.
49. **Robert G. Edwards and Alan D. Sokal (1988). _Generalization of the Fortuin–Kasteleyn–Swendsen–Wang representation and Monte Carlo algorithm_.** [Publisher record](https://journals.aps.org/prd/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevD.38.2009). Develops the representation underlying a family of cluster algorithms.
50. **Marjoram, Molitor, Plagnol, and Tavaré (2003). _Markov chain Monte Carlo without likelihoods_.** [Published paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC307566/). An early MCMC construction for approximate Bayesian computation when likelihood evaluation is unavailable.
51. **Fearnhead and Prangle (2012). _Constructing Summary Statistics for Approximate Bayesian Computation: semi-automatic approximate Bayesian computation_.** [Publisher record](https://academic.oup.com/jrsssb/article-abstract/74/3/419/7075312) and [preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1004.1112). Develops estimand-oriented summary statistics for ABC; any optimality statement depends on its stated loss and asymptotic qualifications.
52. **Simon N. Wood (2010). _Statistical inference for noisy nonlinear ecological dynamic systems_.** _Nature_ 466, 1102–1104. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature09319). Introduces synthetic likelihood in an ecological simulation setting.
53. **Price, Drovandi, Lee, and Nott (2018). _Bayesian Synthetic Likelihood_.** [Institutional manuscript](https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/id/eprint/85427/). Develops Bayesian inference based on a synthetic likelihood for summary statistics.
54. **George Papamakarios, David Sterratt, and Iain Murray (2019). _Sequential Neural Likelihood: Fast Likelihood-free Inference with Autoregressive Flows_.** [PMLR paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v89/papamakarios19a.html). A sequential simulation-based approach that learns a likelihood approximation using autoregressive flows.

## Regeneration, joint precision, and sensitivity

55. **Per Mykland, Luke Tierney, and Bin Yu (1995). _Regeneration in Markov Chain Samplers_.** _Journal of the American Statistical Association_ 90, 233–241. [Author-hosted paper](https://galton.uchicago.edu/~mykland/paperlinks/MyklandTierneyYu2291148.pdf). Develops splitting and regenerative output analysis, including ratios of tour rewards to tour lengths.
56. **Peter W. Glynn (2006). _Simulation Algorithms for Regenerative Processes_.** Chapter 16 in _Handbooks in Operations Research and Management Science_, volume 13. [Author-hosted chapter](https://web.stanford.edu/~glynn/papers/2006/G06.pdf). Supports Chapter 08's randomized regeneration construction and its distinction between one-step splitting and dependent tours from multi-step splitting.
57. **Galin L. Jones (2004 preprint; revised 2005). _On the Markov chain central limit theorem_.** [Author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0409112). Gives sufficient mixing and moment conditions for the limit theorems used in Chapter 13; a law of large numbers alone does not supply a CLT.
58. **Galin L. Jones, Murali Haran, Brian S. Caffo, and Ronald Neath (2006). _Fixed-Width Output Analysis for Markov Chain Monte Carlo_.** _Journal of the American Statistical Association_ 101, 1537–1547. [University-hosted paper](https://www.stat.rice.edu/~dcox/Stat552/Mcmc/JonesEtalJASA2006.pdf). Develops variance consistency and asymptotic justification for stopping at an estimated precision threshold.
59. **James M. Flegal and Galin L. Jones (2010). _Batch means and spectral variance estimators in Markov chain Monte Carlo_.** _The Annals of Statistics_ 38, 1034–1070. [Paper record and manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/0811.1729). Establishes conditions for consistent long-run variance estimation and studies batch-size choices.
60. **Dootika Vats, James M. Flegal, and Galin L. Jones (2019). _Multivariate output analysis for Markov chain Monte Carlo_.** _Biometrika_ 106, 321–337. [Author manuscript, revised 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.07713). Supplies the vector batch-means, determinant ESS, and asymptotic confidence-volume framework in Chapter 13. Joint volume does not bound every directional error.
61. **Ryan Giordano, Tamara Broderick, and Michael I. Jordan (2018). _Covariances, Robustness, and Variational Bayes_.** _Journal of Machine Learning Research_ 19(51), 1–49. [JMLR record](https://jmlr.org/papers/v19/17-670.html). Section 2 supplies the exact posterior covariance identity for local sensitivity used in Chapter 17; subsequent variational approximations are a separate part of the paper.
62. **Noa Kallioinen, Topi Paananen, Paul-Christian Bürkner, and Aki Vehtari (2024; published online 2023). _Detecting and diagnosing prior and likelihood sensitivity with power-scaling_.** _Statistics and Computing_ 34, article 57. [Author-hosted published paper](https://paulbuerkner.com/publications/pdf/2024__Kalloinen_et_al__Statistics_and_Computing.pdf). Develops controlled prior and likelihood perturbations, importance reweighting, and local sensitivity diagnostics with explicit limitations.

## Population paths, exact constructions, and computational limits

63. **Pierre Del Moral, Arnaud Doucet, and Ajay Jasra (2006). _Sequential Monte Carlo samplers_.** _Journal of the Royal Statistical Society: Series B_ 68, 411–436. [Author-hosted paper](https://www.stats.ox.ac.uk/~doucet/delmoral_doucet_jasra_sequentialmontecarlosamplersJRSSB.pdf). Develops weighted particle methods on a common state space, including the static annealed resample–move construction taught in Chapter 28.
64. **Gavin E. Crooks (2007). _Measuring thermodynamic length_.** _Physical Review Letters_ 99, 100602. [Paper record and manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/0706.0559). Connects equilibrium fluctuations and Fisher information to path length, motivating Chapter 25's local temperature-spacing calculation.
65. **S. L. Cotter, G. O. Roberts, A. M. Stuart, and D. White (2013). _MCMC Methods for Functions: Modifying Old Algorithms to Make Them Faster_.** _Statistical Science_ 28, 424–446. [Paper record and manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/1202.0709). Develops function-space proposals that preserve a reference measure, including the Gaussian-prior construction behind pCN.
66. **Michael B. Giles (2015). _Multilevel Monte Carlo methods_.** _Acta Numerica_ 24, 259–328. [Publisher record](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/acta-numerica/article/abs/multilevel-monte-carlo-methods/C5AF9A57ED8FF8FDF08074C1071C5511). Explains telescoping expectations, coupling, and variance–cost allocation; Chapter 24 adapts the allocation argument to long-run variances for MCMC.
67. **T. J. Dodwell, C. Ketelsen, R. Scheichl, and A. L. Teckentrup (2019). _Multilevel Markov chain Monte Carlo_.** _SIAM Review_ 61, 509–545. [Institutional publication record](https://www.research.ed.ac.uk/en/publications/multilevel-markov-chain-monte-carlo/). Studies coupled posterior resolutions and multilevel estimators under specified assumptions. The linked institutional manuscript also carries a cover sheet for the authors' 2015 original article; the next entry records a correction to that original work.
68. **T. J. Dodwell, C. Ketelsen, R. Scheichl, and A. L. Teckentrup (2019). _Erratum: A Hierarchical Multilevel Markov Chain Monte Carlo Algorithm with Applications to Uncertainty Quantification in Subsurface Flow_.** _SIAM/ASA Journal on Uncertainty Quantification_ 7, 1398–1399. [Publisher record](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/19M1278089). Corrects symmetric-proposal claims in the 2015 article's Lemmas 4.6 and 4.7; Chapter 24 explicitly retains this qualification when pointing to multilevel MCMC theory.
69. **Kirill Neklyudov, Max Welling, Evgenii Egorov, and Dmitry Vetrov (2020). _Involutive MCMC: a Unifying Framework_.** PMLR 119, 7273–7282. [Conference record](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/neklyudov20a.html). Unifies augmented-state transformations and their acceptance corrections; Chapter 30 applies the framework to MH, fixed-length HMC, and reversible jump.
70. **Dougal Maclaurin and Ryan P. Adams (2014 preprint). _Firefly Monte Carlo: Exact MCMC with Subsets of Data_.** [Author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1403.5693). Introduces binary auxiliary variables and likelihood lower bounds that preserve the full posterior marginal. Chapter 34 distinguishes this target identity from finite-run accuracy and from any claim of computational speedup.
71. **Pierre E. Jacob, John O'Leary, and Yves F. Atchadé (2020). _Unbiased Markov chain Monte Carlo with couplings_.** _Journal of the Royal Statistical Society: Series B_ 82, 543–600. [Author manuscript, revised 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.03625). Uses coupled chains and telescoping corrections for unbiased expectation estimates under meeting-time and moment conditions; these estimates are not perfect posterior draws.
72. **James Gary Propp and David Bruce Wilson (1996). _Exact Sampling with Coupled Markov Chains and Applications to Statistical Mechanics_.** [University-hosted paper](https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~aldous/206-RWG/RWGpapers/propp_wilson.pdf). Introduces coupling from the past, with shared random histories and all-state coalescence supplying the exact-sampling argument.
73. **James Allen Fill (1998). _An Interruptible Algorithm for Perfect Sampling via Markov Chains_.** _The Annals of Applied Probability_ 8, 131–162. [Author-hosted paper](https://www.ams.jhu.edu/~fill/papers/algo1.pdf). Studies runtime selection bias and gives a distinct perfect-sampling construction with interruption guarantees; adding a timeout to ordinary CFTP does not supply those guarantees.
74. **Lawrence M. Murray, Sumeetpal S. Singh, and Anthony Lee (2021). _Anytime Monte Carlo_.** _Data-Centric Engineering_ 2. [Publisher article](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/data-centric-engineering/article/anytime-monte-carlo/3E8ED0F13E0A1E3AF37D09E1515ABFA2). Derives the length bias from observing computations in real time and studies a multiple-chain correction. Chapter 35 distinguishes this from selecting the first completed task.
75. **Matthew Parno and Youssef Marzouk (2018). _Transport map accelerated Markov chain Monte Carlo_.** _SIAM/ASA Journal on Uncertainty Quantification_ 6, 645–682. [Paper record and manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/1412.5492). Combines approximate transport maps with Metropolis corrections, supplying a foundation for Chapter 36's distinction between learned proposal quality and target invariance.
76. **Vivekananda Roy, Aixin Tan, and James M. Flegal (2016 preprint revision). _Estimating standard errors for importance sampling estimators with multiple Markov chains_.** [Version 2 manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.06310v2). Section 3.2 derives ratio CLTs with numerator–denominator long-run covariance and vector batch-means estimates under explicit mixing, moment, and batch assumptions. Chapter 13 develops an original finite-state conditional-probability calculation using this general output-analysis principle.
77. **Ajay Jasra, Anthony Lee, Chris Yau, and Xiaole Zhang (2013 preprint). _The Alive Particle Filter_.** [arXiv manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/1304.0151). Algorithm 1 and Proposition 3.1 give a correctly normalized particle construction that avoids collapse for indicator potentials using random computational effort. Chapter 28 derives its one-step negative-binomial identity and distinguishes this construction from conditioning an ordinary proposed filter on survival.
78. **Anthony Lee and Nick Whiteley (2016 preprint revision). _Variance estimation in the particle filter_.** [Version 2 manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.00394v2). Section 2, Algorithm 1, and Theorem 1 connect inherited initial-ancestor indices with particle-estimator variance assessment. Chapter 28 derives a finite neutral-resampling example with separate conditional and unconditional moment references.
79. **Alexander Yu. Mitrophanov (2005). _Sensitivity and convergence of uniformly ergodic Markov chains_.** _Journal of Applied Probability_ 42, 1003–1014. [Publisher paper](https://doi.org/10.1239/jap/1134587812). Theorems 3.2–3.3 relate kernel perturbations to contraction and invariant-distribution error. Chapter 34 proves the finite-state one-step contraction case using the book's half-\(L^1\) convention for total variation.
80. **Pierre Alquier, Nial Friel, Richard G. Everitt, and Aidan Boland (2014 preprint revision; published 2016). _Noisy Monte Carlo: convergence of Markov chains with approximate transition kernels_.** [Version 3 manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/1403.5496v3). Section 2 and Theorem 2.1 connect transition approximations with distributional error through mixing constants. Chapter 34's exact two-state example makes their amplification role explicit; its direct contraction proof does not assert a general SGLD bound.
81. **Tianze Wang and Guanyang Wang (2023). _Unbiased Multilevel Monte Carlo Methods for Intractable Distributions: MLMC Meets MCMC_.** _Journal of Machine Learning Research_ 24. [JMLR paper](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume24/22-1468/22-1468.pdf). Sections 1–2 distinguish unbiased expectations from nonlinear functions of expectations. Chapter 31 derives the elementary independent-copy product construction for a target variance; it does not require the paper's general multilevel method for that polynomial functional.
82. **Christian P. Robert, Jean-Marie Cornuet, Jean-Michel Marin, and Natesh S. Pillai (2011). _Lack of confidence in approximate Bayesian computation model choice_.** _Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences_ 108, 15112–15117. [Open primary paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3174657/). Equation 1 and the Poisson–geometric example show why sufficiency within each model need not preserve model probabilities. Chapter 46 develops exact ordered-count calculations and a jointly sufficient statistic for that specific pair of models.
83. **David Bolin and Finn Lindgren (2012 preprint). _Excursion and contour uncertainty regions for latent Gaussian models_.** [Primary manuscript](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1211.3946). The introduction and Definition 2.3 distinguish marginal exceedance probabilities from a simultaneous all-location event. Chapter 40 develops a finite two-site Gaussian reference to make that distinction explicit.
84. **David Bolin and Finn Lindgren (2018). _Calculating Probabilistic Excursion Sets and Related Quantities Using excursions_.** _Journal of Statistical Software_ 86(5). [Primary paper](https://www.jstatsoft.org/article/view/v086i05/1242). ### observational equivalence

[Tsiatis (1975), *A nonidentifiability aspect of the problem of competing risks*, Theorem 2, page 21](https://web.stanford.edu/~lutian/coursepdf/ifcensoring.pdf), DOI [10.1073/pnas.72.1.20](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.72.1.20), supplies the general mechanism. Its admissible crude tails are `Q_i(t)=P(min potential times>t,observed cause=i)`. The independent representation has cause-specific hazards `-Q_i'(t)/sum_j Q_j(t)`. Here each crude tail is `exp(-2t)/2`, so both constructed hazards are one, reproducing the r=1 control. The theorem's regularity concerns continuous first partial derivatives of joint survival; a cusp in density derivatives is a different issue.

Research date: 2026-09-26. This is a curriculum survey, not a claim to have read all books. Evidence below comes from publisher descriptions and tables of contents, plus author-hosted contents and introductory material where specified. Summaries are original and intentionally short. Chapter designs proposed at the end are editorial recommendations, not descriptions of any one source.

### 1. Robert and Casella — Monte Carlo Statistical Methods, second edition (2004)

**Inspected:** Springer description and complete 14-chapter contents. [Publisher and contents](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-4757-4145-2).

The progression builds random generation, integration, variance reduction, and optimization before Markov chains and MCMC. Distinct treatments cover slice sampling, two-stage and multi-stage Gibbs, changing dimension, convergence assessment, perfect simulation, and sequential importance methods. The intended graduate audience is explicit, and supporting Markov-chain theory is developed in the book. This is the strongest structural precedent for a foundations-to-algorithms spine. Our book should preserve mathematically justified transitions between methods while adding current geometry, diagnostics, software practices, and modern algorithms. A separate theory route can keep the initial implementation route approachable.

### 2. Robert and Casella — Introducing Monte Carlo Methods with R (2010)

**Inspected:** Springer description and all eight chapter entries. [Publisher and contents](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-4419-1576-4).

Programming comes first, followed by random generation, integration, convergence acceleration, optimization, Metropolis–Hastings, Gibbs, and monitoring/adaptation. The publisher explicitly describes reduced theoretical depth relative to the authors' earlier text and modifiable programs as a teaching device. Our implementation track should similarly let readers alter working samplers early. However, code must be accompanied by the target distribution, assumptions, invariance argument, numerical checks, and failure cases. Interactive controls should be experiments with stated questions rather than animations alone.

### 3. Liu — Monte Carlo Strategies in Scientific Computing (2004 edition on publisher page)

**Inspected:** Springer description and all 13 substantive chapters. [Publisher and contents](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-0-387-76371-2).

The book brings together sequential Monte Carlo, Metropolis and Gibbs, Ising clusters, conditional simulation, molecular dynamics/hybrid Monte Carlo, multilevel and population methods, and convergence theory. Its examples cross biology, vision, chemistry, and artificial intelligence. This establishes an interdisciplinary precedent: MCMC belongs in a broader family of interacting simulation techniques. Our book should show how augmenting a state, tempering a target, or embedding MCMC in SMC changes the computational problem. It should revisit a small collection of targets across chapters so readers can compare costs and failure modes directly.

### 4. Handbook of Markov Chain Monte Carlo, second edition (2026)

**Inspected:** Routledge publisher description and 23-chapter contents. Editors: Craiu, Vats, Jones, Brooks, Gelman, Meng. [Publisher and contents](https://www.routledge.com/Handbook-of-Markov-Chain-Monte-Carlo/Craiu-Vats-Jones-Brooks-Gelman-Meng/p/book/9781032591575).

This expanded edition contains foundations, HMC, adaptation, run length, multivariate uncertainty, tempering, transdimensional methods, perfect sampling, and augmentation. Applications include spatial models, astronomy, inverse problems, and state-space models. New chapters address multimodality, intractable normalizers, involutions, unbiased estimation, control variates, convergence bounds, perturbations, hardware/software, deep learning, and learning driven by MCMC. The breadth sets a useful minimum for an advanced reference route. Our contribution should be a single notation and prerequisite system joining these topics, with worked examples and visual experiments that do not assume a reader can jump directly into specialist reference chapters.

### 5. Gelman and coauthors — Bayesian Data Analysis, third edition (2013)

**Inspected:** author homepage and author-hosted ten-page contents PDF. [Author resources](https://sites.stat.columbia.edu/gelman/book/), [detailed contents](https://statmodeling.stat.columbia.edu/wp-content/uploads/2013/08/bda3_contents.pdf).

Chapters 10–13 place simulation among integration, approximation, debugging, Markov-chain methods, efficient updates, HMC, and alternative approximations. Earlier chapters establish hierarchy, predictive checking, comparison, and decision analysis; later chapters develop regression, missing data, Gaussian processes, and mixtures. The pedagogical lesson is that successful computation is only one stage of inference. Our case studies should carry a model from generative assumptions through posterior computation to predictive criticism. Algorithm correctness, numerical accuracy, and model adequacy need different checks. Author resources include code, data, exercises, and teaching links, a model for reproducible companion materials.

### 6. Rubinstein and Kroese — Simulation and the Monte Carlo Method, third edition (2016)

**Inspected:** Wiley description and ten-chapter contents. [Publisher and contents](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/book/10.1002/9781118631980).

The sequence covers generating randomness and stochastic processes, discrete-event simulation and output analysis, variance reduction, MCMC, sensitivity/optimization, cross-entropy, splitting, and enumeration. MCMC occupies one chapter in a wider simulation curriculum. This suggests a necessary boundary chapter: readers should learn when independent simulation, importance sampling, rare-event methods, or deterministic approximation may be more suitable. Our MCMC text can include these connections without expanding into a full discrete-event simulation textbook. Rare-event estimation should be taught with estimator uncertainty and weight degeneracy, not merely as a list of algorithms.

### 7. Asmussen and Glynn — Stochastic Simulation: Algorithms and Analysis (2007)

**Inspected:** Springer description and 14-chapter contents. [Publisher and contents](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-0-387-69033-9).

Output analysis and steady-state simulation precede variance reduction and rare events. Later material includes derivative estimation, stochastic optimization, SDEs, Gaussian and Lévy processes, and MCMC. This strengthens the case for treating Monte Carlo error as a central mathematical topic, rather than postponing it until a diagnostics checklist. The advanced route should explain why a law of large numbers does not automatically provide a usable central limit theorem, and why stopping rules require estimand-specific accuracy criteria. Regeneration and batch-means ideas belong in that route.

### 8. Landau and Binder — A Guide to Monte Carlo Simulations in Statistical Physics, fifth edition (2021)

**Inspected:** Cambridge description, edition metadata, and chapter-level contents. [Publisher](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/guide-to-monte-carlo-simulations-in-statistical-physics/8AE32744A1EBA8EEBC4E5BC50732B15A), [contents evidence](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/guide-to-monte-carlo-simulations-in-statistical-physics/guide-to-monte-carlo-simulations-in-statistical-physics/C655E85AE7ECFCD0427C6824B12A97FE).

Coverage spans lattice and off-lattice systems, reweighting, quantum Monte Carlo, renormalization, nonequilibrium dynamics, gauge models, biological molecules, and emerging directions. The description identifies new active-matter and machine-learning coverage. This prevents an overly Bayesian interpretation of MCMC. Our physics route should distinguish equilibrium sampling from physical dynamics, discuss phase transitions and finite-size effects, and explain what cluster updates and free-energy methods solve. Quantum methods merit an orientation with explicit limits; a short introductory chapter should not claim to teach the entire quantum Monte Carlo field.

### 9. Newman and Barkema — Monte Carlo Methods in Statistical Physics (1999)

**Inspected:** Oxford abstract and full chapter-level contents. [Publisher and contents](https://academic.oup.com/book/54894).

The organization moves from equilibrium models and data analysis to nonequilibrium systems and then implementation. Dedicated implementation chapters concern lattice structures, parallel computers, multispin coding, and random numbers. This is a reminder that data layout, random streams, update schedules, and parallel execution can affect scientific correctness. Our implementation route should show a reproducible Ising example and demonstrate how an apparently innocuous parallel-update choice can change the transition kernel. The publisher's extracted chapter labels contain OCR errors; do not propagate those errors into bibliography or chapter titles.

### 10. Levin and Peres, with Wilmer — Markov Chains and Mixing Times, second edition

**Inspected:** author homepage; PDF contents, prefaces, overview, and reader guidance. [Author page](https://pages.uoregon.edu/dlevin/MARKOV/), [author-hosted book](https://pages.uoregon.edu/dlevin/MARKOV/markovmixing.pdf).

The text develops finite chains, mixing distances, coupling, lower bounds, network and spectral methods, path coupling, Ising models, cutoff, continuous-time variants, monotonicity, and coupling from the past. Repeated families of examples and explicit dependency guidance connect techniques. Our theory route should use tiny chains to make stationary distributions and spectral gaps computable, then show bottlenecks and coupling visually. Theoretical convergence bounds and empirical convergence diagnostics must remain distinct. General-state-space MCMC needs additional sources; this finite-chain text alone is insufficient support for drift/minorization claims.

#### 1. Oxford: introductory simulation and statistical programming

**Primary materials:** Julien Berestycki and Geoff Nicholls, Hilary 2019, [course page](https://www.stats.ox.ac.uk/~berestyc/teaching/A12.html); [problem sheet 3](https://www.stats.ox.ac.uk/~berestyc/teaching/ssp_ps3.pdf). Both inspected, including the sheet's three pages.

#### 2. Oxford: advanced simulation methods

**Primary material:** Patrick Rebeschini, Hilary 2018, [course page with notes, slides, and problem sheets](https://www.stats.ox.ac.uk/~rebeschi/teaching/AdvSim/18/index.html), inspected in full.

SMC should precede particle MCMC, and model spaces of changing dimension need their own foundations. The published sequence compresses several topics into individual lectures; a comprehensive book should expand their assumptions, counterexamples, and implementation details.

**Curriculum lesson:** Readers need conditional expectation, not just density manipulation. A short prerequisite path should introduce kernels and stochastic-process thinking before sophisticated samplers. Continuous-time dynamics should be prepared separately from discrete Metropolis arguments. Applications beyond posterior inference help explain why invariant measures and mixing are reusable concepts. Most of the surrounding probability topics belong in selected background sections rather than displacing the book's sampling focus.

**Primary material:** Cosma Shalizi, [Data over Space and Time, 2020, Lecture 18: Markov Chains I](https://stat.cmu.edu/~cshalizi/dst/20/lectures/18/lecture-18.html), inspected including code and convergence discussion.

Exercises ask students to justify convergence and detailed-balance implications.

**Curriculum lesson:** A finite-state interactive laboratory should let the reader modify a transition matrix and watch both paths and probability mass evolve. Simulation validation should include transition behavior, not just a plausible histogram. The book should state finite-state theorem assumptions carefully and avoid presenting diagonalization as universally available: a stochastic matrix need not be diagonalizable. The general-state-space chapters must make clear where the finite-dimensional picture stops being sufficient.

**Primary material:** Liam Paninski, [Stat GR6104, Spring 2018](https://sites.stat.columbia.edu/liam/teaching/compstat-spr18/), inspected in full.

The course places Monte Carlo alongside optimization, numerical linear algebra, dynamic programming, deterministic integration, and variational methods. The Monte Carlo inventory includes rejection, importance sampling, variance reduction, Gibbs, Metropolis–Hastings, Langevin, HMC, slice methods, particle filters, diagnostics, and advanced nonreversible/tempering topics. Students complete exercises and a computational project. High dimensionality and exploiting matrix structure are explicit themes.

**Curriculum lesson:** Sampler efficiency depends on linear algebra, preconditioning, derivatives, and model structure. Include practical Cholesky-based Gaussian calculations and stable log densities. Explain when analytic marginalization or deterministic methods can remove sampling work. A single computational-statistics module cannot provide a complete MCMC treatment; the book should give its most important algorithm families separate chapters and preserve the broader comparison with alternative inference strategies.

#### 6. Minnesota: statistical computing, error, and reproducibility

**Primary material:** Charles J. Geyer, [Statistics 8054, Spring 2024 homework and reading program](https://www.stat.umn.edu/geyer/8054/hw/), inspected including MCMC readings and project requirements.

The course explicitly distinguishes reading, doing, and thinking. Its MCMC reading sequence includes autocovariance material, theory, and the limitations of convergence diagnostics. The final project is a repository containing a functioning package, a vignette, and a presentation.

**Curriculum lesson:** A book should require reproducible deliverables, not merely successful-looking chains. Separate algorithm correctness, asymptotic guarantees, empirical checks, and estimated Monte Carlo error. Ask readers to document a sampler's inputs, failure conditions, and computational cost. The course's skeptical treatment of diagnostics is a prompt to state their limits, not grounds for omitting practical multichain diagnostic workflows.

#### 7. Cambridge: advanced MCMC for probabilistic modeling

**Primary material:** Iain Murray, Michaelmas 2006, [Advanced MCMC methods](https://mlg.eng.cam.ac.uk/zoubin/tut06/mcmc.pdf), 44-slide tutorial inspected, especially auxiliary variables, HMC, and bridging distributions.

The tutorial starts with marginalization in prediction and latent-variable problems, develops transition operators and Metropolis–Hastings, then emphasizes using draws efficiently. Auxiliary variables connect slice sampling and Hamiltonian dynamics. Later material treats exploration, rare events, normalizing constants, and paths through related distributions.

**Curriculum lesson:** Organize advanced algorithms around the obstacle they solve: local diffusion, incompatible scales, disconnected modes, or difficult expectations. Show that augmenting a state can simplify computation even when the inferential goal is marginalization. Keep sampling from an unnormalized density distinct from estimating its normalizing constant. This is historically valuable intuition; modern diagnostics, dynamic HMC details, and adaptation theory need newer supporting research.

#### 9. Oxford/SAMSI: sequential Monte Carlo as preparation for particle MCMC

**Primary material:** Arnaud Doucet, [Sequential Monte Carlo course materials](https://www.stats.ox.ac.uk/~doucet/samsi_course.html), inspected in full. This is an archived course page, with references through 2011 and a link to 2012 resources.

The progression starts with importance sampling and sequential importance sampling, introduces resampling, and continues to auxiliary filters, smoothing, resample-move methods, parameter estimation, adaptive MCMC, and SMC samplers. Linear Gaussian code supports comparisons with Kalman filtering. Final projects involve reading papers, implementing algorithms, writing a report, and presenting results.

**Curriculum lesson:** Use a model with an analytic filtering solution before nonlinear demonstrations. Separate path degeneracy, weight degeneracy, and parameter inference. A particle-MCMC chapter must inherit a concrete understanding of likelihood estimators and extended targets from earlier SMC chapters. Research projects should require independent numerical checks in addition to a literature summary.

#### 10. Aalto: contemporary Bayesian workflow and teaching support

**Primary material:** Aki Vehtari, [Bayesian Data Analysis, Aalto 2025 course schedule](https://avehtari.github.io/BDA_course_Aalto/Aalto2025.html), inspected, especially Monte Carlo and MCMC teaching units. The author's course site was updated in 2026; the inspected syllabus explicitly describes the 2025 offering.

The course moves from single- and multiparameter Bayesian models to integration, MCMC, probabilistic programming, hierarchical models, model checking, comparison, and decisions. Its Monte Carlo unit covers numerical precision and sample requirements; the MCMC unit names warmup, R-hat, and effective sample size. Readings, videos, R and Python demonstrations, assignments, and model solutions provide several entry points.

**Curriculum lesson:** A sampling book should continue past generating draws to predictive checks and decisions. Present code, derivations, and visual explanations together. Treat the target estimand and its required numerical precision as explicit inputs to computation. MCMC theory alone does not establish that a statistical model answers the scientific question.
