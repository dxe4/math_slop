# Bibliography {#sec-comb-bibliography}

The chapter sequence is informed by standard references in enumerative
combinatorics, graph theory, probabilistic methods, random graphs, and matroid
theory ([Stanley 2012](#ref-stanley2012enumerative1); [Wilf 2005](#ref-wilf2005generatingfunctionology); [Diestel 2017](#ref-diestel2017graph); [Alon and Spencer 2016](#ref-alon2016probabilistic); [Oxley 2011](#ref-oxley2011matroid)). Citations mark
routes to deeper proofs and broader variants; all notation needed for the core
route is introduced within this volume.

The convexity route in Chapter 27 follows the finite Helly/Radon and linear
infeasibility results in Amitabh Basu's author-hosted notes
([Basu 2017](#ref-basu2017convexity), Theorems 2.59, 2.62, and 2.87). The chapter proves its
finite Helly statement and the certificate verification implication directly;
the converse existence of linear certificates is cited as Farkas' theorem.

The root-orbit distinction in Chapters 6 and 21 is supported by
([Bodirsky et al. 2007](#ref-bodirsky2007pointing), author version, p. 2), which explains why atom
pointing of unlabeled types does not generally multiply their count by size.
The book uses only the elementary automorphism-orbit argument and its
oriented-necklace bijection, not the paper's more advanced cycle-pointing
machinery.

Chapter 19 distinguishes the projective-plane incidence axioms from the
field construction $PG(2,q)$. Peter Cameron's authored notes
([Cameron n.d.](#ref-cameron1991projective), Section 2.1) give the axioms, affine completion, and
the distinction from non-Desarguesian planes; J. B. Nation's authored survey
([Nation n.d.](#ref-nation2018planes), Section 1) also records finite non-Desarguesian examples.
The chapter proves its finite-field construction and parameter counts
directly. Its finite code certifies particular incidence structures, rather
than the cited existence or classification results.

Chapters 13 and 14 distinguish local vertex and edge Menger theorems
([Diestel 2025](#ref-diestel2025connectivity), Corollary 3.3.5). Darij Grinberg's authored notes
([Grinberg 2026](#ref-grinberg2026menger), Theorems 1.1.22 and 1.1.42) give explicit separator
conventions and flow derivations. The book gives its own certificate examples
and explains why cycle removal, nonadjacency and unit internal capacity
produce distinct undirected paths.

Chapter 28's progression graph and Roth transfer use Yufei Zhao's authored
text ([Zhao 2023](#ref-zhao2023gtacbook), Sections 2.3–2.4). Triangle removal is an assumed
deep theorem; the chapter proves the encoding, inverse, exact deletion
certificate and no-wrap transfer locally. The finite program checks the
declared graph and multiplicities rather than the universal removal theorem.
