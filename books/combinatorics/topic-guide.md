# Topic guide and next routes {#sec-comb-topic-guide}

## A first course

Read Chapters 1–5, 11–15, and 18. This route covers the main proof methods,
recurrences, graph language, trees, connectivity, optimization, coloring, and
the basic probabilistic method. Add Chapter 6 if generating functions belong
in the course.

## Enumerative combinatorics

Read Chapters 1–10, 21, and 23. Work with formal series before invoking analytic
convergence. Integer partitions lead naturally to symmetric functions,
representation theory, and algebraic geometry; species lead to structural
decomposition and random generation. Chapter 23 is the point at which
singularities, transfer conditions, and uniform error estimates become part of
the argument.

## Graph theory and optimization

Read Chapters 1–5, 11–20, 24–25, and 28. Matching, flow, matroid exchange,
and linear or integer programming give complementary views of tractable
structure. Spectra connect cuts with walks, hypergraphs expose higher-order
incidence, and regularity and graphons explain dense structure at scale.

## Probability and algorithms

Read Chapters 1–5, 11–12, 16–18, and 22. Then continue with the collection's
probabilistic-programming volume for inference rather than existence proofs.
Random graph thresholds, concentration, randomized algorithms, and approximate
counting form a broad next course.

## Additive and extremal structure

Read Chapters 2–4, 10–11, 16–18, 24–26, and 28. This route begins with
double counting and extremal constructions, then studies expansion, uniform
set families, sumsets, additive energy, Fourier coefficients, and the
structure-versus-pseudorandomness theme. Chapter 26 states deep inverse and
progression theorems with their hypotheses while keeping their full proofs out
of the elementary route.

## Geometry and topology of finite structure

Read Chapters 2–3, 9–10, 12, 15, 20, and 27. Ferrers diagrams, posets,
matroids, and planar Euler theory prepare convex hulls, face lattices, lattice
point enumeration, arrangements, and triangulations. Continue with the
topology volume for homology and with the calculus volume for continuous
convex optimization.

## An advanced second course

Chapters 23–28 are designed as six independent advanced modules rather than a
new prerequisite chain. Each gives an internal preparation route, exact
finite code, and explicit boundaries around the deeper theorems. A coherent
semester can take 23, 24, 25, 26, and either 27 or 28, with selected review
from Chapters 6–8 and 16–22.

## Connections across the collection

Number theory supplies arithmetic functions and congruences; real analysis
justifies limits used in asymptotics; topology turns complexes and graphs into
shape; calculus supports continuous optimization; and probability supplies
random constructions. None is required to begin this book. Cross-connections
are invitations after the finite core is secure.
