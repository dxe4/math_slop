
# Reading Routes, References, and Attribution Guide

## Purpose and status

This bibliography is a reading map with a specific edition bound for each listed work. It is not a theorem-provenance audit: theorem-name history and claim-level page locators still require specialist verification before release. The chapter-level dependency ledgers distinguish arguments proved here from explicit imported results. In particular, the PID structure theorem in Chapter 14 remains an external proof input; the bibliography also offers alternative emphases and deeper continuations.

## First and second courses

- Axler, S. (2024). *Linear Algebra Done Right* (4th ed.). Springer. https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-41026-0. Open-access operator-first development with invariant subspaces, minimal polynomials, multilinear algebra, and spectral theory.
- Friedberg, S. H., Insel, A. J., & Spence, L. E. (2021; copyright 2022). *Linear Algebra* (5th ed., eText). Pearson. ISBN 9780137515424. https://www.pearson.com/en-us/pearsonplus/p/9780137515424. Broad conventional course with proofs and computations.
- Hoffman, K., & Kunze, R. (1971). *Linear Algebra* (2nd ed.). Prentice-Hall. ISBN 0135367972. Classical structural treatment of canonical forms and bilinear forms.
- Lang, S. (1987). *Linear Algebra* (3rd ed.). Springer. https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4757-1949-9. Concise algebraic route with multilinear constructions.
- Halmos, P. R. (1958). *Finite-Dimensional Vector Spaces*. Springer, Undergraduate Texts in Mathematics. https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4612-6387-6. Conceptual and operator-centered exposition; the DOI identifies the Springer electronic record of this edition.

Readers should verify the edition appropriate to their library. These works use different inner-product conventions, companion-block orientations, and characteristic-polynomial signs; translate conventions before comparing formulas.

## Canonical forms and module structure

For a fuller proof of the finitely generated module theorem and Smith normal form, consult an abstract algebra text covering modules over principal ideal domains. The bridge is $t \cdot v=T(v)$: invariant factors of the resulting $\mathbb{F}[t]$-module are the rational canonical invariants. A treatment centered on Jordan form alone may silently assume an algebraically closed field; retain the original-field distinction.

## Multilinear and exterior algebra

Texts on multilinear algebra develop tensor products through universal properties and exterior powers through alternating quotients. Coordinate-array approaches can be useful computationally, but the universal factorization property is the basis-independent definition used here. Determinant history spans many contributors and computational traditions; the eponym-free structural derivation avoids assigning a single-origin story.

## Numerical continuation

- Golub, G. H., & Van Loan, C. F. (2013). *Matrix Computations* (4th ed.). Johns Hopkins University Press. ISBN 9781421407944. https://doi.org/10.56021/9781421407944.
- Trefethen, L. N., & Bau, D., III. (1997). *Numerical Linear Algebra*. SIAM. https://doi.org/10.1137/1.9780898719574.
- Higham, N. J. (2002). *Accuracy and Stability of Numerical Algorithms* (2nd ed.). SIAM. https://doi.org/10.1137/1.9780898718027.

These routes explain why exact rank, Jordan form, normal equations, and explicit inverses must be paired with conditioning and backward-error analysis. The present book’s numerical warnings are boundaries, not replacements for those subjects.

## Historical attribution policy

Names such as Cayley–Hamilton, Gram–Schmidt, Jordan, Sylvester, and Cauchy–Schwarz are conventional locators, not claims of sole invention. Mathematical ideas frequently developed through multiple authors, earlier special cases, different notation, and later synthesis. A released edition should verify historical statements against specialist histories or primary sources and avoid converting an eponym into a biography.

## Suggested onward routes

- Functional analysis: complete normed spaces, bounded operators, infinite-dimensional spectra.
- Numerical linear algebra: stable factorization, perturbation, sparse and iterative methods.
- Abstract algebra: modules, representations, homological structure.
- Differential equations and dynamics: matrix exponentials, invariant subspaces, stability.
- Statistics and machine learning: least squares, covariance spectra, SVD, regularization.
- Geometry and physics: bilinear/Hermitian forms, exterior algebra, symplectic spaces, quantum observables.

Every external factual or historical assertion still needs claim-level source review. The edition bindings above make the reading routes reproducible; they do not by themselves certify the manuscript's historical attributions.


## Proof inputs and verification scope

Chapter-level import ledgers state the results used, their hypotheses and the supplied proof boundary. The polynomial Bezout and finite certificate arguments developed in the text do not by themselves prove every general module-classification theorem. The retained PID structure input remains explicit; fundamental algebra, integration, compactness or choice inputs likewise need their stated scopes where used.

A reading bibliography is not an exhaustive theorem-priority audit or a quotation-permissions review. Verification of a particular imported statement does not establish all surrounding historical claims. Written source reviews also do not supply human field review, classroom evidence or publication qualification.
