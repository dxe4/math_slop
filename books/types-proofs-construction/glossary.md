## Glossary {#section-5-glossary}

**Beta reduction.** The basic computation step $(\lambda x.\, t)\,u \to_{\beta} t[u/x]$ in the lambda calculus, together with projection and case reductions. Chapter 4 (Reduction, preservation, and normalization).

**BHK interpretation.** Brouwer--Heyting--Kolmogorov interpretation of intuitionistic logic: proofs of compound formulas are built from proofs of components. Chapter 1 (The Brouwer--Heyting--Kolmogorov reading).

**Capture-avoiding substitution.** Substitution of a term for a variable that first alpha-renames binders to prevent accidental binding of free variables. Chapter 3 (Simply typed lambda calculus).

**Constructive existence.** An existence proof that supplies an explicit witness and verification, as opposed to classical existence which may rely on excluded middle without identifying the witness. Chapter 12 (Constructive and Classical Existence), with the retained classical reading.

**Curry--Howard correspondence.** The family of correspondences between logics and calculi; the base case matches intuitionistic propositional natural deduction with the simply typed lambda calculus. Chapter 5 (Curry--Howard: precise scope).

**Definitional equality.** The congruence generated by computation rules, recognized automatically by the type checker. Chapter 9 (Identity types and equality).

**Dependent function type ($\Pi$-type).** A type $\Pi x : A.\, B(x)$ of functions whose codomain depends on the input value. Chapter 7 (Dependent function and pair types).

**Dependent pair type ($\Sigma$-type).** A type $\Sigma x : A.\, B(x)$ of pairs where the second component's type depends on the first. Chapter 7 (Dependent function and pair types).

**Function extensionality.** The principle that pointwise equal functions are equal. Not provable from $J$ alone in many type theories. Chapter 9 (Identity types and equality).

**Identity type ($\operatorname{Id}_{A}(a, b)$ or $a =_{A} b$).** The type of proofs that $a$ and $b$ are equal, with reflexivity as constructor and $J$ as eliminator. Chapter 9 (Identity types and equality).

**Modulus of convergence.** A function $\mu : \mathbb{Q}^{+} \to \mathbb{N}$ giving explicit bounds on Cauchy sequences, required for constructive real analysis. Chapter 13 (Choice Principles and Constructive Reals).

**Natural deduction.** A proof system of introduction and elimination rules formalizing the BHK reading. Chapter 2 (Natural deduction as construction rules).

**Propositional equality.** An inhabited type $a =_{A} b$ requiring explicit proof, as opposed to definitional equality. Chapter 9 (Identity types and equality).

**Proof irrelevance.** The principle that all proofs of the same proposition are equal; relevant to extraction and computation. Chapter 5 (Curry--Howard: precise scope).

**Reducibility candidate.** A set of terms used in Tait's method to prove strong normalization. Chapter 4 (Reduction, preservation, and normalization).

**Simply typed lambda calculus ($\lambda^{\to}$).** The computational language corresponding to intuitionistic propositional natural deduction. Chapter 3 (Simply typed lambda calculus).

**Strong normalization.** The property that every typable term has no infinite reduction sequence. Chapter 4 (Reduction, preservation, and normalization).

**Subject reduction.** If $\Gamma \vdash t : A$ and $t \to t'$, then $\Gamma \vdash t' : A$; types are preserved under reduction. Chapter 4 (Reduction, preservation, and normalization).

**Transport.** The operation of moving a term from one type to another along a propositional equality proof, defined using $J$. Chapter 9 (Identity types and equality).

**Type-theoretic choice.** The constructive unpacking of a dependent witness from $\Pi x : A.\, \Sigma y : B.\, R(x, y)$; distinct from set-theoretic AC. Chapter 13 (Choice Principles and Constructive Reals).

**Univalence.** The axiom that equivalences between types correspond to equalities in a universe. Chapter 15 (Homotopy type theory).

**Universe hierarchy.** A cumulative stratification $\operatorname{Type}_{0} : \operatorname{Type}_{1} : \operatorname{Type}_{2} : \dots$ preventing self-membership and paradox. Chapter 10 (Universes and paradox control).

**Universe polymorphism.** Parameterizing definitions over universe levels so they can be used across the hierarchy. Chapter 10 (Universes and paradox control).

### Theorems {#section-55-theorems}

**Subject reduction (Chapter 4).** If $\Gamma \vdash t : A$ and $t \to t'$, then $\Gamma \vdash t' : A$. Together with a progress theorem for closed terms, it yields the usual type-safety statement that evaluation does not get stuck.

**Strong normalization (Chapter 4).** Every typable term in the simply typed lambda calculus has no infinite reduction sequence. The chapter gives a full Tait reducibility proof for its specified finite calculus with products and sums; general recursion and stronger calculi are outside that proof.

**Euclid's theorem, constructive form (Chapter 12).** For every $n \in \mathbb{N}$, there exists a prime $p > n$, computed as the least divisor of $n! + 1$ greater than $1$.

<a id="section-61-independence-results"></a>
### Independence results {#section-63-independence-results}

**Independence of AC from ZF (Chapter 13).** Gödel proved ZF + AC is consistent if ZF is consistent; Cohen proved ZF + $\neg$AC is consistent if ZF is consistent.

<a id="section-65-research-directions"></a>
### Research directions {#section-67-research-directions}

**Univalent foundations in practice (Chapter 15).** Researchers continue to develop univalent and cubical foundations, formal libraries, semantics, and computational implementations. Which foundation a community adopts is also a methodological choice, rather than a single yes-or-no mathematical conjecture.

**Efficient witness generation (Chapter 12).** Complexity questions about producing a prime above a bound must specify whether time is measured in $n$ or in the bit length of $n$, whether randomness is allowed, and how large an admissible output may be. The factorial construction is offered for witness content, not as a complexity benchmark.
