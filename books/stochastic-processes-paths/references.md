
# References and Further Reading {#section-1-references-and-further-reading}

This bibliography identifies standard comparison texts, historical sources, and current research directions. The book is written under CC BY-SA 4.0; standard definitions and probabilistic conventions belong to shared practice. References are included for further reading, historical context, alternative treatments, and broader coverage.

A named result or study is a retrieval convention, not a complete history. Probabilistic ideas are often the product of long development across communities; a modern statement may differ sharply from an early source.

<a id="section-5-probability-and-stochastic-processes"></a>
## Probability and stochastic processes {#section-7-probability-and-stochastic-processes}

- Durrett, R. (2019). *Probability: Theory and Examples* (5th ed.). Cambridge University Press. A comprehensive graduate text covering measure-theoretic probability, laws of large numbers, central limit theorems, martingales, Markov chains, and Brownian motion.
- Grimmett, G. R., & Stirzaker, D. R. (2020). *Probability and Random Processes* (4th ed.). Oxford University Press. A broad first-course text with extensive exercises on Markov chains, Poisson processes, and martingales.
- Norris, J. R. (1997). *Markov Chains*. Cambridge University Press. A concise, rigorous treatment of discrete-time and continuous-time chains, recurrence, reversibility, convergence, and coupling.
- Karlin, S., & Taylor, H. M. (1975). *A First Course in Stochastic Processes* (2nd ed.). Academic Press. A classic reference for Markov chains, Poisson processes, renewal theory, and branching processes.
- Karlin, S., & Taylor, H. M. (1981). *A Second Course in Stochastic Processes*. Academic Press. Extends the first course to Brownian motion, diffusion processes, and spectral methods.

These texts use different notational conventions and levels of measure-theoretic rigor. Comparison is most useful when those conventions are recorded rather than blended.

<a id="section-15-continuous-time-processes-and-stochastic-calculus"></a>
## Continuous-time processes and stochastic calculus {#section-17-continuous-time-processes-and-stochastic-calculus}

- Øksendal, B. (2003). *Stochastic Differential Equations: An Introduction with Applications* (6th ed.). Springer. A standard introduction to Itô calculus, stochastic differential equations, and applications.
- Revuz, D., & Yor, M. (1999). *Continuous Martingales and Brownian Motion* (3rd ed.). Springer. A deep, encyclopedic reference for continuous-time martingales, local times, and excursion theory.
- Karatzas, I., & Shreve, S. E. (1991). *Brownian Motion and Stochastic Calculus* (2nd ed.). Springer. A rigorous graduate text on Brownian motion, stochastic integration, and stochastic differential equations.
- Rogers, L. C. G., & Williams, D. (2000). *Diffusions, Markov Processes, and Martingales, Volume 1: Foundations* (2nd ed.). Cambridge University Press. Foundations of measure-theoretic probability, martingales, and Markov processes.
- Rogers, L. C. G., & Williams, D. (2000). *Diffusions, Markov Processes, and Martingales, Volume 2: Itô Calculus* (2nd ed.). Cambridge University Press. The companion treatment of continuous-time martingales, diffusions, and stochastic calculus.

The continuous-time literature varies in its treatment of filtrations, localization, and the completeness assumptions needed for the Itô integral. This book uses the natural filtration and standard completeness conventions for concreteness.

<a id="section-25-markov-chains-and-mixing"></a>
## Markov chains and mixing {#section-27-markov-chains-and-mixing}

- Levin, D. A., & Peres, Y. (2017). *Markov Chains and Mixing Times* (2nd ed.; with contributions by Elizabeth L. Wilmer). American Mathematical Society. The definitive treatment of coupling, spectral methods, and mixing-time bounds for finite Markov chains.
- Aldous, D., & Fill, J. A. *Reversible Markov Chains and Random Walks on Graphs*. Unfinished web monograph, version accessed 6 September 2026 at https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~aldous/RWG/book.html. A rich source of examples, coupling arguments, and spectral techniques.

<a id="section-30-renewal-theory-and-point-processes"></a>
## Renewal theory and point processes {#section-32-renewal-theory-and-point-processes}

- Feller, W. (1971). *An Introduction to Probability Theory and Its Applications* (Vol. 2, 2nd ed.). Wiley. A classic source for renewal theory, Laplace transforms, and Tauberian theorems.
- Daley, D. J., & Vere-Jones, D. (2003). *An Introduction to the Theory of Point Processes, Volume I: Elementary Theory and Methods* (2nd ed.). Springer. The foundational volume on point-process models and methods.
- Daley, D. J., & Vere-Jones, D. (2008). *An Introduction to the Theory of Point Processes, Volume II: General Theory and Structure* (2nd ed.). Springer. The companion volume on the general theory and structure of point processes.

<a id="section-36-historical-attribution-checklist"></a>
## Historical attribution checklist {#section-38-historical-attribution-checklist}

Every historical or priority claim should record:

1. a classification as contemporary exposition, secondary history, translated primary source, or primary source;
2. verified author, title, edition, publisher or archive, and stable locator;
3. a note explaining whether the citation supports provenance, formulation, evidence, example, or broader context;
4. a check that modern terminology is not projected unchanged backward;
5. careful comparison of primary sources for culturally broad claims and disputed priority.

<a id="section-46-reading-route-recommendations"></a>
## Reading-route recommendations {#section-48-reading-route-recommendations}

For a deeper treatment of Markov chains and mixing, proceed to Levin, Peres, and Wilmer. For continuous-time processes and stochastic calculus, continue into Karatzas and Shreve or Revuz and Yor. For renewal theory and point processes, Daley and Vere-Jones provide encyclopedic coverage. For probability foundations, Durrett remains the standard graduate reference.

<a id="section-50-citation-policy-for-contributors"></a>
## Citation policy for contributors {#section-52-citation-policy-for-contributors}

Paraphrase sources and cite the job they perform. Do not copy exercise sequences, distinctive examples, diagrams, or prose merely because a theorem is standard. Direct quotations require exact locators and license or fair-use review. When sources disagree, record the disagreement. When provenance is unknown, label it unknown rather than inventing a clean lineage.
