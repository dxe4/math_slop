## Glossary {#section-5-glossary}

**Adapted process.** A process $(Y_{t})$ is adapted to a filtration $(\mathcal{F}_{t})$ if $Y_{t}$ is $\mathcal{F}_{t}$-measurable for every $t$. Chapter 1 (Specifying a Process).

**Aperiodic state.** A state $i$ with period $d(i)=1$; in an irreducible chain, all states share the same period. Chapter 2 (Discrete-time Markov Chains).

**Brownian motion.** A continuous Gaussian process with independent mean-zero increments, $B_0=0$, and $\operatorname{Var}(B_t-B_s)=t-s$. Chapter 13 (Brownian Motion).

**Chapman--Kolmogorov equations.** $P^{m+n}_{ij} = \sum_{k} P^{m}_{ik}P^{n}_{kj}$; the semigroup property of transition matrices. Chapter 2 (Discrete-time Markov Chains).

**Coalescing coupling.** A coupling of two Markov chains using any joint updates with the correct marginals until meeting, then identical updates thereafter. Chapter 4 (Coupling and Convergence).

**Compound Poisson process.** $X(t) = \sum_{n=1}^{N(t)} Y_{n}$ with i.i.d. jumps $Y_{n}$ independent of the Poisson process $N$. Chapter 6 (Poisson Processes).

**Conditional expectation.** $\mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{G}]$ is the $\mathcal{G}$-measurable random variable satisfying the partial-averaging property. Chapter 1 (Specifying a Process); Chapter 10 (Martingales and Stopping).

**Coupling.** A joint construction of two processes on a common probability space to compare their distributions. Chapter 4 (Coupling and Convergence).

**Ergodic chain.** An irreducible, aperiodic finite Markov chain. Chapter 2 (Discrete-time Markov Chains).

**Exponential martingale.** $M_{t} = e^{\theta B_{t} - \theta^{2}t/2}$ is a martingale for standard Brownian motion. Chapter 13 (Brownian Motion).

**Filtration.** An increasing family $(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t \in T}$ of $\sigma$-algebras representing information accumulated over time. Chapter 1 (Specifying a Process).

**Finite-dimensional distribution (fdd).** The joint law of $(X_{t_{1}}, \dots, X_{t_{k}})$ for any finite set of times. Chapter 1 (Specifying a Process).

**First-step analysis.** Solving hitting probabilities or expected hitting times by conditioning on the first jump of a Markov chain. Chapter 2 (Discrete-time Markov Chains).

**Gambler's ruin.** The probability of hitting $N$ before $0$ for a simple random walk starting at $i$ is $i/N$ in the fair case. Chapter 2 (Discrete-time Markov Chains); Chapter 5 (Random Walks and Gambler’s Ruin).

**Gaussian process.** A process whose finite-dimensional distributions are all multivariate Gaussian, determined by mean and covariance functions. Chapter 1 (Specifying a Process).

**Hitting time.** $T_{A} = \min\{n \ge 0 : X_{n} \in A\}$; the first time a process enters a set. Chapter 2 (Discrete-time Markov Chains); Chapter 10 (Martingales and Stopping).

**Independent increments.** The increments of a process over disjoint time intervals are independent random variables. Chapter 6 (Poisson Processes); Chapter 13 (Brownian Motion).

**Inspection paradox.** In a renewal process, the interval containing a random inspection time is longer on average than a typical interarrival time. Chapter 9 (Renewal Processes).

**Irreducible chain.** A Markov chain with a single communication class; every state can reach every other state. Chapter 2 (Discrete-time Markov Chains).

**Itô integral.** $\int_0^T H_t\,dB_t$ for predictable $H$ with $\mathbb E\int_0^T H_t^2dt<\infty$; its integral process is a square-integrable martingale. Chapter 15 (The Ito Integral).

**Itô isometry.** $\mathbb{E}[(\int_{0}^{T} H_{t}\,dB_{t})^{2}] = \mathbb{E}[\int_{0}^{T} H_{t}^{2}\,dt]$. Chapter 15 (The Ito Integral).

**Itô's formula.** The chain rule for Itô processes with a second-order correction: $df(X_{t}) = f'(X_{t})\,dX_{t} + \frac{1}{2}f''(X_{t})\sigma_{t}^{2}\,dt$. Chapter 15 (The Ito Integral).

**Kolmogorov extension theorem.** Existence of a process with specified consistent finite-dimensional distributions. Chapter 1 (Specifying a Process).

**Length-biased distribution.** $\mathbb{P}(W^{\mathrm{lb}} \in dw) = w\,\mathbb{P}(W \in dw)/\mathbb{E}[W]$. Chapter 9 (Renewal Processes).

**Markov property.** The future is conditionally independent of the past given the present state. Chapter 2 (Discrete-time Markov Chains).

**Martingale.** An adapted integrable process $(M_{n})$ with $\mathbb{E}[M_{n+1} \mid \mathcal{F}_{n}] = M_{n}$. Chapter 10 (Martingales and Stopping).

**Maximal coupling.** A coupling $(X,Y)$ with $\|\mu - \nu\|_{TV} = \mathbb{P}(X \neq Y)$. Chapter 4 (Coupling and Convergence).

**Natural filtration.** $\mathcal{F}_{t} = \sigma(X_{s} : s \le t)$; the information generated by observing a process up to time $t$. Chapter 1 (Specifying a Process).

**Optional stopping theorem.** Conditions under which $\mathbb{E}[M_{T}] = \mathbb{E}[M_{0}]$ for a martingale and stopping time $T$. Chapter 10 (Martingales and Stopping).

**Poisson process.** A counting process with independent increments and $N(t) \sim \text{Poisson}(\lambda t)$. Chapter 6 (Poisson Processes).

**Positive recurrent state.** A state $i$ with $\mathbb{E}_{i}[T_{i}] < \infty$; for finite irreducible chains, all states are positive recurrent. Chapter 2 (Discrete-time Markov Chains).

**Quadratic variation.** $\sum (B_{t_{i}} - B_{t_{i-1}})^{2} \to t$ in probability; the key property distinguishing Brownian motion from differentiable paths. Chapter 13 (Brownian Motion); Chapter 15 (The Ito Integral).

**Renewal equation.** $U(t) = F(t) + \int_{0}^{t} U(t-s)\,dF(s)$ for the renewal function $U(t) = \mathbb{E}[N(t)]$. Chapter 9 (Renewal Processes).

**Renewal process.** A counting process with i.i.d. positive interarrival times generalizing the Poisson process. Chapter 9 (Renewal Processes).

**Simple process.** A predictable process constant on each interval of a finite partition, with each coefficient measurable at the preceding endpoint; the basic building block of the Itô integral. Chapter 15 (The Ito Integral).

**Stationary distribution.** A probability vector $\pi$ with $\pi P = \pi$; preserved by the Markov chain dynamics. Chapter 2 (Discrete-time Markov Chains).

**Stopping time.** A random time $T$ with $\{T \le n\} \in \mathcal{F}_{n}$ for all $n$; the decision to stop uses only current information. Chapter 10 (Martingales and Stopping).

**Strong Markov property.** The process restarts afresh at a stopping time, not just at deterministic times. Chapter 6 (Poisson Processes).

**Submartingale.** An adapted integrable process with $\mathbb{E}[M_{n+1} \mid \mathcal{F}_{n}] \ge M_{n}$. Chapter 10 (Martingales and Stopping).

**Supermartingale.** An adapted integrable process with $\mathbb{E}[M_{n+1} \mid \mathcal{F}_{n}] \le M_{n}$. Chapter 10 (Martingales and Stopping).

**Thinning.** Independently retaining each arrival of a Poisson process with probability $p$ yields a Poisson process of rate $p\lambda$. Chapter 6 (Poisson Processes).

**Total variation distance.** $\|\mu - \nu\|_{TV} = \frac{1}{2}\sum_{s}|\mu(s) - \nu(s)|$; measures the maximum discrepancy between probabilities of events. Chapter 4 (Coupling and Convergence).

**Transition matrix.** $P = (P_{ij})$ with $P_{ij} = \mathbb{P}(X_{n+1}=j \mid X_{n}=i)$ for a time-homogeneous Markov chain. Chapter 2 (Discrete-time Markov Chains).

**Wald's identity.** $\mathbb{E}[\sum_{n=1}^{T} \xi_{n}] = \mu\,\mathbb{E}[T]$ for i.i.d. increments with mean $\mu$ and a suitable stopping time $T$. Chapter 10 (Martingales and Stopping).

### Theorems {#section-97-theorems}

**Convergence to stationarity (Chapter 2).** For a finite irreducible aperiodic Markov chain with transition matrix $P$ and stationary distribution $\pi$, we have $P^{n}_{ij} \to \pi_{j}$ as $n \to \infty$ for every $i,j$.

**Return-time formula (Chapter 2).** For a finite irreducible chain, $\pi_{i} = 1/\mathbb{E}_{i}[T_{i}]$.

**Coupling inequality (Chapter 4).** For any coupling $(X,Y)$ of $\mu$ and $\nu$, $\|\mu - \nu\|_{TV} \le \mathbb{P}(X \neq Y)$.

**Elementary renewal theorem (Chapter 9).** $\mathbb{E}[N(t)]/t \to 1/m$ as $t \to \infty$, where $m = \mathbb{E}[W_{1}]$.

**Optional stopping, bounded case (Chapter 10).** If $(M_{n})$ is a martingale and $T$ is a bounded stopping time, then $\mathbb{E}[M_{T}] = \mathbb{E}[M_{0}]$.

**Martingale convergence theorem (Chapter 10).** A nonnegative martingale converges almost surely to an integrable limit.

**Itô isometry (Chapter 15).** For a simple predictable process $H$, $\mathbb{E}[(\int_{0}^{T} H_{t}\,dB_{t})^{2}] = \mathbb{E}[\int_{0}^{T} H_{t}^{2}\,dt]$.

**Itô's formula, one dimension (Chapter 15).** For $f \in C^{2}$ and an Itô process $X_{t}$ satisfying the chapter's local integrability hypotheses, $df(X_{t}) = f'(X_{t})\,dX_{t} + \frac{1}{2}f''(X_{t})\sigma_{t}^{2}\,dt$.

<a id="section-113-quantitative-examples"></a>
### Quantitative examples {#section-115-quantitative-examples}

**Random-to-top shuffle coupling bound (Chapter 4).** A coupon-collector coupling proves a mixing-time upper bound of order $N\log N$.

**Poisson thinning example (Chapter 6).** For $\lambda = 12$ and $p = 1/4$, urgent calls form an independent Poisson process of rate $3$ per hour.

**Uniform interarrival inspection paradox (Chapter 9).** For $W \sim \text{Uniform}(0,1)$, the length-biased mean is $2/3 > 1/2 = \mathbb{E}[W]$.
