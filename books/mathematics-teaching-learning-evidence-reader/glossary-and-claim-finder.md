
# Glossary and Claim Finder

This is a retrieval aid. Compact entries cannot replace the full definitions or hypotheses in their chapters. Chapter numbers follow reading order.

## Glossary

**Access audit.** A multidimensional review of whether a mathematical goal is reachable for a given learner, covering economic, infrastructure, linguistic, sensory, motor, pedagogical, and governance barriers. Ch. 2a.

**Anticipated response.** A hypothesized learner strategy prepared before a lesson so observers can recognize it; not a label assigned to a child. Ch. 4.

**Claim-status ledger.** A running record classifying each major statement as mathematical result, external research finding, local observation, or teaching conjecture. Front matter; Chs. 1–4a.

**Commensurability.** Sufficient similarity of constructs, measures, and contrasts to justify quantitative pooling across studies. Ch. 3a.

**Conjecture map.** A table linking lesson moments to mathematical purposes, anticipated activities, evidence criteria, and contingent responses. Ch. 4.

**Construct validity.** The argument that a measure represents the intended idea and not construct-irrelevant demand. Ch. 3.

**Counting-all.** A strategy in which the learner counts the entire collection from one, even when one addend is already known. Ch. 1a.

**Counting-on.** A strategy in which the learner treats the first addend as a cardinal quantity and continues the count sequence. Ch. 1a.

**Data minimization.** Collecting only the data needed for the stated educational or research purpose. Ch. 2a.

**Derived fact.** A computation using known facts and structural relations rather than counting. Ch. 1a.

**Design-based research (DBR).** Iterative development of an intervention and a theory of how it functions in context. Ch. 3.

**Discriminating task.** A task whose possible responses, combined with follow-up questions, make different reasoning hypotheses more or less plausible. Ch. 1a.

**Dynamic geometry.** Software in which geometric objects maintain defined constraints under dragging; dragging creates cases, not proof. Ch. 2.

**Effect size.** A quantitative measure of contrast magnitude. It may be expressed in original units, such as a mean difference, or standardized by a declared scale; standardized effects do not replace raw-scale interpretation. Ch. 3.

**Evidence-first discussion.** A post-lesson protocol in which records are presented before interpretations, rival explanations are surfaced, and one revision is proposed with a new prediction. Ch. 4a.

**External research finding.** A bounded result reported by a named study under stated design, sample, and analysis. Front matter; Chs. 1–4a.

**Facilitation move.** An intervention in team conversation that advances inquiry without substituting the facilitator's judgment for the team's evidence. Ch. 4a.

**Funnel plot.** A scatterplot of effect estimates against precision used to probe for publication bias or other asymmetry. Ch. 3a.

**Generative system.** A large language model or image system that produces fluent but not necessarily correct output; requires independent verification. Ch. 2.

**Heterogeneity.** Variation in true effects or study characteristics that may explain differences in observed results. Ch. 3a.

**Hypothesis ledger.** A record of the assumptions built into each task, measure, or design. Front matter.

**Inquiry task.** An extended exercise requiring reasoning across sections; demand **I** in the marker scheme. Front matter.

**Internal validity.** The degree to which a design rules out rival explanations for an observed association. Ch. 3.

**Jugyou kenkyuu.** The Japanese tradition of collaborative lesson research, emphasizing long-term study, public research lessons, and curriculum connection. Ch. 4.

**Lesson study.** Collaborative inquiry in which educators investigate mathematics, plan a research lesson, observe learner activity, discuss evidence, and revise. Ch. 4.

**Local observation.** Something seen, heard, or recorded in a specific setting without a controlled design. Front matter; Chs. 1–4a.

**Low-inference record.** A timestamped note of exact words, actions, or artifacts without evaluative summary. Ch. 4.

**Mathematical result.** A statement true by definition, deduction, or calculation within stated axioms. Front matter; Chs. 1–4a.

**Meta-analysis.** A statistical synthesis of effect estimates from multiple studies, justified only when constructs, measures, and contrasts are sufficiently commensurable. Ch. 3a.

**Monte Carlo standard error.** The estimated standard deviation of a simulation-based estimator. For an average of independent draws with finite variance it is proportional to $1/\sqrt{n}$; dependence or infinite variance changes that rate or invalidates the usual formula. Ch. 2.

**Part-whole structure.** The relation among a total quantity and its constituent parts; foundational for addition, subtraction, and place value. Ch. 1.

**Place value.** A positional exchange system in which ten units of one place exchange for one unit of the next. Ch. 1.

**Preregistration.** Timestamping hypotheses, outcomes, exclusions, and analysis plans before data collection or synthesis. Ch. 3a.

**Purpose limitation.** Using data only for the purpose for which it was collected. Ch. 2a.

**Random-effects model.** A meta-analytic model representing variation in true effects across studies; does not explain heterogeneity or remove bias. Ch. 3a.

**Randomized controlled trial (RCT).** A design in which units are randomly assigned to conditions; supports causal identification under stated assumptions. Ch. 3.

**Research theme.** A long-term professional focus that bounds the lesson-study question. Ch. 4.

**Single-case design (SCD).** Repeated measures across planned phase changes for individual cases. Ch. 3.

**Standardized mean difference (SMD).** A mean contrast divided by a standard deviation, used to compare effects across different measures. Ch. 3.

**Subitizing.** Rapid recognition of a small quantity without serial counting; perceptual or conceptual. Ch. 1.

**Systematic review.** A study whose data are prior studies, using transparent, reproducible methods with a prespecified protocol. Ch. 3a.

**Teaching conjecture.** A reasoned prediction about what may help a particular group, to be tested locally. Front matter; Chs. 1–4a.

**Technology audit.** A ten-item review of goal, affordance, automation, hidden conventions, evidence, failure state, access, data, fallback, and stop condition. Ch. 2.

**Transfer task.** A problem whose surface form differs from worked examples, testing whether structure is recognized. Front matter.

**Vote counting.** The discouraged practice of counting statistically significant results rather than reporting effect sizes and precision. Ch. 3a.

## Claim finder with status guards

### Mathematical results

**Place-value exchange (Ch. 1).** In base ten, ten units of one place exchange for one unit of the next. The identity map on individual objects is a bijection before and after grouping.

**Fraction comparison by common denominator (Ch. 4).** For positive denominators, $a/b < c/d$ exactly when $ad < bc$. Cross-multiplication is valid but can obscure the multiplicative structure.

**Euclid's algorithm invariant (Ch. 2).** $\gcd(a, b) = \gcd(b, a \bmod b)$. Termination follows because the second argument strictly decreases to zero.

**Probability of at least one success (Ch. 2).** For $n$ independent trials with success probability $p$, the probability of at least one success is $1 - (1 - p)^{n}$.

**Random-effects variance (Ch. 3a).** In a random-effects model, the between-study variance $\tau^{2}$ is estimated from observed dispersion. At a fixed number of independent studies, larger $\tau^{2}$ generally reduces precision for the mean effect and widens the distinction between an interval for that mean and a prediction interval for a new study. With many informative independent studies, the mean can still be estimated precisely even when effects vary substantially.

### External research findings (bounded)

**IES practice guide on early mathematics (Ch. 1).** The What Works Clearinghouse guide *Teaching Math to Young Children* recommends developmental progressions and grades the evidence behind its recommendations. It does not make one sequence a law of development.

**Technology meta-analysis (Ch. 2).** Rakes and colleagues reported small average associations/effects for mathematics achievement and educational technology, with publication-bias and clustering adjustments materially changing estimates.

**Lesson-study randomized trial (Chs. 4, 4a).** Lewis and Perry studied lesson study supported by fractions resource kits across 39 educator teams and reported improvements on named educator and learner outcomes relative to controls. That intervention included particular resources and support; it does not isolate a context-free "lesson study effect."

### Teaching conjectures

**Container hypothesis (Ch. 1a).** Providing a container for counted objects may reduce spatial memory demand and allow one-to-one coordination to become visible. It does not guarantee transfer to open arrangements.

**Prediction-before-movement hypothesis (Ch. 1).** Prediction before rearrangement may foreground conservation of cardinality for some learners. Local evidence: predictions, counts, and explanations.

**Number-line transfer hypothesis (Ch. 4a).** If learners first encounter a conflict created by unequal wholes and then rebuild both fractions on one reusable whole, they may treat common-whole coordination as necessary rather than decorative.

**Procurement-checklist hypothesis (Ch. 2a).** A procurement checklist that includes goal alignment, data minimization, consent, retention, accessibility, and fallback may reduce adoption of tools that undermine mathematical goals or learner privacy. Needs local testing.
