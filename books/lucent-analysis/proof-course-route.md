# 180-hour real analysis proof route

This route turns the real-analysis book into 180 hours of scheduled mathematical work. It is a planning specification rather than a credit or equivalence claim. The times have not yet been calibrated with a cohort. Instructors should record actual completion times, proof errors, hint use, and marking disagreements, then revise the allocation without removing independent or delayed work.

The route assumes computational single-variable calculus and series, plus algebra with inequalities and absolute values. Start with the [logic and proof readiness chapter](../lucent-refresher/logic-proof.md). A learner who cannot reliably negate nested quantifiers or distinguish an example from a proof should complete that bridge before the clock below begins.

Every block contains four kinds of work:

- **Preparation** recalls definitions and earlier proof moves without notes, then makes a prediction before any visual is operated.
- **Developed study** reconstructs definitions, theorems, counterexamples, and proofs from the lesson. Reading a displayed proof is only the first pass.
- **Independent work** produces complete arguments before hints or solutions are opened. A proof is revised after feedback.
- **Retrieval or assessment** returns to an older result without a method label, defends a proof orally, or attempts a timed paper.

Every row sums to five hours. Each of the six units is 30 hours, for a route total of exactly 180 hours.

## D6 mixed paper: continuity, derivatives, and integrals

Use exactly D6.1–D6.3 below, in that order: 135 minutes and 45 marks. These are changed variants of [continuity Problem 1](continuity-problem-set.md), [differentiation Problem 2](differentiation-problem-set.md), and [integration Problems 2 and 4](integration-problem-set.md). They use no function-sequence or limit-exchange material. Their new task identities keep earlier problem-set drafts intact. They replace the unspecified “unused tasks” pool; the original problem sets and later examinations remain assigned.

The five-hour block contains 15 minutes of preparation, 30 minutes reconstructing definitions and theorem statements, the 135-minute first attempt, then 120 minutes for criterion-based marking (40), an oral explanation (20), corrected proofs (40), and recording a delayed retry (20). In solo study, speak and save the explanation yourself; another mathematical reader is optional. Use the timed-paper reference policy below if desired. All three complete models and rubrics are available here, so these are voluntary self-study conditions. Save first drafts and record hints, model openings, and outside help honestly. Rubric checkmarks record self-review, not automatically graded proof evidence.

## Proof dependencies and release points

The route follows dependencies rather than page numbers. Problems and primary worked solutions are public. Attempt-before-opening is a voluntary self-study convention: save a substantial first attempt before comparing it with a model. The native reader allows a primary model to be opened immediately and records that opening locally; it does not enforce submission, independence, a secure timed paper, or proof correctness. Outside help remains self-reported. The changed tasks below are public too, so an earlier opening should be recorded as exposure rather than described as an unseen assessment.

| Product | Depends on | Later retrieval |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Problem set A, initial A6 portion | Quantifiers, completeness, sequence definitions, and Cauchy estimates | Foundations examination; metric completeness in F1 |
| Problem set A, deferred Problems 2 and 6 | Sequential closedness, Bolzano–Weierstrass, and real-line compactness from B4 | Uniform continuity in C1; metric compactness in F2 |
| Problem set B | Series tails, compactness, limits, and connectedness | Uniform continuity in C1; cumulative foundation corrections |
| Continuity and differentiation sets | Compactness, local and uniform quantifiers, mean-value hypotheses | Integration in D; changed-case examination in E6 |
| Integration set | Completeness, uniform continuity on compact intervals, Darboux bounds, and derivatives | Exchange theorems in E2; mixed paper in D6 |
| Function-sequence set | Sequence tails, continuity, integration, and explicit quantifier order | Real-functions examinations; Bernstein approximation in F4 |
| Structure set | Metric completeness and compactness, quantitative iteration, uniform approximation | Continuation examination and cumulative oral defence |

A failed dependency triggers a targeted return: name the earliest false or unjustified statement, complete one changed problem at that level, and retry the blocked proof. Opening the eventual solution is not a repair.

## How visuals and computation enter a proof course

For each linked visual, write three statements before moving a control: the predicted change, the invariant, and the theorem the picture cannot establish. After operating it, record a counterexample the finite display could miss. The displayed samples may suggest a witness, modulus, subsequence, partition, or error split. The submitted proof must quantify that object independently of the pixels.

Notebook output may supply exact finite arithmetic or test a candidate bound. Save the source and state which step was computed. A numerical recurrence does not establish convergence; a dense-looking graph does not establish continuity; a sampled maximum does not establish uniform control. These distinctions are assessed explicitly.

## Marking and reference policy

For a conventional marked offering, use the following weights:

| Evidence | Weight |
| --- | ---: |
| Independent problem sets and rewritten proofs | 35% |
| Foundations, real-functions, and continuation examinations | 35% |
| Oral proof defences | 10% |
| Counterexample and theorem-hypothesis portfolio | 10% |
| Visual/notebook prediction records | 5% |
| Retrieval log and correction quality | 5% |

Unless a task supplies a more specific rubric, mark a proof for statement and hypotheses (20%), construction or strategy (20%), justified deductions (35%), boundary cases or counterexamples (15%), and readable mathematical exposition (10%). A correct final claim with a circular or missing central step cannot receive full credit. Automated exercise feedback is formative and does not mark open proofs.

Lessons, recitations, and first problem-set attempts are open-note. Timed papers permit one handwritten A4 sheet containing definitions and theorem statements, with no worked examples, proof outlines, CAS, notebook, or interactive graph. An oral defence is closed-note after two minutes of silent planning. Publish accommodations and any changed policy before the relevant unit begins.

## Delayed retrieval and calibration

Schedule a 20-minute unlabelled proof from the prior unit every week. Reattempt one changed examination problem 3–7 days after correction and again after 3–4 weeks. The second attempt must start from a blank page; comparing a remembered script is not retrieval.

Before treating this route as calibrated, independent instructors should solve and blind-mark a stratified sample from every unit. Record median completion time, inter-rater disagreements, common missing hypotheses, and any question whose intended method cannot be inferred fairly from its statement. Learner trials should measure whether the prediction–manipulation–proof sequence improves transfer to an unseen problem. Those observations, plus keyboard, screen-reader, zoom, and print testing, remain required evidence for a claim of university-course equivalence.
