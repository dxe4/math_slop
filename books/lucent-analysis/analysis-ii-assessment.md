# Real-functions practice assessment: limits and infinite processes

**Role:** cumulative written assessment for A6–A10. This is an independent practice paper, not a secure examination or a proof of mastery. A useful timed attempt is 120–150 minutes. Work from definitions and theorems already proved in the course; name every hypothesis used. A graph or computed sample may inform a conjecture but earns no proof credit by itself.

There are five original problems worth 20 points each. Record a separate attempt for each problem. Each task offers two staged hints and a worked argument after your attempt; the [solutions companion](analysis-ii-solutions.md) adds detailed point-by-point marking. After reading a solution, revise the saved proof and identify the first step that needed repair.

## 1. Extending a function to missing endpoints (20 points)

\[
|f(x)-f(y)|\le C|x-y|^\alpha
\quad\text{for all }x,y\in(0,1),
\]
where \(C>0\) and \(0<\alpha\le1\). Prove that \(f\) has a **unique** continuous extension \(F:[0,1]\to\mathbb R\), and that the same estimate holds for all \(x,y\in[0,1]\). Explain why continuity of \(f\) on \((0,1)\) alone would not suffice. This tests the extension hypothesis from A6, not just the ability to name a theorem.

## 2. FTC, mean values, and a certified approximation (20 points)

On \([0,1]\), define
\[
G(x)=\int_0^x\frac{t^2}{1+t^2}\,dt.
\]
Prove \(G\) is increasing and Lipschitz with constant \(1/2\). Show that
\[
0\le \frac{x^3}{3}-G(x)\le\frac{x^5}{5}
\quad(0\le x\le1).
\]
What error does this certify when \(x=1/2\) and \(G(1/2)\) is approximated by \(x^3/3\)? State which uses of the FTC and Mean Value Theorem are valid at endpoints.

Check endpoint hypotheses.","rubric":["4 points: applies FTC to obtain G'(x)=x^2/(1+x^2) on (0,1)","5 points: proves increasing and 1/2-Lipschitz behavior with correct MVT hypotheses","7 points: derives the exact nonnegative error integral and bounds it","2 points: computes the bound 1/160 at x=1/2","2 points: handles endpoints as continuity plus interior differentiability"],"feedback":"The integrand's sign and upper bound should both appear; a decimal quadrature result is not an error certificate.","hints":["The integrand h(t)=t²/(1+t²) is continuous and satisfies 0≤h≤1/2 on [0,1]; apply FTC I and then MVT on each [x,y].","Subtract G(x) from the integral of t² before estimating: the exact error integrand is t⁴/(1+t²)."],"solution":"The integrand \\(h(t)=t^2/(1+t^2)\\) is continuous on \\([0,1]\\). This is a bound, not an assertion that the actual error equals \\(1/160\\)."}
```

## 3. Oscillation that survives every partition (20 points)

Define \(w:[0,1]\to\mathbb R\) by \(w(x)=x\) when \(x\) is rational and \(w(x)=-x\) when \(x\) is irrational. Determine its points of continuity. Prove directly from Darboux upper and lower sums that \(w\) is **not** Riemann integrable. Your proof should give one positive lower bound for \(U(w,P)-L(w,P)\) for *every* partition \(P\), not just for uniform partitions.

```exercise

At any c>0, approach c through rational and irrational sequences.","Refine an arbitrary partition at 1/2. On each resulting cell [u,v] inside [1/2,1], determine its supremum and infimum by density, then sum a lower bound for its gap."],"solution":"At zero, \\(|w(x)-w(0)|=|w(x)|=x\\to0\\), so \\(w\\) is continuous. At any \\(c>0\\), choose rational \\(r_k\\to c\\) and irrational \\(s_k\\to c\\). Then \\(w(r_k)=r_k\\to c\\) and \\(w(s_k)=-s_k\\to-c\\). Since \\(c\\ne-c\\), no single limit exists there; \\(w\\) is discontinuous at every positive point.\n\nTake any partition \\(P\\) of \\([0,1]\\) and refine it by adding \\(1/2\\), obtaining \\(P'\\). Refinement cannot increase \\(U-L\\), so \\(U(w,P)-L(w,P)\\ge U(w,P')-L(w,P')\\). On every subinterval \\([u,v]\\) of \\(P'\\) lying in \\([1/2,1]\\), density of rational and irrational numbers gives\n\\[\n\\sup_{[u,v]}w=v,\\qquad \\inf_{[u,v]}w=-v.\n\\]\nThe subinterval's contribution to \\(U-L\\) is \\(2v(v-u)\\ge v-u\\), because \\(v\\ge1/2\\). ## 4. A peak whose area vanishes (20 points)

For positive integers \(n\), let
\[
H_n(x)=n^2x^n(1-x)^2,\qquad 0\le x\le1.
\]
Find the pointwise limit, locate the maximum and its limiting height, and compute \(\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_0^1H_n(x)\,dx\). Compare this integral limit with the integral of the pointwise limit. Does equality establish uniform convergence? Explain with an exact estimate or witness.

## 5. A power series and a logarithm (20 points)

For \(|x|<1\), consider
\[
P(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{2n+1}}{2n+1}.
\]
Find its radius of convergence in the variable \(x\). Justify differentiating the series on every compact subinterval of \((-1,1)\), then derive a closed expression for \(P(x)\) using \(P(0)=0\) and the logarithm established earlier in the course. Test \(x=1\) and \(x=-1\) separately. State precisely why an interior differentiation theorem alone says nothing about those endpoints.
