
# Prerequisite Diagnostic

Try these five prompts before opening the answers. (1) Why is F[x]/(p) a field for irreducible p? (2) If [E:K]=3 and [K:F]=2, what is [E:F]? (3) What proves a finite permutation action faithful? (4) What is the difference between a normal subgroup and a normal field extension? (5) Why does a group containing a nonsolvable subgroup fail to be solvable?

<details>
<summary>Worked answers and repair route</summary>

1. A nonzero residue class has a representative a not divisible by p. Euclid gives ua+vp=1, so the class of u is its inverse. Repair polynomial Euclidean algorithms and ideals in the algebra book.

2. Products of a K-basis of E with an F-basis of K form an F-basis of E, giving 6. Spanning follows by two expansions; independence follows by collecting coefficients first over K and then over F. Repair the tower-law proof before using degrees as arithmetic labels.

3. Its kernel must contain only the identity. For a field generated by roots, a base-fixing automorphism that fixes every root fixes all rational expressions in them and is therefore the identity. Faithfulness does not mean every permutation is realised.

4. Normality of a subgroup concerns conjugation in a specified ambient group. Normality of an algebraic extension requires every irreducible polynomial over its base with a root in the extension to split there. The Galois correspondence eventually connects these different definitions under finite Galois hypotheses.

5. For a subgroup H≤G, each derived subgroup H^(r) lies in G^(r), by induction using commutators. If G has terminating derived series then H does too. Thus a nonsolvable H rules out solvable G. Repair the algebra book's solvable-groups chapter before the radical-solvability continuation.
