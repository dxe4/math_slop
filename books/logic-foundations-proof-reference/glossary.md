## Glossary {#section-5-glossary}

**Arithmetical hierarchy.** Classification of definable sets of natural numbers by quantifier complexity: $\Sigma^{0}_{n}$, $\Pi^{0}_{n}$, $\Delta^{0}_{n}$. Chapter 12 (Computability and Turing Machines); "Descriptive Set Theory".

**BHK interpretation.** Brouwer--Heyting--Kolmogorov interpretation of intuitionistic logic: proofs of compound formulas are built from proofs of components. Chapter 15 (Intuitionistic and Constructive Logic).

**Borel hierarchy.** Classification of subsets of a Polish space by countable unions and complements: $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{0}_{\alpha}$, $\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{0}_{\alpha}$, $\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{0}_{\alpha}$. "Descriptive Set Theory".

**Canonical model.** For a normal modal logic $\Lambda$, the Kripke model whose worlds are maximal $\Lambda$-consistent sets. "Modal Logic".

**Capture-avoiding substitution.** Substitution of a term for a variable that first alpha-renames binders to prevent accidental binding of free variables. "Reference desk A --- levels of language".

**Compactness theorem.** A set of first-order sentences has a model iff every finite subset has a model. "Model Theory".

**Completeness theorem.** If $T \models \varphi$, then $T \vdash \varphi$ (for classical first-order logic). "Model Theory".

**Consistency.** A theory $T$ is consistent if $T \not\vdash \bot$. Chapter 13 (Incompleteness and Arithmetization).

**Constructible universe $L$.** Goedel's inner model built by transfinite recursion; satisfies ZFC + GCH. "Forcing"; Chapter 16 (The Continuum Problem and Independence).

**Continuum hypothesis (CH).** $2^{\aleph_{0}} = \aleph_{1}$. Independent of ZFC. "Forcing"; Chapter 16.

**Cut elimination.** Transformation of a sequent calculus proof into a cut-free proof; yields the subformula property. Chapter 14 (Sequent Calculus and Proof Theory).

**Dense linear order without endpoints.** A total order where between any two elements there is a third, with no first or last element. "Model Theory".

**Determinacy (AD).** Axiom that every subset of $\omega^{\omega}$ is determined. Contradicts AC. "Descriptive Set Theory".

**Diagonal lemma.** For every formula $\psi(x)$, there exists a sentence $\varphi$ such that $T \vdash \varphi \leftrightarrow \psi(\ulcorner \varphi \urcorner)$. Chapter 13.

**Forcing.** Method for constructing models of set theory with prescribed properties; proves independence results. "Forcing".

**Forcing relation ($\Vdash$).** Ground-model definable relation $p \Vdash \varphi$ connecting conditions to truth in the generic extension. "Forcing".

**Frame correspondence.** Equivalence between validity of a modal formula and a property of the accessibility relation. "Modal Logic".

**Generic extension $M[G]$.** The model obtained by evaluating $\mathbb{P}$-names via a generic filter $G$. "Forcing".

**Gödel numbering.** Effective injective coding of formulas and derivations by natural numbers; not every number need code a well-formed expression. Chapter 13.

**Halting problem.** The set $\{\langle M, w \rangle : M \text{ halts on } w\}$ is undecidable. Chapter 12.

**Henkin construction.** Method for building models from maximally consistent sets with witness constants. "Model Theory".

**Higher inductive type (HIT).** Type defined by generators for points and paths; e.g., $S^{1}$. "Homotopy Type Theory".

**Inaccessible cardinal.** Uncountable regular strong-limit cardinal; $V_{\kappa} \models \text{ZFC}$. "Large Cardinals".

**Independence result.** A statement shown unprovable and unrefutable from a specified base theory. "Forcing"; Chapter 16.

**Intuitionistic logic.** Logic without excluded middle or double-negation elimination. Chapter 15.

**Kripke frame.** Pair $(W, R)$ of worlds and accessibility relation. "Modal Logic"; "Temporal Logic".

**Large cardinal axiom.** Axiom asserting the existence of a cardinal with strong properties not provable in ZFC. "Large Cardinals".

**Linear logic.** Resource-sensitive logic with multiplicative and additive connectives and exponential modalities. "Linear Logic".

**Löwenheim--Skolem theorems.** Downward: an infinite structure has elementary substructures of controlled size containing prescribed parameters, subject to the language-size bound. Upward: a theory with an infinite model has models in each cardinality at least the size of its language. "Model Theory".

**Measurable cardinal.** Uncountable cardinal $\kappa$ carrying a nonprincipal $\kappa$-complete ultrafilter. "Large Cardinals".

**Modal logic.** Extension of propositional logic with necessity ($\Box$) and possibility ($\Diamond$). "Modal Logic".

**Model checking.** Algorithmic verification that a finite transition system satisfies a temporal formula. "Temporal Logic".

**Nonstandard model.** Model elementarily equivalent to the standard model but not isomorphic to it. "Model Theory".

**Ordinal analysis.** Assignment of proof-theoretic ordinals to theories, measuring consistency strength. Chapter 14.

**Polish space.** Separable completely metrizable topological space. "Descriptive Set Theory".

**Projective hierarchy.** Extension of the Borel hierarchy by continuous images and complements: $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{1}_{n}$, $\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{1}_{n}$, $\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{1}_{n}$. "Descriptive Set Theory".

**Proof-theoretic ordinal.** Supremum of ordinals of well-founded orderings that a theory can prove well-founded. Chapter 14.

**Realizability.** Computational semantics for intuitionistic logic; extracts algorithms from proofs. Chapter 15.

**Reverse mathematics.** Program determining the weakest subsystem of second-order arithmetic needed to prove theorems. "Reverse Mathematics".

**Rice's theorem.** Every nontrivial semantic property of Turing machines is undecidable. Chapter 12.

**Rosser sentence.** A sentence $R_{T}$ whose independence from $T$ is provable from consistency alone. Chapter 13.

**Sequent calculus.** Proof system using sequents $\Gamma \Rightarrow \Delta$ with structural and logical rules. Chapter 14; "Linear Logic".

**Soundness theorem.** If $T \vdash \varphi$, then $T \models \varphi$. "Model Theory".

**Stone space.** Space of ultrafilters on a Boolean algebra; compact, Hausdorff, totally disconnected. "Set-Theoretic Topology".

**Strongly compact cardinal.** In the filter formulation, an uncountable regular $\kappa$ for which every $\kappa$-complete filter on any set extends to a $\kappa$-complete ultrafilter. "Large Cardinals".

**Subformula property.** Strict subformulas suffice in propositional cut-free proofs; first-order cut-free proofs may contain substitution instances of endsequent subformulas. Chapter 14.

**Tarski--Vaught test.** Criterion for a substructure to be elementary. "Model Theory".

**Temporal logic.** Modal logic specialized to reasoning about time; includes LTL and CTL. "Temporal Logic".

**Topos.** Category with finite limits, exponentials, and a subobject classifier; generalizes $\mathbf{Set}$. Chapter 16.

**Turing machine.** Formal model of computation: finite control, infinite tape, transition function. Chapter 12.

**Univalence axiom.** $(A =_{\mathcal{U}} B) \simeq (A \simeq B)$; paths between types correspond to equivalences. "Homotopy Type Theory".

**Weakly compact cardinal.** Inaccessible cardinal with the tree property; satisfies compactness for $L_{\kappa\kappa}$. "Large Cardinals".

**$\varepsilon_{0}$.** Smallest ordinal $\alpha$ with $\omega^{\alpha} = \alpha$; proof-theoretic ordinal of PA. Chapter 14.

### Theorems {#section-115-theorems}

**Soundness and completeness ("Model Theory").** First-order logic is sound ($T \vdash \varphi \Rightarrow T \models \varphi$) and complete ($T \models \varphi \Rightarrow T \vdash \varphi$). The completeness proof uses the Henkin construction.

**Compactness ("Model Theory").** A set of first-order sentences has a model iff every finite subset has a model. Follows from completeness.

**Löwenheim--Skolem ("Model Theory").** The downward and upward theorems provide elementary substructures and larger models under explicit language and cardinality bounds.

**Goedel--Rosser incompleteness (Chapter 13).** Every consistent, recursively axiomatizable extension of Robinson arithmetic $Q$ is incomplete.

**Goedel's second incompleteness theorem (Chapter 13).** A consistent, sufficiently strong, recursively axiomatizable theory $T$ cannot prove $\text{Con}(T)$.

**Turing undecidability of HALT (Chapter 12).** The halting problem is undecidable; proved by diagonalization.

**Rice's theorem (Chapter 12).** Every nontrivial semantic property of the language recognized by a Turing machine is undecidable.

**Martin's Borel determinacy ("Descriptive Set Theory").** All Borel games are determined; a theorem of ZFC.

**Fundamental theorem of forcing ("Forcing").** If the transitive ground model $M$ satisfies ZFC and $G$ is $M$-generic for $\mathbb P\in M$, then $M[G]$ is a transitive model of ZFC containing $M$ and $G$, with the same ordinals.

<a id="section-133-independence-results"></a>
### Independence results {#section-135-independence-results}

**Independence of CH from ZFC ("Forcing"; Chapter 16).** Cohen's forcing constructs a model of ZFC + $\neg$CH. Goedel's $L$ is a model of ZFC + CH. Hence CH is independent.

**Independence of the perfect set property for $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{1}_{2}$ ("Descriptive Set Theory").** In ZFC + $V = L$, there is a $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{1}_{2}$ set without the perfect set property. Under projective determinacy, every projective set has it.

<a id="section-139-open-problems"></a>
### Open problems {#section-141-open-problems}

**Consistency of a Reinhardt cardinal with ZF (Chapter 16).** A nontrivial elementary embedding $j: V \to V$ is known inconsistent with ZFC (Kunen's theorem). Whether it is consistent with ZF remains open.

**Metatheory of univalent type theories (Chapter 16).** Normalization, canonicity, models, and computational behavior are precise only after a particular univalent calculus is fixed. Whether a community should “replace ZFC” is a methodological question rather than one yes/no theorem.

## Graduate-route additions and identity note {#section-147-graduate-route-additions-and-identity-note}

**Associate.** A finite-string code for a continuous map on Cantor space whose positive labels determine persistent output values and which is positive on a prefix of every path. Reverse Mathematics.

**Essential undecidability.** Every consistent computably enumerable extension of the specified theory has undecidable theorem set. Decidable and Undecidable Theories.

**Full second-order semantics.** Relation variables range over all actual relations of their arity on the object domain. Second-Order Logic and Lindström’s Theorem.

**Henkin semantics.** Available relation families are part of the structure, with explicitly chosen comprehension axioms. The many-sorted first-order translation does not assert fullness. Second-Order Logic and Lindström’s Theorem.

**Hereditary Cantor notation.** A finite recursive ordinal notation whose exponents are themselves represented in normal form; comparison is a structural calculation. Ordinal Analysis and Gentzen’s Consistency Proof.

**Primitive-recursive representability.** The chapter’s specified numeralwise arithmetic representation input, distinct from Q proving arbitrary totality. Recursive Functions and Representability.

**Regular abstract logic.** The framework with isomorphism invariance, finite occurrence, renaming, Boolean/first-order closure and relativisation specified before invoking Lindström’s characterization. Second-Order Logic and Lindström’s Theorem.
