
# References and Further Reading {#section-1-references-and-further-reading}

This bibliography identifies standard comparison texts, historical sources, and current research directions. The book is written under CC BY-SA 4.0; standard definitions and logical conventions belong to shared practice. References are included for further reading, historical context, alternative treatments, and broader coverage.

A named result or study is a retrieval convention, not a complete history. Logical ideas are often the product of long development across languages, institutions, and communities; a modern statement may differ sharply from an early source.

<a id="section-5-logic-and-model-theory"></a>
## Logic and model theory {#section-7-logic-and-model-theory}

- Enderton, H. B. (2001). [*A Mathematical Introduction to Logic* (2nd ed.)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/book/9780122384523/a-mathematical-introduction-to-logic). Academic Press. A first course in propositional and first-order logic, with soundness, completeness, and compactness.
- Enderton, H. B. (1977). [*Elements of Set Theory*](https://www.sciencedirect.com/book/9780122384400/elements-of-set-theory). Academic Press. Introductory coverage of cardinals, ordinals, and the cumulative hierarchy.
- Marker, D. (2002). [*Model Theory: An Introduction*](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/b98860). Graduate Texts in Mathematics 217. Springer.
- Hodges, W. (1997). [*A Shorter Model Theory*](https://assets.cambridge.org/97805215/87136/frontmatter/9780521587136_frontmatter.pdf). Cambridge University Press.

These texts use different proof calculi and notational conventions. Comparison is most useful when those conventions are recorded rather than blended.

<a id="section-14-set-theory-and-forcing"></a>
## Set theory and forcing {#section-16-set-theory-and-forcing}

- Kunen, K. (2011). [*Set Theory*](https://www.collegepublications.co.uk/logic/mlf/?00016=). Studies in Logic: Mathematical Logic and Foundations 34. College Publications. ISBN 978-1-84890-050-9. The publisher description supports constructibility, forcing, and infinitary combinatorics; this entry does not promise a specific treatment of proper forcing.
- Jech, T. (2003). [*Set Theory: The Third Millennium Edition, Revised and Expanded*](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/3-540-44761-X) (3rd ed.). Springer. The copyright year is 2003; the hardcover publication date listed by Springer is 2002.
- Shoenfield, J. R. (1971). “Unramified forcing.” In *Axiomatic Set Theory*, Proceedings of Symposia in Pure Mathematics 13(1), 357--381. American Mathematical Society. [Volume record](https://bookstore.ams.org/PSPUM/13.1). This is an early systematic exposition; the wording does not assign priority for introducing forcing.

The set-theoretic literature varies in its treatment of the countable transitive model assumption versus Boolean-valued models. This book uses the countable transitive model approach for concreteness.

## Exact locators used for repaired claims {#section-55-exact-locators-used-for-repaired-claims}

- The HoTT Book, §4.9, Theorems 4.9.4--4.9.5, supplies the chain univalence $\Rightarrow$ weak function extensionality $\Rightarrow$ function extensionality.
- Simpson's author-hosted introduction, §I.10 item 8 and §IV.3, gives the $\mathsf{WKL}_0$ calibration for the countable-consistent-theory/countable-model completeness formulation and warns through nearby variants that the coding matters.
- Schweber's dissertation locator above supports assigning both open and clopen determinacy for games on natural numbers to $\mathsf{ATR}_0$, rather than splitting equivalent formulations between two Big Five systems.

<a id="section-59-historical-attribution-checklist"></a>
## Historical attribution checklist {#section-61-historical-attribution-checklist}

Every historical or priority claim should record:

1. a classification as contemporary exposition, secondary history, translated primary source, or primary source;
2. verified author, title, edition, publisher or archive, and stable locator;
3. a note explaining whether the citation supports provenance, formulation, evidence, example, or broader context;
4. a check that modern terminology is not projected unchanged backward;
5. careful comparison of primary sources for culturally broad claims and disputed priority.

<a id="section-69-reading-route-recommendations"></a>
## Reading-route recommendations {#section-71-reading-route-recommendations}

For a deeper treatment of model theory, proceed to Marker or Hodges. For set-theoretic independence, continue into Kunen's treatment of iterated forcing and large cardinals. For computability, Sipser provides complexity-theoretic context. For proof theory, Troelstra and Schwichtenberg cover ordinal analysis and advanced cut-elimination results. For constructive mathematics, Troelstra and van Dalen treat realizability and choice principles in depth. For HoTT, the HoTT Book is the natural next step.

<a id="section-73-citation-policy-for-contributors"></a>
## Citation policy for contributors {#section-75-citation-policy-for-contributors}

Paraphrase sources and cite the job they perform. Do not copy exercise sequences, distinctive examples, diagrams, or prose merely because a theorem is standard. Direct quotations require exact locators and license or fair-use review. When sources disagree, record the disagreement. When provenance is unknown, label it unknown rather than inventing a clean lineage.
