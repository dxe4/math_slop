
# Reader Guide and Conventions

The level is L3: advanced undergraduate. Bring fluency with ordinary proof, sets, relations, functions, induction and elementary cardinality from Introduction to Proof. The first six chapters introduce formal logic rather than assuming a previous logic course. The next four make set existence and transfinite constructions explicit. The final three distinguish computation, formal provability and relative consistency.

First-order structures have nonempty domains, total interpreted functions and actual identity for equality. Signatures are set-sized and finitary. Quantifier substitution is capture avoiding; bound variables are renamed when necessary. Deduction is classical natural deduction, with the discharge and eigenvariable conditions stated in the text. The countable Henkin construction is developed first. The general-signature model and cardinal-size arguments take place in ambient ZFC; this is a metatheoretic assumption, separate from whether an object model satisfies Choice.

Chapters on sets explicitly use ZF until Choice is added. Quantifiers within an object model range over that model's domain. Internal functions, power sets and natural numbers must not silently be replaced by every corresponding external object. Arbitrary models from completeness need not be transitive or externally well-founded. Infinite cardinal arithmetic is restricted to well-orderable sets before Choice is available.

Every chapter has three or more worked examples and ten exercises written for this route. Exercise identifiers use the semantic chapter stem and remain stable. Read the question, try your own proof, and open hints one at a time. A full solution supplies a mathematical model answer; it does not assess or grade your own attempt. An exercise asking for an explanation has a model explanation and explicit criteria, rather than an unspecified “answers vary”.

The proofs of elementary core results appear in the chapters. Some deeper inputs are stated and cited: arithmetic representability, Rosser's strengthening, second incompleteness, constructibility and forcing independence. Do not cite an illustrative construction as a proof of a larger theorem. In particular the last chapter's generic binary sequence is conditional on a countable transitive model and does not establish the forcing theorem or a continuum calculation.
