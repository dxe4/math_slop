
# References, Provenance and Proof Boundaries {#section-1-references-provenance-and-proof-boundaries}

Seven earlier Euler readings are available as separate companions. Their numbered cross-references refer to the earlier book; use the links in the current chapters for this reading route.

Daniel A. Marcus, *Number Fields*, and Tom M. Apostol, *Introduction to
Analytic Number Theory*, provide further reading. Joseph H.
Silverman, *The Arithmetic of Elliptic Curves*, is a further comparison for
the curve and descent chapters.  Primary author or institutional references consulted for
theorem scope and normalization include:

- [Don Zagier, Newman's Short Proof of the Prime Number Theorem](https://people.mpim-bonn.mpg.de/zagier/files/doi/10.2307/2975232/fulltext.pdf).
  The method is credited; the contour proof and supported exercise route
  are supplied in original Euler wording.
- [J. S. Milne, Algebraic Number Theory](https://www.jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/ANT.pdf).
  This author-hosted text provides comparison terminology for ideal, class,
  unit, cyclotomic and valuation results. The new primary proofs do not
  treat the Quarto surveys as proofs.
- [Andrew V. Sutherland, MIT Riemann–Roch lecture](https://math.mit.edu/classes/18.782/2023sp/LectureNotes22.pdf).
  Riemann–Roch and basic smooth-projective-curve divisor facts are explicit
  geometric inputs to the associativity deduction.
- [Sutherland, MIT Mordell–Weil lecture](https://math.mit.edu/classes/18.782/2023sp/LectureNotes25.pdf).
  The book proves its own explicit-halving, weak-finiteness and height-descent
  route; weak Mordell–Weil is not silently imported.
- [Sutherland, MIT Modular Forms and L-functions](https://math.mit.edu/classes/18.783/2021/LectureNotes24.pdf),
  [Discrete Logarithms](https://math.mit.edu/classes/18.783/2025/LectureNotes9.pdf),
  and [Integer Factorisation](https://math.mit.edu/classes/18.783/2025/LectureNotes10.pdf).
  These are official comparisons for the final chapters' hypotheses and
  algorithmic distinctions.
- [NIST DLMF, zeta reflection formulas](https://dlmf.nist.gov/25.4).
  The completion's normalization is compared here; the theta proof is given
  in the manuscript, and gamma properties come from the actual linked Euler
  complex-analysis proof.

The character seed explicitly imports nonprincipal Dirichlet nonvanishing at
one; its periodic convergence argument alone does not prove nonvanishing.
Hasse's theorem, general elliptic modularity and named rank conjectures retain
their stated boundaries in the earlier elliptic material. The new modular
chapter proves full-level results and distinguishes the nontrivial-level
modularity theorem. Its eta-product identity and general eigenbasis theorem
are not claimed proved or used. The local chapter proves unramified and
Eisenstein constructions and identifies the broader finite-extension theorem
as further theory.
