
# Prerequisite Diagnostic

Before reading the main chapters, try these four questions. The answers identify preparation rather than a graded threshold.

1. Why can a matrix with two negative eigenvalues still amplify Euclidean distance briefly? A nonnormal matrix may have nonorthogonal eigendirections; asymptotic spectral decay does not imply monotone contraction in the chosen norm. Review matrix flows in the [ODE course](../ode-first-course/linear-systems-matrix-exponential.md).
2. If a symmetric matrix is positive definite, why is its quadratic form a useful state-size measure? Orthogonal diagonalisation bounds it above and below by positive eigenvalue multiples of the squared Euclidean norm. Review [linear algebra](#/books/linear-algebra-structure-proofs).
3. Can a stable uncontrolled system be unable to reach a selected state? Yes. Stability concerns its homogeneous evolution; controllability concerns the range generated by its input map. A missing input direction can remain unreachable even when it decays naturally.
4. Does a decreasing energy prove convergence to one equilibrium? Only with an appropriate attraction argument. A semidefinite derivative requires an invariant-set analysis under compactness and existence hypotheses. Review [Lyapunov functions](../dynamical-systems-first-course/lyapunov-functions.md).

Repair these distinctions before interpreting rank conditions, Gramian inverses or observer gains. Matrix rank is exact algebra; a small singular value signals numerical sensitivity, which is an additional question. A derivative taken formally through an ODE requires common existence and smooth-dependence assumptions before it becomes a theorem.

When probability enters, a covariance matrix describes second moments under a specified law. It does not turn process disturbances into independent white noise by notation alone. The [filtering chapter](kalman-filtering.md) states its process, observation and independence assumptions and links the probability preparation.
