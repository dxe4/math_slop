## Glossary

**Aperiodic tiling.** A tiling of the plane that admits no translation symmetry. Ch. 13.

**Binomial coefficient.** $\binom{n}{k} = \frac{n!}{k!(n-k)!}$, the number of $k$-element subsets of an $n$-element set. Chs. 7, 17.

**Birthday collision.** The probability that at least two people in a group share a birthday; at $n=23$ the probability exceeds $1/2$. Chs. 6, 17.

**Bouton's theorem.** A Nim position is losing exactly when its nim-sum (bitwise XOR of heap sizes) is zero. Ch. 3.

**Crossing number.** The minimum number of crossings in any diagram of a knot. Ch. 14.

**Degree (of a vertex).** The number of incident edge-ends; a loop contributes two. Chs. 5, 8, 12.

**Domino tiling.** A covering of a grid region by $1 \times 2$ rectangles with no gaps or overlaps. Chs. 2, 13.

**Euler trail.** A trail that uses every edge of a graph exactly once. A finite connected graph has an Euler trail if and only if it has $0$ or $2$ vertices of odd degree. Chs. 8, 12.

**Expected value.** The weighted average of a random variable's outcomes, $\mathbb{E}[X] = \sum x \, P(X=x)$. Chs. 6, 17.

**Faro shuffle.** A perfect shuffle that splits a deck into two equal halves and interleaves them exactly. Ch. 16.

**Fractal dimension.** A measure of the complexity of a self-similar set, often computed as $\log N / \log (1/r)$ for $N$ copies at scale $r$. Ch. 18.

**Gray code.** A list of all $n$-bit strings in which consecutive strings differ in exactly one bit. Ch. 9.

**Grundy number.** The minimum excluded value (mex) of the Grundy numbers of a position's options; a position is losing exactly when its Grundy number is $0$. Ch. 3.

**Hamming distance.** The number of positions in which two equal-length bit strings differ. Ch. 7.

**Handshake lemma.** The sum of vertex degrees in any finite undirected graph equals twice the number of edges; consequently, the number of odd-degree vertices is even. Chs. 8, 12.

**Hausdorff dimension.** A dimension measure for fractal sets based on covering at arbitrarily small scales. Ch. 18.

**Huzita–Hatori operations.** Seven idealized fold operations, under the convention fixed in Chapter 15. The simultaneous two-point-to-two-line operation can require a cubic root and enlarges the classical straightedge-and-compass construction field. Ch. 15.

**Impartial game.** A game in which both players have the same legal moves from every position. Ch. 3.

**Invariant.** A quantity that remains unchanged under every legal move of a process or game. Ch. 1.

**Julia set.** The boundary between points whose orbits escape and those that remain bounded under iteration of $z \mapsto z^2 + c$ for a fixed $c$. Ch. 18.

**Kawasaki–Justin theorem.** An unassigned single-vertex crease pattern with consecutive sector angles $a_1,\dots,a_{2n}$ is locally flat-foldable if and only if $a_1+a_3+\dots+a_{2n-1} = a_2+a_4+\dots+a_{2n} = 180^{\circ}$. Ch. 15.

**Latin square.** An $n \times n$ array in which each symbol $1,\dots,n$ appears exactly once in each row and column. Ch. 11.

**Linking number.** An integer invariant that counts how many times one component of a link winds around another, with sign. Ch. 14.

**Magic constant.** The common sum of every row, column, and diagonal in a magic square. Ch. 10.

**Magic square.** An $n \times n$ array filled with $1,\dots,n^2$ so that every row, column, and both main diagonals have the same sum. Ch. 10.

**Maekawa–Justin theorem.** At a single interior vertex in a flat folding, the number of mountain folds minus the number of valley folds is $\pm 2$. Ch. 15.

**Mandelbrot set.** The set of complex numbers $c$ for which the iteration $z_{n+1} = z_n^2 + c$ starting from $z_0 = 0$ remains bounded. Ch. 18.

**Mex (minimum excluded value).** The smallest nonnegative integer not in a given set. Ch. 3.

**Modular arithmetic.** Arithmetic on residue classes modulo $n$; $a \equiv b \pmod{n}$ when $n \mid (a-b)$. Chs. 1, 3, 10.

**Nim-sum.** The bitwise XOR of heap sizes in Nim; Bouton's theorem characterises losing positions by nim-sum zero. Ch. 3.

**Orthogonal Latin squares.** Two Latin squares whose superposition yields every ordered pair exactly once. Ch. 11.

**Parity.** The property of being even or odd; often used as an invariant modulo $2$. Ch. 1.

**Penrose tiling.** An aperiodic tiling of the plane using kite and dart tiles with matching rules. Ch. 13.

**Perfect shuffle.** A deterministic shuffle that interleaves two halves of a deck exactly; the out-shuffle preserves the top card. Ch. 16.

**Planar graph.** A graph that can be drawn in the plane without edge crossings. Chs. 5, 8, 12.

**Pigeonhole principle.** If $n+1$ objects occupy $n$ boxes, some box contains at least two objects. Ch. 9.

**Reidemeister move.** One of three local moves (type I, II, III) that characterise equivalence of knot diagrams. Ch. 14.

**Riffle shuffle.** A realistic shuffle modelled by cutting the deck into two piles and interleaving them probabilistically. Ch. 16.

**Self-similarity.** The property of a set that it contains copies of itself at smaller scales. Ch. 18.

**Semimagic square.** A square with equal row and column sums but without the diagonal condition. Ch. 10.

**Sierpinski carpet.** A fractal obtained by recursively removing the centre square from a $3 \times 3$ grid. Ch. 18.

**Sierpinski triangle.** A fractal obtained by recursively removing the central triangle from an equilateral triangle. Ch. 18.

**Simpson's paradox.** A trend that appears in separate groups of data but disappears or reverses when the groups are combined. Ch. 17.

**Sprague–Grundy theorem.** The Grundy number of a sum of independent impartial games is the XOR of the component Grundy numbers. Ch. 3.

**Strategy stealing.** A nonconstructive argument that the first player wins certain placement games because a second-player winning strategy could be "stolen." Ch. 3.

**Total variation distance.** A measure of the difference between two probability distributions; used to quantify how many shuffles are needed to randomise a deck. Ch. 16.

**Tree.** A connected graph with no cycles. Chs. 8, 12.

**Tromino.** A tile composed of three edge-connected squares; the L-tromino is the most common shape. Ch. 13.

**Unknot.** The trivial knot, equivalent to a simple circle. Ch. 14.

**Writhe.** The sum of crossing signs in an oriented knot diagram; not a knot invariant because it changes under Reidemeister type I moves. Ch. 14.

## Index of theorems and constructions

**Bouton's theorem (Ch. 3).** A Nim position is losing exactly when its nim-sum is zero.

**Euler's formula (Chs. 5, 8, 12).** For a connected planar graph, $v - e + f = 2$.

**Kawasaki–Justin theorem (Ch. 15).** An unassigned single-vertex crease pattern is locally flat-foldable if and only if its alternating sector-angle sums are both $180^\circ$.

**Maekawa–Justin theorem (Ch. 15).** At a single interior vertex in a flat folding, $M - V = \pm 2$.

**Planar graph bounds (Chs. 5, 8, 12).** A simple planar graph with $v \ge 3$ satisfies $e \le 3v - 6$; a simple bipartite planar graph satisfies $e \le 2v - 4$.

**Sprague–Grundy theorem (Ch. 3).** The Grundy number of a disjunctive sum is the XOR of component Grundy numbers.

## Index of algorithms and procedures

**Breadth-first search (Ch. 19).** Explores states in order of distance from the start; guarantees shortest paths in unweighted graphs.

**Gray code construction (Ch. 9).** Recursive prefixing of $0$ and $1$ in forward and reverse order.

**Nim strategy (Ch. 3).** Compute nim-sum; if nonzero, change one heap to make the nim-sum zero.

**Siamese method (Ch. 10).** Construction of odd-order magic squares by upward-right placement with wrap-around and fallback.
