
# References, Attribution, and Further Routes {#section-1-references-attribution-and-further-routes}

This edition-bound bibliography maps each source to the job it performs. Named results are conventional retrieval labels, not claims of first or sole invention. Modern sources control the formulations used in the manuscript; original papers provide selected provenance. Specialist human review of historical priority and interpretation remains required before release.

<a id="section-3-foundational-textbooks"></a>
## Foundational textbooks {#section-5-foundational-textbooks}

- P. A. M. Dirac, *The Principles of Quantum Mechanics* (4th ed., revised; 1981 reprint). Clarendon Press/Oxford University Press. ISBN 9780198520115. Transformation theory and bra-ket language; historically central, not a modern domain-first introduction.
- J. J. Sakurai and J. Napolitano, *Modern Quantum Mechanics* (3rd ed., 2020). Cambridge University Press. https://doi.org/10.1017/9781108587280. Hardback ISBN 9781108473224. Symmetry, spin, approximation, physical problem solving, and the modern Wigner-theorem formulation in Section 4.4.1.
- C. Cohen-Tannoudji, B. Diu, and F. Laloë, *Quantum Mechanics*, Volume 1 (2nd ed., 2020). Wiley-VCH. eISBN 9783527822713; print ISBN 9783527345533. Extensive derivations of the fundamental formalism and standard applications. This entry deliberately binds Volume 1 only.
- D. J. Griffiths and D. F. Schroeter, *Introduction to Quantum Mechanics* (3rd ed., 2018). Cambridge University Press. https://doi.org/10.1017/9781316995433. Chapters 1 and 3 support the Born/projection core; Chapters 8 and 9 support the variational and WKB treatments.

<a id="section-10-mathematical-foundations"></a>
## Mathematical foundations {#section-12-mathematical-foundations}

- M. Reed and B. Simon, *Methods of Modern Mathematical Physics*. Academic Press: Volume I, *Functional Analysis* (revised ed., 1980; official electronic record https://shop.elsevier.com/books/i-functional-analysis/reed/978-0-08-057048-8); Volume II, *Fourier Analysis, Self-Adjointness* (1975; https://shop.elsevier.com/books/ii-fourier-analysis-self-adjointness/reed/978-0-08-092537-0); Volume III, *Scattering Theory* (1979; https://shop.elsevier.com/books/iii-scattering-theory/reed/978-0-08-092538-7); Volume IV, *Analysis of Operators* (1978; https://shop.elsevier.com/books/iv-analysis-of-operators/reed/978-0-08-057045-7). The volume is stated whenever this series is used; the linked publisher records identify later electronic formats of the selected print editions.
- B. C. Hall, *Quantum Theory for Mathematicians* (2013). Springer. https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4614-7116-5. Mathematical structures with physics connections; Chapter 13 supports the modern uncertainty formulation and Chapter 16, pages 305--331, the variational and semiclassical routes.
- G. Teschl, *Mathematical Methods in Quantum Mechanics: With Applications to Schrödinger Operators* (2nd ed., 2014), Graduate Studies in Mathematics 157. American Mathematical Society. https://doi.org/10.1090/gsm/157.
- J. von Neumann, *Mathematical Foundations of Quantum Mechanics* (new ed., 2018), translated by R. T. Beyer and edited by N. A. Wheeler. Princeton University Press. ISBN 9780691178561. Foundational operator formulation in historical context.

<a id="section-17-information-and-open-systems"></a>
## Information and open systems {#section-19-information-and-open-systems}

- M. A. Nielsen and I. L. Chuang, *Quantum Computation and Quantum Information* (10th anniversary ed., 2010). Cambridge University Press. https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511976667. Channels, entanglement, protocols, and computational models; Section 2.5, page 109, supports the Schmidt-decomposition formulation.
- J. Watrous, *The Theory of Quantum Information* (2018). Cambridge University Press. https://doi.org/10.1017/9781316848142. Finite-dimensional channel and information theory with theorem-level precision; Chapter 6 and the index discussion at page 375 support the Bell/CHSH route.
- H.-P. Breuer and F. Petruccione, *The Theory of Open Quantum Systems* (2002 print ed.). Oxford University Press. https://doi.org/10.1093/acprof:oso/9780199213900.001.0001. Master equations, approximations, and non-Markovian directions; the DOI identifies a later online/reprint record of the 2002 work.
- Á. Rivas and S. F. Huelga, *Open Quantum Systems: An Introduction* (2012). Springer. https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-23354-8. Chapter 4, pages 33--48, supports the modern GKSL formulation.

<a id="section-24-approximation-and-computation"></a>
## Approximation and computation {#section-26-approximation-and-computation}

- T. Kato, *Perturbation Theory for Linear Operators* (2nd ed.; Springer Classics reprint, 1995). Springer. https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-66282-9. Analytic and operator perturbation theory beyond formal series.
- M. Born and V. Fock (1928), "Beweis des Adiabatensatzes," *Zeitschrift für Physik* 51, 165--180. https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01343193. Historical adiabatic-theorem source; modern treatments control present hypotheses and error bounds.
- J. C. Light and T. Carrington Jr. (2000), "Discrete-Variable Representations and their Utilization," *Advances in Chemical Physics* 114, 263--310. https://doi.org/10.1002/9780470141731.ch4. A source for grid and basis methods; every implementation still needs its own convergence audit.

<a id="section-30-historical-attribution-ledger"></a>
## Historical attribution ledger {#section-32-historical-attribution-ledger}

Names such as Born rule, Lüders update, Robertson and Schrödinger uncertainty, Wigner theorem, Schmidt decomposition, GKSL generator, Bell/CHSH inequality, Landau–Zener transition, Rayleigh–Ritz method, and WKB approximation denote families of results developed across different conventions and sources. Griffiths and Schroeter control the manuscript's Born/projection, Rayleigh--Ritz, and WKB formulations; Sakurai and Napolitano control its modern Wigner-symmetry route; Hall supports its modern uncertainty statement; Nielsen and Chuang support Schmidt decomposition; Rivas and Huelga support the GKSL formulation; and Sakurai, Watrous, and the primary Bell/CHSH papers support the stated Bell boundary. The manuscript defines its own basis and probability conventions for the Landau--Zener model; the modern review below is used to compare conventions, while Zener's paper is provenance.

- H. P. Robertson (1929), "The Uncertainty Principle," *Physical Review* 34, 163--164. https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.34.163.
- H. Maassen and J. B. M. Uffink (1988), "Generalized Entropic Uncertainty Relations," *Physical Review Letters* 60, 1103--1106. https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.60.1103. The general finite-basis Shannon bound in Chapter 6 is a cited input; its Pauli X/Z special case is proved there directly.
- G. Lüders (1950), "Über die Zustandsänderung durch den Meßprozeß," *Annalen der Physik* 443, 322--328. https://doi.org/10.1002/andp.19504430510.
- J. S. Bell (1964), "On the Einstein Podolsky Rosen Paradox," *Physics Physique Fizika* 1, 195--200. https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysicsPhysiqueFizika.1.195.
- J. F. Clauser, M. A. Horne, A. Shimony, and R. A. Holt (1969), "Proposed Experiment to Test Local Hidden-Variable Theories," *Physical Review Letters* 23, 880--884. https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.23.880. The journal record also identifies the 1970 erratum.
- V. Gorini, A. Kossakowski, and E. C. G. Sudarshan (1976), "Completely Positive Dynamical Semigroups of N-Level Systems," *Journal of Mathematical Physics* 17, 821--825. https://doi.org/10.1063/1.522979.
- G. Lindblad (1976), "On the Generators of Quantum Dynamical Semigroups," *Communications in Mathematical Physics* 48, 119--130. https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01608499.
- S. N. Shevchenko, S. Ashhab, and F. Nori (2010), "Landau--Zener--Stückelberg Interferometry," *Physics Reports* 492(1), 1--30. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.physrep.2010.03.002. A modern two-level-system review used to compare adiabatic/diabatic and transition-probability conventions.
- C. Zener (1932), "Non-Adiabatic Crossing of Energy Levels," *Proceedings of the Royal Society A* 137, 696--702. https://doi.org/10.1098/rspa.1932.0165. Historical provenance, not the sole source for the manuscript's modern convention.

<a id="section-63-citation-and-evidence-policy"></a>
## Citation and evidence policy {#section-65-citation-and-evidence-policy}

Primary theorem sources or authoritative modern references should support technical statements. Historical priority claims require specialist verification. Empirical claims should cite experiment papers and report what was actually tested. Software or numerical results require versioned source, inputs, tolerances, and residuals. No reference can convert an approximation into an exact statement outside its hypotheses.
