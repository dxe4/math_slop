
# Preface and Study Practice

This L2 book follows one question through increasingly rich examples: what does a rule for change determine? A rate law alone usually permits many trajectories. Initial data select a trajectory when an existence and uniqueness theorem applies. Boundary data can instead select no solution, one solution or a family. An exact formula is useful only on an interval where its operations and coefficients are defined.

Begin each chapter by naming the independent variable, state variables and admissible domain. In a physical model attach units to every coefficient. Before solving, ask whether the initial point lies inside the rate law's domain and whether the available regularity theorem applies. After solving, substitute the result into both the equation and the data. A substitution confirms a candidate; it does not by itself prove that no other solution exists.

The book's first five chapters develop scalar equations. The next seven connect linear structure with forcing, oscillation, convergent series, transforms, numerical approximations and boundary spectra. The final three use matrices and planar geometry, ending with a short nonlinear preview. The [prerequisite diagnostic](reader-guide-and-prerequisite-diagnostic.md) gives exact preparation links. The [notation and theorem map](notation-and-theorem-map.md) locates the principal assumptions.

A good study session has four pieces. First reconstruct one definition without looking. Next work through an example, pausing before each decision about division, branch or endpoint. Then attempt an exercise before opening a hint. Finally compare the completed solution with its hypotheses and record one changed case where the same method fails. If separation divides by an equilibrium, restore that equilibrium to the final answer. If a Laplace formula is used, write its convergence half-line beside it.

The numerical chapter has a different object of study: a sequence of approximate states and the discrepancy from a specified exact IVP. A visually plausible computed curve is not an existence proof. Small local defects become useful only after a stability estimate controls their accumulation. Distinguish that mathematical estimate from roundoff, finite grids and limitations of a particular implementation.

Written exercises have two hints and full solutions. Opening a solution is a learning choice; a study checkmark records that choice or a personal intention, not a graded result. Preserve your own attempt and explain the step it was missing. For the proof exercises, an instructor or capable peer can assess whether the argument establishes every stated quantifier.

The nonlinear ending is deliberately short. It proves selected attraction and energy arguments and explains why a zero eigenvalue does not decide nonlinear stability. Further planar dynamics belongs to Dynamical Systems: A First Course; weak PDE theory and general operator spectra require their own preparation. Completing this book supplies a first ODE course, not those later subjects by implication.
