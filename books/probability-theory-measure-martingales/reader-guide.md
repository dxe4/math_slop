
# Reader Guide

This L4 course develops probability using measure and integration. Required preparation is [Probability: Models, Bounds, and Proofs](#/books/probability-models-proofs) and [Measure and Integration](#/books/measure-and-integration). You should be comfortable with measurable functions, product measures, monotone and dominated convergence, Radon-Nikodym, and elementary Gaussian vectors. The early chapters revisit these tools in their probability setting; they do not replace an introductory measure course.

The first four chapters set up event classes, independent coordinates, integrals and convergence. Chapters 5–9 establish independent-series criteria, almost-sure laws, characteristic-function limits, compactness of probability laws and triangular-array normal limits. Chapters 10–14 develop conditional expectation, martingale convergence, coordinate concentration, stationary averages and general-state Markov kernels. The final two chapters build Brownian paths and introduce exponential rates for rare events.

Read chapters in their native order for the full course. For a limits route read 1–9, then 16. For a conditional-process route read 1–4 and 10–15, returning to the extension proof in Chapter 2 and compactness proof in Chapter 8 wherever they are used. The stationary theorem in Chapter 13 uses the Hilbert orthogonal-projection theorem, the one explicitly named functional-analytic input there. The standard Borel encoding theorem used in Chapter 2 and Riesz representation used in Chapter 8 are separately identified inputs.

Every chapter contains at least three worked examples and ten exercises with two staged hints and a full solution. Attempt a proof before opening its help. Write the sigma-algebra, measure and integrability assumptions explicitly. In a solution comparison, a missing conditional test, null-set qualification, or order of quantifiers can matter more than an algebraic discrepancy.

The major proof milestones are the three-series theorem in Chapter 5, Etemadi's strong law in Chapter 6, Levy continuity in Chapter 7, Polish Prokhorov compactness in Chapter 8, both directions of Lindeberg-Feller under their stated assumptions in Chapter 9, upcrossing and UI martingale convergence in Chapter 11, and the L1 ergodic theorem in Chapter 13. Later results have deliberately scoped hypotheses: global Doeblin convergence does not stand for general Harris theory, bounded Cramer theory does not stand for every unbounded distribution, and continuous Brownian paths do not by themselves supply stochastic integration.

The Brownian construction leads to the retained [Ito orientation](../stochastic-processes-paths/stochastic-integration-orientation.md) and [SDE introduction](../stochastic-processes-paths/sdes-diffusions-generators.md). For statistical asymptotics, proceed to [Advanced Statistical Inference](#/books/advanced-statistical-inference-reference). These are continuations with their own preparation; study completion is not graded mathematical evidence.
