## Glossary {#section-5-glossary}

**Analytic continuation.** A method of extending the domain of an analytic function beyond the disk of convergence of its power series by using overlapping disks and the identity theorem. Ch. 6.

**Bijective.** A function that is both injective and surjective; equivalent to having a two-sided inverse. Ch. 1.

**Cauchy product.** The product of two power series expressed as a single power series whose coefficients are convolution sums. Ch. 5.

**Characteristic equation.** The polynomial equation obtained by substituting a trial exponential solution into a linear homogeneous differential or difference equation with constant coefficients. Chs. 8, 9.

**Codomain.** The target set $B$ in a function $f:A\to B$; distinguished from the image. Ch. 1.

**Complex exponential.** The extension of the real exponential to $\mathbb{C}$ defined by $e^{z}=e^{x}(\cos y+i\sin y)$ for $z=x+iy$. Ch. 4.

**Dirichlet kernel.** The kernel $D_{N}(x)=\sum_{n=-N}^{N}e^{inx}$ used in the study of Fourier series convergence. Ch. 13.

**Domain.** The set $A$ of inputs for which a function $f:A\to B$ is defined. Ch. 1.

**Domain obligation.** A restriction on the input set required for a formula or operation to be well-defined, such as positivity for logarithms. Chs. 1, 2.

**Equilibrium point.** A constant solution of a differential or difference equation; studied for stability in dynamical systems. Ch. 10.

**Euler's formula.** The identity $e^{i\theta}=\cos\theta+i\sin\theta$ for real $\theta$. Ch. 4.

**Fourier coefficient.** The coefficients $a_{n}$ and $b_{n}$ (or complex $c_{n}$) in the expansion of a periodic function as a trigonometric series. Ch. 13.

**Fourier series.** An expansion of a periodic function as a sum of sines and cosines with coefficients determined by orthogonality integrals. Ch. 13.

**Fourier transform.** The integral transform $\hat{f}(\xi)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(x)e^{-2\pi ix\xi}\,dx$ mapping a function to its frequency representation. Ch. 13.

**Fundamental theorem of algebra.** Every nonconstant polynomial in $\mathbb{C}[z]$ has at least one root in $\mathbb{C}$. Ch. 4.

**Gamma function.** The extension of factorial to complex arguments, $\Gamma(s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{s-1}e^{-t}\,dt$ for $\operatorname{Re}(s)>0$. Ch. 7.

**Geometric series.** The series $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}z^{n}=\frac{1}{1-z}$ for $|z|<1$. Ch. 5.

**Holomorphic.** Complex differentiable at every point of an open set; equivalent to analytic for complex functions. Chs. 5, 6.

**Image.** The set $f(A)=\{f(a):a\in A\}$ of actual outputs of $f:A\to B$. Ch. 1.

**Injective.** A function for which distinct inputs produce distinct outputs; $f(x_{1})=f(x_{2})$ implies $x_{1}=x_{2}$. Ch. 1.

**Integrating factor.** A function by which a first-order linear ODE is multiplied to make the left side an exact derivative. Ch. 9.

**Inverse function.** A function $g:B\to A$ such that $g\circ f=\operatorname{id}_{A}$ and $f\circ g=\operatorname{id}_{B}$; exists if and only if $f$ is bijective. Ch. 1.

**Left inverse.** A function $g:B\to A$ with $g\circ f=\operatorname{id}_{A}$. Its existence implies that $f$ is injective. Conversely, an injective $f:A\to B$ has a left inverse when $A$ is nonempty; the empty-domain edge case also requires $B$ to be empty. Ch. 1.

**Linear difference equation.** A recurrence relation in which each term is a linear combination of earlier terms plus a known sequence. Ch. 8.

**Linear ODE.** A differential equation in which the unknown function and its derivatives appear linearly. Ch. 9.

**Modulus.** For $z=a+bi\in\mathbb{C}$, the nonnegative real number $|z|=\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}}$. Ch. 4.

**Natural logarithm.** The inverse of the real exponential, $\ln:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}$. Ch. 2.

**Parseval's identity.** The equality $\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|f(x)|^{2}\,dx=\frac{a_{0}^{2}}{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(a_{n}^{2}+b_{n}^{2})$ relating the $L^{2}$ norm of a function to the sum of squared Fourier coefficients. Ch. 13.

**Periodicity.** A function $f$ is periodic with period $T$ if $f(x+T)=f(x)$ for all $x$. The complex exponential has period $2\pi i$. Chs. 3, 4.

**Phase portrait.** A graphical representation of the trajectories of a dynamical system in phase space. Ch. 10.

**Power series.** A series of the form $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}(z-c)^{n}$; converges absolutely inside its radius of convergence $R$. Ch. 5.

**Radius of convergence.** The unique $R\in[0,\infty]$ such that a power series converges absolutely for $|z-c|<R$ and diverges for $|z-c|>R$. Ch. 5.

**Resonance.** The phenomenon in which a forcing term matches a homogeneous solution, requiring a modified guess for the particular solution. Chs. 8, 9.

**Riemann zeta function.** $\zeta(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{-s}$ for $\operatorname{Re}(s)>1$; analytically continuable to $\mathbb{C}\setminus\{1\}$. Ch. 7.

**Right inverse.** A function $g:B\to A$ with $f\circ g=\operatorname{id}_{B}$; its existence implies that $f$ is surjective. Conversely, choosing a preimage for every $b\in B$ gives a right inverse to a surjection; for arbitrary families of nonempty fibers, this converse uses the axiom of choice. Ch. 1.

**Surjective.** A function $f:A\to B$ for which every $b\in B$ is hit by some $a\in A$; equivalently $f(A)=B$. Ch. 1.

**Term-by-term differentiation.** The operation of differentiating a convergent power series inside its radius of convergence to obtain the derivative series. Ch. 5.

**Typed assignment.** The view of a function as a triple $(A,B,\text{rule})$ rather than merely a formula. Ch. 1.

**Uniform convergence.** Convergence of a sequence of functions such that the rate of convergence is independent of the point in the domain. Ch. 12.

**Weierstrass function.** A continuous everywhere, differentiable nowhere function constructed as a series of rapidly oscillating terms. Ch. 14.

## Index of theorems and principles {#section-87-index-of-theorems-and-principles}

**Bijectivity and invertibility.** A function has a two-sided inverse if and only if it is bijective. Ch. 1.

**Cantor's theorem.** The real numbers are uncountable. Ch. 14.

**Dirichlet's theorem on Fourier series.** For piecewise smooth functions, the Fourier series converges to the average of left and right limits. Ch. 13.

**Existence and uniqueness for first-order linear ODEs.** The integrating-factor method produces the unique solution satisfying a given initial condition. Ch. 9.

**Fundamental theorem of algebra.** Every nonconstant complex polynomial has a root. Ch. 4.

**Pigeonhole principle.** If $n$ objects are placed into $m$ containers with $n>m$, at least one container contains more than one object. Ch. 14.

**Radius of convergence theorem.** Every power series has a unique radius of convergence with the stated convergence properties. Ch. 5.

**Term-by-term differentiation of power series.** A convergent power series defines a holomorphic function whose derivative is the term-by-term differentiated series. Ch. 5.
