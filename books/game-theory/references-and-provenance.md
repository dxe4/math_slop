
# References and Provenance {#section-1-references-and-provenance}

This book contains original explanations, proofs, worked examples and exercise solutions. Its original prose is offered under CC BY-SA 4.0. Linking to a third-party paper or book does not relicense that work. The references below identify primary sources and theorem provenance, not copied passages or a claim that an external author reviewed this manuscript.

<a id="section-3-general-foundations"></a>
## General foundations {#section-5-general-foundations}

Martin J. Osborne and Ariel Rubinstein, [*A Course in Game Theory*](https://www.economics.utoronto.ca/osborne/cgt/), MIT Press, 1994, provides an author-hosted primary textbook route through strategic and extensive games, mixed strategies, repeated games and information. [Rubinstein's books page](https://arielrubinstein.tau.ac.il/books.html) supplies the author's publication context. This manuscript develops its own sequence, calculations and supported exercises rather than reproducing that text.

John F. Nash, [“Equilibrium Points in N-Person Games”](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.36.1.48), *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences* 36 (1950), 48–49, is the primary finite-equilibrium existence reference. The chapter here supplies a full finite-game proof through a continuous gain map, after proving the fixed-point result it uses.

Shizuo Kakutani, [“A Generalization of Brouwer's Fixed Point Theorem”](https://www.math.ucdavis.edu/~deloera/TEACHING/READINGSEMINAR/PAPERS/kakutanioriginal.pdf), *Duke Mathematical Journal* 8 (1941), 457–459, is the primary source for the alternative set-valued theorem stated in Chapter 7. That alternative theorem is cited, not proved in this book. It is not needed as an unproved step in the book's Sperner–Brouwer–Nash route.

<a id="section-11-time-and-information"></a>
## Time and information {#section-13-time-and-information}

Drew Fudenberg and Eric Maskin, [“The Folk Theorem in Repeated Games with Discounting or with Incomplete Information”](https://doi.org/10.2307/1911307), *Econometrica* 54 (1986), 533–554, is a primary source for stronger folk-theorem results. Chapter 10 proves narrower, explicitly specified Nash-reversion and public-lottery constructions. It does not present those constructions as a proof of every feasible individually rational payoff result.

John C. Harsanyi, [“Games with Incomplete Information Played by ‘Bayesian’ Players, I. The Basic Model”](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.14.3.159), *Management Science* 14 (1967), 159–182, provides primary model provenance for private types and Bayesian players. The finite-type existence proof here explicitly reduces to the finite Nash theorem and permits correlated types under the stated common prior.

<a id="section-17-coalitions-and-institutions"></a>
## Coalitions and institutions {#section-19-coalitions-and-institutions}

Lloyd S. Shapley, [“A Value for n-Person Games”](https://doi.org/10.1515/9781400881970-018), in *Contributions to the Theory of Games II*, supplies the primary value reference. Chapter 12 proves the permutation formula, its axioms and uniqueness through the unanimity-game decomposition. Shapley's [“On Balanced Sets and Cores”](https://doi.org/10.1002/nav.3800140404), *Naval Research Logistics Quarterly* 14 (1967), 453–460, is a primary balancedness source. The finite balancedness proof here uses the linear-programming duality developed earlier in the book.

William Vickrey, [“Counterspeculation, Auctions, and Competitive Sealed Tenders”](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1961.tb02789.x), *The Journal of Finance* 16 (1961), 8–37, provides primary auction context. Theodore Groves, [“Incentives in Teams”](https://ranger.uta.edu/~weems/NOTES6319/PAPERSTWO/groves.pdf), *Econometrica* 41 (1973), provides the primary efficient-transfer construction. Chapter 13 proves the stated truthfulness results and a particular uniform first-price equilibrium. Its revenue comparison is that explicit model calculation, not an unproved general revenue-equivalence theorem.

John Geanakoplos, [*Three Brief Proofs of Arrow's Impossibility Theorem*](https://cowles.yale.edu/node/145476), Cowles Foundation, is a primary proof reference for Chapter 14. This manuscript gives its own complete finite strict-order decisive-coalition proof. It derives the cross-alternative transfer property rather than adding anonymity or neutrality to the theorem's hypotheses.

John Maynard Smith, [*Evolution and the Theory of Games*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/evolution-and-the-theory-of-games/A3BDF54AF5C6297E308AB15BBEF45E48), Cambridge University Press, is the primary ESS book source. The [MIT Picard–Lindelöf lecture](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-100b-real-analysis-spring-2025/resources/mit18_100b_s25_lec23_pdf/) identifies the imported ordinary-differential-equation existence input. Chapter 15 proves its static ESS criterion, simplex preservation and stated convergence arguments with their solution assumptions explicit.

## Earlier local treatments remain available {#section-29-earlier-local-treatments-remain-available}

The earlier [Games, Equilibria, and Response](../applied-modeling-proof-reference/games-equilibria-response.md) chapter introduces strategic response inside applied modelling. The articles in the Unhurried collection whose original source names begin game-social-choice cover payoff matrices, dominance, mixed strategies, Nash equilibrium, repeated play and Arrow's theorem. Those articles and their identities remain in their original module. They are acknowledged as local seeds for scope and pedagogical continuity; no source, worked solution or learner record is retired by this book.

The new chapters develop an independent continuous course from the model through existence, time, information, allocation and population questions. Familiar small games serve a different proof sequence here. Earlier references continue to belong to their earlier sources. The book's source completion record does not overwrite historical release or PDF evidence for any other book.

<a id="section-33-status-and-attribution-conventions"></a>
## Status and attribution conventions {#section-35-status-and-attribution-conventions}

A theorem proved in a chapter has its argument there, including the hypotheses on which the argument relies. An imported theorem is identified as such, and its use is limited to the stated model. A further-reading reference signals a continuation, not a proof supplied by this manuscript. Chapter-level references remain the closest guide when a particular claim is in question.


<a id="section-39-summary"></a>
## Summary {#section-41-summary}

Use primary references to trace results and deepen the course. Read the local proof before deciding what has been established here. Keep original new writing, imported theorem inputs, further results and retained earlier readings distinct in both attribution and mathematical scope.
