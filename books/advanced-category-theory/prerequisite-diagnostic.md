
# Prerequisite Diagnostic

Before Chapter 1, explain the Yoneda bijection and its naturality, construct a product by a universal property, and write both unit and counit of an adjunction with the triangle identities. These are developed in [Introduction to Category Theory](#/books/introduction-to-category-theory). For tensor examples, derive the tensor–Hom adjunction and a projective resolution from [Homological Algebra](#/books/morphisms-modules-categories), chapters 1–11.

For Chapter 6, distinguish a presheaf restriction map from a sheaf gluing assertion. Ordinary topology supplies open covers; no prior sheaf theory is assumed. For Chapters 9–11, compute a chain-complex homotopy and a quasi-isomorphism, and say why the latter need not have a strict inverse. The corresponding homological chapters supply these calculations.

The finite-set, module and poset examples are the core route. Specialists may bring topology or simplicial sets to the higher chapters, but the manuscript labels every theorem that requires imported model-category or infinity-category machinery. Missing a specialist background is a reason to follow the finite and chain-complex examples, not a reason to accept an unproved general statement as elementary.
