
# Notation, Variance and Size

Categories called small have sets of objects and arrows; locally small categories have hom-sets. Functor categories, coends and presheaf categories use a fixed small indexing category unless a larger universe is explicitly chosen. A category may be large without its collection of all objects being a set. These conventions prevent a coend over all sets from being written as if it were an ordinary small colimit.

Composition is \(gf=g\circ f\), so \(f:A\to B\) acts first. The opposite category reverses arrows; \(C^{\mathrm{op}}\times C\) is the standard variance of an end integrand. A monoidal product is written \(\otimes\), with unit \(I\). Coherence isomorphisms are displayed when a proof needs them; omission elsewhere follows a specified coherence theorem, not literal equality of differently bracketed objects.

In homotopical chapters, \(W\) denotes a declared class of weak equivalences. A homotopy category formally inverts \(W\) but may forget mapping-space information. The symbol \(\infty\)-category refers to a specified model, usually quasicategories, and any comparison between models is a named imported theorem with hypotheses.
