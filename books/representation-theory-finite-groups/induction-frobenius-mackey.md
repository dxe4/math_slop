
# Induction, Frobenius Reciprocity and Mackey

Restriction turns a G-representation into a representation of a subgroup by using fewer action operators. Induction goes in the other direction: it builds a G-representation from an H-representation. It does not merely extend each matrix by guessing how the remaining group elements should act. Instead it transports a copy of the H-space to every coset and lets G move those copies.

Read [the group algebra](group-algebra-wedderburn.md) and [character inner products](characters-and-orthogonality.md). G is finite, H,K are subgroups, and all spaces are finite-dimensional. The module constructions and adjunction work over any field k. Character and irreducibility calculations below use C.

## 1. Restriction and the balanced construction

For a G-representation V, Res_H^G V means the same vector space with only the H-action retained. An irreducible G-representation can become reducible on restriction: the standard S_3 plane restricts to two eigenlines for its cyclic order-three subgroup.

For an H-representation W define
$$\operatorname{Ind}_H^G W=k[G]\otimes_{k[H]}W.$$
Here k[G] has its usual left G-action and a right H-action by multiplication. To make the tensor notation explicit, begin with the k-vector space generated by symbols a⊗w and impose bilinearity in a and w together with
$$(ah)\otimes w=a\otimes(hw)\qquad(h\in H).$$
The same relation then holds for every element of k[H] by linearity. G acts on the left by g(a⊗w)=(ga)⊗w. This respects every relation and obeys the group law.

Choose representatives T for the left cosets G/H. Every group element has a unique form th with t∈T and h∈H, so k[G]=⊕_(t∈T)t k[H] as right k[H]-modules. Therefore
$$\operatorname{Ind}_H^G W\cong\bigoplus_{t\in T}t\otimes W,
\qquad
\dim\operatorname{Ind}_H^G W=[G:H]\dim W.$$
One can verify this direct-sum model without an assumed tensor theorem: send th⊗w to the vector hw in the t-labelled copy of W. The unique coset factorisation makes the map well defined, balanced and inverse to the sum of the maps w↦t⊗w.

If gt=t'h, the action sends t⊗w to t'⊗hw. This gives a concrete matrix recipe: permute coset blocks and apply the appropriate H-matrix inside the destination block. Changing representatives changes the coordinate model by an equivariant isomorphism, not the induced representation itself.

## 2. Frobenius reciprocity as an explicit bijection

**Theorem 7.1 (Frobenius reciprocity).** Over any field,
$$\operatorname{Hom}_G(\operatorname{Ind}_H^G W,V)
\cong\operatorname{Hom}_H(W,\operatorname{Res}_H^G V).$$
The correspondence sends F to f(w)=F(e⊗w); its inverse sends f to F(g⊗w)=g f(w).

*Proof.* If F is G-equivariant, then for h∈H,
f(hw)=F(e⊗hw)=F(h⊗w)=hF(e⊗w)=hf(w).
Thus f is H-equivariant. Conversely, for such an f, define the displayed F linearly. It respects balancing because
F(gh⊗w)=ghf(w)=gf(hw)=F(g⊗hw).
It is G-equivariant since F(xg⊗w)=xgf(w)=xF(g⊗w). Evaluating the reconstructed map at e⊗w returns f, and G-equivariance forces every F to be determined by those values. Thus the constructions are inverse. ∎

This is an adjunction: induction is left adjoint to restriction. The proof gives an actual natural correspondence of maps, stronger than a numerical equality. It also shows why induction is the appropriate construction for extending maps rather than arbitrarily extending an action on W.

Over C, taking dimensions and applying the Hom formula yields
$$\langle\chi_{\operatorname{Ind}W},\chi_V\rangle_G
=\langle\chi_W,\chi_{\operatorname{Res}V}\rangle_H.$$
Our inner product is linear in its first argument. The displayed orientation follows because dim Hom_G(Ind W,V)=⟨χ_V,χ_IndW⟩; Hermitian symmetry conjugates that real integer to the equivalent displayed form. This convention matters for general complex class functions, even though multiplicities themselves are real integers.

## 3. The induced-character formula

**Theorem 7.2.** For a complex H-representation W,
$$\chi_{\operatorname{Ind}_H^G W}(g)
=\sum_{\substack{t\in T\\t^{-1}gt\in H}}\chi_W(t^{-1}gt)
=\frac1{|H|}
\sum_{\substack{x\in G\\x^{-1}gx\in H}}\chi_W(x^{-1}gx).$$

*Proof.* In the coset direct-sum model, g sends the t-block to the gtH-block. Only blocks that return to themselves contribute to the trace. The condition is gtH=tH, or t^(−1)gt∈H; the returning block operator is the W-action of that element. Summing these block traces gives the first formula.

For x=th, the membership condition is unchanged and x^(−1)gx=h^(−1)(t^(−1)gt)h. The character value is constant on H-conjugacy classes, so all |H| elements of tH give the same term. Dividing the all-element sum by |H| gives the second formula. ∎

For W trivial, this reduces to the number of left H-cosets fixed by g. Thus Ind_H^G1 is exactly the permutation representation C[G/H]. The formula also shows that its induced character vanishes on any G-class that never meets H.

Induction is additive on direct sums. It is transitive: for L≤H≤G,
$$\operatorname{Ind}_H^G\operatorname{Ind}_L^H W
\cong\operatorname{Ind}_L^G W.$$
An explicit isomorphism sends g⊗(h⊗w) to gh⊗w. It respects balancing at both stages. The inverse sends g⊗w to g⊗(e⊗w); the L-balancing relation follows through the intermediate H tensor factor. Their composites fix every generating tensor, proving the claim.

## Tensor compatibility: a transport identity

There is a useful compatibility with tensor products. For a G-representation V and an H-representation W, give V⊗W the diagonal H-action h(v⊗w)=hv⊗hw. Then, over any field,
$$\operatorname{Ind}_H^G(\operatorname{Res}_H^G V\otimes W)
\cong V\otimes\operatorname{Ind}_H^G W.$$
Define the forward map on generators by
$$g\otimes(v\otimes w)\longmapsto gv\otimes(g\otimes w).$$
The balancing relation is respected: replacing gh⊗(v⊗w) by g⊗(hv⊗hw) produces the same output because gh⊗w=g⊗hw in the induced factor. The formula is G-equivariant for the diagonal target action.

Its inverse sends v⊗(g⊗w) to g⊗(g^(−1)v⊗w). To check balancing in that formula, replacing gh⊗w by g⊗hw gives
gh⊗(h^(−1)g^(−1)v⊗w)
=g⊗(g^(−1)v⊗hw),
which is exactly the other representative's image. Both composites fix every generating tensor. This proves the isomorphism rather than merely comparing dimensions.

Over C it implies a character identity: multiplying an induced character by χ_V is the same as inducing the product of the restricted χ_V with χ_W. For instance twisting an induced representation by a linear G-character can be performed on W before induction. This explains the relation between the two transposition-subgroup inductions in Section 7: the nontrivial C_2-line is the restriction of S_3 sign.

## 4. Mackey's double-coset decomposition

Restriction after induction has a combinatorial partition by double cosets K\G/H. Choose one representative x for each double coset KxH. Put
$$L_x=K\cap xHx^{-1}.$$
On the vector space W define the L_x-action l·_xw=(x^(−1)lx)w, which makes sense because x^(−1)L_xx≤H. Denote this L_x-representation by W_x.

**Theorem 7.3 (Mackey decomposition).** Over any field,
$$\operatorname{Res}_K^G\operatorname{Ind}_H^G W
\cong\bigoplus_{x\in K\backslash G/H}
\operatorname{Ind}_{L_x}^K W_x.$$

*Proof.* The left H-cosets inside each double coset KxH form a K-orbit in G/H. Hence the sum of their W-blocks is K-invariant, and these orbit sums give a direct-sum decomposition of the restricted induced representation.

For the x-th summand define
$$F_x:k[K]\otimes_{k[L_x]}W_x\longrightarrow
\text{the blocks supported on }KxH,\qquad
k\otimes w\longmapsto kx\otimes w.$$
For l∈L_x,
klx⊗w=kx(x^(−1)lx)⊗w=kx⊗(x^(−1)lx)w.
This is exactly the required balancing relation, so F_x is well defined. It is K-equivariant by left multiplication.

Representatives k for K/L_x correspond bijectively to left H-cosets kxH within KxH: kxH=k'xH exactly when k'^(−1)k∈K∩xHx^(−1)=L_x. On the corresponding direct-sum copies of W, F_x is the identity on the W-coordinate. It is therefore a linear isomorphism. Summing these isomorphisms proves the theorem. ∎

The intersection subgroup and conjugated action are both essential. Replacing L_x by H∩K, independent of x, is generally wrong. Also, the double-coset representative is not merely a class label: it specifies which conjugation transports the H-action to L_x.

## 5. Mackey's irreducibility criterion

Now work over C and let W be irreducible over H. Apply the preceding theorem with K=H. Frobenius reciprocity and its conjugate-symmetric inner-product form give
$$
\left\langle\chi_{\operatorname{Ind}W},
\chi_{\operatorname{Ind}W}\right\rangle_G
=\sum_{x\in H\backslash G/H}
\left\langle
\chi_{\operatorname{Res}_{L_x}^H W},
\chi_{W_x}
\right\rangle_{L_x}.
$$
To justify the second reciprocity use, first write the right side of the first adjunction as ⟨χ_W,χ_ResIndW⟩_H. Substitute the Mackey direct sum. For each induced term, Hermitian symmetry and ordinary reciprocity give ⟨χ_W,Ind χ_(W_x)⟩=⟨Res χ_W,χ_(W_x)⟩. Each term is a nonnegative integer Hom dimension.

The identity double coset has L_e=H and contributes ⟨χ_W,χ_W⟩=1. Thus:

**Corollary 7.4.** Ind_H^G W is irreducible exactly when, for every nonidentity double coset x,
$$\operatorname{Hom}_{L_x}(W_x,\operatorname{Res}_{L_x}^H W)=0.$$

This is a criterion rather than a universal irreducibility assertion. Even induction of a one-dimensional irreducible often splits. In the normal-subgroup case H◁G, every L_x=H, so the criterion says that none of the nontrivial conjugate representations W_x may be isomorphic to W. It turns a large induced action into smaller Hom comparisons.

## 6. Worked example: inducing from C_3 to S_3

Let H=⟨r=(123)⟩, choose s=(12), and take the H-line r↦ω. The two left coset representatives are e,s. On the basis e⊗1,s⊗1, r acts by diag(ω,ω²), because rs=sr^(−1), while s swaps the two basis vectors. Thus
$$\rho(r)=\begin{pmatrix}\omega&0\\0&\omega^2\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\rho(s)=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.$$
Their traces on the S_3 classes are (2,0,−1), the standard irreducible character.

The normal-subgroup Mackey criterion gives another proof. Conjugation by s replaces r↦ω with r↦ω², a different one-dimensional representation. There is no nonzero H-map between them. The identity coset contributes one to the norm and the other contributes zero, so induction is irreducible.

If we induce the trivial H-line instead, its conjugate is unchanged. The resulting character is (2,0,2), which is trivial plus sign. Thus two different H-lines of the same degree can induce representations of very different structure.

## 7. Worked example: inducing from a transposition subgroup

Let H=⟨(12)⟩ in S_3. The coset action on S_3/H has degree three and is isomorphic to the coordinate action on three points. Its character is (3,1,0), hence
$$\operatorname{Ind}_H^{S_3}1\cong1\oplus W.$$

Let ε_H be the nontrivial H-character. Frobenius reciprocity computes its multiplicities without building the induced matrices. Restriction of the trivial S_3 character to H is trivial, so contributes zero; restriction of sign is ε_H, contributing one. The standard character restricts to (2,0) on e,(12), which is 1_H+ε_H, contributing one. Therefore
$$\operatorname{Ind}_H^{S_3}\varepsilon_H\cong\varepsilon\oplus W.$$

For an explicit Mackey check, H\S_3/H has representatives e and r=(123). The second intersection H∩rHr^(−1) is trivial. Thus
$$\operatorname{Res}_H^{S_3}\operatorname{Ind}_H^{S_3}1
\cong1_H\oplus\operatorname{Ind}_{\{e\}}^H1
\cong 2\cdot1_H\oplus\varepsilon_H.$$
Its inner product with 1_H is two, agreeing with the induced character norm 1²+1²=2. This example shows the intersection term that is invisible if one treats all double cosets like the normal-subgroup case.

## 8. Worked example: a dihedral irreducible from a cyclic line

Let D_4=⟨r,s:r⁴=s²=e,srs=r^(−1)⟩ and H=⟨r⟩≅C_4. Take W to be the line with r acting as i. H is normal of index two, and its conjugate under s has generator scalar −i, so the two lines are inequivalent.

The induced representation has matrices diag(i,−i) for r and [[0,1],[1,0]] for s. They satisfy the dihedral relations and have character (2,−2,0,0,0) on the five classes. Mackey's criterion proves irreducibility directly from i≠−i, and the character norm gives the same conclusion.

Inducing the line r↦−1 instead produces two linear characters of D_4, distinguished by the choice s↦±1. In that case conjugation leaves the H-line fixed, so the norm is two rather than one. The extendibility of the H-line is reflected in the reducibility of its induction.

Inducing the regular representation of H gives the regular representation of G. In the tensor model, multiplication sends C[G] tensored over C[H] with C[H] to C[G]. It is well-defined by the balancing relation and is G-equivariant. Its inverse sends g to g tensored with one; the same relation shows every tensor returns to itself under the two composites. Thus the isomorphism preserves the entire action, beyond the numerical dimension identity. It provides a useful exact reference case for reciprocity and induction conventions.

## 9. Exercises

### REP-07-01 — Dimension of induction

Prove dim Ind_H^G W=[G:H]dim W directly from the balanced relations.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

Choose left coset representatives and write every g as th.

</details>
<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

Construct maps in both directions between the tensor quotient and copies of W.

</details>
<details>
<summary>Full solution</summary>

Let T represent G/H. Send t⊗w to the vector w in the t-copy of ⊕_T W. For a group element th, define its tensor image by th⊗w↦the t-copy of hw. Unique coset factorisation makes this assignment balanced and linear. Its inverse sends the t-copy vector w to t⊗w. Both composites fix generators. Thus induction is the direct sum of [G:H] copies of W as a vector space, proving the formula.

</details>

### REP-07-02 — Changing representatives

Replace a representative t by th_t with h_t∈H. Describe the coordinate change on its W-block.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

The new tensor is th_t⊗w=t⊗h_tw.

</details>
<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

Every h_t acts invertibly on W.

</details>
<details>
<summary>Full solution</summary>

A vector w in the new th_t-labelled block represents t⊗ρ_W(h_t)w in the old block. Thus the block coordinate change is ρ_W(h_t), independently on each coset block. It is invertible because the H-action is a group representation. The induced action operators in the two coordinate systems are simultaneously conjugate by this block diagonal map.

</details>

### REP-07-03 — Reciprocity maps

Given an H-map f:W→Res_H V, prove the formula F(g⊗w)=gf(w) is well defined and is the unique G-map extending f.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

Check gh⊗w and g⊗hw produce equal outputs.

</details>
<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

Every tensor g⊗w is the G-translate of e⊗w.

</details>
<details>
<summary>Full solution</summary>

The two proposed outputs are ghf(w) and gf(hw), equal by H-equivariance of f. Bilinearity is immediate, so F descends to the tensor quotient. It intertwines the G-action by F(xg⊗w)=xgf(w). Every G-map with F(e⊗w)=f(w) must satisfy F(g⊗w)=gF(e⊗w)=gf(w), proving uniqueness. This argument is valid in every characteristic.

</details>

### REP-07-04 — Induced trivial action

Construct an isomorphism Ind_H^G1≅k[G/H] over any field.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

Send g⊗1 to the basis vector of gH.

</details>
<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

The balancing relation identifies gh⊗1 with g⊗1.

</details>
<details>
<summary>Full solution</summary>

Define F(g⊗a)=a e_(gH). Since H acts trivially on k, F(gh⊗a)=a e_(ghH)=a e_(gH)=F(g⊗ha). Thus F is balanced and G-equivariant. Choosing coset representatives shows it maps a basis bijectively to the coset basis, so it is an isomorphism. In modular characteristic the isomorphism still holds even when the resulting permutation module is not completely reducible.

</details>

### REP-07-05 — Inducing from the identity subgroup

Identify Ind_(e)^G W when W is simply a k-vector space, and give its character over C.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

The tensor product is now over k alone.

</details>
<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

Choose a basis of W; G acts only on the group-algebra factor.

</details>
<details>
<summary>Full solution</summary>

Ind_(e)^G W=k[G]⊗_k W, with G acting by left multiplication on k[G] and trivially on the second factor. A basis of W decomposes it into dim W copies of the regular representation. Over C its character is (dim W)χ_reg, equal to |G|dim W at e and zero elsewhere.

</details>

### REP-07-06 — Vanishing outside the subgroup classes

Show χ_IndW(g)=0 when no conjugate of g lies in H. Apply this to S_3 induced from C_3.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

Use the induced-character sum.

</details>
<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

A transposition cannot be conjugate to an element of C_3.

</details>
<details>
<summary>Full solution</summary>

If x^(−1)gx never lies in H, the induced-character sum has no terms and equals zero. For H=C_3 in S_3, its elements have orders one or three. A transposition has order two and conjugation preserves order, so every induced character from C_3 vanishes on the transposition class. This holds for both the induced nontrivial line and the induced trivial line.

</details>

### REP-07-07 — Transitivity without dimensions

Prove the transitivity isomorphism g⊗(h⊗w)↦gh⊗w has the stated inverse.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

Check the H-balancing relation in the outer tensor and the L-balancing relation inside.

</details>
<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

For the reverse composite use g⊗(h⊗w)=gh⊗(e⊗w).

</details>
<details>
<summary>Full solution</summary>

Moving a∈H across the outer tensor gives ga⊗(h⊗w)=g⊗(ah⊗w); both images are gah⊗w. Moving l∈L inside gives h l⊗w=h⊗lw; both images are gh l⊗w=gh⊗lw. Thus the forward map is well defined. The inverse g⊗w↦g⊗(e⊗w) respects L-balancing through the H action on the inner tensor. The forward composite fixes g⊗w; the reverse sends g⊗(h⊗w) to gh⊗(e⊗w), equal to the original by outer balancing.

</details>

### REP-07-08 — Double-coset dimensions

Prove [G:H]=Σ_(x∈K\G/H)[K:K∩xHx^(−1)].

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

Partition G/H into K-orbits.

</details>
<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

The stabiliser in K of xH is K∩xHx^(−1).

</details>
<details>
<summary>Full solution</summary>

The K-orbit of xH consists of left H-cosets contained in KxH. Its stabiliser is {k∈K:kxH=xH}=K∩xHx^(−1). Orbit–stabiliser gives its size as the displayed index. Distinct double cosets give disjoint orbits exhausting G/H, so summing their sizes gives [G:H]. Multiplying by dim W checks the vector-space dimensions on both sides of Mackey's theorem.

</details>

### REP-07-09 — A normal-subgroup criterion

Let H◁G and W be an irreducible complex H-representation. Prove Ind_H^G W is irreducible exactly when every conjugate W_x for x∉H is nonisomorphic to W.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

All the intersection subgroups in Mackey's theorem equal H.

</details>
<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

Conjugating an irreducible representation preserves irreducibility.

</details>
<details>
<summary>Full solution</summary>

Normality gives H∩xHx^(−1)=H. The conjugated action W_x is irreducible because an invariant subspace for it is invariant for W after transporting the subgroup by conjugation. Schur therefore makes Hom_H(W_x,W) zero unless the two are isomorphic, and one-dimensional when they are. The identity coset already contributes one to the induced norm. Mackey's criterion says the norm is one exactly when no other coset contributes, proving the equivalence.

</details>

### REP-07-10 — Two different inductions from C_3

In S_3 compare induction of the trivial C_3-character with induction of r↦ω. Compute their inner product.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

Their class values are (2,0,2) and (2,0,−1).

</details>
<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

Use class weights 1,3,2.

</details>
<details>
<summary>Full solution</summary>

The trivial C_3-line induces 1⊕ε, while r↦ω induces W. Their inner product is [1·2·2+3·0·0+2·2·(−1)]/6=0. They share no irreducible constituent. Although both induced representations have degree two, one is reducible and the other irreducible, and equal degrees do not imply any nonzero equivariant map.

</details>

### REP-07-11 — Sign from a transposition

Use Frobenius reciprocity to decompose Ind_(C_2)^(S_3) ε_(C_2).

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

Restrict each of the three S_3 irreducible characters to e and a transposition.

</details>
<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

The standard restriction (2,0) is trivial plus sign for C_2.

</details>
<details>
<summary>Full solution</summary>

The restrictions are (1,1), (1,−1) and (2,0). Their inner products with ε_(C_2)=(1,−1) are zero, one and one, respectively. Reciprocity gives the induced decomposition ε⊕W. Its dimension is 1+2=3, agreeing with the subgroup index. The argument uses constructed irreducibles and exact inner products, rather than inferring a decomposition from dimensions alone.

</details>

### REP-07-12 — Fixed vectors in induction

Over C, prove dim(Ind_H^G W)^G=dim W^H.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

Both dimensions are multiplicities of trivial representations.

</details>
<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

Apply reciprocity with the trivial G-representation and use symmetry of Hom multiplicities in the semisimple setting.

</details>
<details>
<summary>Full solution</summary>

The fixed-space dimension of Ind W is its trivial multiplicity, equal to dim Hom_G(Ind W,1) by complete reducibility. Reciprocity makes this dim Hom_H(W,1_H), which is the trivial multiplicity in W. Over C that equals dim W^H. Thus the dimensions agree. The initial identification of maps to a trivial quotient with fixed vectors uses semisimplicity; in arbitrary characteristic one must supply a separate argument rather than using ordinary character multiplicities.

</details>

## 10. Summary and next connection

Induction transports an H-space across cosets. Frobenius reciprocity identifies its equivariant maps, the induced-character formula computes its traces, and Mackey's theorem partitions a restricted induced action by double cosets and intersection subgroups. These tools explain both irreducible and reducible examples in S_3 and D_4. The next chapter uses subgroup structure and combinatorics to study [representations of S_n](representation-theory-of-sn.md).
