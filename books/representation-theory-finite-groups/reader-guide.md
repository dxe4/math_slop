
# Reader Guide

A representation remembers a group through linear transformations. Its character remembers only traces. The main question of this book is why those much smaller data nevertheless classify finite-dimensional complex representations of a finite group.

This is a complete L4 first draft. The fourteen-chapter order is deliberate: construct actions; prove invariant complements; study equivariant maps; derive orthogonality; calculate tables; prove algebra structure and completeness; then induce representations. Symmetric groups, arithmetic applications, real forms, tensor products, worked groups and modular phenomena follow. Every chapter supplies its commissioned exposition, worked examples and twelve supported exercises. Independent review remains outstanding.

## Preparation

Use [Finite-Dimensional Linear Algebra: Structure and Proof](#/books/linear-algebra-structure-proofs) Chapters 1–8 and 17–19 for subspaces, quotients, bases, matrices, invariant spaces, duals, inner products, adjoints and the finite-dimensional spectral theorem. Use [Groups, Rings, and Fields](#/books/algebra-patterns-structure) Chapters 1–10 for homomorphisms, quotients, conjugacy, group actions, class equations and Sylow theory. Chapter 1 recalls the precise representation vocabulary; it does not reteach elimination or elementary group theory.

Complex numbers are the default coefficient field after the first three chapters. Any theorem stated over a more general field names its characteristic hypothesis. The modular chapter deliberately drops the hypothesis that the characteristic is coprime to the group order. Tensor and exterior constructions receive definitions where used; familiarity with their linear-algebra chapters is optional preparation for the later representation-ring chapter.

## Reading and solving

For each constructed representation, write down the field, vector space, action convention and group law before calculating a trace. Prove that a proposed subspace is invariant under a generating set. Decide whether a claimed decomposition is a direct sum of invariant subspaces, an isomorphism to such a sum, or merely a list of dimensions. These statements require different evidence.

New exercises use stable REP identifiers. Read the first hint only after an attempt, the second after identifying the missing step, and the complete solution after writing a proof. Personal reading marks record your choice to move on; they do not certify mathematical mastery.

The retained Chapters 5, 8 and 14 begin from the three earlier Euler readings. Their old sources and book-scoped identities remain available in Computational Algebra while compatibility work continues. The new sequence proves character completeness, the Specht classification, standard basis and hook-length formula. The additional Murnaghan–Nakayama rule remains a clearly named theorem import, with locally verified small examples. No independent mathematical review is asserted.
