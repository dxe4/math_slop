
# Reader Guide and Scope {#section-1-reader-guide-and-scope}

The eight chapters include worked examples and exercises with two staged hints and full solutions.

[Riemannian Metrics](riemannian-metrics.md) proves the distance/topology theorem, carefully qualified metric comparisons, gradient bounds and volume density. [Connections and the Levi-Civita Connection](levi-civita-connection.md) supplies the full Koszul construction, moving-frame laws and metric parallel transport. [Geodesics and the Exponential Map](geodesics-exponential-map.md) proves Gauss' lemma, local radial minimization including escaping competitors, and first variation. [Curvature Tensors](curvature-tensors.md) fixes its sign convention and proves tensoriality, symmetries, Bianchi and contraction rules. [Jacobi Fields](jacobi-fields.md) proves the variation equation, conjugate-point criterion and second variation, including the interior-conjugate obstruction and its smoothing step.

[Comparison Theorems](comparison-theorems.md) proves constant-model radial Jacobi/Hessian comparison, normal-ball volume bounds and the positive Ricci minimizing-segment estimate. Its range and existence hypotheses remain explicit. [Hopf–Rinow and Completeness](hopf-rinow-completeness.md) proves all completeness equivalences, global minimizing existence and the positive Ricci compactness corollary. [Higher Gauss–Bonnet and Hodge Theory: An Introduction](higher-gauss-bonnet-hodge.md) constructs star, the formal adjoint, the harmonic energy identity and Euler curvature forms, with a complete finite-dimensional Hodge model and four-sphere normalization. General elliptic solvability/regularity and Chern–Gauss–Bonnet are explicitly cited deep inputs, whose algebraic consequences are derived. The manuscript is Draft. Global Bishop–Gromov and Toponogov are outside its proved comparison scope.

Required preparation is linked on the [book page](#/books/riemannian-geometry). The level is L4–L5. The commissioned scope and source provenance appear there too. A chapter title is a subject to be written, not evidence that its theorems have been proved here.

Definitions and theorem statements must name their hypotheses, including compactness, orientability, coefficient choices, regularity and basepoints where relevant. Later cited machinery remains a named input until its proof has actually been supplied. Exercises retain semantic IDs and receive staged hints and complete solutions.

New text commissioned as a continuation of the Riemannian first-look chapter in Smooth Manifolds. All exercises are written fresh.

## Reference comparison and continuation {#section-15-reference-comparison-and-continuation}

The source comparisons below identify reference editions and differences in theorem scope.

The accessible [2018 second-edition contents of Lee's *Introduction to Riemannian Manifolds*](https://sites.math.washington.edu/~lee/Books/RM/front-matter.pdf), the successor to *Riemannian Manifolds*, supply the Lee comparison. Our first seven chapters share its metric, connection, geodesic, curvature and Jacobi foundations. Lee separately develops submanifolds, cut points, comparison theory and curvature/topology. Our normal-ball estimates and Bonnet–Myers consequence cover a narrower range: no general submanifold fundamental equations, global volume comparison, or full cut-locus theory is proved here. Our final chapter adds an introductory higher-dimensional Euler/Hodge direction with explicitly imported analytic and global inputs.

The [1992 English publisher description of do Carmo](https://link.springer.com/book/9780817634902) identifies a course reaching the sphere theorem. For detailed topic comparison we read the [IMPA sixth-edition 2019 contents](https://impa.br/books/riemannian-geometry/?lang=en); this is a different edition, not an inspected English-edition contents page. Its progression through immersions, Hadamard, space forms, variation, Rauch, Morse index and negative curvature makes the remaining scope concrete. Our completeness proof and positive Ricci argument overlap part of that route. We do not supply full Rauch/Morse-index theory, Cartan–Hadamard, Synge, Preissmann or the sphere theorem.

Choose these references when a later argument needs those omitted results. Keep our radial bounds, normal-domain restrictions and imported elliptic hypotheses visible when using this book independently.
