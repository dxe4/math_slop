
# References, Reading Routes, and Attribution Guide

## Citation roles and edition boundary

The records below distinguish verification, comparison, and continuation. A named inequality or theorem is a retrieval label, not a claim of sole discovery. Page and theorem locators refer to the edition named. Living author files are tied to a version or retrieval date. The book's proofs remain responsible for their own arguments; imported results are mapped explicitly at the end of this guide.

## Foundations and broad probability texts

[Publisher record](https://uat.store.wiley.com/en-us/probability-and-measure-anniversary-edition-p-9781118341919). - Olav Kallenberg, *Foundations of Modern Probability*, 3rd ed., Springer, 2021. [Springer record](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-030-61871-1). Modern measure-theoretic continuation for kernels, conditioning, coupling, and processes.

[Author edition page](https://sites.math.duke.edu/~rtd/PTE/pte.html); [frozen author PDF](https://sites.math.duke.edu/~rtd/PTE/PTE5_011119.pdf). Exact imported-result locators appear below.
- David Williams, *Probability with Martingales*, Cambridge University Press, 1991. [Publisher record](https://www.cambridge.org/highereducation/books/probability-with-martingales/B4CFCE0D08930FB46C6E93E775503926). Comparison for conditional expectation, martingales, and stopping.

- J. R. Norris, *Markov Chains*, Cambridge University Press, 1997. [Publisher record](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/markov-chains/A3F966B10633A32C8F06F37158031739).
- David Aldous and James Allen Fill, *Reversible Markov Chains and Random Walks on Graphs*, unfinished monograph, suggested citation year 2002; author-hosted recompilation 2014, retrieved 6 September 2026. [Author status and file page](https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~aldous/RWG/book.html). The recompilation did not repair the work's unreliable cross-references, so every locator must be checked against the dated file.
- Victor M. Panaretos and Yoav Zemel, *An Invitation to Statistics in Wasserstein Space*, Springer, 2020. [Springer book record](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-38438-8); [open-access Chapter 1](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-38438-8_1), pp. 1--35. Section 1.5, Theorem 1.5.1, Proposition 1.5.2, and Corollary 1.5.3 give the one-dimensional quantile transport and $W_1$ formulas used in Chapter 17.

The manuscript uses total variation as the supremum over events, equivalently one half of the density $L^1$ distance. A source using the full $L^1$ distance differs by a factor of two. It also spells out irreducibility, aperiodicity, finiteness, and reversibility rather than importing an ambiguous use of “ergodic.”

## Exact imported-result map

- **Product-measure existence and uniqueness:** Durrett, 5th ed., Theorem 1.7.1, p. 38. The chapter invokes this theorem only after proving the finite product construction directly.
- **Berry–Esseen with the chapter's $C=1$ convention:** Shevtsova, arXiv:1111.6554, proves the stronger iid bound $C<0.4748$. Durrett, Theorem 3.4.17, p. 157, has constant $3$ and is therefore comparison rather than support for $C=1$.
- **Lindeberg triangular-array CLT:** Durrett, 5th ed., Theorem 3.4.10, pp. 148–149. Dividing each row by its positive standard deviation $s_n$ gives the unit-total-variance form printed in the chapter. The chapter states the sufficient direction and separately notes the infinitesimality needed for a converse.
- **McDiarmid's bounded-difference inequality:** McDiarmid 1989, pp. 148–188, DOI 10.1017/CBO9781107359949.008. The displayed one-sided and two-sided constants are tied to that bounded-coordinate-change formulation.
- **Bennett's inequality:** Boucheron–Lugosi–Massart, Chapter 2, Section 2.7, p. 35. The manuscript uses the independent centered upper-bound formulation with variance sum $v>0$; Bernstein is then obtained from the scalar lower bound on $h$ displayed locally.
- **Regular conditional distributions on standard Borel spaces:** Durrett, official author file Version 5 dated 11 January 2019, Section 4.1.3, Theorem 4.1.17, pp. 214–215, proves existence when the target state space is “nice”; Section 2.1, pp. 53–54, identifies this with the standard-Borel setting used here. Theorem 4.1.18, pp. 215–216, gives the measurable factorization through a conditioning random variable $Y$ when the conditioning sigma-field is $\sigma(Y)$.
- **Characteristic-function inversion and uniqueness:** Durrett, 5th ed., Theorem 3.3.11, pp. 128--130. The endpoint-continuity inversion formula yields the uniqueness claim used in the transform chapter.
- **Lévy continuity theorem:** Durrett, 5th ed., Section 3.3.2, Theorem 3.3.17, pp. 132--133. This is the pointwise-characteristic-function criterion used in the transform chapter and the iid CLT proof.
- **Kac's return-time identity:** Durrett, 5th ed., Theorem 5.5.11, pp. 305--306. It gives $\pi(x)=1/\mathbb{E}_{x}[T_x^+]$ for the finite irreducible-chain setting used here.
- **Finite irreducible aperiodic-chain convergence:** Durrett, 5th ed., Theorem 5.6.6, beginning on p. 313. The manuscript uses its convergence-to-stationarity conclusion in the finite-state setting.
- **One-dimensional optimal quantile transport and $W_1$:** Panaretos and Zemel, Chapter 1, Section 1.5, Theorem 1.5.1, p. 12, and Proposition 1.5.2 and Corollary 1.5.3, p. 13, DOI 10.1007/978-3-030-38438-8_1. These results identify the quantile coupling as optimal and give both the quantile and CDF integrals.

The map covers the substantive imports identified in this audit. A named result added later still needs either a local proof or an exact locator; a broad comparison entry alone does not verify its hypotheses or constants. The subject index names only results that occur in the current text; it is a navigation device, not a priority record.

## Provenance and release boundary

No exercise adaptation from an external source is asserted unless an exercise note supplies an exact record. An external dataset, diagram, or distinctive adapted problem would require its own provenance and license entry. This guide binds bibliographic identities and the high-risk imported claims above. It does not certify exhaustive historical priority, originality, permissions, human approval, or release readiness.

## Sources for the L3 expansion

The Gaussian-vector chapter adds original proofs using affine Gaussian constructions and orthogonal geometry. Yen-Chi Chen's University of Washington [STAT 512 lecture on multivariate normal distributions](https://faculty.washington.edu/yenchic/20A_stat512/Lec8_Linear.html) is a primary teaching reference for multivariate normal linear transformations and conditioning. Dennis Sun's author text [normal-sample decomposition](https://dlsun.github.io/skis/multivariate/normal-sample.html) provides a primary teaching reference for the orthogonal normal and chi-square sample decomposition. The chapter proves the forms it uses, including the singular-covariance boundary, rather than assuming a density exists for every Gaussian vector.

The probabilistic-method chapter now proves the asymmetric local lemma and derives its symmetric criterion, explicitly using joint independence of each event from its nonneighbors' sigma-algebra. Yufei Zhao's [author course page](https://yufeizhao.com/pm/) and the [MIT lecture-note collection](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-226-probabilistic-methods-in-combinatorics-fall-2022/lists/lecture-notes/) are primary references for these methods. The inference bridge proves the simple Neyman–Pearson comparison; the [Berkeley Statistics 210A reader](https://stat210a.berkeley.edu/fall-2025/reader/hypothesis-testing.html) is a primary teaching reference.

All earlier bibliographic identities, exact theorem locators, imported-result qualifications, and provenance boundaries above remain in force. The three process chapters remain registered continuations with their original IDs; no destination-volume migration or deletion is asserted by this expansion.
