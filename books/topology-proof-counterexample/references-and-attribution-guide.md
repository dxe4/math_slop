
# References, Reading Routes, and Attribution Guide

## How the sources are used

The manuscript distinguishes four citation roles:

- **verification** checks a statement, hypothesis, convention, or proof mechanism;
- **attribution** supports a historical or source-dependent claim;
- **comparison** offers a materially different organization or proof route;
- **continuation** takes the reader beyond this book.

The chapters and exercises were written or assembled for this project. No adaptation from an external source is asserted except where a note says so. Standard definitions, examples, and theorem statements belong to the shared mathematical literature; this process statement is not a claim of historical priority or exhaustive originality.

The records below bind editions, reissues, and dated online files where those distinctions affect locators. Retrieval dates are 6 September 2026. A conventional theorem name is used as a retrieval label, not as a complete historical attribution.

## General topology

[Publisher record for the current Classic Version](https://www.pearson.com/en-ca/subject-catalog/p/topology-classic-version-/P200000006299/9780137848669). Verification and comparison for elementary point-set topology, compactness, separation, metrization, and the later algebraic-topology chapters. Locators below refer to the 2000 second edition, not a repaginated reprint.
- Stephen Willard, *General Topology*, Addison-Wesley, 1970; unabridged Dover republication, 2004, ISBN 978-0-486-43479-7. [Dover record](https://store.doverpublications.com/products/9780486434797). Comparison for nets, filters, compactness, separation, uniformity, and metrization. Page locators must name the issue consulted.
- John L. Kelley, *General Topology*, Van Nostrand, 1955; Springer, Graduate Texts in Mathematics 27, reprint 1975. [Springer record](https://link.springer.com/book/9780387901251). Comparison for nets, filters, uniform spaces, products, and function spaces.
- Ryszard Engelking, *General Topology*, revised and completed ed., Heldermann Verlag, 1989. [Publisher record and contents](https://www.heldermann.de/SSPM/SSPM06/sspm06.htm). Verification and continuation, especially choice (§I.4, p. 8), products (§2.3, p. 77), compact-open function spaces (§3.4, p. 156), Stone–Čech compactification (§3.6, p. 172), perfect mappings (§3.7, p. 182), complete metric spaces (§4.3, p. 266), and metrization theorems (§§4.4 and 5.4, pp. 280 and 329).
- James Dugundji, *Topology*, Allyn and Bacon, 1966. Comparison for general topology, uniform structures, function spaces, and extension methods. This book has no authoritative digital edition bound here, so this guide assigns it no page-level verification duty.
- N. Bourbaki, *General Topology: Chapters 1–4*, Elements of Mathematics, Springer, 1995 record for the English translation of the 1971 edition; Springer softcover reprint, 1998. [Springer volume record](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-642-61701-0). Comparison for filters and uniform spaces. No page locator in this guide depends on a differently paginated issue.
- Lynn Arthur Steen and J. Arthur Seebach Jr., *Counterexamples in Topology*, 2nd ed., Springer, 1978; corrected Dover republication, 1995, ISBN 978-0-486-68735-3. [Dover record](https://store.doverpublications.com/products/9780486687353). Comparison only: property tables are checked against the exact topology and convention before use.
- Sidney A. Morris, *Topology Without Tears*, author-hosted online book, file version 6 August 2024, retrieved 6 September 2026. [Author file](https://www.topologywithouttears.net/topbook.pdf). ## Algebraic topology and covering spaces

- Allen Hatcher, *Algebraic Topology*, Cambridge University Press, 2002; author-hosted corrected file retrieved 6 September 2026. [Author page and corrected chapters](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher/AT/ATpage.html). Theorem 1.20 is a continuation source for van Kampen. Theorem 1.38 verifies the classification of connected covering spaces under path-connected, locally path-connected, and semilocally simply connected hypotheses, including the based subgroup and unbased conjugacy-class forms.
- Edwin H. Spanier, *Algebraic Topology*, McGraw-Hill, 1966; Springer softcover reprint, 1994, ISBN 978-0-387-94426-5; Springer digital DOI edition, 2012. [Springer edition record](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-4684-9322-1). Advanced continuation; no page locator from this work is used here.
- J. Peter May, *A Concise Course in Algebraic Topology*, University of Chicago Press, 1999. [Publisher record](https://press.uchicago.edu/ucp/books/book/chicago/C/bo3777031.html). Graduate continuation.
- William S. Massey, *A Basic Course in Algebraic Topology*, Springer, 1991. [Springer record](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-4939-9063-4). Continuation through van Kampen (pp. 86–116) and covering spaces (pp. 117–146).
- Ronald Brown, *Topology and Groupoids*, 3rd ed., BookSurge, 2006; author-hosted corrected file dated 20 January 2020. [Author edition page](https://groupoids.org.uk/orderbook.html). Continuation for groupoid forms of van Kampen and multiple basepoints; locators must identify the 2006 print or the dated corrected file.

This volume develops homotopy and elementary covering theory but stops before proving van Kampen or the covering-classification theorem. It does not develop singular homology, cohomology, spectral sequences, or generalized covering theories.

## Uniformity, compactification, and function spaces

- John R. Isbell, *Uniform Spaces*, Mathematical Surveys 12, American Mathematical Society, 1964. [AMS record](https://bookstore.ams.org/SURV/12). Specialist continuation for uniformity and completeness.
- Leonard Gillman and Meyer Jerison, *Rings of Continuous Functions*, Van Nostrand, 1960; Springer digital archive edition released 2013, eBook ISBN 978-1-4615-7819-2. [Springer archive record](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-4615-7819-2). Continuation for completely regular spaces and Stone–Čech methods; the archive edition’s Stone–Čech material is at pp. 82–100.
- Russell C. Walker, *The Stone–Čech Compactification*, Springer, 1974. [Springer record](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-642-61935-9). Specialist continuation; core construction and universal-property material occupies pp. 1–42.
- R. H. Fox, “On topologies for function spaces,” *Annals of Mathematics* 45(3) (1944), 429–456. [DOI record](https://doi.org/10.2307/1969181). Historical comparison for function-space topologies; it is not used as the sole verification source for the modern Arzelà–Ascoli statement.
- Norman E. Steenrod, “A convenient category of topological spaces,” *Michigan Mathematical Journal* 14(2) (1967), 133–152. [Primary journal record](https://doi.org/10.1307/mmj/1028999711). Historical continuation for convenient categories. The manuscript’s compactly generated weak Hausdorff conventions are stated directly and should not be read back into this paper.
- N. P. Strickland, *The Category of CGWH Spaces*, author manuscript dated 19 August 2009, retrieved 7 September 2026. [Open manuscript](https://www.math.ksu.edu/~dav/diffeos/StricklandCGHWSpaces.pdf). Definition 2.8, p. 4, defines the probe-generated mapping-space topology; Propositions 2.11--2.12, pp. 4--5, establish evaluation and the exponential law for the compactly generated construction; and Proposition 2.24, p. 7, verifies that the mapping object is weak Hausdorff when its target is, completing the closure claim for the CGWH category. Lemma 1.4, p. 1, identifies compact Hausdorff probe images with compact Hausdorff subspaces when the target is weak Hausdorff.

## Manifolds and geometric continuation

- John M. Lee, *Introduction to Smooth Manifolds*, 2nd ed., Springer, 2013. [Springer record](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-4419-9982-5). Theorem 1.2, proved as Theorem 17.26, verifies topological invariance of dimension for nonempty manifolds. Smooth partitions and smooth structures are continuation material, not evidence for omitted point-set proofs.
- Morris W. Hirsch, *Differential Topology*, Springer, 1976. [Springer record](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-4684-9449-5). Later smooth-manifold continuation.
- Glen E. Bredon, *Topology and Geometry*, Springer, 1993. [Springer record](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-4757-6848-0). Continuation from general topology into manifold and algebraic-topology methods.

For the chapter’s imported Euclidean form of invariance of domain, use Munkres, §62; the manifold-open-map consequence is obtained by applying that form in charts. Munkres, §50, and Lee, *Introduction to Smooth Manifolds*, Theorems 1.2 and 17.26, give two routes to invariance of dimension. The intrinsic boundary distinction is a consequence of the same invariance machinery; the chapter treats its well-definedness as imported and does not present an elementary proof.

## Exact map from imported results to sources

The following list records proof boundaries. “Local” means the book supplies the argument; the source is a convention check or further route.

- **Kuratowski closure axioms:** local proof in Chapter 1. The name is only a retrieval label.
- **Heine–Borel theorem:** now proved in `compactness-covers-and-finite-control`, using real completeness and finite products; compare Munkres, §§27–28. The metric Lebesgue-number lemma used there is proved locally.
- **Tychonoff theorem:** now proved for arbitrary products in `compactness-covers-and-finite-control`, with explicit choice, Zorn, filter extension and pushforward lemmas. Compare Munkres, §37, and Engelking, §2.3 together with the choice discussion in §I.4. The manuscript does not use the name to suppress its choice boundary.
- **Urysohn lemma and Urysohn metrization theorem:** both are proved locally. The lemma uses the manuscript's raw normality convention and does not require $T_1$; the metrization theorem explicitly assumes $T_1$, regularity, and second countability. Compare Munkres, §§33–34 after aligning conventions.
- **Nagata–Smirnov metrization theorem:** imported orientation in Chapter 12; Engelking, §§4.4 and 5.4, is the bound route. The local chapter does not claim a proof.
- **Baire category theorem:** the complete-metric form is proved in `complete-metric-spaces-baire-category`; compare Munkres, §48, and Engelking, §4.3.
- **Arzelà–Ascoli theorem:** the compact-Hausdorff-domain, metric-target formulation is checked against Munkres, §47, while Chapter 14 supplies the proof mechanisms in both directions and locally proves that compact-open and uniform convergence agree for compact domain. The product step now refers to the local full Tychonoff proof. Engelking, §3.4, supplies a broader function-space route.
- **Compactly generated weak Hausdorff mapping spaces:** Strickland, Definition 2.8, p. 4, uses the probe sets $W(u,K,U)$ and then k-ifies; Propositions 2.11--2.12, pp. 4--5, give evaluation and the exponential law for the compactly generated construction; Proposition 2.24, p. 7, supplies the weak-Hausdorff closure of the mapping object. Lemma 1.4, p. 1, is the exact weak-Hausdorff route from probe images to compact Hausdorff subspaces.
- **Stone–Čech compactification:** imported universal property for Tychonoff spaces in Chapter 15; compare Walker, pp. 1–42, Gillman–Jerison, pp. 82–100, or Engelking, §3.6.
- **One-point compactification and proper maps:** Chapter 15 proves the construction. For noncompact locally compact Hausdorff $X$ and $Y$, a continuous $f:X\to Y$ is proper exactly when the extension $\alpha f:\alpha X\to\alpha Y$ defined by $\alpha f(\infty_X)=\infty_Y$ is continuous. Compact edge cases are stated separately in the chapter; compare Lee, pp. 104–121.
- **Covering-space classification:** imported orientation in Chapter 19; Hatcher, Theorem 1.38, verifies both based and unbased forms under the stated hypotheses.
- **van Kampen theorem:** continuation boundary in Chapter 18, not a theorem asserted or proved there; use Hatcher, Theorem 1.20, or Lee, Chapter 10.
- **Invariance of domain, dimension, and manifold boundary:** imported in Chapter 17; use the exact locators in the preceding section. The chapter’s consequences retain nonempty and same-dimension hypotheses where required.

Alexander’s subbase theorem is not used as the product proof. Bounded and unbounded Tietze extension are now proved in `urysohn-tietze-extension`, including the geometric correction series and endpoint repair.

## Counterexamples and variant control

A named counterexample is not a complete specification. Before using one, record:

1. its underlying set;
2. the basis, subbasis, metric, order topology, or quotient map;
3. the separation and countability conventions;
4. the exact implication it refutes;
5. any choice principle or uncountable ordinal involved; and
6. whether a “line,” “plank,” “fan,” or “earring” is the standard version or a modification.

This protocol applies particularly to the Sorgenfrey line and plane, excluded-point and particular-point topologies, ordinal spaces, long rays, the line with two origins, comb and sine-curve examples, and the Hawaiian earring. Direct proofs in the chapters carry the mathematical burden; counterexample tables are comparison aids.
