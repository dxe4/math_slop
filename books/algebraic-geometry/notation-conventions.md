
# Notation and Hypotheses

In the classical opening, $k$ is algebraically closed and may have positive characteristic. The affine space $\mathbb A^n(k)$ is the set of coordinate tuples. An affine algebraic set is reduced through its vanishing ideal, and a variety is nonempty and irreducible. The coordinate ring $k[X]$ therefore is reduced for an algebraic set and a domain for a variety. An arbitrary quotient by an equation ideal may carry additional nilpotents and must be named as that quotient.

$V(J)$ denotes common zeros, $I(T)$ denotes all vanishing polynomials, and $D_X(f)$ denotes the principal open where a coordinate function is nonzero. The full prime spectrum uses $\operatorname{Spec}$ in the scheme route; its points are prime ideals rather than just classical coordinate points. A residue field is written $\kappa(x)$. Ring maps preserve identities; maps of $k$-algebras fix the ground field.

A local ring has its maximal ideal and residue field named when used. Tangent dimensions are dimensions over that residue field. Degree of a divisor on a curve includes residue-field degrees unless a stated algebraically closed ground field makes them one. A principal divisor requires a nonzero rational function. Positive, negative and zero valuations retain their signs; a pole is not a point at which the function can be evaluated as a regular scalar.

A morphism, rational map, open immersion, closed immersion and birational equivalence have different contracts. A rational map is represented on a dense open, and equality is tested after restriction to a common dense open. Isomorphism needs actual inverse morphisms everywhere in its domain. A bijection on classical points alone is insufficient, especially in positive characteristic.

Sheaf sections, stalk germs and values in a residue field are different objects. Restriction is written explicitly when gluing. Complexes and cohomology have their degree convention stated at first use. Exactness is a statement about the actual category and functor involved; passing to global sections need not preserve surjections.
