
# Glossary and Chapter Finder

**Affine algebraic set:** a common zero set of polynomials in finite-dimensional affine space. Its classical coordinate ring uses the full vanishing ideal and is reduced. Chapter 1.

**Variety:** a nonempty irreducible algebraic set under the opening classical convention. Scheme-theoretic uses state their additional base and finiteness conventions locally. Chapter 1.

**Vanishing ideal:** all polynomials zero on every point of the specified set. It is radical over a field. Chapter 1.

**Zariski topology:** closed sets are common zero sets. Principal nonvanishing opens form a basis. Chapter 1.

**Irreducible:** nonempty and not a union of two proper closed subsets; equivalently any two nonempty opens intersect. Chapter 1.

**Coordinate ring:** $k[X]=k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]/I(X)$. A domain detects irreducibility, while reducedness alone permits several components. Chapter 1.

**Irreducible component:** a maximal irreducible closed subset. Noetherianity proves there are finitely many, uniquely determined. Chapter 1.

**Quasi-compact:** every open cover has a finite subcover. It does not include Hausdorffness in its definition. Chapter 1.



**Regular function:** locally a quotient of coordinate functions with a nonvanishing denominator; on the whole affine algebraic set it is a coordinate-ring element by the proved patching theorem. Chapter 2.

**Morphism:** a map with regular coordinate functions; its affine pullback is a homomorphism of coordinate rings in the opposite direction. Chapter 2.

**Dominant:** having dense image. For affine algebraic sets this is equivalent to injectivity of pullback, while surjectivity of pullback characterizes a closed embedding. Chapter 2.

**Principal affine open:** the graph model of a nonvanishing coordinate function, with coordinate ring its localization. An arbitrary union of affine opens need not be affine. Chapter 2.

**Function field and birational map:** rational functions are regular on specified dense opens. Dominant rational maps reverse to field embeddings; a field isomorphism gives isomorphic dense opens. Chapter 4.

**Nonsingular classical point:** its Noetherian local ring is regular. The proved tangent/Jacobian calculation compares local dimension with cotangent generator dimension. Chapter 5.

**Divisor:** a finite integer combination of closed curve points; a principal divisor records local valuation orders and has degree zero on the smooth projective curve. Chapter 6.

**Intersection multiplicity:** the length of the local plane quotient by both equations. Plane Bézout counts these lengths, including boundary and repeated contributions. Chapter 7.

**Sheaf and stalk:** compatible local sections glue uniquely, while a stalk is a neighborhood germ ring or module, distinct from its residue value. Chapter 8.

**Affine scheme:** the prime spectrum with localization structure sheaf. Its global ring and all nilpotents are recovered, and locally ringed morphisms reverse to ring homomorphisms. Chapter 9.

**Separated morphism:** its relative diagonal is a closed immersion. Properness additionally requires finite type and universal closedness. Chapter 11.

**Quasi-coherent and coherent:** the former is locally an associated module; the latter adds finite generation and finite kernel conditions, equivalent to local finite generation on a locally Noetherian scheme. Chapter 12.

**Line bundle and Picard group:** locally free rank-one module sheaves form a group under tensor product. On the smooth curve their classes are divisors modulo principal divisors. Chapter 12.

**Cohomology:** derived global sections, computed here by proved acyclic resolutions and finite affine covers under the stated hypotheses. Chapter 13.

**Canonical divisor and genus:** a rational section of the differential line gives the former; genus is the dimension of first structure-sheaf cohomology. Riemann–Roch and canonical degree are proved in Chapter 14.

**Elliptic group scheme:** the pointed smooth proper genus-one curve carries the jointly regular addition constructed in Chapter 15. A Frobenius kernel retains its local ring and group law, beyond its closed-point support.
