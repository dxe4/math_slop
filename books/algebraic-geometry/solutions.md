
# Exercise Solution Guide

Every primary chapter places two specific hints and a full revealable solution beside each exercise. Those local solutions are the authority for the exercise's exact field, finiteness and domain hypotheses. The first hint identifies a construction or invariant; the second isolates its decisive reduction. A valid alternate proof may use different notation while establishing every same obligation.

For a zero-set problem, distinguish ideal equality, radical equality and equality of points. For a map, check both definition on the stated open set and compatibility of its coordinate functions. For a claimed isomorphism, construct both morphisms and check their composites; a set-theoretic inverse is insufficient. For a cohomological calculation, state the cover, sheaf, degree convention and theorem justifying the calculation before simplifying its complex.

A counterexample must satisfy the retained hypotheses and violate the exact proposed conclusion. In the first chapter the hyperbola projection refutes closed direct images for arbitrary morphisms, while the double line distinguishes nilpotent thickness from the same classical point set. Neither example refutes a theorem whose additional projectivity or reducedness hypothesis explicitly excludes it.

The curve and elliptic exercises use the theorem dependencies proved in Chapters 6 and 12–15. Keep track of when a statement concerns closed points, all scheme points, local rings, or the operation on every test scheme; these are different claims with different proof obligations.
