## Routes through the advanced material

Every route below remains internal to this book. A reader who starts from Part 0
can follow the numbered chapters in order; the preparation links are shortcuts
for returning readers, not external prerequisites.

**Scientific-computing route.** Review error bounds in [limits and
continuity](02-limits-continuity.md), the Fundamental Theorem in
[Chapter 6](06-integrals-ftc.md), and Euler's method in
[Chapter 9](09-differential-equations.md). Then read [numerical
calculus](16-numerical.md), the numerical-system sections of [phase
space](17-phase-space.md), and the validation workflow in the [modeling
studio](23-modeling-studio.md). This route distinguishes approximation error,
rounding error, conditioning, solver stability, and model discrepancy rather
than treating them as one vague notion of inaccuracy.

**Dynamics route.** Begin with [first-order differential
equations](09-differential-equations.md), then study [phase
space](17-phase-space.md), [oscillation](18-oscillation.md), and [discrete
calculus](19-discrete-calculus.md). Continuous flows, forced second-order
systems, and iterations use related stability language but different evolution
rules. The phase portrait, resonance, and cobweb figures provide three distinct
visual tests; none substitutes for the hypotheses of the corresponding theorem.

**Optimization and data route.** Read the one-variable extrema material in
[Chapter 5](05-shape-optimization.md), multivariable derivatives in
[Chapter 13](13-partial-derivatives.md), and multiple integration in
[Chapter 14](14-multiple-integrals.md). Continue to [constrained
optimization](20-constrained-optimization.md), [probability
calculus](21-probability.md), and [modeling](23-modeling-studio.md). This path
connects geometry of feasible sets, distributions of uncertain quantities,
parameter fitting, and honest validation boundaries.

**Geometry route.** Move from [vectors](12-vectors-space.md) through [partial
derivatives](13-partial-derivatives.md), [multiple
integrals](14-multiple-integrals.md), and [vector
calculus](15-vector-calculus.md) before reading [coordinates and differential
forms](22-forms.md). The final chapter does not assume a prior course in
manifolds: it defines the local geometric language it needs and uses forms to
unify the Fundamental Theorem, Green, classical Stokes, and divergence.

**Linear algebra, data, and inverse route.** Begin with vectors and multivariable
derivatives, then read Chapters 32, 37, and the probability portions of Chapter
21. The route constructs matrix multiplication and least squares before singular
values, regularization, Bayesian language, or filters appear.

**Differential-equation engineering route.** Read Chapters 9, 16–18, 25, 27,
and then 33, 35, and 36. It moves from elementary ODEs through numerical
stability, continuum balance laws, and feedback control. Units, state variables,
stability regions, and modeling assumptions stay visible throughout.

**Nonlinear dynamics route.** Read functions, sequences, phase space, and
discrete calculus before Chapter 34. The chapter rebuilds the extra ideas—local
linearization, Lyapunov growth, symbolic coding, and box counts—rather than
assuming a separate dynamical-systems course.

**Modern analysis route.** Read [measure and integration](38-measure-integration.md)
before [functional analysis](39-functional-analysis.md). The first chapter
replaces interval-based accumulation by a measure-space theory with explicit
limit hypotheses; the second turns norms, inner products, completeness, and
weak convergence into an operator language. Chapters 6, 10, 21, 25, 26, and 32
provide focused preparation without requiring an external analysis course.

**Algorithmic computation route.** Continue from [linear algebra for calculus
and data](32-linear-algebra-data.md) to [numerical linear
algebra](40-numerical-linear-algebra.md), then read [computational
optimization](41-computational-optimization.md). Residuals, conditioning,
factorizations, Krylov spaces, line searches, trust regions, and KKT residuals
are treated as one auditable pipeline rather than isolated recipes.

**Conservation and inference route.** Move from balance laws in Chapters 27 and
35 to [conservation laws](42-conservation-laws.md), where discontinuities force
weak and entropy formulations. Then use [statistical
inference](43-statistical-inference.md) to design measurements and quantify
what calibrated parameters can support. The route keeps numerical diffusion,
sampling uncertainty, and model discrepancy as distinct sources of error.

**Executable Python route.** The [Python laboratories for
calculus](app-h-python-laboratory.md) begin with no programming assumptions and
provide thirty independently runnable blocks. Labs 1–7 support functions,
derivatives, roots, and integration; Labs 8–14 support ODEs, series,
multivariable methods, and vector calculus; Labs 15–18 support Monte Carlo,
least squares, PDEs, and inference; Labs 19–24 add automatic differentiation,
adaptive quadrature, events, constraints, flux, and complex-step methods; Labs
25–29 translate inverse derivatives, implicit tangents, arc length, vector area,
and the Fundamental Theorem into code. Together with the opening Python
orientation, these 29 labs form thirty executable blocks. Seventeen paired
Plotnine diagnostics make convergence rate,
stability, uncertainty, and finite scope visible.

**Nonsmooth and geometric route.** Continue from Chapters 20, 22, 26, 28, 33,
and 41 into Chapters 44–49. Subgradients handle corners; manifold charts remove
preferred coordinates; kernels and fractional operators handle nonlocality;
geometric integrators preserve selected dynamics; and optimal transport supplies
a geometry for evolving distributions.

**Weak and spectral route.** Read measure and functional analysis in Chapters
38–39 before [distributions and Sobolev calculus](50-distributions-sobolev.md).
Then combine transforms, PDEs, and numerical linear algebra with [spectral
methods](51-spectral-methods.md). This route explains the weak derivative and
test-space language already used by PDE chapters before turning Fourier and
Chebyshev formulas into finite differentiation and evolution algorithms.

## Course-level project prompts

1. **Change and evidence:** collect time-dependent data, compare two plausible models, interpret derivatives, and document extrapolation limits.
2. **Accumulation from measurements:** turn sampled rates or densities into a numerical integral with convergence and unit checks.
3. **Approximation under a tolerance:** select a Taylor degree or numerical mesh from an explicit error target and verify it independently.
4. **A field and its boundary:** choose a two- or three-dimensional field, visualize its local behavior, and use a boundary theorem to compute a global quantity.
5. **Solver certificate:** construct a sparse positive-definite model, compare an unpreconditioned and preconditioned Krylov solve, and report residual, backward error, work, and sensitivity separately.
6. **Shock and sensor design:** evolve a scalar conservation law with a conservative flux, then design noisy measurements for one model parameter and state which numerical and inferential assumptions control the reported interval.
