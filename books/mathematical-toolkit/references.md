
# References and Further Reading

This bibliography identifies useful comparison texts, professional guidance, and historical context. The book is written under CC BY-SA 4.0; standard definitions and professional conventions belong to shared practice. References are included for further reading, alternative treatments, and broader coverage.

A named result or work is a retrieval convention, not a complete history. Mathematical and professional ideas are often the product of long development across communities; a modern statement may differ sharply from an early source.

## Mathematical writing and reasoning

- Hardy, G. H. (1940). *A Mathematician's Apology*. Cambridge University Press. A reflective account of the motivation and value of pure mathematics; useful for understanding the culture that the practical skills in this book serve.
- Halmos, P. R. (1970). "How to Write Mathematics." *L'Enseignement Mathematique*, 16, 123--152. Reprinted in several collections. A concise guide to clarity, structure, and notation discipline.
- Knuth, D. E., Larrabee, T., & Roberts, P. M. (1989). *Mathematical Writing*. Mathematical Association of America. Lecture notes on writing at the research level, with attention to proof presentation and refereeing.
- Velleman, D. J. (2006). *How to Prove It: A Structured Approach* (2nd ed.). Cambridge University Press. A first course in proof techniques and logical structure.

## LaTeX and typesetting

- Lamport, L. (1994). *LaTeX: A Document Preparation System* (2nd ed.). Addison-Wesley. The original reference for LaTeX; the amsmath package documentation supersedes it for mathematical content.
- American Mathematical Society. (2017). *AMS Author Handbook: Journal Classes*. https://www.ams.org/arc/journals/packages/proc/proc_amslatex/Author_Handbook_Journals.pdf. Venue-specific guidance for manuscripts using AMS journal classes; authors must also check the current instructions for their journal.
- Mittelbach, F., with Fischer, U. (2025). *The LaTeX Companion* (3rd ed., Parts I--II). Addison-Wesley Professional. Current edition listed by the LaTeX Project for document classes, packages, and typography: https://www.latex-project.org/help/books/.

## Literature search and research methods

- American Mathematical Society. (interface documented 2023). *Introduction to MathSciNet* and MathSciNet Help Pages. https://mathscinet.ams.org/mathscinet/info/docs/intro. Instructions for using a specialist secondary bibliographic and review database to discover mathematical literature.
- zbMATH Open. *General Help*. https://zbmath.org/general-help/. Live guidance for searching, author identification, and MSC classification; the service is produced by FIZ Karlsruhe and edited in cooperation with the European Mathematical Society and Heidelberg Academy of Sciences and Humanities.

## Collaboration and software practice

- Hunt, A., & Thomas, D. (1999). *The Pragmatic Programmer: From Journeyman to Master*. Addison-Wesley. A general guide to professional software practice, including version control, testing, and automation, with direct application to computational mathematics.
- Chacon, S., & Straub, B. (2014). *Pro Git* (2nd ed.). Apress. Freely available. The standard reference for Git version control.

## Peer review and scientific communication

- Committee on Publication Ethics (COPE). *Core Practices*. Available from the COPE website. Ethical guidelines for editors, reviewers, and authors.
- Sense About Science. (2004). *Peer Review and the Acceptance of New Scientific Ideas*. A plain-language guide to what peer review does and does not accomplish; publication date confirmed in the organization's history: https://senseaboutscience.org/activities/history/.

## Grant writing and research funding

- National Science Foundation. (2024). *Proposal & Award Policies & Procedures Guide* (PAPPG), NSF 24-1. https://www.nsf.gov/policies/pappg/24-1. This version applies to proposals submitted or due on or after May 20, 2024; always check https://www.nsf.gov/policies/pappg, applicable policy notices, and the specific solicitation for current requirements.
- Reif-Lehrer, L. (2005). *Grant Application Writer's Handbook*. Jones and Bartlett. Practical guidance on proposal structure, budgeting, and revision.

## Ethics in mathematics and science

- American Mathematical Society. (2005; modified 2019). *Ethical Guidelines of the American Mathematical Society*. https://www.ams.org/about-us/governance/policy-statements/sec-ethics. Professional expectations for research presentation, attribution, publication, and other responsibilities.
- Resnik, D. B. (1998). *The Ethics of Science: An Introduction*. Routledge. ISBN 9780415166980. A survey of ethical issues in research, including data integrity, authorship, and social responsibility.
- O'Neil, C. (2016). *Weapons of Math Destruction: How Big Data Increases Inequality and Threatens Democracy*. Crown. An examination of how mathematical models can produce harmful social outcomes when deployed without adequate scrutiny.

## Career guidance

- Jackson, A. (2003). "The State of the Job Market for Mathematicians." *Notices of the American Mathematical Society*, 50(5), 564--567. A historical snapshot of the early-2000s job market, not current career guidance.
- Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics (SIAM). *Career Resources*. Available from the SIAM website. Information on industry, government, and academic careers for mathematicians.

## Reading-route recommendations

For a deeper treatment of proof techniques, proceed to *Math Atlas: Discrete Mathematics and Proof*. For computational mathematics, retain the reproducibility and version-control practices from Chapter 5. For research design and measurement, continue into *Math Atlas: Data, Probability, and Statistical Reasoning*. For academic career planning, consult the professional societies (AMS, MAA, SIAM, LMS) and their mentoring programs.

## Citation policy for contributors

Paraphrase sources and cite the job they perform. Do not copy exercise sequences, distinctive examples, diagrams, or prose merely because a concept is standard. Direct quotations require exact locators and license or fair-use review. When sources disagree, record the disagreement. When provenance is unknown, label it unknown rather than inventing a clean lineage.
