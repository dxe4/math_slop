<a id="section-3-primary-references-and-benchmarks"></a>
## Primary references and benchmarks {#section-5-primary-references-and-benchmarks}

Elias M. Stein and Rami Shakarchi, *Complex Analysis*, Princeton Lectures in Analysis, volume II, Princeton University Press, 2003, is a syllabus benchmark for the progression from holomorphic functions through Cauchy theory to global constructions. The [publisher's book page](https://press.princeton.edu/books/hardcover/9780691113852/complex-analysis) identifies this edition. A benchmark citation identifies the level and breadth of the course; it does not make that copyrighted book part of Euler's license.

Lars V. Ahlfors, *Complex Analysis*, third edition, is the other named syllabus benchmark. Its treatment of conformal mapping, analytic continuation, and global function theory provides a useful comparison for the later route. Readers consulting another edition should use theorem names and hypotheses rather than expect matching page numbers or notation.

Jiří Lebl's [*Guide to Cultivating Complex Analysis: Working the Complex Field*](https://www.jirka.org/ca/) is an author-maintained primary exposition of one-variable complex analysis. Its treatment includes Cauchy theory, zero counts, normal families, conformal mapping, and continuation. Its own edition and dual licensing remain controlled by its author. Euler's chapter citations identify it as a mathematical reference; the present exposition and exercises are independently written.

For the special-function chapter, the [NIST Digital Library of Mathematical Functions, gamma definitions](https://dlmf.nist.gov/5.2), [gamma functional relations](https://dlmf.nist.gov/5.5), and [zeta definition and continuation](https://dlmf.nist.gov/25.2) give authoritative reference conventions. This book proves the gamma and zeta facts it uses within the scope stated in Chapter 18; the links also lead to broader theory beyond that scope. In particular, a reference containing a functional equation is not a claim that this manuscript has proved it.

## Retained local source lineage {#section-15-retained-local-source-lineage}

The earlier Functions and Equations readings on complex functions, power series, analytic continuation, and special functions remain independent resources with their existing semantic identities. The Quarto complex-variables chapter also remains in its original book. Their shorter treatments were preparation and topic seeds for the present course. They were not deleted, renamed, or silently replaced by these new chapter identities.

Real Analysis supplies the prerequisite proof, compactness, integration, uniform-convergence, equicontinuity, and power-series results. Its [power-series chapter](../proof-safe-real-analysis/power-series-and-analytic-functions.md) remains available independently of the present complex power-series chapter. Montel's proof names its [Arzelà–Ascoli input](../proof-safe-real-analysis/function-spaces-and-equicontinuity.md). The finite-region residue proof names the additional [Green theorem preparation](../../apps/calculus/index.html#chapter/26); that imported real theorem has hypotheses stated at the point of use.

The domain-colouring laboratory and residue laboratory are illustrations with their own identities. An image or numerical exploration is not evidence that a theorem's hypotheses hold. The [Number Theory book](#/books/number-theory-proof-reference) supplies the arithmetic continuation from the final chapter. Existing PDF editions and earlier source histories are retained; this manuscript does not claim to have generated a new PDF.

<a id="section-21-scope-of-the-proofs"></a>
## Scope of the proofs {#section-23-scope-of-the-proofs}

The Cauchy–Goursat, Cauchy formula, Laurent, argument-principle, Poisson, normal-family, and Riemann mapping results have proofs in their named chapters. Imported Real Analysis and Green theorems are named rather than disguised as new proofs. Weierstrass and Mittag-Leffler are proved for the complex plane and their stated discrete data. The monodromy theorem assumes continuation along every path in the given domain. The zeta continuation is proved, while the functional equation, the Riemann hypothesis, and general value-distribution theory are outside this edition's claims.
