
# Subject Index and Continuations

Use this subject guide when a problem presents an object rather than the name of a theorem. Begin with the domain and the requested conclusion: evaluating an integral, locating zeros, constructing a function, and proving uniqueness call for different tools. The guide points back into this book and forward to retained resources without changing their identities.

## Starting from a problem

**A derivative given by real components.** Begin with [Cauchy–Riemann](holomorphic-cauchy-riemann.md). Check real differentiability, or use the sufficient continuous-partials hypothesis on a neighborhood. A few directional quotients do not prove complex differentiability.

**An infinite series of functions.** Use [power series](power-series-analytic-functions.md) when the terms have one fixed center. Use the compact uniform limit theorem in [Cauchy's formula](cauchy-integral-formula.md) for more general holomorphic sequences. For a family without a specified limit, use [normal families](normal-families-riemann-mapping.md). Pointwise boundedness and local uniform boundedness are different conditions.

**A root, logarithm, or fractional power.** Use [exponential and logarithm](exponential-logarithm-powers.md) to fix a local or slit-domain branch. For global existence, consult [primitives and simple connectedness](cauchy-goursat.md). For continuation around loops, consult [monodromy](analytic-continuation.md). Check the domain before simplifying an expression involving powers.

**A closed contour integral.** First ask whether a primitive exists using [paths and contour integrals](paths-contour-integrals.md). If the contour surrounds singularities, inspect the legal domain and complete boundary in the [residue theorem](residue-theorem.md). For an annulus, consult [Laurent series](laurent-series-singularities.md). A deformation must avoid every singularity throughout the deformation.

**A real improper integral.** Use [real integrals by residues](real-integrals-residues.md) to choose and justify a contour. The limiting arc estimate belongs to the calculation. Distinguish absolute convergence, ordinary one-sided improper limits, and a Cauchy principal value before reporting an answer.

**Zeros whose exact positions are unknown.** Use [Rouché and the argument principle](argument-principle-rouche.md) for a count in a bounded region with a verified boundary condition. Use [identity and isolation](zeros-identity-maximum-modulus.md) for uniqueness and accumulation questions. Always specify whether a count includes multiplicities.

**An equilibrium field with boundary values.** Use the [Poisson kernel](harmonic-functions-poisson.md) for continuous data on a circle. The construction proves boundary attainment as well as interior harmonicity. For a different domain, a justified [conformal map](conformal-mobius.md) may transport the problem; the existence of a map and the behavior of its boundary extension are separate questions.

**A function required to have prescribed zeros or principal parts.** Use [entire and meromorphic constructions](entire-meromorphic-functions.md). The discrete-set hypothesis excludes finite accumulation points. The correction factors and polynomials enforce compact convergence; deleting them can destroy the construction.

## Connections with retained readings

The [Real Analysis book](#/books/proof-safe-real-analysis) is the repair route for compactness, uniform convergence, equicontinuity, and integration. Its existing chapters remain separate learning resources. Consult them when the missing step is an analytical interchange of limits, rather than a complex-variable identity.

The domain-colouring laboratory illustrates zeros, poles, arguments, and branch cuts. The residue laboratory connects local singularity data with contour integrals. Use these to formulate a prediction, then supply a proof with the theorems and estimates in the course.

The final chapter's Euler product connects to the [Number Theory book](#/books/number-theory-proof-reference). Unique prime factorization and absolute convergence have separate roles: arithmetic organizes the product, while analytic estimates justify passage to its infinite limit. Neither role should be replaced by a formal expansion.

## Further study and explicit limits

Several natural continuations require new theorems. Boundary extension of conformal maps needs geometric boundary hypotheses and a boundary correspondence theory. General potential theory extends the disk Dirichlet problem to less regular domains and data. Several complex variables changes both the geometry of holomorphy and the nature of singularities.

Entire-function growth and value distribution go beyond the plane existence constructions proved here. Mittag-Leffler on arbitrary domains needs additional approximation tools. The gamma–zeta chapter proves continuation and selected values; a derivation of the zeta functional equation requires another argument, and the Riemann hypothesis is not a theorem supplied by this text.

A productive next project is to state one of these missing conclusions precisely and list what the present course already proves toward it. Keep that list separate from the desired conclusion. Knowing exactly where a proof stops is part of using an advanced course responsibly.

## Summary

Choose a method by its conclusion and hypotheses. This course supplies local expansions, boundary identities, zero counts, harmonic solutions, compactness arguments, continuation, and plane constructions. The retained analysis, arithmetic, and laboratory resources support further work without replacing proofs or changing earlier learning identities.
