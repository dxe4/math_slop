
# References and Further Reading

This bibliography identifies useful comparison texts, curriculum guidance, and historical sources. The book is written under CC BY-SA 4.0; standard definitions and educational conventions belong to shared practice. References are included for further reading, historical context, alternative treatments, and broader coverage.

A named result or study is a retrieval convention, not a complete history. Mathematical ideas are often the product of long development across languages, institutions, and communities; a modern statement may differ sharply from an early source.

## Algebra teaching and learning

- National Council of Teachers of Mathematics. (2000). *Principles and Standards for School Mathematics*. NCTM. A widely referenced framework for school mathematics content and process standards; claims should be read as professional consensus rather than intervention-effect estimates.
- Kieran, C. (2007). "Developing algebraic reasoning: The role of sequenced tasks and teacher questions from the primary to the early secondary school levels." *Quadrante*, 16(1), 5--26. https://doi.org/10.48489/quadrante.22814. Discusses transitions from arithmetic to algebraic reasoning through structured tasks and teacher questions.
- Mason, J., Graham, A., & Johnston-Wilder, S. (2005). *Developing Thinking in Algebra*. Sage. A task-rich text organised around expressing, manipulating, and interpreting generality.

These works use different conventions for task design, progression, and evidence quality. Comparison is most useful when those conventions are recorded rather than blended.

## Word problems and modelling

- Verschaffel, L., Greer, B., & De Corte, E. (2000). *Making Sense of Word Problems*. Swets & Zeitlinger. Examines how learners interpret word-problem statements and the role of realistic modelling.
- Boaler, J. (2016). *Mathematical Mindsets*. Jossey-Bass. Discusses narrative and visual approaches to algebra accessible to a broad audience; read as professional guidance rather than a controlled-effect study.

## History of algebra

- Boyer, C. B., & Merzbach, U. C. (2011). *A History of Mathematics* (3rd ed.). Wiley. Chapter treatments of early symbolic algebra and the development of equations.
- Høyrup, J. (2002). *Lengths, Widths, Surfaces: A Portrait of Old Babylonian Algebra and Its Kin*. Springer. A scholarly reconstruction of pre-symbolic algebraic reasoning from cuneiform sources.

Historical claims in this book are drawn from secondary sources. For priority disputes or precise dating, consult specialist historical scholarship and the primary sources it identifies.

## Curriculum and assessment standards

- Department for Education (UK). (2013). *Mathematics Programmes of Study: Key Stage 3*, DFE-00179-2013. https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/national-curriculum-in-england-mathematics-programmes-of-study. National curriculum document specifying algebra content for early secondary years.
- National Governors Association Center for Best Practices & Council of Chief State School Officers. (2010). *Common Core State Standards for Mathematics*. https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf. Standards for expressions, equations, functions, and modelling in grades 6–8 and high school.

These standards differ in scope, sequence, and emphasis. This book does not follow any single standard exactly; it selects topics that support the story-first method.

## Formulas and applications

- Tipler, P. A., & Mosca, G. (2008). *Physics for Scientists and Engineers* (6th ed.). W. H. Freeman. Standard reference for the physics formulas used in **Formulas from Science**.
- Chang, R., & Goldsby, K. A. (2016). *Chemistry* (12th ed.). McGraw-Hill Education. ISBN 9780078021510. Reference for density, concentration, and unit conventions.

## Reading-route recommendations

For a deeper treatment of number and early algebra, proceed to the Math Atlas volume on number, operations, and early algebra. For functions and graphs beyond linear, continue into the Math Atlas volume on patterns, functions, and algebraic structure. For proof and abstract algebra, seek a dedicated transition-to-proof or abstract algebra text.

## Citation policy for contributors

Paraphrase sources and cite the job they perform. Do not copy exercise sequences, distinctive examples, diagrams, or prose merely because a concept is standard. Direct quotations require exact locators and license or fair-use review. When sources disagree, record the disagreement. When provenance is unknown, label it unknown rather than inventing a clean lineage.
