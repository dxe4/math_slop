# Bibliography and Further Reading {#sec-bibliography}

The exposition in this book is original. The works below are recommended routes to fuller proofs, historical context, and advanced continuations. Edition details are included so statements can be located reliably.

## Foundational texts

- Apostol, Tom M. *Introduction to Analytic Number Theory*. Springer, 1976. A bridge from elementary arithmetic functions to Dirichlet series.
- Burton, David M. *Elementary Number Theory*, 7th ed. McGraw–Hill, 2011. Broad undergraduate treatment with many exercises.
- Hardy, G. H., and E. M. Wright. *An Introduction to the Theory of Numbers*, 6th ed., revised by D. R. Heath-Brown and J. H. Silverman. Oxford University Press, 2008. Classical reference spanning elementary and analytic themes.
- Ireland, Kenneth, and Michael Rosen. *A Classical Introduction to Modern Number Theory*, 2nd ed. Springer, 1990. Especially valuable for reciprocity, characters, and algebraic connections.
- Niven, Ivan, Herbert S. Zuckerman, and Hugh L. Montgomery. *An Introduction to the Theory of Numbers*, 5th ed. Wiley, 1991. Careful classical development.
- Rosen, Kenneth H. *Elementary Number Theory and Its Applications*, 6th ed. Pearson, 2011. Algorithmic emphasis and applications.

## Computational and cryptographic references

- Bach, Eric, and Jeffrey Shallit. *Algorithmic Number Theory, Volume I:
  Efficient Algorithms*. MIT Press, 1996. Careful algorithms, certificates,
  and complexity language for primality and factorisation.
- Cohen, Henri. *A Course in Computational Algebraic Number Theory*. Springer, 1993. Algorithms for number fields and arithmetic.
- Crandall, Richard, and Carl Pomerance. *Prime Numbers: A Computational Perspective*, 2nd ed. Springer, 2005. Primality, factorisation, and prime computation.
- Katz, Jonathan, and Yehuda Lindell. *Introduction to Modern Cryptography*, 3rd ed. CRC Press, 2020. Formal security definitions and protocol design; use current standards for deployment guidance.
- Menezes, Alfred J., Paul C. van Oorschot, and Scott A. Vanstone. *Handbook of Applied Cryptography*. CRC Press, 1996. Freely available reference for classical algorithms, with the caveat that practical parameter guidance ages.
  The author-hosted [Chapter 4](https://cacr.uwaterloo.ca/hac/about/chap4.pdf),
  Fact 4.23, states the exact residue domain for the Miller–Rabin liar bound;
  Chapter 44 develops how changing the sampling space changes the probability.
- Conrad, Keith. [*The Miller–Rabin Test*](https://kconrad.math.uconn.edu/blurbs/ugradnumthy/millerrabin.pdf).
  Author's teaching notes, especially Definition 2.3, Example 2.5, and Theorem
  2.9. A direct route from modular squaring to the exact witness-count theorem.
- SageMath Developers. *SageMath Documentation*. A reproducible environment for exact arithmetic and experimentation.

## Partitions, cyclotomy, and enumerative references

- Andrews, George E. *The Theory of Partitions*. Cambridge University Press,
  1998 reissue. The classical analytic and q-series treatment.
- Andrews, George E., and Kimmo Eriksson. *Integer Partitions*. Cambridge
  University Press, 2004. An accessible route through bijections, generating
  functions, congruences, and asymptotics.
- Hardy, G. H., and S. Ramanujan. “Asymptotic Formulae in Combinatory
  Analysis.” *Proceedings of the London Mathematical Society* 17 (1918):
  75–115. Original circle-method asymptotic for the partition function.
- Rademacher, Hans. “On the Partition Function $p(n)$.” *Proceedings of the
  London Mathematical Society* 43 (1937): 241–254. Convergent refinement of
  the Hardy–Ramanujan expansion.
- Washington, Lawrence C. *Introduction to Cyclotomic Fields*, 2nd ed.
  Springer, 1997. Primitive roots, cyclotomic polynomials, splitting, and
  explicit abelian extensions.

## Analytic and algebraic continuations

- Cox, David A. *Primes of the Form $x^2+ny^2$*, 2nd ed. Wiley, 2013. A sustained local-to-global story connecting forms, fields, and class groups.
- Davenport, Harold. *Multiplicative Number Theory*, 3rd ed., revised by Hugh L. Montgomery. Springer, 2000. Dirichlet characters and primes in progressions.
- Khinchin, A. Ya. *Continued Fractions*. Dover, 1997. Compact classical account of approximation theory.
- Marcus, Daniel A. *Number Fields*. Springer, 1977. Accessible first course in algebraic number theory.
- Neukirch, Jürgen. *Algebraic Number Theory*. Springer, 1999. Advanced systematic treatment.
- Serre, Jean-Pierre. *A Course in Arithmetic*. Springer, 1973. Quadratic forms, $p$-adic ideas, and modular forms.
- Silverman, Joseph H., and John Tate. *Rational Points on Elliptic Curves*, 2nd ed. Springer, 2015. Entry into arithmetic geometry.

## Algebraic, modular, and probabilistic continuations

- Bombieri, Enrico, and Walter Gubler. *Heights in Diophantine Geometry*.
  Cambridge University Press, 2006. Systematic height machinery and
  Diophantine applications.
- Diamond, Fred, and Jerry Shurman. *A First Course in Modular Forms*. Springer,
  2005. Modular curves, Hecke operators, and arithmetic q-expansions.
- Iwaniec, Henryk, and Emmanuel Kowalski. *Analytic Number Theory*. American
  Mathematical Society, 2004. Modern analytic methods, characters, sieves, and
  automorphic perspectives.
- Milne, J. S. *Algebraic Number Theory* and *Class Field Theory*. Continuously
  maintained course notes; useful for explicit theorem dependencies.
- Montgomery, Hugh L., and Robert C. Vaughan. *Multiplicative Number Theory I:
  Classical Theory*. Cambridge University Press, 2007. Detailed L-functions
  and primes in progressions.
- Neukirch, Jürgen. *Algebraic Number Theory*. Springer, 1999. Ideals, class
  groups, local methods, and reciprocity in a unified treatment.
- Ono, Ken. *The Web of Modularity*. American Mathematical Society, 2004.
  Accessible routes among partitions, modular forms, and arithmetic.
- Tenenbaum, Gérald. *Introduction to Analytic and Probabilistic Number Theory*.
  American Mathematical Society, 2015. Normal orders, smooth numbers, and
  probabilistic models.

## Local fields, arithmetic geometry, dynamics, and automorphic continuations

- Bump, Daniel. *Automorphic Forms and Representations*. Cambridge University
  Press, 1997. A systematic route from adelic groups and Hecke algebras to
  automorphic representations.
- Cassels, J. W. S., and Albrecht Fröhlich, eds. *Algebraic Number Theory*.
  Academic Press, 1967. Classic seminar volume containing local and global
  class field theory from several complementary viewpoints.
- Gelbart, Stephen S. *Automorphic Forms on Adele Groups*. Princeton University
  Press, 1975. Concise foundational account of the passage from classical to
  adelic automorphic forms.
- Goldfeld, Dorian, and Joseph Hundley. *Automorphic Representations and
  L-Functions for the General Linear Group*, Volume I. Cambridge University
  Press, 2011. Detailed local components, Whittaker models, and global
  $L$-functions.
- Kuipers, L., and Harald Niederreiter. *Uniform Distribution of Sequences*.
  Wiley, 1974. Standard reference for discrepancy, Weyl's criterion, and
  quantitative equidistribution.
- Neukirch, Jürgen. *Class Field Theory*. Springer, 1986. Local and global
  reciprocity, norm groups, conductors, and cohomological architecture.
- Rosen, Michael. *Number Theory in Function Fields*. Springer, 2002. Curve
  zeta functions, finite-field arithmetic, and the analogy between number
  fields and function fields.
- Serre, Jean-Pierre. *Local Fields*. Springer, 1979. The standard compact
  treatment of valuations, finite extensions, differents, ramification groups,
  and local class field theory.
- Silverman, Joseph H. *The Arithmetic of Dynamical Systems*. Springer, 2007.
  Canonical heights, reduction, periodic points, and arithmetic iteration on
  projective space.

## Reliable resources for theorem status

- Clay Mathematics Institute. [Riemann Hypothesis](https://www.claymath.org/millennium/riemann-hypothesis/). Official Millennium Prize problem description and status.
- Helfgott, Harald A. “The ternary Goldbach conjecture is true.” 2013; expanded monograph *The Ternary Goldbach Problem*, 2015. Proof of weak Goldbach.
- Maynard, James. “Small gaps between primes.” *Annals of Mathematics* 181 (2015): 383–413. An independent bounded-gap method with many-prime extensions.
- Tao, Terence. “Almost all orbits of the Collatz map attain almost bounded values.” *Forum of Mathematics, Pi* 10 (2022), e12. A major density-type partial result, not a proof of the Collatz conjecture.
- Zhang, Yitang. “Bounded gaps between primes.” *Annals of Mathematics* 179 (2014): 1121–1174. The breakthrough unconditional bounded-gap theorem.

Status pages and computational records can change. Consult dated primary literature or the responsible institution before quoting a current record. The fixed logical statuses used in this edition are: PNT and Dirichlet's theorem proved; weak Goldbach proved; bounded prime gaps proved; strong Goldbach, twin primes, Collatz, odd perfect numbers, and the Riemann hypothesis open.

## Exact reduction and discrepancy certificates

- Sharifi, Romyar. [*Abstract Algebra*, Chapter 5, Proposition
  5.3.6](https://www.math.ucla.edu/~sharifi/notes/algebra-ch05.html).
  Author-hosted notes stating the leading-coefficient hypothesis for
  irreducibility under reduction. Chapter 23 proves the integer-content
  argument and develops counterexamples and exact small-degree certificates.
- Weiß, Christian. [“Deviation from equidistance for one-dimensional
  sequences”](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00010-023-00958-x).
  *Aequationes mathematicae* (2023), Lemma 3.2. Its one-dimensional midpoint
  formula gives the sharp finite star-discrepancy bound. Chapter 37 proves
  the formula with its strict-endpoint convention, including repeated points.

## Reading routes

- For a second elementary course: Niven–Zuckerman–Montgomery, then Ireland–Rosen.
- For computation: Crandall–Pomerance alongside exact experiments.
- For analytic theory: Apostol, followed by Davenport.
- For algebraic theory: Marcus, followed by Neukirch or Cox for a thematic route.
- For cryptography: first study formal definitions in Katz–Lindell, then consult current platform standards and reviewed libraries.
