## Statistical sources

**Avanzi, Benjamin; Boglioni Beaulieu, Guillaume; Lafaye de Micheaux, Pierre; Ouimet, Frédéric; and Wong, Bernard.** “A Counterexample to the Central Limit Theorem for Pairwise Independent Random Variables Having a Common Arbitrary Margin.” [arXiv:2003.01350](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.01350), 2020; *Journal of Mathematical Analysis and Applications* 499(1), 2021, 1–13, [DOI 10.1016/j.jmaa.2021.124982](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmaa.2021.124982). Consulted in Chapter 8 to check the scope of the iid finite-variance central limit theorem and the warning that pairwise independence alone is insufficient. The chapter states the imported theorem and does not adapt the paper's example.

**Clopper, C. J., and Pearson, E. S.** “The Use of Confidence or Fiducial Limits Illustrated in the Case of the Binomial.” *Biometrika* 26(4), 1934, 404–413. [DOI 10.1093/biomet/26.4.404](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/26.4.404). Bibliographic metadata were checked for Chapter 12. The implemented interval and its at-least-nominal coverage description were checked in current `binom.test` help; the author pass did not inspect the full primary article, and the chapter independently derives its endpoint calculation.

**Efron, Bradley.** “Bootstrap Methods: Another Look at the Jackknife.” *The Annals of Statistics* 7(1), 1979, 1–26. [DOI 10.1214/aos/1176344552](https://doi.org/10.1214/aos/1176344552). The publisher record and abstract were consulted for the historical origin and general construction of the bootstrap in Chapter 10. The chapter proves its conditional mean identities and gives no universal bootstrap-validity or finite-sample coverage claim.

**Phipson, Belinda, and Smyth, Gordon K.** “Permutation P-values Should Never Be Zero: Calculating Exact P-values When Permutations Are Randomly Drawn.” *Statistical Applications in Genetics and Molecular Biology* 9(1), Article 39, 2010. [DOI 10.2202/1544-6115.1585](https://doi.org/10.2202/1544-6115.1585). The full author-hosted preprint and DOI metadata were consulted for Chapter 9's finite Monte Carlo p-value boundary and the conservative \((b+1)/(B+1)\) value for independent permutation draws with replacement. The chapter's exact enumerated randomization p-value and finite tail-rank theorem have separate definitions and a displayed proof.

**Student.** “The Probable Error of a Mean.” *Biometrika* 6(1), 1908, 1–25. [DOI 10.1093/biomet/6.1.1](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/6.1.1). The primary record identifies the author as “Student”; this bibliography does not add a historical identity without a separate source. An accessible reproduction was consulted in Chapter 12 for the normal-sample construction. The book's modern exact theorem states iid normal observations, positive variance, and the required independence result explicitly.

**Welch, B. L.** “The Generalization of ‘Student's’ Problem when Several Different Population Variances Are Involved.” *Biometrika* 34(1–2), 1947, 28–35. [DOI 10.1093/biomet/34.1-2.28](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/34.1-2.28). An accessible primary-paper scan was consulted for the unequal-variance approximation and effective degrees of freedom in Chapter 12. Welch intervals and tests remain labeled approximate in this book, including the capstone.
