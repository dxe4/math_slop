# Bibliography and further pathways

The point-set sequence follows the durable language represented by Munkres
([Munkres 2000](#ref-munkres2000topology)). Hatcher provides a broad algebraic-topology route
([Hatcher 2002](#ref-hatcher2002algebraic)), Lee develops topological manifolds
([Lee 2011](#ref-lee2011topological)), Ghrist connects topology to applications
([Ghrist 2014](#ref-ghrist2014elementary)), and Edelsbrunner and Harer develop computational
topology ([Edelsbrunner and Harer 2010](#ref-edelsbrunner2010computational)).

Coverage was audited against the official MIT 18.901 point-set topology course
([OpenCourseWare 2004](#ref-mit18901)) and the MIT 18.905 algebraic topology course ([OpenCourseWare 2016](#ref-mit18905)). The
latter's sequence through singular and relative homology, CW methods,
coefficients, tensor and Tor, universal coefficients, Künneth, products,
orientations, Čech methods, and Poincaré duality motivated the deeper
calculation sequence in Chapters 24–28. These curriculum references guide
scope; the proofs and examples here remain self-contained.

These references vary greatly in prerequisite and emphasis. This book supplies
an internal path through their shared definitions and marks where it offers
only a gateway: full proofs of classification, the paracompactness and
dimension theorems, general characteristic classes, derived sheaf cohomology,
stable higher homotopy, and persistence-stability theorems belong to specialist
courses.

Suggested next projects include implementing sparse boundary reduction,
constructing explicit covering spaces of graphs, comparing coefficient fields
on a triangulated surface, and auditing persistent homology under metric and
sampling perturbations.
