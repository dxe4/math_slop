
# Reader Guide and Prerequisites

This L4 route begins where classical differentiation becomes too restrictive. Read the [classical PDE models](../partial-differential-equations/classification-model-problems.md) before the Sobolev opening. Preparation includes Lebesgue integration, almost-everywhere equivalence, Cauchy–Schwarz, completeness, bounded linear functionals, and integration by parts. The [Hilbert space](../functional-analysis/hilbert-spaces.md) and [Riesz representation](../functional-analysis/orthonormal-bases-riesz.md) chapters supply the abstract input used in the opening's coercive existence proof. [Real analysis](#/books/proof-safe-real-analysis) supplies the wider analysis route.

The nine chapters develop Sobolev foundations, elliptic solvability, regularity, parabolic energy existence, semigroup evolution, hyperbolic energy, scalar entropy selection, direct methods and reaction–diffusion patterns. Registered companions preserve both earlier integrated elliptic/parabolic and hyperbolic/conservation readings. Every chapter has developed models and staged practice; the main route supplies 140 supported exercises. The hyperbolic chapter retains twenty earlier conservation bridges and adds ten wave/system problems.

Read the regularity failure examples before interpreting a weak solution as a classical one. In the evolution chapters distinguish continuous state traces from classical time derivatives. In the nonlinear chapters distinguish compactness from admissible closure, and a growing linear mode from existence or stability of a stationary pattern.

For each equation record the domain, data space, boundary condition, solution space and test space. Then state separately what existence, uniqueness, stability, regularity, or approximation theorem establishes. Cover the worked examples before attempting them. Use the first hint for a direction, the second for the decisive construction, and the full solution only after making an attempt. Study completion is a personal record, not graded evidence.

The [continuum-model book](#/books/fields-fluids-continuum) supplies physical balance and closure examples. The numerical-analysis book owns computational error. This book's first task is analytic meaning: a weak equation must still say exactly what is tested and which boundary information is encoded.
