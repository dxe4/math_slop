
# Notation and Solution Contracts

$\Omega$ is open unless a stronger domain assumption is stated. $L^2$ functions and Sobolev functions are equivalence classes modulo equality almost everywhere. $C_c^\infty$ consists of smooth compactly supported tests. $D_i u$ denotes a weak derivative; $\nabla u$ collects the first derivatives. $H^1=W^{1,2}$, while $H_0^1$ is defined by closure of compact tests, not by choosing pointwise values of an arbitrary representative. The interval representative and trace interpretation are proved in the opening.

The real Hilbert inner product is bilinear. Where complex fields are used, the chosen slot of linearity will be stated. $V^*$ denotes bounded linear functionals, and $\langle F,v\rangle$ is a dual pairing, not automatically an integral of ordinary functions. In the opening $H^{-1}$ is the dual of $H_0^1$ equipped with the derivative norm, equivalent to its full norm on the bounded interval.

Our positive elliptic prototype is $-\operatorname{div}(a\nabla u)+cu=f$. Coercivity refers to a positive lower bound in a specified norm. A nonnegative coefficient need not have a positive uniform lower bound. A weak identity is tested against every member of its declared test space, not just a finite basis. Its boundary conditions live in the solution space or an explicit boundary pairing.

Existence in $H^1$ does not assert two classical derivatives. A distributional equation alone need not identify boundary data. A stable discrete approximation does not establish a regularity theorem. Every later chapter must preserve these distinctions when adding its own solution contract.
