
# Notation and Hypotheses

A finite family has a stated equality convention. Labels, component orders,
rotations, repeated types and multiedges are retained or identified only
when the definition says so. A coefficient counts those specified objects,
not every informal object that resembles an example.

Formal power series are interpreted coefficientwise. An infinite operator
must have only finitely many contributions at each bounded degree.
Analytic assertions additionally require convergence and the stated complex
continuation domain. The coefficient notation selects an exact coefficient;
a displayed asymptotic concerns a specified limit, with other parameters
fixed unless a uniform range is proved.

A partition is a weakly decreasing list of positive integers, extended
by zeros when a fixed matrix size needs it. A tableau shape and
its permitted labels are specified separately. Standard tableaux use each
label once; semistandard tableaux allow the stated row repetitions.
The distinction between evaluation and coefficient extraction is maintained.

Groups and representation spaces are finite in the character chapter,
with complex scalars. Polynomial-method fields are named locally, and
nonzero coefficients are interpreted in that field. A real positivity
argument is not automatically a finite-field rank argument.

Hypergraphs are finite and simple with nonempty edges unless a different
convention is explicitly introduced. Uniformity is a fixed positive edge
size. Maximum codegrees, average degrees, fractional weights and integral
solutions are separate quantities. Maximal and maximum refer to different
conditions.

Graphs are simple and undirected. Dense limits normalize discrepancies
by vertex count squared. Homomorphisms, injective labelled copies and
unlabelled subgraphs have different exact multiplicities. In regularity
statements, the chapter explicitly says whether pair counts are ordered.
Sparse statements require their own normalization and hypotheses.

All logarithms in the entropy chapter are natural, with zero logarithmic
probability terms interpreted by continuity. Variables have finite discrete
support. A uniform object makes entropy equal log family-size; an
arbitrary nonuniform generator does not. Fingerprints need a proved
reconstruction rule before their count can bound a family.

The sources appendix identifies benchmarks, retained local readings and
primary references for expressly cited inputs. These conventions keep a
formal identity, finite certificate, asymptotic, example and imported theorem
from being treated as interchangeable forms of evidence.
