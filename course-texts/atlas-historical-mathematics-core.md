## Course contract and evidence language

Use four distinct labels in every notebook entry: **W** for what the selected witness presents; **T** for a translator's or editor's stated intervention; **M** for a modern mathematical reconstruction; **I** for a historical inference. A printed claim about an earlier event is W evidence that the later author made that claim; independent confirmation of the event requires more evidence. In the older Euclid unit, its P/E/M labels mean printed argument/editorial intervention/modern reconstruction. Here portfolio P/C/R mean primary, changed, and return work, so retain the labels explicitly.

A source card records author or attributed author; work; composition or original-publication date when established; the actual edition and language consulted; translator/editor; passage locator; digital host and access date; relevant interventions; and what remains unverified. A manuscript-copy date, a translation date, a reprint date, and today's access date answer different questions. A later transcription can preserve an earlier argument and still introduce errors.

The course's comparative unit is a **specified problem in a specified witness**, not a civilisation's supposed ability. When a representation changes, ask what objects it permits, what operations it licenses, and what counts as a certificate. A positive length and a signed unknown need not have the same answer set. A geometric construction and a symbolic identity need not certify the same existence claim. A correct reconstruction explains a procedure; it does not by itself recover an author's thought or a reader's experience. ## H0 — Witnesses, chronology, and the shape of a historical claim

Begin with the existing global course's entry diagnostic and Sections 1–5. Its four lenses—object, operation, argument, setting—prevent a manuscript from becoming merely a container for present-day formulas. Then complete all five exercises and keys in Reading Historical Algorithms. Its examples are explicitly pedagogical reconstructions, so use them to learn a method before making claims about the new witnesses below.

A chronology should have parallel rows. Rosen's English publication is 1831; the Arabic manuscript he describes in his preface is a copy dated AH 743, which he converts to 1342 CE. Neither date is the composition date of every mathematical procedure in it. Karpinski's 1915 volume prints a Latin tradition attributed to Robert of Chester and an English version; a heading gives a Spanish Era date that the editor converts to 1145 CE. These are different textual routes, not two direct windows onto an identical original. Consult A1 and A2 in the source register for their prefatory limitations.

**H0 checkpoint, history-core-h0-v1.** Classify these claims: (a) a physical copy dates a procedure's invention; (b) two texts give \(3\) for the same quadratic, so one copied the other; (c) a later editor has made notation more familiar, so the earlier argument used that notation; (d) two editions differ in how they discuss an assumption.

**H0 key.** (a) Unsupported without evidence connecting composition and copy; the copy establishes a witness at a date. (b) Arithmetic agreement supports comparison, while transmission needs a route, intermediaries, chronology, and textual evidence. (c) Editorial notation is T, our formula is M, and neither is automatically W for the earlier text. (d) A bounded observation supported by the actual passages; it does not establish intent, dependence, or teaching effectiveness. Repair an overclaim by naming the witness, locator, and additional evidence that would be needed.

## H1 — Translation changes the object being read

Read Rosen's p. 8 beside Karpinski's Latin p. 70 and English p. 71. The selected problem concerns a square and ten roots with total 39. Rosen uses the token “dirhems”; the Latin page has “drachmis” and “substantia”; Karpinski's English has “units” and “square”. These few visible words allow a precise translation question: does an English numerical abstraction conceal the monetary or magnitude vocabulary of the witnesses? They do not let us decide the full semantic range of the Arabic. We have not collated Arabic manuscripts.

Rosen's introductory discussion of roots, squares, and numbers groups positive kinds of magnitude. The translator's symbolic footnote is a separate layer. Karpinski explicitly offers an accessible English version rather than a mechanically literal one. Therefore write two columns: what the running passage does with quantities, and what a modern equation makes possible. \(x^2+10x=39\) is a useful M bridge. It also permits signed values and operations not established merely by a positive-magnitude procedure.

The method halves 10, squares 5, adds 25 to 39, extracts the positive square root of 64, and subtracts 5. Rosen's inspected passage finishes with numerical root 3 and sought square 9. Karpinski's inspected English page reaches 64; completing its calculation to 3 is our reconstruction, because the continuation was not inspected. A modern factorisation \((x-3)(x+13)=0\) also finds \(-13\). Calling the passage “wrong because it forgot \(-13\)” would impose the modern domain without evidence that it was the passage's problem. Calling it identical to modern signed algebra would erase the change of domain. The contrast concerns the question being answered.

**H1 checkpoint, history-core-h1-v1.** Produce a three-row audit: one observable word change; one unchanged operation; one mathematical change introduced by signed notation. Then repair “Karpinski proves what every Arabic reader understood.”

**H1 key.** A valid audit pairs dirhems/units and identifies the Latin drachmis as a third witness term without equating all meanings. Both English passages halve 10 and add its half-square, 25, to 39. Signed algebra supplies the second real root \(-13\), which does not satisfy a positive-length requirement. Repair the last claim to “Karpinski's 1915 English version presents this operation in this way; its relation to Arabic readers requires language competence, manuscript evidence, and reception evidence.” See the complete [E4 model source card](#section-178-e4-model-a-complete-source-card-and-its-audit) and [primary portfolio](#section-224-primary-dossier-two-translated-quadratic-witnesses).

## H2 — From numerical procedures to equations and area arguments

Read Chace's free translation of Rhind Problems 24 and 79, then Rosen's numerical and area passages. The object being manipulated changes: a trial quantity scaled to meet a target, a list of repeated sevens with a checked total, and a square accompanied by rectangular strips. All can be expressed with modern symbols, but that shared expression does not establish a common historical category or a transmission chain.

For Problem 24, set a trial quantity 7. Its seventh is 1, so the trial output is 8. Scaling by \(k=19/8=2+1/4+1/8\) scales the output to 19 and the quantity to \(7k=133/8\). Chace's translation includes the check. Our explanation is linearity of \(L(q)=q+q/7\): \(L(7k)=kL(7)\). This makes explicit the invariant that justifies the operation. It is M, not a claim that the manuscript defines a linear operator.

For Problem 79 the five terms are 7,49,343,2401,16807. Partial totals are 7,56,399,2800,19607. The alternative multiplication \(7\cdot 2801\) yields the same total because \(2801=1+7+49+343+2401\). In modern notation, for \(r\ne1\),
\[
 S=r+r^2+r^3+r^4+r^5,\qquad (r-1)S=r^6-r.
\]
For \(r=1\), \(S=5\); division by \(r-1\) is unavailable. The agreement of two totals is a useful check. A total that lists different kinds of objects need not be a homogeneous physical measure. Chace's proposed stories and generalisations belong to his commentary unless independently attributed to the older witness.

Now use an area certificate for \(x^2+10x=39\), with \(x>0\). Two strips each \(5x\) can be placed beside a square \(x^2\); a missing \(5\)-by-\(5\) corner completes a square of side \(x+5\). Thus \((x+5)^2=64\) and \(x=3\). Rosen also presents a four-strip arrangement with strip width \(5/2\), whose four missing corners have total area \(4(5/2)^2=25\). The invariant extra area is the same. This explains why the numerical half-and-square rule is geometrically effective for positive magnitudes; it does not turn every algebraic operation into a cut-and-paste argument for negative lengths.

**H2 checkpoint, history-core-h2-v1.** (a) Change the Rhind 24 target from 19 to 23 while retaining the seventh. (b) Change the five-term ratio from 7 to 3. (c) Change Rosen's modern equation to \(x^2+6x=16\), finding the positive-magnitude answer and all real roots.

**H2 key.** (a) \(k=23/8\), \(q=161/8\); \(q/7=23/8\), so their sum is 23. (b) Terms 3,9,27,81,243 sum 363; \(3(1+3+9+27+81)=3\cdot 121=363\). (c) Add 9 to obtain \((x+3)^2=25\); the positive root is 2 and the signed roots are 2 and \(-8\). These changed problems are our modern exercises, not newly found historical examples. Return to each witness to mark which check is actually printed and which certificate we supplied.

## H3 — Geometry, proof, and the changing role of a unit

Complete the required primary work, changed-radius work, and six claim repairs in the existing Euclid unit, using its full keys. Its two English witnesses are Lardner's 1855 eleventh edition, I.1 §§57–58, and Heath's 1908 first edition, I.1. These are edition-specific transcriptions. Their place in a longer history of Euclid is a question for further evidence, not a manuscript genealogy supplied by this course.

In both selected arguments, \(AC=AB\) because the two are radii of one circle, and \(BC=BA\) because they are radii of the other. Since \(BA=AB\), equality to the same segment gives \(AC=BC\). Provided the common point C exists off AB, all three sides are equal. Lardner explicitly discusses circle intersection in §58, and defers the proof that there are at most two intersections. Heath's selected proposition takes a common C without a parallel extended paragraph there. This is a difference in local presentation. It does not establish that either editor copied the other or that one taught better.

A modern coordinate supplement separates existence from equal-side reasoning. With centres \((0,0),(d,0)\), equal radius r, and \(d,r>0\), subtracting
\[
 x^2+y^2=r^2,\qquad (x-d)^2+y^2=r^2
\]
gives \(x=d/2\) and \(y^2=r^2-d^2/4\). The circles intersect exactly when \(r\ge d/2\), and make a nondegenerate triangle exactly when \(r>d/2\). Setting \(r=d\) supplies the equilateral construction. Setting \(d=6,r=4\) gives \((3,\pm\sqrt{7})\) and sides 4,4,6. This is our modern existence check, not Euclid's argument.

Compare Descartes's 1637 Book I. His opening passage chooses a unit line so that multiplication and powers can be represented by lines; a modern dimensional interpretation must restore the implicit unit. On p. 303 the positive construction for \(y^2=-ay+b^2\) gives \(y=-a/2+\sqrt{a^2/4+b^2}\). Our substitution \(a=10,b=\sqrt{39}\) returns 3; that numerical substitution is ours. Here the changed problem is the systematic translation between a symbolic relation and a constructed line, while a geometric certificate still matters. A shared numerical result with Rosen does not establish direct dependence.

**H3 checkpoint, history-core-h3-v1.** Explain why (a) radii 4 over base 6 do not reproduce the equilateral proof; (b) radius 3 gives no nondegenerate triangle; (c) a chosen unit matters when \(x^2\) names a line.

**H3 key.** (a) The radius equalities give AC=BC=4, while AB=6; the step equating each radius with the base fails. (b) \(y^2=9-9=0\), so the common point is \((3,0)\) and the three points are collinear. Smaller positive radii are disjoint. (c) Multiplying lengths \(a,b\) relative to a unit \(u>0\) produces a line of length \(ab/u\), not a physical area. Setting the numerical unit to 1 suppresses this distinction; restoring it shows what is represented. The modern supplement and the historical unit convention answer different explanatory questions.

## H4 — Calculus as a problem of motion and justification

Read N1, B1, and C1 in the source register in that order, without treating their dates as a proof of a direct causal chain. Newton's introduction appeared in Latin in 1704; the selected English is Harris's 1710 translation, in Wilkins's corrected electronic transcription. Newton describes quantities generated by motion, then compares increments through prime or ultimate ratios. His power calculation is one part of an argument about fluxions, not a present-day epsilon proof hidden in old spelling.

Berkeley's 1734 §§XIII–XVI challenge the reasoning that introduces a nonzero increment, divides by it, and then suppresses increment terms. Read the challenge as an argument about the justification of a procedure. Its religious and polemical setting is visible in the work's title and opening; that does not settle whether every criticism succeeds. A correct derivative is not by itself an answer to a complaint about the logic of its derivation.

Make the distinction with a modern cube example. For every real fixed x and every \(h\ne0\),
\[
 Q(x,h)=\frac{(x+h)^3-x^3}{h}=3x^2+3xh+h^2.
\]
We have not set h to zero. For \(0<|h|<1\),
\[
 |Q(x,h)-3x^2|\le 3|x||h|+|h|^2\le(3|x|+1)|h|.
\]
Given \(\varepsilon>0\), choose
\(\delta=\min(1,\varepsilon/(3|x|+1))\).
Then \(0<|h|<\delta\) makes the error smaller than epsilon. The limit equals \(3x^2\) although a nonzero-increment quotient usually differs from it. This is a complete modern answer to this example's limiting problem; it is not a transcription of Newton or a verdict on all of Berkeley's argument.

Cauchy's 1821 preliminary definition describes successive variable values approaching a fixed value with arbitrarily small difference, and an infinitesimal as a variable tending to zero. We consult a **1897 collected-works reprint**, not an 1882 edition inferred from a filename. The passage changes what must be discussed about variable quantities. It does not entitle us to identify every Cauchy statement with modern quantified epsilon-delta analysis. The history concerns changing objects and warrants, not the eventual triumph of one vocabulary.

**H4 checkpoint, history-core-h4-v1.** At \(x=2\), supply a valid delta for error below \(1/10\); state the nonzero-h quotient. Repair “Berkeley's objection proves the derivative is wrong” and “Cauchy wrote our delta formula in 1821.”

**H4 key.** \(Q(2,h)=12+6h+h^2\). A valid delta is \(1/70\), since \(3|2|+1=7\) and \(7|h|<1/10\). The limiting value is 12. Berkeley contests a warrant; the result and the validity of a particular argument are separate. Our delta is M; Cauchy's selected verbal definition is W through the stated later witness. No direct Berkeley-to-Cauchy causal explanation has been established by these three passages.

## H5 — From the price of a chance to repeated-trial inference

Huygens's 1657 Latin work is read here through Browne's 1714 English translation and a Leiden electronic reprint retaining original pagination. Proposition IV, pp. 7–8, concerns division of a stake when a first-to-three game stops with A leading two games to one. Equal chances in future rounds and a value determined by possible future outcomes are hypotheses of the model. Counting past wins as a fraction of the stake answers a different question.

Let the total stake be S. A needs one win and B needs two. If A wins the next round, A receives S. If B wins it, the remaining position is level and A's value is \(S/2\). Averaging the equally likely alternatives gives
\[
 V(1,2)=\tfrac{1}{2}S+\tfrac{1}{2}(\tfrac{1}{2}S)=3S/4.
\]
Equivalently extend play to two imagined rounds: AA,AB,BA win for A; BB wins for B. This is an exact finite argument under its assumptions. Fairness is a stated model, not an empirical finding about every game.

De Moivre's 1756 third edition includes repeated-trial approximation and discusses the movement from observed proportions toward probabilities. His example with 3600 fair trials gives an approximate probability 0.682688 for the inclusive band from 1770 to 1830, 30 either side of 1800. Treat the printed decimal as an approximation, not an exact finite count. For a modern independent Bernoulli model with constant \(p=1/2\), \(X\) has mean 1800 and variance 900. A precise modern inclusive-band probability is
\[
 P(1770\le X\le 1830)=2^{-3600}\sum_{k=1770}^{1830}\binom{3600}{k}.
\]
Endpoint conventions and an approximation's error matter. No equality between that finite sum and the printed decimal is asserted.

This is a change of problem as well as scale: the game values a known hypothetical chance, while repeated-trial discussion connects a probability model, frequencies, and uncertainty. Neither passage licenses an inference from any convenient real data set without assumptions. A history of statistics must ask what observations were available, what homogeneity was assumed, and whose decisions a calculation served. The selected pages teach the model/inference distinction; they do not establish a full history of statistical institutions.

**H5 checkpoint, history-core-h5-v1.** (a) In the same stopped game with stake 80, give each player's share. (b) Under the explicitly changed model \(p(A)=2/3\), independent future rounds, derive A's share. (c) Separate the exact and approximate claims in the 3600-trial example.

**H5 key.** (a) A receives 60 and B 20. (b) A wins next with probability \(2/3\), or loses next then wins with probability \((1/3)(2/3)\); total \(8/9\), giving \(640/9\) and \(80/9\). This changes Huygens's equal-chance hypothesis; it is our extension. (c) The binomial sum is exact under the stated modern model; 1800 and 900 follow exactly from it; 0.682688 is a historical approximation, not a measured frequency or exact rational probability. See the [return work](#section-324-return-dossier-value-frequency-and-evidence).

## H6 — Tables, machines, labour, and two meanings of a register

Finish the existing global course's Sections 6–10 before drawing a network. Those sections keep the selected printed European materials within a wider history of mathematical activity, institutions, exclusion, and surviving evidence. Complete its Exercises 1–3 with their keys. Its source-card and network tasks now have the fixed E4 and E5 models below. Do not infer that the convenient availability of these digitised books measures the importance of unrepresented communities.

Compare the squares calculation in Menabrea's memoir, translated by Lovelace in 1843, pp. 672–674, with Babbage's 1846 fourth edition, Chapter XX. In the memoir a square 9 accompanies first difference 5 and constant second difference 2; update the difference before adding it to the square. In Babbage's forward convention the square 1 accompanies next difference 3; add that difference to the square before increasing it. Different register meanings explain the different instruction orders.

Here is our formal reconstruction. In the **backward-difference convention**, after completed step n,
\[
 S_n=n^2,\quad D_n=n^2-(n-1)^2=2n-1,\quad C=2.
\]
Set \(D\leftarrow D+2\), then \(S\leftarrow S+D\), then increment n. The invariant follows because the new D is \(2n+1\) and the new S is \(n^2+2n+1=(n+1)^2\).

| completed n | S | D | C |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 3 | 9 | 5 | 2 |
| 4 | 16 | 7 | 2 |
| 5 | 25 | 9 | 2 |
| 6 | 36 | 11 | 2 |
| 7 | 49 | 13 | 2 |

In the **forward-difference convention**, \(A_n=n^2\), \(B_n=(n+1)^2-n^2=2n+1\), C=2. Set \(A\leftarrow A+B\), then \(B\leftarrow B+2\), then increment n.

| completed n | A | B | C |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 1 | 3 | 2 |
| 2 | 4 | 5 | 2 |
| 3 | 9 | 7 | 2 |
| 4 | 16 | 9 | 2 |
| 5 | 25 | 11 | 2 |

A hybrid is wrong: applying A-first instructions to initial 9,5 gives 14 rather than 16; applying D-first instructions to initial 1,3 gives 6 rather than 4. A correct algorithm needs meanings as well as numbers. The selected Babbage transcript also has a prose continuation containing 6 where the square differences require 7 and its table has 7. Record an unresolved transcription-or-print discrepancy; we have not collated the print to assign responsibility.

Babbage's Chapter XX connects numerical tables, division of labour, and machinery. Menabrea's memoir distinguishes a difference calculation from a more general proposed Analytical Engine; Lovelace's added Notes are a separate voice. Do not treat the squares algorithm as evidence that the full proposed machine was built or as a complete account of programming. The later 1864 autobiography supplies participant testimony about communications and translation, with the interests and distance in time of retrospective self-report.

**H6 checkpoint, history-core-h6-v1.** Explain each register convention to a reader without symbols, then repair a hybrid and an unsupported network claim.

**H6 key.** Backward D records the addition that produced the current square, so first increase it to obtain the next addition. Forward B already records the next addition, so use it before increasing it. To repair 9,5 with A-first instructions, either change the initial next difference to 7 or change to D-first instructions; show the corrected first result 16. A line from Babbage to an institution on the basis of reported possession records a holding, not proof that he donated it or that every institutional member knew its mathematics. Use the complete [E5 network model](#section-186-e5-model-an-eight-node-network-with-traceable-edges).

**E5 interpretation model.** This network separates a memoir, a translation with Notes, correspondence, academies, and the custody of an object. The same mathematical procedure can circulate through several media without every participant occupying the same role. Two source types are involved: contemporary printed editorial and memoir material in 1843, and an 1864 autobiography. Agreement between their claims would deserve checking for dependence; it would not automatically provide independent corroboration. The graph shows a reported communication structure within a bounded printed archive. To study who was excluded, how readers used the material, or which drafts supplied particular examples, we would need further correspondence, institutional records, and drafts.

**R8. Model revision (300 words).**

The decimal belongs to an approximation argument, while the sum belongs to my stated modern model. They need not be identical. In the model, each sequence of fair independent trials has a known probability, and the binomial coefficient counts sequences with a specified number of successes. Adding the relevant terms defines the inclusive band probability exactly.

The expectation and variance describe that model as well. They explain why eighteen hundred is the centre and thirty is the standard deviation for thirty six hundred trials. They do not guarantee that every observed count lies in the selected band. Variation outside it is possible even when the assumptions hold.

The stopped game asks a different question. Its value depends on future chances after interruption. Three of four equally likely imagined continuations give the stake to the leading player, so that player's share is three quarters. The share is not the fraction of games already won. This enumeration should not be confused with an approximation for thousands of trials.

A real application needs further evidence. I would ask how success was defined, whether the probability stayed constant, whether trials influenced one another, and how observations were selected or recorded. A mathematically correct calculation cannot supply those facts by itself. Historical purposes and audiences also require evidence beyond the numerical example.

The consulted witnesses have their own layers. Huygens reaches me through a later English translation and electronic reprint. De Moivre's selected edition incorporates earlier work. I should retain those dates and locators rather than flatten them into a single moment of discovery.

My revised claim distinguishes model, exact calculation, approximation, and observation. It uses the historical passages to formulate questions about valuation and variation. It leaves empirical adequacy and precise transmission routes open until evidence bears on them. That is a defensible comparison.

## Feedback, scoring, and revision

Use the original global project's weighted rubric for **each** P/C/R portfolio: **30% mathematical accuracy; 25% source traceability and dating; 20% historical context; 15% comparison without ranking; 10% accessibility and revision**. Score each category from 0 to 4, then multiply its weight by score/4. A 4 means complete, correct, and precisely limited; 3 means a local omission that leaves the argument sound; 2 means a substantial missing distinction; 1 means a mostly unsupported attempt; 0 means absent or incompatible with the fixed task. The total is a feedback aid out of 100, not a university grade or a claim of assessment validity.

For mathematics, a 4 requires the domain or state definition, all requested outputs, and a certificate; a correct final number alone earns at most 2. For traceability, a 4 requires both cards, exact locators, date layers, and named interventions. For context, a 4 explains a relevant setting and distinguishes an author's avowal from demonstrated use. For comparison, a 4 supplies all five axes and specific evidence needed for transmission. For accessibility/revision, a 4 supplies an equivalent text/table and a substantive 300-word claim repair. Unsupported resemblance-to-priority or resemblance-to-direct-transmission claims cannot pass: mark “repair required” irrespective of the arithmetic total.

Work through a feedback cycle: attempt; check mathematics against the appropriate full model; check every W/T claim against its linked page; mark an omission or error by category; repair the calculation or claim; explain why the repair changes the conclusion. For a failed invariant, return to the definition of the stored difference. For an unsupported genealogy, return to the date layers and distinguish a possible route from an evidenced route. For an unlabelled signed root, return to the problem's admissible objects. Reattempt the corresponding checkpoint with its changed numbers before revising the portfolio.

Millar, 1756: [digitised volume](https://archive.org/details/doctrineofchance00moiv), pp. 242–243 and 245–246, Lemma and Corollaries 3–4, including earlier approximation work. The selected edition names inclusive endpoints and gives an approximate band probability for 3600 fair trials; our explicit inclusive finite-binomial expression is modern. The access route is the digitised volume, not a claim that OCR alone is a definitive mathematical edition.
