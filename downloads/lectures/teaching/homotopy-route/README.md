# Introductory homotopy teaching and assessment route

Version **1.0.0**. Use the task handouts and instructor arguments with the [first homotopy lecture](../../../../apps/lectures/homotopy.html) to teach checking, constructing and evaluating continuous deformations. The pack supplies three tasks before teaching, three immediately afterwards and three after a delay. The optional stretch is separate. These are original formative instruments: the forms have not been equated for difficulty, and there is no phase total, passing score or mastery threshold.

Every form includes the **same reference card**. Learners may consult it throughout. Responses therefore show application of supplied definitions and facts, rather than unaided recall of a theorem. Mathematical arguments still require human reading; no automatic proof grade is produced.

## Quick use

1. Give the [baseline handout](../../../../apps/lectures/teaching/homotopy-route/baseline.html) before teaching. Let the learner use its reference card, write an attempt and request help when wanted.
2. Teach the [listed core sections and five practice exercises](#preparation-and-route-boundaries), leaving the optional continuity clinic closed.
3. Give the [immediate handout](../../../../apps/lectures/teaching/homotopy-route/immediate.html) after practice. Return to the [delayed handout](../../../../apps/lectures/teaching/homotopy-route/delayed.html) on another visit; approximately seven days is a proposed schedule, not a requirement for useful discussion.
4. Read the [task-specific instructor arguments](../../../../apps/lectures/teaching/homotopy-route/instructor.html) alongside the learner's work. Discuss which boundary, continuity or target conditions justify each conclusion, and accept other correct methods.

The handouts and keys can be used directly. The **35-field observation CSV is optional**; no sheet, account or tracking procedure is needed to teach the route. Detailed administration and response-history notes follow the teaching route for anyone who wants to document attempts across visits.

## Preparation and route boundaries

The route assumes continuous functions and composition, vector arithmetic and lengths in the plane, sine and cosine circle parametrizations, and the ability to justify an inequality for every allowed input. Before assigning it, invite a learner to explain one endpoint evaluation, one vector norm and why a denominator is nonzero. Arrange any needed repair and record the extra teaching. These observations do not establish every prerequisite or assign an ability label.

Use the lecture in this order:

1. [The opening question](../../../../apps/lectures/homotopy.html#start): write a prediction about removing the origin.
2. [Moving paths](../../../../apps/lectures/homotopy.html#paths): distinguish traveler position from deformation time and justify interpolation in the stated target.
3. [The definition](../../../../apps/lectures/homotopy.html#definition): check the whole product, the initial and final maps, and the fixed endpoints. Leave the **optional joint continuity clinic closed**; its shrinking peak and two clinic tasks are outside this route.
4. [The failed contraction](../../../../apps/lectures/homotopy.html#hole): give the exact obstruction and distinguish rejection of one formula from rejection of all formulas.
5. [The safe radial deformation](../../../../apps/lectures/homotopy.html#retraction): justify positivity, the two time boundaries and the fixed circle.
6. [Comparing spaces](../../../../apps/lectures/homotopy.html#equivalence): identify the maps and their composites, and separate homotopy equivalence from homeomorphism.
7. [Winding](../../../../apps/lectures/homotopy.html#winding): use the stated lifting ingredient and keep the one-puncture classification within its declared space.
8. Attempt lecture [Exercises 1, 3, 4, 5 and 7](../../../../apps/lectures/homotopy.html#exercises), each before its hint or worked solution. They concern reversal, an invalid target, signed winding, an annular retraction and the missing continuity step in an invariance argument. Discuss the reason for each repaired step.

Complete the selected practice before the immediate form. If teaching spans two visits, record the actual end of the last practice session as the learning finish. Leave the other lecture exercises, the clinic, the later homotopy lectures and proof pathways outside the assigned route. Any additional reading or practice remains useful learning; record it as extra exposure rather than silently treating the route as unchanged.

A planning allowance is **90–120 minutes** for the reading, discussion and five practice attempts, with additional time or another visit where needed. The lecture's own 75–90 minute estimate leaves selected exercises for outside class. Neither estimate is an observed completion time. Allow approximately **20–30 minutes per three-task form**, without a speed score; record actual time and pauses. The optional stretch may need another **15–25 minutes**. Adjustments should serve the learner and remain visible in the record.

## Discuss the task arguments

Read each response against that task's instructor criteria and accept alternative correct methods. Use **missing**, **partial** or **clear** only for the stated evidence, with a quoted step or a precise description of the gap. The rubric word *missing* concerns an argument in work that was actually attempted. It must not turn an unobserved follow-up into an incorrect proof.

Keep the task's mathematical conclusion separate from the justification. A correct formula with no target or boundary argument can leave material obligations unanswered; a numerical drawing cannot replace a universal argument. Support, time and confidence, if discussed, provide context rather than proof quality. If using a second scorer, ask them to read a sample using the same task criteria, retain both judgments and describe any disagreement and resolution. No scorer agreement or learner result is supplied by these blank materials.

Compare item-level arguments and the obligations they address. The baseline, immediate and delayed forms deliberately vary construction demands and transfer situations; differences between their totals would confound those demands with learning. Do not sum the anchors, convert them into pass labels, or infer a causal effect from this route. Use difficulties to select the next explanation or problem.

## Administration details for repeated attempts

The following administration, exposure and response-history guidance supports optional documentation across visits. Teachers using the problems for ordinary practice can proceed directly from the handouts to their instructor arguments.

Use scratch paper or an accessible blank workspace. The printed reference card and the facts supplied inside each task are available in every phase. Screen readers, magnification, ordinary language clarification that preserves the mathematics, oral responses and a scribe are permitted; record accommodations without classifying them as mathematical hints. For an initial independent attempt, put aside the lesson, worked solutions, search, CAS and AI assistance. If the learner chooses any of these supports, preserve the attempt and record the support instead of discarding useful work.

1. **Baseline:** offer only the baseline handout before the assigned lesson. Explain that uncertainty, an incomplete argument and a decision to stop are acceptable. Do not reveal another phase or an answer key while collecting this attempt.
2. **Learning:** follow the route above. Let learners request hints and explanations. Preserve their initial practice attempts where they wish to keep them; practice help is part of instruction.
3. **Immediate:** offer the immediate handout after the last selected practice. Record the actual interval since learning and any intervening activity.
4. **Delayed:** arrange a target of approximately **seven days after learning**, then record the actual elapsed hours, scheduling changes, intervening study and prior task exposure. Seven days is a scheduling choice, not an optimal delay established for topology. A late return is still useful evidence with its actual delay.

At any point a learner can request mathematical help. Preserve the original pre-help response or an agreed snapshot of unfinished work before giving the help. Save a supported response as a **new response version** linked to the earlier response, and keep later revisions separate as well. A solution reveal cannot retrospectively change the assistance attached to an earlier submission. If help was already present and no earlier response exists, record that fact; do not invent an independent attempt.

After collecting a form, offer feedback using its instructor arguments. State when answers were discussed, because that feedback becomes intervening exposure for later phases. Learners need not postpone useful feedback to preserve a measurement. A teacher choosing to postpone it should agree that plan with the learner and record the timing.

The optional **C-S** stretch concerns the strict hypograph below a parabola and a supplied change of coordinates. Offer it separately after the three delayed core tasks, or on a later visit. It is a further construction challenge, not a tenth required core task. Record its assignment and support independently; it does not alter interpretation of the three-task form.

## Record exposure before interpreting a response

Ask about earlier topology study and previous encounters with these tasks or their arguments. Record a report or uncertainty; absence of a reported encounter is not proof of novelty. A task seen earlier cannot supply unseen-task evidence on a later encounter.

Several related families are already public. The [pedagogy note](../../research/pedagogy-and-homotopy.md#exercises) supplies solved exercises on translated or changed-radius retractions, moving endpoints in an angle interpolation, retraction in three dimensions and finite-sampling claims. The [neighboring loop homotopy lesson](../../../unhurried/content/articles/ga-top-loop-homotopy.html) includes convex contraction, retracing, a basepoint trap and the distinction between a failed formula and impossibility. Log these encounters, other lecture exercises, answer keys and additional help. The pack changes particular givens and construction demands; familiar loop families should not be advertised as wholly unseen mathematics.

## Optional observation sheet

The **35-field CSV is optional** and contains **only a header**. It creates no records, uploads nothing and connects to no account or grading system. If wanted, make a local copy and use a pseudonymous `learner_code`; keep names, contact details and any identity linkage out of this sheet. A pseudonym and timestamps can still identify someone in context. Agree what to retain, who can read it and when the local copy and any backups will be deleted. Do not add observations without the learner's agreement.

Use one row per **item and response version**. Give each recorded row a unique `record_id`. A submitted response has a `response_id` and starts at `response_version` 1; a revision gets a new response ID, increments the version and names its predecessor in `previous_response_id`. Preserve the earlier row. `response_stage` distinguishes `initial`, `supported_revision` and `independent_revision`; later independent revision does not erase earlier assistance or exposure.

| Fields | What to enter |
| --- | --- |
| `pack_version`, `session_code`, `learner_code`, `phase`, `task_id`, `task_version`, `task_source_sha256` | The actual edition and task, a local session code and pseudonym. Use `baseline`, `immediate`, `delayed` or `stretch` as the phase; copy the task identifier printed on the handout. |
| `record_id`, `response_id`, `response_version`, `previous_response_id`, `response_stage`, `response_status` | Distinguish the row, the response and its history. Status choices are explained below. |
| `learning_started_at`, `learning_finished_at`, `phase_started_at`, `response_submitted_at`, `actual_delay_hours`, `timing_notes` | Actual times with timezone where recorded, elapsed hours from learning finish to phase start, and pauses or deviations. Do not substitute the planned day for elapsed time. |
| `assistance_status`, `help_events`, `prior_exposure_status`, `exposure_notes`, `accommodation_notes` | Use `independent`, `supported` or `unknown` for mathematical assistance at submission. List help and reveal events in order. Exposure status is `none_reported`, `reported` or `unknown`; describe what was seen and when. Record accessibility support separately. |
| `response_path`, `response_text` | A locally controlled copy of the original work, its transcription, or both. A path alone is useful only while that file is retained. Use CSV quoting for commas and line breaks; retain the actual mathematical notation. |
| `scorer_code`, `rubric_version`, `criterion_anchors`, `reasoning_evidence`, `alternative_valid_method` | The scorer's local code, the rubric edition and task-specific descriptions. For example, list the relevant criterion names with their descriptive anchors, then explain the evidence; do not insert a numerical total. |
| `second_scorer_code`, `second_criterion_anchors`, `disagreement_resolution`, `followup_notes` | A second reading where available, both judgments and their resolution, or the actual reason that no response was collected. Leave unknown facts blank. |

Use `response_status=submitted` for work received, including an incomplete attempt. Use `submitted_blank` only when an administered item was returned without an answer; do not infer a reason. Retain that blank submission's response ID and version, but leave its reasoning anchors empty. Use `declined` for an explicit decision not to attempt, `interrupted` when an administered attempt ended without a submission, `followup_missing` when the delayed session did not occur or no work was received, and `not_assigned` when the optional stretch or another item was not offered. For a row with no actual response, leave `response_id`, its predecessor and `response_stage` empty, set version to 0, and leave rubric judgments empty. Record the circumstances in `followup_notes`.

If documenting a wholly missing delayed form, enter a `followup_missing` row for each of its three expected core tasks. A row absent from the sheet means **no recorded observation**; it is neither a blank attempted answer nor evidence of failure. A header-only file means that no observations have been entered. Report missing follow-ups and declined or supported attempts explicitly rather than manufacturing zero scores.

## Evidence behind the design

[Mejía-Ramos, Lew, de la Torre and Weber (2017), *Developing and validating proof comprehension tests in undergraduate mathematics*](https://www.math.rutgers.edu/~jpmejia/files/Mejia_TUES_method.pdf), distinguish local justification from understanding a proof's structure and transferring its ideas. Their development combined expert review, interviews and larger administrations for **three specific proofs**. It did not establish a general proof-mastery measure. This pack borrows the distinction between obligations; it has not inherited those tests' validation.

[Hodds, Alcock and Inglis (2014), *Self-Explanation Training Improves Proof Comprehension*](https://mjinglis.github.io/files/JRME_SelfExpl_Paper.pdf), report three experiments with mathematics undergraduates at Loughborough University. The classroom experiment analyzed 107 learners present at both sessions, 20 days apart, and found a benefit on the particular proof-comprehension tasks. Asking why a condition permits a step is a design application here; the study does not establish effects for this homotopy lesson or an optimal return interval.

[Butler (2010), *Repeated testing produces superior transfer of learning relative to repeated studying*](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0019902), studied four experiments with psychology undergraduates using prose materials and a final test one week later. Transfer questions cued the connection to prior study; rephrasing initial questions did not outperform repeating them. Those results motivate returning to an idea in a changed task, without demonstrating spontaneous transfer or topology proof learning. They do not equate these three forms, establish an optimal delay or predict an improvement from this pack. A delayed attempt here is evidence to inspect, not an efficacy claim.
