# Irrationality of √2

*Difficulty 3 of 5 · [Proof foundations](index.md) · exercise 3 of 5*

Prove that $\sqrt{2}$ is irrational. You may use unique factorization or the fact that an even square has an even root.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

Assume $\sqrt{2} = a/b$ in lowest terms

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

Show both $a$ and $b$ must be even

</details>

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

Assume $\sqrt{2} = a/b$ for positive integers $a,b$ with no common factor. Squaring gives $a^2 = 2b^2$, so $a^2$ is even and hence $a$ is even. Write $a = 2k$. Substitution gives $4k^2 = 2b^2$, so $b^2 = 2k^2$; thus $b$ is also even. Then $a$ and $b$ share the factor $2$, contradicting that the fraction was in lowest terms. Therefore no rational representation exists and $\sqrt{2}$ is irrational.

**Answer.** Assuming a lowest-terms fraction forces both numerator and denominator to be even, a contradiction.

</details>
