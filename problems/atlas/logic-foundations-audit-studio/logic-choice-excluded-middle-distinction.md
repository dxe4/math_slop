# Choice is not excluded middle

*Difficulty 3 of 5 · [Logic and foundations audit studio](index.md) · exercise 11 of 11*

In classical ZF, classify the status of (i) A∨¬A for an arbitrary formula A, (ii) every finite family of nonempty sets has a choice function, (iii) every set can be well ordered, and (iv) every arbitrary family of nonempty sets has a choice function. Explain why moving to an intuitionistic foundation requires restating the comparison.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

Classical ZF already uses classical first-order logic

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

Finite choice can be proved by finite induction

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 3</summary>

The well-ordering theorem is equivalent to full choice over ZF

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 4</summary>

Changing the ambient logic can change relationships among principles

</details>

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

In classical ZF, (i) is an instance of excluded middle supplied by the background classical logic, not the Axiom of Choice. Statement (ii) is provable in ZF by making finitely many choices via induction. Statements (iii) and (iv) are equivalent over ZF to the Axiom of Choice and are not theorems of ZF if the usual relative-consistency results hold; adjoining them gives ZFC. In an intuitionistic set or type theory, excluded middle is no longer automatically available and the exact formulation of choice matters. Some choice principles interact strongly with classical logic, so conclusions proved about classical ZF/ZFC cannot simply be relabelled without naming the new foundation.

**Answer.** Excluded middle belongs to classical logic; finite choice is provable in ZF; well-ordering and arbitrary choice are equivalent forms of AC over ZF; intuitionistic settings must be analyzed separately.

</details>
