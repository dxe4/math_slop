# Roots, multiplicity, and graph behavior

*Difficulty 4 of 5 · [Algebra & Functions Core Practice](index.md) · exercise 11 of 12*

For $p(x)=(x+1)^{3}(x-2)^{2}$, state the roots, their multiplicities, and whether the graph crosses or touches the $x$-axis at each. State the degree and the end behavior.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

A root with odd multiplicity means the sign changes: the graph crosses the axis.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

A root with even multiplicity means the sign does not change: the graph touches and turns.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 3</summary>

The leading term is $x^{5}$.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

The roots are $x=-1$ with multiplicity $3$ and $x=2$ with multiplicity $2$. Because $3$ is odd, the sign of $p(x)$ changes across $x=-1$, so the graph crosses the $x$-axis there. Because $2$ is even, the sign does not change across $x=2$, so the graph touches the $x$-axis and turns around. The degree is $3+2=5$, which is odd. The leading term is $x^{5}$, whose coefficient is positive. Therefore as $x\to\infty$, $p(x)\to\infty$, and as $x\to-\infty$, $p(x)\to-\infty$.

**Answer.** Root $-1$ (multiplicity $3$, crosses); root $2$ (multiplicity $2$, touches); degree $5$; falls left, rises right.

</details>
