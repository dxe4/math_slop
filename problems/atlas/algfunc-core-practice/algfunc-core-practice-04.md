# Diagnose a squaring mistake

*Difficulty 2 of 5 · [Algebra & Functions Core Practice](index.md) · exercise 4 of 12*

A student writes $(x+3)^2=x^2+9$. Find the mistake, explain why it is wrong, and give the correct expansion.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

Write $(x+3)^2$ as $(x+3)(x+3)$ and distribute every term.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

Compare the middle term of your expansion with the student's answer.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

The student applied the incorrect rule $(a+b)^2=a^2+b^2$, omitting the cross term. The correct expansion is $(x+3)^2=(x+3)(x+3)=x^2+3x+3x+9=x^2+6x+9$. The missing $6x$ comes from the two products $x\cdot 3$ and $3\cdot x$. In general, $(a+b)^2=a^2+2ab+b^2$; the middle term is essential because area is not preserved when the rectangle is ignored.

**Answer.** $(x+3)^2=x^2+6x+9$; the student omitted the cross term $6x$.

</details>
