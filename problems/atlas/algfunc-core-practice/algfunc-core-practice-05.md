# Solve an absolute-value inequality

*Difficulty 3 of 5 · [Algebra & Functions Core Practice](index.md) · exercise 5 of 12*

Solve $|2x-5|\le 3$ and express the solution as a closed interval.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

Recall that $|u|\le c$ means $-c\le u\le c$ when $c\ge 0$.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

Isolate $x$ by adding $5$ and dividing by $2$.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

The inequality $|2x-5|\le 3$ is equivalent to $-3\le 2x-5\le 3$. Add $5$ throughout: $2\le 2x\le 8$. Divide by $2$: $1\le x\le 4$. In interval notation the solution is $[1,4]$. Boundary check: at $x=1$, $|2(1)-5|=|-3|=3$; at $x=4$, $|2(4)-5|=|3|=3$; at $x=2.5$, $|0|=0\le 3$. All values inside the interval satisfy the inequality.

**Answer.** $[1,4]$

</details>
