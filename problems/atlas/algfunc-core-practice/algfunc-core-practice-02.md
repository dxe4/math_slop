# Expand and factor back

*Difficulty 2 of 5 · [Algebra & Functions Core Practice](index.md) · exercise 2 of 12*

Expand $(x-5)(x+2)$, then factor the resulting quadratic back into linear factors.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

Use the distributive law twice: $(x-5)(x+2)=x(x+2)-5(x+2)$.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

To factor back, look for two numbers that multiply to the constant term and add to the coefficient of $x$.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

Expanding: $(x-5)(x+2)=x^2+2x-5x-10=x^2-3x-10$. To factor $x^2-3x-10$, we need two numbers that multiply to $-10$ and add to $-3$. Those numbers are $-5$ and $+2$. Therefore $x^2-3x-10=(x-5)(x+2)$, which matches the original expression.

**Answer.** $(x-5)(x+2)$

</details>
