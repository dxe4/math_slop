# Solve a quadratic by factoring

*Difficulty 2 of 5 · [Algebra & Functions Core Practice](index.md) · exercise 3 of 12*

Solve $x^2-x-12=0$ by factoring. State the zero-product principle and verify each root.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

Find two numbers that multiply to $-12$ and add to $-1$.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

Check each candidate in the original equation.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

We need two numbers that multiply to $-12$ and add to $-1$. Those numbers are $-4$ and $+3$. So $x^2-x-12=(x-4)(x+3)$. By the zero-product principle, if a product of real factors equals zero then at least one factor equals zero. Thus $x-4=0$ or $x+3=0$, giving $x=4$ or $x=-3$. Verification: $4^2-4-12=16-16=0$ and $(-3)^2-(-3)-12=9+3-12=0$.

**Answer.** $x=4$ or $x=-3$

</details>
