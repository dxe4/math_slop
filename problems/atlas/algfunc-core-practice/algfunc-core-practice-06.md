# Simplify a rational expression

*Difficulty 3 of 5 · [Algebra & Functions Core Practice](index.md) · exercise 6 of 12*

Simplify $\dfrac{x^2-4}{x-2}$ and state the domain restriction that remains even after simplification.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

Factor the numerator as a difference of squares.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

Cancel the common factor only where it is nonzero.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

Factor the numerator: $x^2-4=(x-2)(x+2)$. For $x\ne 2$, we may cancel the common factor $x-2$ to obtain $x+2$. However, the original expression is undefined at $x=2$ because the denominator is zero. Simplification does not restore excluded inputs, so the simplified form is $x+2$ with the condition $x\ne 2$. The graph is the line $y=x+2$ with a hole at $(2,4)$.

**Answer.** $x+2$ for $x\ne 2$; hole at $(2,4)$.

</details>
