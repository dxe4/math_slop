# Solve a compound inequality

*Difficulty 2 of 5 · [Algebra and functions from first principles](index.md) · exercise 3 of 4*

Solve the compound inequality $-2 \le 3x + 1 < 10$. Express the solution both as an inequality and in interval notation.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

Perform the same operation on all three parts

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

Subtract 1, then divide by the positive number 3

</details>

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

Subtracting $1$ throughout gives $-3 \le 3x < 9$. Dividing every part by $3$ gives $-1 \le x < 3$. Division is by a positive number, so neither inequality sign reverses. The left endpoint is included because of $\le$, while the right endpoint is excluded because of $<$.

**Answer.** $-1 \le x < 3$, or $[-1, 3)$.

</details>
