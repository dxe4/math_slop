# Which sign belongs to the base?

*Difficulty 1 of 5 · [Powers, roots, and scale solved studio](index.md) · exercise 1 of 12*

Evaluate `(-2)^4`, `-2^4`, `(-2)^5`, and `-2^-3`. For each expression, identify the base to which the exponent applies.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

Parentheses make the sign part of the base

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

A negative exponent requests a reciprocal

</details>

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

In `(-2)^4`, the base is `-2`, so four negative factors give `16`. In `-2^4`, the exponent applies only to the base `2`; the leading sign is applied afterward, giving `-(16)=-16`. In `(-2)^5`, the base is `-2` and an odd number of negative factors gives `-32`. In `-2^-3`, exponentiation again applies to the positive base `2` before the leading sign: `-(2^-3)=-(1/2^3)=-1/8`. Parentheses, rather than visual proximity, decide whether the sign belongs to the base.

**Answer.** 16, -16, -32, and -1/8; only the parenthesized expressions use -2 as the base.

</details>
