# Length, area, and volume do not scale alike

*Difficulty 3 of 5 · [Powers, roots, and scale solved studio](index.md) · exercise 8 of 12*

Two cubes have corresponding lengths in the ratio `3:5`. The smaller cube has surface area `54 m^2` and volume `27 m^3`. Find the larger cube's surface area and volume. Explain why using the length factor directly for all three quantities is wrong.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

The length scale factor from smaller to larger is `5/3`

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

Area contains two length factors and volume contains three

</details>

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

The linear scale factor is `s=5/3`. Surface area scales by `s^2=25/9`, so the larger area is `54*(25/9)=6*25=150 m^2`. Volume scales by `s^3=125/27`, so the larger volume is `27*(125/27)=125 m^3`. The given smaller measurements are consistent with a cube of side 3 m: its area is `6*3^2=54 m^2` and its volume is `3^3=27 m^3`. A length is one-dimensional, an area contains two length factors, and a volume contains three. Multiplying every quantity by `5/3` would ignore that dimensional dependence and therefore give the wrong geometry.

**Answer.** The larger surface area is `150 m^2`; the larger volume is `125 m^3`.

</details>
