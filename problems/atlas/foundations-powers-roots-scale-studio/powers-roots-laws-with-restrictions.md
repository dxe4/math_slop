# Simplify and retain the restrictions

*Difficulty 2 of 5 · [Powers, roots, and scale solved studio](index.md) · exercise 2 of 12*

Simplify `(3x^-2 y^3)^2/(9x y^-1)` using positive exponents. State every restriction inherited from the original expression.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

Apply the outer square to every factor in the numerator

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

Subtract exponents when dividing like bases

</details>

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

The negative powers in the original expression require `x != 0` and `y != 0`. Expanding the numerator gives `9x^-4 y^6`. Dividing by `9x y^-1` cancels the coefficient and subtracts exponents: `x^(-4-1)y^(6-(-1))=x^-5 y^7`. Rewriting with positive exponents gives `y^7/x^5`. Although `y` no longer appears in a denominator in the final display, `y != 0` remains an inherited restriction because the original factor `y^-1` was undefined at zero.

**Answer.** `y^7/x^5`, with `x != 0` and `y != 0`.

</details>
