# One radical, two equation solutions

*Difficulty 1 of 5 · [Powers, roots, and scale solved studio](index.md) · exercise 4 of 12*

Evaluate `sqrt((-7)^2)` and solve `(x-4)^2=45`. Explain why the radical in the first task has one value while the equation in the second can have two solutions.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

The principal square root is nonnegative

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

If `u^2=45`, then `u` can have either sign

</details>

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

Because the principal square root is nonnegative, `sqrt((-7)^2)=sqrt(49)=7=|-7|`. For the equation, set `u=x-4`. The equation `u^2=45` has the two real solutions `u=3sqrt(5)` and `u=-3sqrt(5)`. Hence `x=4+3sqrt(5)` or `x=4-3sqrt(5)`. The radical symbol `sqrt(49)` names the single nonnegative square root. Solving `u^2=45` asks for every real input whose square is 45, so both signs must be considered.

**Answer.** `sqrt((-7)^2)=7`; the equation solutions are `x=4 +/- 3sqrt(5)`.

</details>
