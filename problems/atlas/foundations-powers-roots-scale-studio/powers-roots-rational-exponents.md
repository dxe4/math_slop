# Roots, powers, then reciprocals

*Difficulty 2 of 5 · [Powers, roots, and scale solved studio](index.md) · exercise 5 of 12*

Evaluate `64^(2/3)`, `81^(-3/4)`, and `(-32)^(2/5)` as real numbers. State why the last expression has a real interpretation but `(-32)^(1/2)` does not.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

A denominator in a reduced exponent names a root

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

Odd real roots accept negative radicands

</details>

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

First, `64^(2/3)=(cuberoot(64))^2=4^2=16`. Next, `81^(-3/4)=1/(fourthroot(81))^3=1/3^3=1/27`. Finally, `(-32)^(2/5)=(fifthroot(-32))^2=(-2)^2=4`. The reduced denominator 5 names an odd root, so a negative radicand has a unique real fifth root. A square root has even index, and no real number has square `-32`, so `(-32)^(1/2)` is not real.

**Answer.** 16, 1/27, and 4; the odd fifth root is real for a negative base, whereas the even square root is not.

</details>
