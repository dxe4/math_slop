# Reverse a percentage discount

*Difficulty 2 of 5 · [Number sense in everyday quantities](index.md) · exercise 3 of 4*

After a 20% discount, a jacket costs £72. What was its original price? Explain why adding 20% of £72 does not undo the discount.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

The sale price is $80\%$ of the original

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

Solve $0.8p = 72$

</details>

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

Let $p$ be the original price. A $20\%$ discount leaves $80\%$ of it, so $0.8p = 72$ and $p = 72/0.8 = 90$. Adding $20\%$ of £72 gives £86.40, not £90, because that $20\%$ is taken from the smaller sale-price base. The forward and reverse percentage calculations use different bases.

**Answer.** The original price was £90.

</details>
