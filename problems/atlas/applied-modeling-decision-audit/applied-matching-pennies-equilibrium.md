# Equilibrium by making the opponent indifferent

*Difficulty 3 of 5 · [Applied modeling and decision audit studio](index.md) · exercise 8 of 12*

In matching pennies, player 1 receives +1 when choices match and -1 otherwise, while player 2 receives the negative. Derive the mixed Nash equilibrium and state two conclusions that equilibrium existence does not provide.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

Let the opponent choose heads with probability q

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

Compare payoff from heads and tails

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 3</summary>

Indifference determines q

</details>

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

If player 2 uses heads with probability q, player 1's expected payoff from heads is $q-(1-q)=2q-1$, while payoff from tails is $(1-q)-q=1-2q$. Player 1 mixes only when these are equal, giving $q=1/2$. Symmetry requires player 1 also use heads with probability 1/2. Neither player then has a profitable unilateral deviation and value is zero. Existence does not establish that empirical play converges to this profile, nor that an equilibrium is fair or socially desirable in a nonzero-sum application.

**Answer.** Each player mixes heads/tails with probability one-half; this does not guarantee learning convergence, fairness, or welfare.

</details>
