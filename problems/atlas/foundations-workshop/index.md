
# Foundations Workshop: Eight Problems, Hints, and Fully Explained Answers

This workshop is a low-stakes check across number, algebra, shape, chance, and problem solving. Try each problem before opening its answer. A drawing, physical model, or spoken explanation is as welcome as symbols.

## Quick toolkit

- Keep units beside measurements.
- Estimate before exact calculation.
- A fraction names equal-sized parts.
- An equation says two quantities are equal.
- In a right triangle, side names depend on the chosen angle.
- Probability starts by listing possible outcomes and assumptions.

## Problems and hints

### 1. Place value detective

A meter shows $2,040.06$ litres. What value does each nonzero digit represent? Round the reading to the nearest litre.

**Hint:** Expand it as thousands + tens + hundredths.

### 2. Recipe scaling

Six pancakes need 210 ml of milk. You want 15 pancakes with the same recipe. How much milk is needed? Give a second method that checks the first.

**Hint:** Find milk per pancake, or notice that 15 is $2.5\times 6$.

### 3. Successive percentage changes

A £50 item rises in price by 10%, then is discounted by 10%. What is the final price? Explain why the changes do not cancel.

**Hint:** Each percentage uses the price at that moment.

### 4. A mystery number

“When I triple a number and subtract 4, I get 20.” Write an equation, solve it, and check it in the sentence.

**Hint:** Let the number be $x$; undo subtraction before multiplication.

### 5. Compare two rules

A bicycle hire shop offers Plan A for £6 plus £3 per hour and Plan B for £5 per hour. Write both cost rules. At how many hours do they cost the same? Which is cheaper for 2 hours and for 5 hours?

**Hint:** Set the two outputs equal.

### 6. Floor-tile geometry

A rectangular floor is 4.5 m by 3 m. Square tiles have side 0.5 m. Ignoring gaps and cuts, how many tiles cover the floor? Solve using rows and columns, then check using areas.

**Hint:** Count how many tile side lengths fit along each room side.

### 7. Shadow triangle

At the same time on level ground, a 1.2 m stick casts a 0.8 m shadow and a tree casts a 5 m shadow. Assume the stick and tree are vertical and model the sunlight by parallel rays. Estimate the tree’s height. Name one source of measurement uncertainty and one way the geometric assumptions could fail in a real setup.

**Hint:** The height-to-shadow ratio is shared by similar triangles.

### 8. Counter bag

A bag contains 2 red, 2 blue, and 1 green counter. Draw one counter. For parts a–c, each counter remaining in the bag has the same chance of being selected. After replacement, mix again and use a fresh selection independent of the first draw.

a. Find the probability it is not green.
b. The counter is replaced, and a second draw is made. Find the probability of two green draws.
c. Instead, if the first green were not replaced, could two green draws occur?
d. In a different experiment, choose one of the five counters uniformly at the start and show that same counter twice. Each showing has a one-in-five chance of being green. Are the two showings independent, and what is the probability that both are green?

**Hint:** “Not green” is often easiest by counting all other counters. Replacement restores the contents; the sampling rule determines whether the two selections are independent. In part d, ask how many choices are actually made.

## Fully explained answers

### 1. Place value

The 2 represents 2,000 litres, the 4 represents 40 litres, and the 6 represents 6 hundredths of a litre, or 0.06 litres. The exact reading is 2,040.06 L. Since 0.06 is below half a litre, it rounds to **2,040 L**.

Common error: reading the 6 as tenths. The first place after the decimal is tenths; the second is hundredths.

### 2. Recipe

Per pancake, $210\div 6=35 ml$. For 15, $35\times 15=525 ml$.

Check by scale factor: $15\div 6=2.5$, and $210\times 2.5=525 ml$. Both ingredient amount and pancake count scale by the same factor.

For a changed question, continue with [What does this quotient measure?](../../../apps/atlas/index.html#/labs?lab=visual-lab-quantity-division) and its [live quantity workshop](../../../apps/atlas/index.html#/labs?lab=visual-lab-quantity-division). Decide whether division finds a number of portions or an amount of liquid, then distinguish complete portions from a fractional portion and compare once-only loss with loss on every portion. The six worked tasks include constructing every serving size that fits a specified whole-number count.

### 3. Percent changes

Ten percent of £50 is £5, so the rise gives £55. The discount is 10% of £55, which is £5.50. Final price: **£49.50**.

The second percentage has a larger reference amount. In multiplier form, $50\times 1.10\times 0.90 = 50\times 0.99$.

### 4. Equation

The equation is $3x-4=20$. Add 4 to both sides: $3x=24$. Divide by 3: $x=8$. Check the original words: triple 8 is 24; subtracting 4 gives 20.

### 5. Rules

For $h$ hours, $A(h)=6+3h$ and $B(h)=5h$. Equal cost means $6+3h=5h$, so $6=2h$ and $h=3$. Both then cost £15.

At 2 hours, A costs £12 and B costs £10, so B is cheaper. At 5 hours, A costs £21 and B £25, so A is cheaper. The crossing point separates the two decisions.

### 6. Tiles

Along the 4.5 m side fit $4.5\div 0.5=9$ tiles. Along the 3 m side fit $3\div 0.5=6$. Thus $9\times 6=54$ tiles.

Area check: floor area is $4.5\times 3=13.5 m^{2}$; one tile area is $0.5\times 0.5=0.25 m^{2}$; $13.5\div 0.25=54$. Do not divide by the tile’s side length when using area.

### 7. Shadows

The shared ratio is $1.2/0.8=1.5$. Tree height is $1.5\times 5=7.5 m$.

A fuzzy shadow endpoint or an imprecise ruler reading introduces measurement uncertainty. A tilted stick, a leaning tree, or sloping ground changes the geometric setup and can invalidate the shared-ratio model. A sensible report is “about 7.5 m under the stated assumptions”; more decimal places do not repair a failed assumption.

### 8. Probability

a. Four of five counters are not green, so the probability is $4/5$.

b. With replacement and the stated independent sampling rule, each green chance is $1/5$: $1/5\times 1/5=1/25$.

c. No. Without replacement, drawing the only green first leaves zero green counters. The probability of green then green is $1/5\times 0/4=0$.

d. The showings are dependent: after seeing green once, the second showing is certainly green. Both are green exactly when the single initial choice is green, so the probability is $1/5$. The individual green chances match part b, but the joint chance differs. Restoring the same bag contents alone does not establish independence.

## Reflection and next move

Mark each problem green (secure), amber (needed a hint), or blue (new idea worth revisiting)—avoid “bad at maths” labels. For one amber or blue problem:

1. redo it with different small numbers;
2. draw or act out the relationship;
3. explain one common wrong turn;
4. write your own nearby problem and solve it.

Learning is visible when you can transfer a method, not when you answer quickly.
