# Read a derivative with units

*Difficulty 1 of 5 · [Change and accumulation](index.md) · exercise 1 of 5*

A tank contains $V(t)$ liters after $t$ minutes, and $V'(6) = -2.4$. Explain this statement in context, including units. What does it not claim?

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

A derivative is an instantaneous rate

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

Do not turn a local rate into a global prediction

</details>

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

At six minutes, the tank's volume is decreasing at an instantaneous rate of $2.4$ liters per minute. Equivalently, for very small time increments $h$, $V(6+h)$ is approximately $V(6)-2.4h$. It does not say the tank loses exactly $2.4$ liters during every minute, nor that the rate stays constant away from $t=6$.

**Answer.** At $t=6$, volume decreases at $2.4$ L/min; it is a local rate, not a claim of constant change.

</details>
