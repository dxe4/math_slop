# Proper time from an invariant interval

*Difficulty 3 of 5 · [Mathematical physics audit studio](index.md) · exercise 3 of 10*

In an inertial frame, two timelike-separated events differ by $\Delta t=5$ microseconds and $\Delta x=900$ metres, with no y or z separation. Using $c=3\times 10^8$ m/s and signature (-,+,+,+), find their invariant interval and the proper time of an inertial clock joining them. Identify a frame-dependent and a frame-independent quantity.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

Δx/c equals 3 microseconds

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

For timelike separation, $\Delta \tau^2=\Delta t^2-\Delta x^2/c^2$

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 3</summary>

The coordinate differences change under a boost but the interval does not

</details>

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

The spatial light-travel time is $\Delta x/c=900/(3\times10^8)\,\mathrm{s}=3\,\mu\mathrm{s}$. Thus $\Delta\tau=\sqrt{(5\,\mu\mathrm{s})^2-(3\,\mu\mathrm{s})^2}=4\,\mu\mathrm{s}$. The interval is $\Delta s^2=-c^2\Delta\tau^2=-(1200\,\mathrm{m})^2=-1.44\times10^6\,\mathrm{m}^2$. Coordinate time 5 microseconds and coordinate distance 900 m depend on the inertial frame. The interval and the proper time along the unique straight timelike worldline between these events are Lorentz invariant. An accelerated path between the same events would generally accumulate less proper time and must be integrated along that path.

**Answer.** $\Delta\tau=4\,\mu\mathrm{s}$ and $\Delta s^2=-1.44\times10^6\,\mathrm{m}^2$; coordinate separations are frame-dependent, while interval and straight-worldline proper time are invariant.

</details>
