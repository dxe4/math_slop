# Static gravitational redshift with hypotheses

*Difficulty 4 of 5 · [Mathematical physics audit studio](index.md) · exercise 4 of 10*

In a static spacetime with signature (-,+,+,+), two observers follow the static time coordinate. At emission $g_0_0=-0.64$ and at reception $g_0_0=-1$. A photon follows the stationary geometry between them. Find received frequency divided by emitted frequency and state the geometric assumption that supplies the conserved quantity.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

A static observer has proper time $d\tau=\sqrt{-g_0_0}dt$

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

For a null geodesic in a stationary spacetime, contraction with the timelike Killing field is conserved

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 3</summary>

Frequency measured by a static observer scales as $1/\sqrt{-g_0_0}$ times that conserved energy

</details>

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

Stationarity gives a timelike Killing field and conserves the photon's covariant time momentum along its geodesic. A static observer normalizes that coordinate direction by $1/\sqrt{-g_{00}}$, so measured frequency is proportional to the conserved quantity divided by $\sqrt{-g_{00}}$. Therefore $\nu_{\mathrm{rec}}/\nu_{\mathrm{emit}}=\sqrt{(-g_{00})_{\mathrm{emit}}}/\sqrt{(-g_{00})_{\mathrm{rec}}}=\sqrt{0.64}/\sqrt{1}=0.8$. The received light is redshifted. This calculation requires a static or at least stationary symmetry and observers aligned with its timelike flow; it is not a universal formula for an arbitrary dynamical spacetime.

**Answer.** $\nu_{\mathrm{rec}}/\nu_{\mathrm{emit}}=0.8$, using the conserved photon energy associated with the stationary timelike Killing symmetry.

</details>
