# Conditional consequences do not run backward

*Difficulty 3 of 5 · [Research frontiers claim-boundary studio](index.md) · exercise 4 of 12*

Researchers prove `RH implies bound B(x)` and computations strongly support `B(x)` for all tested x. A commentator calls this evidence a proof of RH. Diagnose the logic and state what extra theorem would change the conclusion.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

From `A implies B`, observing B does not establish A

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

An equivalence requires the converse with all quantifiers and hypotheses

</details>

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

The argument affirms the consequent: both RH and not-RH worlds may satisfy the tested instances of B. Finite computations of B add another finite qualifier. To prove RH by this route, one would need an unconditional converse showing that the full, precisely quantified bound B implies RH, and then a proof of that full bound rather than sampled agreement. If B is an established equivalent criterion, proving every hypothesis and quantifier of B would suffice; approximate or finite versions still do not.

**Answer.** `RH => B` cannot be reversed. A proved converse plus a proof of the complete B statement would be required.

</details>
