# Stability is one arrow, not the whole pipeline

*Difficulty 3 of 5 · [Research frontiers claim-boundary studio](index.md) · exercise 9 of 12*

A persistence-diagram stability theorem bounds bottleneck distance by input-function sup distance. An applied paper concludes that its longest bar is a statistically significant biological mechanism. List at least four missing inferential bridges.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

The theorem controls a specified mathematical perturbation, not sample-to-population error by itself

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

Metric choice, selection, null variation, and scientific interpretation are separate

</details>

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

The paper must justify that the empirical input is close to the target population object in the theorem's metric; that preprocessing and the chosen biological distance preserve the relevant notion of similarity; that the bar was not selected and tested on the same outcomes without correction; and that sampling variation or a defensible null model makes its lifetime unusual. It also needs sensitivity to filtration, coefficients and hyperparameters, independent validation, and a domain argument connecting a topological summary to the proposed mechanism. Stability supplies continuity of one input-to-diagram map under hypotheses; it supplies none of these bridges automatically.

**Answer.** Sampling-to-population control, metric validity, selection/null uncertainty, sensitivity, validation, and a biological mechanism argument remain separate requirements.

</details>
