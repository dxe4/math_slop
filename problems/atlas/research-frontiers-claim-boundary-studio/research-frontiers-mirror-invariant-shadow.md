# A swapped Hodge pair is a shadow

*Difficulty 2 of 5 · [Research frontiers claim-boundary studio](index.md) · exercise 7 of 12*

Two Calabi–Yau threefolds have `(h11,h21)=(2,86)` and `(86,2)`. Compute their Euler characteristics and evaluate `the swap proves homological mirror symmetry for the pair.`

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

Use `chi=2(h11-h21)` in this setting

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

A categorical equivalence requires functors, morphisms, higher structure, and generation

</details>

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

The Euler characteristics are `2(2-86)=-168` and `2(86-2)=168`, so they have the expected opposite signs under the numerical mirror pattern. This is a consistency check only. Homological mirror symmetry requires an equivalence between the appropriate enhanced Fukaya and coherent-sheaf categories under precise hypotheses. The Hodge numbers contain far less information and do not construct a functor, prove full faithfulness or essential surjectivity, or address obstruction and coefficient choices.

**Answer.** The Euler characteristics are -168 and 168. The swap is numerical evidence, not a categorical-equivalence proof.

</details>
