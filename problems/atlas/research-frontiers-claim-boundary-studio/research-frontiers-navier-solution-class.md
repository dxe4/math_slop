# Keep weak and smooth claims separate

*Difficulty 4 of 5 · [Research frontiers claim-boundary studio](index.md) · exercise 2 of 12*

A paper proves nonuniqueness in a stated class of finite-kinetic-energy weak solutions and a news summary says `Navier–Stokes smooth solutions are nonunique, so the Millennium problem is solved by breakdown.` Audit every invalid step.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

A distributional weak solution may not lie in the classical or other energy-inequality class at issue

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

Nonuniqueness and finite-time loss of smoothness are different conclusions

</details>

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

The summary silently replaces the paper's specific weak class with smooth solutions, which have stronger regularity and may satisfy additional admissibility properties. It then replaces nonuniqueness with breakdown, although two rough weak solutions do not show that an allowed smooth initial datum loses smoothness in finite time. Finally, the official Clay alternatives impose exact domains, data, forcing, decay or periodicity, and solution requirements. A theorem outside that target can illuminate weak-solution flexibility without satisfying an alternative. The correct report names the weak class and says only that uniqueness fails there; it explicitly excludes smooth nonuniqueness and Clay resolution unless separately proved.

**Answer.** The result concerns its stated weak class; it proves neither nonuniqueness of smooth solutions nor an allowed finite-time breakdown under the Clay statement.

</details>
