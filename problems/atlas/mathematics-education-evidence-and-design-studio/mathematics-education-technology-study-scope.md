# Scope a technology result

*Difficulty 3 of 5 · [Mathematics education evidence and design studio](index.md) · exercise 5 of 12*

Twelve high-school geometry classrooms are randomly assigned to a particular dynamic-geometry unit or the school's usual unit. The prespecified geometry assessment favors the assigned dynamic unit, with an uncertainty interval excluding zero. Evaluate the claims `this unit improved this measured outcome in the study` and `technology improves mathematics learning`.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

Name the assignment unit, intervention version, comparison, population, and outcome

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

Random assignment strengthens internal causal inference but does not erase implementation or transport limits

</details>

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

Subject to the randomization, clustering, attrition, implementation, and scoring analysis being sound, the first claim can be a defensible causal statement about assignment to this unit versus this usual-unit comparison for the participating classrooms and measured outcome. Its estimate and interval should be reported, not only direction. The second claim is far too broad: `technology` includes many tools and pedagogies, mathematics includes many outcomes, and other populations, teachers, durations, access conditions, and comparisons may differ. The experiment cannot identify which affordance produced the contrast unless the design and process evidence address that mechanism.

**Answer.** The bounded intervention-versus-comparison claim may be warranted; the generic technology claim is not.

</details>
