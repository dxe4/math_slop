# Operationalize without shrinking the construct

*Difficulty 2 of 5 · [Mathematics education evidence and design studio](index.md) · exercise 6 of 12*

A project defines `fraction understanding` as speed on twenty same-denominator addition items. Give a more defensible bounded construct and a small evidence set, then state what the evidence would still not establish.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

Name a mathematical relation rather than the whole domain

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

Use more than one representation or task demand

</details>

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

A bounded construct could be `coordinates part-whole and quotient representations to justify equivalent fractions and compare values with a common whole.` Evidence could include building equivalent area partitions, locating fractions on a number line, explaining why multiplying numerator and denominator by the same nonzero number preserves value, and transferring the explanation to unfamiliar denominators. Scoring should record reasoning as well as answers. These tasks still do not exhaust fraction understanding, establish performance under every language or access condition, or make the score identical to the learner's knowledge. A response-process and fairness argument remains necessary.

**Answer.** Define a specific representational relation, sample it with linked area, number-line, and explanation tasks, and retain limits on domain coverage and score interpretation.

</details>
