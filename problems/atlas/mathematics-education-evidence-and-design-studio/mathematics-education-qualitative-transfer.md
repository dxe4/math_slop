# Transfer a mechanism, not a prevalence estimate

*Difficulty 4 of 5 · [Mathematics education evidence and design studio](index.md) · exercise 9 of 12*

A purposefully selected case study of two multilingual algebra groups documents how gesture, home-language talk, and graph annotations jointly support revision of a covariation explanation. State a warranted result, an unwarranted result, and the contextual information needed for transfer.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

Purposeful cases can support a process account without estimating frequency

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

Describe episode selection and discrepant evidence

</details>

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

A warranted result is a traceable account of how the three resources functioned in the recorded episodes, including competing interpretations and cases that did not fit. An unwarranted result is a numerical claim about how often all multilingual learners use this mechanism or a universal causal claim that permitting home-language talk raises algebra achievement. Transfer requires the mathematical tasks, participants and language ecology, instructional norms, tools, researcher relationship, episode-selection logic, corpus boundaries, analytic process, quoted evidence, discrepant cases, and missing records. Readers can then judge similarity to another setting without mistaking transferability for population prevalence.

**Answer.** Claim the contextualized interaction mechanism, not universal frequency or effect; report the task, language ecology, selection, analysis, counterevidence, and missingness.

</details>
