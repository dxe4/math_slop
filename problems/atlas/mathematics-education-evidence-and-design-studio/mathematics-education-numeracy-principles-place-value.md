# Separate counting principles and bundle a ten

*Difficulty 2 of 5 · [Mathematics education evidence and design studio](index.md) · exercise 3 of 12*

A learner counts fourteen identical counters accurately from left to right. When asked to count from right to left, the learner predicts a different total; when shown `14`, the learner says `one and four` and builds five counters. State which counting principles the first performance supports, what remains open, and design a tens-and-ones follow-up that does not turn one response into a fixed label.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

Separate stable order and one-to-one correspondence from cardinality, abstraction, and order irrelevance

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

Let the learner physically exchange ten ones for one bundle

</details>

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

The accurate first count supports stable order and one-to-one correspondence for this visible homogeneous collection; if the learner used the final word as the total, it also supports cardinality in that episode. The prediction leaves order irrelevance open, and the task did not probe abstraction across object types or sensory events. For place value, establish fourteen loose counters by counting, invite the learner to bundle exactly ten, and connect the result `one ten and four ones` to `1*10+4*1=14`. Unbundle to confirm conservation, then show one bundle and six loose ones and ask for a numeral. Record which unit each digit is connected to. Language, task familiarity, and grouping remain alternative explanations, so do not diagnose a trait from the original response.

**Answer.** The first count supports stable order and one-to-one locally; probe order irrelevance separately, then exchange ten of fourteen ones for one ten and link the units to the digits in 14.

</details>
