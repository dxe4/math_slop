# A null test is not an absence certificate

*Difficulty 3 of 5 · [Mathematics education evidence and design studio](index.md) · exercise 8 of 12*

A study estimates a standardized mean difference of `0.12` with a 95% interval from `-0.18` to `0.42`, and its null-hypothesis test is not significant. Write a responsible conclusion and identify what would be required for an equivalence claim.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

The interval includes effects with different practical meanings

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

Equivalence requires bounds chosen before seeing the result

</details>

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

Report that the estimated contrast is 0.12 standard deviations and that, under the design and model, the interval ranges from a modest negative contrast to a moderate positive one. The data are therefore too imprecise to distinguish among several practically different possibilities; failure to reject zero is not proof of exactly no effect. An equivalence claim would require substantively justified lower and upper equivalence bounds specified in advance, a design with adequate precision for those bounds, and an appropriate equivalence analysis, plus the usual measurement, attrition, and design checks.

**Answer.** The result is inconclusive across -0.18 to 0.42, not evidence of exact absence; equivalence needs prespecified meaningful bounds and sufficient precision.

</details>
