# Repair an ambiguous comparison

*Difficulty 2 of 5 · [Mathematics education evidence and design studio](index.md) · exercise 2 of 12*

An adult shows four large buttons in a long row and five small buttons in a compact cluster, then asks which is `bigger`. Diagnose at least three task features that compete with numerosity and redesign the task to investigate whether a learner can compare collections by number.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

Object size, spatial extent, and the word bigger can each change the question

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

Keep only one deliberate conflict after making the target explicit

</details>

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

Individual object size, total spatial extent, and arrangement all compete with number; `bigger` is ambiguous between object size, occupied space, and cardinality. Use identical objects and ask, `Which collection has more objects, or are they the same? Show how you know.` If resistance to spacing is the target, arrange four widely and five compactly while holding object size constant. Invite one-to-one pairing or counting. Then swap arrangements to see whether the justification follows the numbers. The result remains evidence for these tasks, not a global diagnosis.

**Answer.** Use identical objects, name `more objects`, retain at most one spacing conflict, and ask for pairing or counting evidence across a rearrangement.

</details>
