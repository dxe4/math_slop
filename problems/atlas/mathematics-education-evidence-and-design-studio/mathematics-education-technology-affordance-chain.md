# Turn a slider into a mathematical design

*Difficulty 2 of 5 · [Mathematics education evidence and design studio](index.md) · exercise 4 of 12*

A lesson plan says only `Students explore y=mx+b with sliders.` Complete an affordance chain containing a precise target, learner action, automated response, one hidden convention, an accessible alternative, and independent evidence of learning.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

A graph update is feedback, not the learning outcome

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

Choose a transfer task that does not require dragging

</details>

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

A precise target is to predict and justify how `m` changes slope and `b` changes vertical intercept while the other parameter is held fixed. Learners record a prediction, change one numeric parameter, select two displayed points, compute rise/run, and revise an explanation. The tool updates linked graph and table. Fix the viewing window, since automatic rescaling can hide slope differences. Provide numeric entry and keyboard increments plus a complete coordinate table and text summary. Independent evidence is a new paper or accessible-text line for which learners predict parameter changes and justify them without the slider. This chain makes the tool's role inspectable.

**Answer.** Specify a slope/intercept explanation target, prediction and controlled parameter action, fixed window, keyboard/table alternative, and no-tool transfer task.

</details>
