# What did the count establish?

*Difficulty 1 of 5 · [Mathematics education evidence and design studio](index.md) · exercise 1 of 12*

A child recites the numbers one through ten, then counts six scattered counters by touching each exactly once. When asked `How many?`, the child begins the count again. List what this episode supports, what remains uncertain, and one low-stakes follow-up task.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

Separate the word sequence, one-to-one coordination, and cardinality

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

Recounting can be a checking strategy rather than proof of a deficit

</details>

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

The episode supports a stable spoken sequence at least through ten and successful one-to-one coordination for this six-object arrangement. It does not by itself establish or refute the cardinal principle, because recounting may be a deliberate check. It also says little about comparison, conservation, composition, or performance in another language or representation. A focused follow-up is to ask the child to give exactly six counters from a larger collection, then rearrange those six without adding or removing any and ask whether the total changed. Record the actions and words rather than assigning a fixed label.

**Answer.** Sequence and one-to-one coordination are supported locally; cardinality remains open. Use a give-me-six or rearrangement probe and record behavior.

</details>
