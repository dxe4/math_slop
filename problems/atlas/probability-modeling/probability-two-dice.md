# Sums of two dice

*Difficulty 1 of 5 · [Probability modeling](index.md) · exercise 1 of 15*

Two fair six-sided dice are rolled. What is the probability that the sum is 8? Explain why treating the possible sums 2 through 12 as equally likely is wrong.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

Use 36 ordered pairs

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

List the pairs summing to 8

</details>

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

The equally likely elementary outcomes are the $36$ ordered pairs. The pairs with sum $8$ are $(2,6),(3,5),(4,4),(5,3),(6,2)$, so the probability is $5/36$. Sums are not equally likely because they arise from different numbers of ordered pairs: for example sum $2$ has one representation, while sum $7$ has six.

**Answer.** $5/36$; sums have unequal numbers of underlying ordered outcomes.

</details>
