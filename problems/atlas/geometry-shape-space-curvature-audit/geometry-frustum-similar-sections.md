# Use the frustum formula with its hypothesis

*Difficulty 3 of 5 · [Geometry from proof to curvature audit studio](index.md) · exercise 8 of 12*

A frustum has parallel similar end faces of areas 4 and 16 and perpendicular height 6. Find its volume. Explain why the same data do not determine the volume of an arbitrary solid.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

Use $h(B1+\sqrt{B1B2}+B2)/3$

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

Similarity controls every intermediate linear scale

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 3</summary>

Arbitrary cross-sectional area can vary differently between the ends

</details>

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

The frustum formula gives $V=(6/3)(4+\sqrt{64}+16)=2(4+8+16)=56$ cubic units. It is derived by subtracting similar pyramids or cones; parallel similarity forces intermediate lengths to vary linearly and areas quadratically. Two end areas and a height alone do not specify the cross-sectional area at intermediate heights, so an arbitrary solid can have many different volumes with the same three numbers.

**Answer.** Volume is 56 cubic units, using the essential parallel-similar-section hypothesis.

</details>
