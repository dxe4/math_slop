# Derive both branches from a focus and directrix

*Difficulty 4 of 5 · [Geometry from proof to curvature audit studio](index.md) · exercise 6 of 12*

For focus $F=(0,0)$, directrix $x=1$, and eccentricity $e=3/2$, derive a standard hyperbola equation. Find both axis vertices and verify each against the unsquared distance ratio.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

Start with $x^2+y^2=(9/4)(x-1)^2$

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

Complete the square around $x=9/5$

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 3</summary>

At a vertex $y=0$

</details>

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

Multiplying the squared equation by 4 and rearranging gives $5x^2-18x-4y^2+9=0$. Completing the square yields $5(x-9/5)^2-4y^2=36/5$, hence $(x-9/5)^2/(36/25)-y^2/(9/5)=1$. The center is 9/5 and transverse semiaxis is 6/5, so vertices are $x=9/5\pm 6/5$, namely 3/5 and 3. At (3/5,0), focus distance is 3/5 and directrix distance 2/5, ratio 3/2. At (3,0), distances are 3 and 2, again ratio 3/2. Checking the unsquared rule verifies both squared-equation branches.

**Answer.** $(x-9/5)^2/(36/25)-y^2/(9/5)=1$, with vertices (3/5,0) and (3,0), both having focus/directrix ratio 3/2.

</details>
