# Compute a spherical navigation triangle

*Difficulty 4 of 5 · [Geometry from proof to curvature audit studio](index.md) · exercise 9 of 12*

On a sphere of radius R, let N be the North Pole and P,Q be equatorial points separated by 60 degrees longitude. Find all three side lengths, all three angles, shorter P-to-Q distance, spherical excess, and area.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

Both meridian sides subtend 90 degrees

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

Meridians meet the equator orthogonally

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 3</summary>

Area equals R squared times excess

</details>

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

Angular sides $NP$ and $NQ$ are $\pi/2$, so their lengths are $R\pi/2$. Equatorial side $PQ$ has angular length $\pi/3$, so its shorter surface distance is $R\pi/3$. Angles at $P$ and $Q$ are both $\pi/2$ because equator and meridians meet orthogonally; angle at $N$ equals the longitude separation $\pi/3$. The angle sum is $4\pi/3$, excess is $\pi/3$, and area is $R^2\pi/3$. These are shorter great-circle arcs; an antipodal or longer-arc convention would require a different triangle specification.

**Answer.** Sides $R\pi/2,R\pi/2,R\pi/3$; angles $90^\circ,90^\circ,60^\circ$; excess $\pi/3$ and area $R^2\pi/3$.

</details>
