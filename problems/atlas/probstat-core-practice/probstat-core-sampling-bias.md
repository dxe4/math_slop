# Identify a biased sampling design

*Difficulty 2 of 5 · [Probability and statistics core practice](index.md) · exercise 5 of 12*

A university surveys students about dining satisfaction by standing outside the main dining hall at lunchtime on a Monday. Name the sampling frame, the target population, and one source of selection bias. Explain whether increasing the sample size would remove this bias.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

Consider who is excluded from the frame

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

Bias is a property of the design, not of the sample size

</details>

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

The sampling frame is students who pass the main dining hall at lunchtime on Monday. The target population is likely all enrolled students. Selection bias arises because students who eat elsewhere, have class conflicts, or avoid the dining hall are excluded. These groups may have systematically different satisfaction levels. Increasing the sample size only collects more data from the same biased frame; it does not represent the excluded students and therefore cannot remove the selection bias.

**Answer.** Frame: Monday lunchtime hall visitors; bias: excludes non-visitors; larger sample does not fix it.

</details>
