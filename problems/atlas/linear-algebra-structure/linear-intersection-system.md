# Classify a parameterized system

*Difficulty 2 of 5 · [Linear algebra as structure](index.md) · exercise 1 of 11*

For which values of $a$ does the system $x+2y=3$ and $2x+4y=a$ have no solutions, one solution, or infinitely many solutions? Explain geometrically.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

The left side of the second equation is twice the first

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

Compare the right sides

</details>

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

Doubling the first equation gives $2x+4y=6$. If $a=6$, the two equations describe the same line, so every point on that line is a solution and there are infinitely many. If $a\ne6$, the equations have proportional left sides but inconsistent right sides: they are distinct parallel lines and have no solution. A unique solution never occurs.

**Answer.** Infinitely many for $a=6$; none for $a\ne6$; never exactly one.

</details>
