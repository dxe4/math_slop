# Reciprocal involution fixed

*Medium · [Original Putnam-style training](index.md)*

Let $f(x)=(2x+3)/(x-2)$ on its domain. Find the unique real fixed point $x>2$ of $f$, if it exists.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

Solve $f(x)=x$ while keeping $x\ne2$.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

The resulting quadratic has one root on each side of $2$.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Model proof</summary>

The unique fixed point with $x>2$ is $2+\sqrt7$.

1. $x=(2x+3)/(x-2)$ is equivalent for $x\ne2$ to $x^2-4x-3=0$.
2. The two roots are $2\pm\sqrt7$, one above and one below $2$.
3. The unique fixed point with $x>2$ is $2+\sqrt7$.

**Self-assessment rubric**

- Solve the quadratic and distinguish roots by the x&gt;2 condition.
- State the unique permitted fixed point.
