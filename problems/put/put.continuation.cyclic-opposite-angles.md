# Cyclic opposite angles

*Hard · [Original Putnam-style training](index.md)*

For a convex cyclic quadrilateral $ABCD$, prove $\angle A+\angle C=\pi$ and $\angle B+\angle D=\pi$.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

Use the inscribed-angle theorem.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

Opposite vertices intercept complementary arcs of the circle.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Model proof</summary>

The same argument with the complementary arcs intercepted at $B$ and $D$ gives the second equality.

1. $\angle A$ intercepts the arc $BCD$, while $\angle C$ intercepts the complementary arc $DAB$. Their arc measures sum to $2\pi$.
2. Each inscribed angle is half its intercepted arc measure, so their angle sum is $\pi$.
3. The same argument with the complementary arcs intercepted at $B$ and $D$ gives the second equality.

**Self-assessment rubric**

- Identify arcs intercepted by opposite vertices as a full circle.
- Apply half-arc inscribed-angle theorem.
- Repeat for both pairs of opposite angles.

</details>
