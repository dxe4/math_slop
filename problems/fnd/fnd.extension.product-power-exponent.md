# Use the same-base product law

*Easy · [Foundations bridge](index.md)*

For nonzero real x, write $x^3x^4=x^n$. Find the integer exponent n.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

Count how many factors of x occur in each power.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

Multiplication puts all those factors into one product.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

The product of same-base positive powers adds their exponents.

1. The first factor has three copies of x.
2. The second has four copies of x.
3. Together there are seven, so n=7.

</details>
