# Use perpendicular slope before locating an intercept

*Medium · [Foundations bridge](index.md)*

A line is perpendicular to a line of slope $2/3$ and passes through (−1,2). Find its y-intercept, using ordinary Cartesian axes with the same scale.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

Perpendicular nonvertical slopes multiply to −1.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

Substitute the point into y=mx+b after finding m.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

A direction condition and one point determine the intercept.

1. The required slope is m=−3/2.
2. Substituting (−1,2) gives 2=3/2+b.
3. Hence b=1/2.

</details>
