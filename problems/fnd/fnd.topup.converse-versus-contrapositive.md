# Distinguish a false converse from a true implication

*Hard · [Foundations bridge](index.md)*

Over the real numbers consider: if $x>2$, then $x^2>4$. State its contrapositive and its converse. Give a counterexample to the converse and explain why that example does not refute the original implication.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

The converse reverses the two assertions without negating them.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

A negative real number can have a square greater than four while failing the original positive hypothesis.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Model proof</summary>

Keeping the three logical forms separate prevents a counterexample from being applied to the wrong claim.

1. The contrapositive is $x^2\leq4\Rightarrow x\leq2$. The converse is $x^2>4\Rightarrow x>2$.
2. Take x=-3. Its square is nine, greater than four, but x is not greater than two, so it refutes the converse.
3. The original implication has hypothesis x&gt;2, which this example does not satisfy. For a real x&gt;2, both sides are positive and squaring gives x^2&gt;4, so the original remains valid.

**Self-assessment rubric**

- Write the contrapositive and converse with their different negations.
- Check the converse hypothesis and failure of its conclusion.
- Explain why the same value does not meet the original hypothesis.
