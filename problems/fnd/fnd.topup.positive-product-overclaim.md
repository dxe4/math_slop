# Refute an overstrong conclusion and prove its correction

*Medium · [Foundations bridge](index.md)*

Refute the claim: for positive real x,y, $xy>1$ implies both x&gt;1 and y&gt;1. Then prove the weaker conclusion that at least one of x,y is greater than one.

<details>
<summary>Model proof</summary>

A valid counterexample can coexist with a true weaker statement.

1. Take x=2 and y=3/4. Both are positive and their product is 3/2&gt;1, but y is not greater than one.
2. To prove the weaker statement, suppose neither positive factor exceeds one. Then 0&lt;x&lt;=1 and 0&lt;y&lt;=1, giving xy&lt;=1.
3. This contradicts the product condition. Therefore at least one factor exceeds one, although both need not.

**Self-assessment rubric**

- Check every original hypothesis for a concrete pair.
- Identify which required conclusion fails.
- Prove the at-least-one conclusion for arbitrary permitted factors.
