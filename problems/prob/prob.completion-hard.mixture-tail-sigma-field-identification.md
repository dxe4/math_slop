# Identify the full tail information of a Bernoulli mixture

*Hard · [Probability problems](index.md)*

Let $\Theta$ have an arbitrary probability law on $[0,1]$ and, conditionally on $\Theta$, let $(X_n)$ be iid Bernoulli($\Theta$), on the usual mixture product space. Prove that the completed tail sigma-field of $(X_n)$ equals the completed sigma-field generated by $\Theta$. You may use the strong law and Kolmogorov zero--one law under each fixed Bernoulli product measure. Explain why the unconditional tail sigma-field need not be trivial.

<details>
<summary>Model proof</summary>

The parameter is exactly the residual information left after every finite initial segment is discarded.

1. Conditional strong laws and integration over the mixing law give $n^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^nX_j\to\Theta$ almost surely. The same limit is obtained using only observations after any fixed $k$. Defining the limit as a measurable function of the sequence shows that $\Theta$ is measurable in the completed tail sigma-field.
2. For a tail event $A$ of the coordinate sequence, let $h(\theta)=P_\theta(A)$, a measurable function under the mixture kernel. Under each fixed parameter the coordinates are independent, so the given zero--one law yields $h(\theta)\in\{0,1\}$ (in particular for mixing-almost every parameter).
3. Since $E[\mathbf1_A\mid\Theta]=h(\Theta)$, the conditional variance of the indicator is $h(\Theta)(1-h(\Theta))=0$. Equivalently $E[(\mathbf1_A-h(\Theta))^2]=0$. Thus $A$ agrees modulo null sets with $\{h(\Theta)=1\}$, giving the reverse inclusion after completion. Events added by completion do not change this inclusion.
4. For example, if $\Theta$ is uniform on $[0,1]$, the event that the limiting empirical mean is at most $1/2$ is a tail event of probability $1/2$. Independence only holds conditionally on the parameter; applying the unconditional independent-coordinate zero--one law would therefore be invalid.
