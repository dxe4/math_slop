# Compute a distance across zero

*Easy · [Real analysis](index.md)*

Two points on the real line are $p=-7/6$ and $q=5/3$. Give their distance $|p-q|$ as an exact number.

```answer
{"answer":{"kind":"rational","expected":{"numerator":17,"denominator":6},"checker_scope":"Checks one exact absolute difference only."}}
```

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

Express both coordinates with denominator $6$.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

Distance is nonnegative, regardless of which coordinate you subtract first.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

Distance is the absolute difference between coordinates.

1. q=5/3=10/6, so $p-q=-7/6-10/6=-17/6$.
2. Take the absolute value: $|p-q|=17/6$.

</details>
