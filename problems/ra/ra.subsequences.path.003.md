# Use incompatible subsequences to refute convergence

*Medium · [Real analysis](index.md)*

Let $a_{n}=(-1)^n$. Its even subsequence is constantly $1$ and its odd subsequence is constantly $-1$. Which conclusion is justified? Select the mathematically correct statement from the four alternatives.

**Options**

1. The full sequence has no real limit, because a convergent sequence gives every subsequence the same limit.
2. The full sequence converges to the average $0$ of the two subsequential limits.
3. The full sequence converges because it is bounded.
4. Every subsequence diverges because the full sequence alternates.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

Convergence passes to every subsequence with the same limit.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

Two different subsequential limits cannot both equal one full-sequence limit.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

Two distinct subsequential limits obstruct convergence of the original sequence.

1. The even subsequence tends to $1$ and the odd subsequence tends to −1.
2. If the full sequence had a real limit, both subsequences would have that same limit. Their different limits therefore rule out convergence of the full sequence.

</details>
