# Simplify a product derivative at a zero of one factor

*Easy · [Calculus](index.md)*

At a point a, f(a)=0, f′(a)=2 and g(a)=3; g is differentiable there. Find $(fg)′(a)$.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

Use the product rule.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

The term containing f(a) vanishes regardless of g′(a).

</details>

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

A zero factor removes one term, but does not force the product derivative to vanish.

1. The product derivative is f′(a)g(a)+f(a)g′(a).
2. Its second term is zero because f(a)=0.
3. The result is 2×3=6.

</details>
