# Use the secant derivative at the origin

*Easy · [Calculus](index.md)*

In radians, find the derivative of sec x at x=0, using $(\sec x)'=\sec x\tan x$.

<summary>Hint 1</summary>

Evaluate both factors at zero.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

One zero factor makes the product zero.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

The secant derivative couples secant and tangent.

1. At zero sec 0=1 and tan 0=0.
2. Their product is 1×0.
3. Thus the derivative is 0.

</details>
