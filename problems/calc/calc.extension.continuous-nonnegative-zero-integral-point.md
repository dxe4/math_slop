# Apply the zero-integral theorem with its hypotheses

*Easy · [Calculus](index.md)*

Use the theorem that a continuous nonnegative function with zero integral on a nondegenerate closed interval is identically zero. If f≥0 is continuous on \[0,2\] and $\int_0^2f=0$, find f(1).

Nonnegativity prevents cancellation and continuity prevents an isolated positive spike.

1. The interval has positive length and f is continuous and nonnegative there.
2. The stated theorem therefore gives f(x)=0 throughout \[0,2\].
3. In particular f(1)=0.
