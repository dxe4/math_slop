# Interpret a finite zero derivative geometrically

*Easy · [Calculus](index.md)*

A function is differentiable at a and $f'(a)=0$. What is the slope of its tangent line there? No claim about a local extremum is requested.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

The derivative is the tangent slope when finite.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

A zero slope specifies a horizontal tangent.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

A zero derivative is slope information alone.

1. Differentiability gives a tangent with slope f′(a).
2. The supplied derivative value is zero.
3. Thus the tangent slope is 0, whether or not an extremum occurs.

</details>
