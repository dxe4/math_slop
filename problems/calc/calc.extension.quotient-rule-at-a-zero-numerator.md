# Simplify a quotient derivative at a numerator zero

*Easy · [Calculus](index.md)*

At a point a, f(a)=0, f′(a)=6 and g(a)=2, with both functions differentiable. Find $(f/g)′(a)$.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

The denominator value is nonzero, so the quotient rule applies.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

The term involving f(a) vanishes, leaving f′(a)/g(a).

</details>

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

A zero numerator does not remove the denominator’s scaling effect on slope.

1. The quotient rule gives \[f′g−fg′\]/g² at a.
2. Substitution gives (6×2−0)/4.
3. Thus the derivative is 3.

</details>
