# Derivative from a difference quotient: constant difference quotient

*Medium · [Calculus](index.md)*

Use a difference quotient to find the derivative of $f(x)=5x-1$ at $x=4$.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

Subtract $f(4)$ from $f(4+h)$.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

A constant difference quotient has that same constant as its limit.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

For every $h\neq 0$ the quotient is 5, so its limit and the derivative are $5$.

1. $f(4+h)-f(4)=5h$.
2. For every $h\neq 0$ the quotient is 5, so its limit and the derivative are $5$.
