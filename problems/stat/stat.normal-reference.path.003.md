# Check whether a reference model is justified

*Medium · [Statistics and probability](index.md)*

An observed dataset is strongly skewed. An analyst assumes every individual observation is normally distributed solely because a mean and standard deviation can be computed. Which assessment is appropriate? Select the mathematically correct statement from the four alternatives.

**Options**

1. Those two summaries do not establish normality; a normal probability calculation needs a justified model for the quantity being analyzed.
2. Every nonempty dataset with a mean and standard deviation is exactly normal.
3. Strong skew guarantees a symmetric normal distribution.
4. Subtracting the mean and dividing by the standard deviation always produces a normal distribution.

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

A reference distribution is an assumption or approximation, not a consequence of computing summaries.

1. Mean and standard deviation exist for many different distribution shapes.
2. They do not certify a normal model; the statement “Those two summaries do not establish normality; a normal probability calculation needs a justified model for the quantity being analyzed.” states the missing justification.

</details>
