# Expose an aggregate difference from unequal mixtures

*Hard · [Statistics and probability](index.md)*

In stratum H, protocol A has $72$ successes among $80$ people and B has $18$ among $20$. In stratum L, A has $2$ successes among $20$ and B has $8$ among $80$. Show that within-stratum success rates agree but overall rates differ. Explain why this aggregate association alone does not establish a causal protocol advantage.

<details>
<summary>Model proof</summary>

Unequal stratum mixtures can produce aggregate association without a within-stratum rate difference.

1. In H both rates are nine tenths; in L both are one tenth.
2. A pools to seventy-four successes among one hundred people, whereas B pools to twenty-six among one hundred. A includes far more H participants.
3. The pooled difference is compatible with stratum composition rather than a causal protocol advantage. Equal observed stratum rates also do not by themselves prove every individual causal effect zero.
