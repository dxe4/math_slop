# State what random assignment of volunteers supports

*Medium · [Statistics and probability](index.md)*

Twelve volunteers are assigned uniformly to two routines, $6$ per group. Outcomes are well-defined and there is no interference. No random sampling from a wider population occurred. Which claim has support from this design? Choices: Random assignment supports a causal comparison for these participants under the stated conditions, without guaranteeing wider population representativeness.; The volunteers are automatically a simple random sample of the entire population.; Any observed difference must equal the population causal effect exactly.; Random assignment guarantees identical observed outcomes in the two routine groups.

**Options**

1. Random assignment supports a causal comparison for these participants under the stated conditions, without guaranteeing wider population representativeness.
2. The volunteers are automatically a simple random sample of the entire population.
3. Any observed difference must equal the population causal effect exactly.
4. Random assignment guarantees identical observed outcomes in the two routine groups.

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

Assignment and sampling address distinct inferential questions.

1. The routines were randomized among the participating volunteers, supporting comparison of their treatment outcomes under the stated conditions.
2. The design includes no population sampling mechanism.
3. It therefore supplies no automatic guarantee of population representativeness or an exactly known population effect.
