# Separate random invitation from final response

*Easy · [Statistics and probability](index.md)*

A school randomly invites $100$ families to a schedule survey. Fifteen reply. Which conclusion is justified about the response process? Select the mathematically correct statement from the four alternatives.

**Options**

1. The $15$ responses automatically represent every family because invitations were random.
2. The final respondents may differ from nonrespondents, so nonresponse can limit generalization.
3. The $15$ responses prove that every unresponsive family agrees with them.
4. Every nonresponding family must be counted as answering no.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

The selection of invitees and the selection of respondents are two different steps.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

Nothing in the statement makes response independent of schedule preferences.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

Random selection at one stage does not remove bias introduced at a later stage.

1. Random invitations do not force every selected family to reply.
2. If response relates to preferences, the responding group can be biased; the statement “The final respondents may differ from nonrespondents, so nonresponse can limit generalization.” states the remaining uncertainty.

</details>
