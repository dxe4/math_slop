# Interpret a frequentist confidence level

*Medium · [Statistics and probability](index.md)*

A frequentist interval procedure has $95$% coverage under its stated model. Which interpretation follows without adding a Bayesian model? Select the mathematically correct statement from the four alternatives.

**Options**

1. After every observed interval, the fixed parameter becomes uniformly distributed inside it.
2. Exactly $95$% of individual future observations lie inside every interval.
3. Across repeated samples from the model,$95$% of the generated intervals contain the fixed target parameter.
4. At least $95$% of the observations in every observed sample lie inside its confidence interval.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

The randomness in coverage comes from the sample and its resulting interval.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

The target parameter is fixed in this frequentist statement.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

Confidence coverage is a property of an interval procedure under a model.

1. Before sampling, the interval varies across repetitions while the parameter stays fixed.
2. The $95$% coverage statement concerns how often those intervals capture that target; the statement “Across repeated samples from the model,$95$% of the generated intervals contain the fixed target parameter.” expresses that repeated-sampling meaning.

</details>
