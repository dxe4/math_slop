# Limit a standard-error claim under changing variance

*Medium · [Statistics and probability](index.md)*

A regression’s conditional mean is correctly specified, but conditional error variance is known to grow with x. The analyst uses classical homoskedastic standard errors without adjustment. Which claim is appropriate? Select the mathematically correct statement from the four alternatives.

**Options**

1. A correct mean guarantees every standard-error formula remains valid.
2. Changing variance proves the slope is causal.
3. The constant-variance standard-error assumptions fail, so the reported inference needs a method justified for the variance pattern.
4. Changing conditional variance necessarily means the conditional mean is misspecified.

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

A correct fitted mean does not validate an incompatible uncertainty formula.

1. The scenario supplies nonconstant conditional error variance.
2. That violates the stated constant-variance standard-error justification, even with a correct mean model; the statement “The constant-variance standard-error assumptions fail, so the reported inference needs a method justified for the variance pattern.” is appropriate.

</details>
