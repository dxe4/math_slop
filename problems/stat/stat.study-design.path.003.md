# Limit a claim from a random observational sample

*Medium · [Statistics and probability](index.md)*

A representative random sample of commuters records existing cycling choices and reported travel satisfaction. Nobody is assigned a travel mode. Which interpretation is justified from association alone? Select the mathematically correct statement from the four alternatives.

**Options**

1. The sample can describe an association in its target population, but that association alone does not establish that cycling causes greater satisfaction.
2. Random sampling proves that changing any commuter to cycling increases satisfaction.
3. Recording satisfaction secretly randomizes travel mode.
4. The association cannot describe the target population because the study is observational.

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

Representativeness helps a population claim but does not remove observational confounding.

1. Random sampling can support generalizing a described association to the sampling population under the stated design.
2. Travel mode was not assigned, so confounding can remain; the statement “The sample can describe an association in its target population, but that association alone does not establish that cycling causes greater satisfaction.” states the appropriate causal limit.
