# Keep a predictive slope separate from a causal claim

*Medium · [Statistics and probability](index.md)*

An observational study fits a positive slope between time spent at a library and test score. Which claim follows from the fitted slope alone? Select the mathematically correct statement from the four alternatives.

**Options**

1. Forcing any student to spend an extra hour there must increase that student’s score by the fitted slope.
2. The line gives a predictive association in the studied data; causation requires additional design or assumptions.
3. A positive slope proves there are no confounders.
4. Every observation lies exactly on the fitted line because its slope is positive.

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

Calculating a prediction does not validate a causal mechanism.

1. The positive slope describes how fitted predictions vary with observed library time.
2. That alone does not isolate an intervention’s effect, so the statement “The line gives a predictive association in the studied data; causation requires additional design or assumptions.” is the supported interpretation.
