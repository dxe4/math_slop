# Compute a signed dot product

*Easy · [Linear algebra problems](index.md)*

Compute $(1,2,-1)\cdot(3,0,4)$ using the ordinary Euclidean inner product.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

Multiply corresponding coordinates, then add.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

The last coordinate product retains its negative sign.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

The Euclidean product pairs matching coordinates.

1. The coordinate products are $1\cdot3=3$, $2\cdot0=0$ and $(-1)\cdot4=-4$.
2. Adding gives $3+0-4=-1$.
3. The dot product is $-1$; a negative value is permitted for two different vectors.

</details>
