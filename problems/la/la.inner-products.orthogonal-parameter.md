# Choose an orthogonal parameter

*Easy · [Linear algebra problems](index.md)*

For which real $t$ are $(1,t,2)$ and $(2,1,-1)$ orthogonal in the ordinary Euclidean inner product? Enter the complete set.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

Orthogonality means that the dot product vanishes.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

Collect the fixed coordinate products before solving for the remaining parameter.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

A single inner-product equation determines the parameter.

1. The dot product is $1\cdot2+t\cdot1+2\cdot(-1)=t$.
2. It vanishes exactly when $t=0$.
3. The complete parameter set is $\{0\}$.

</details>
