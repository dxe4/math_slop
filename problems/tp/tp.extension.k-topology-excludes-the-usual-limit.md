# Compute accumulation after refining neighborhoods at a missing point

*Medium · [Topology problems](index.md)*

Let $K=\{1/n:n\ge1\}$ and give R the topology generated by usual intervals and sets $(a,b)\setminus K$. Which points of $\{0,1,2\}$ are accumulation points of K? Return the actual point set.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

At zero, a new basic neighborhood can delete all of K.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

At a point of K, usual intervals still isolate it relative to K.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

A finer topology can destroy a formerly convergent sequence.

1. Zero has the open neighborhood $(-1,1)\setminus K$, disjoint from K, so it is not an accumulation point.
2. The point one can be isolated from every other reciprocal by a sufficiently small usual interval.
3. The point two also has a usual interval missing K entirely. Thus none of the listed points is an accumulation point.

</details>
