# Calculate the homology Bockstein on a Moore space

*Hard · [Topology problems](index.md)*

For prime p and n≥1, let M be obtained by attaching one (n+1)-cell to Sⁿ by degree p. In the coefficient sequence $0\to\mathbb Z\xrightarrow{p}\mathbb Z\to\mathbb F_p\to0$, compute the homology connecting map $\beta:H_{n+1}(M;\mathbb F_p)\to H_n(M;\mathbb Z)$. Use compatible cellular orientations and show it is an isomorphism.

<details>
<summary>Model proof</summary>

A mod-p cycle can carry a nonzero divided integral boundary, producing the torsion class.

1. The reduced integral cellular complex has C\_{n+1}=Z generated by e\_{n+1}, C\_n=Z generated by e\_n, and d(e\_{n+1})=p e\_n. Hence H\_{n+1}(M;Z)=0 and H\_n(M;Z)=Z/p generated by \[e\_n\].
2. After reducing modulo p the differential vanishes, so H\_{n+1}(M;F\_p)=F\_p generated by the reduction of e\_{n+1}. Lift this generator to e\_{n+1} integrally. The boundary is p e\_n, and the connecting construction divides it by p, giving β(\[ē\_{n+1}\])=\[e\_n\].
3. If an integral lift changes by p times a chain, the divided boundary changes by an integral boundary; if the mod-p cycle representative changes by a mod-p boundary, the same connecting construction changes by a boundary. Thus the formula is well defined on homology, in agreement with the coefficient long exact sequence.
4. The displayed generator maps to the generator of Z/p, so β is an isomorphism of additive groups. This is the connecting map into integral homology, not the reduction of an integral H\_{n+1} class, since that group is zero.
