# Distinguish inverted primes from a surviving ideal factor

*Hard · [Abstract algebra problems](index.md)*

Let $R=\mathbb Z[1/6]$, and let I be its ideal generated by 10 and 15. Find the number of elements in R/I.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

The integer Bezout combination of ten and fifteen still determines their ideal after localization.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

Reduction modulo five must send the inverted element six to a unit.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

Localization removes only the inverted prime factors, not every factor occurring in an ideal generator.

1. In R, the ideal (10,15) equals (5): both generators are multiples of five, and 15-10=5.
2. Reduction modulo five extends to R because six has nonzero residue, indeed residue one, and is therefore invertible modulo five.
3. Its kernel is (5), and it is onto F5. Thus R/I has five elements rather than being the zero ring.

</details>
