# Prove the Frattini formula and the finite-p-group generator criterion

*Hard · [Abstract algebra problems](index.md)*

For a finite p-group G let Phi(G) be the intersection of all maximal proper subgroups, and let G^p be the subgroup generated by pth powers. Prove $Phi(G)=G^p[G,G]$. Deduce a set generates G exactly when its images span the Fp vector space G/Phi(G), and identify the minimum number of generators. Include the trivial group convention.

<summary>Model proof</summary>

The Frattini quotient records precisely the indispensable generating directions.

1. For nontrivial G, each maximal subgroup is normal of index p by the p-group normalizer theorem. Its quotient C\_p kills all pth powers and commutators, so P=G^p\[G,G\] is contained in Phi(G). The quotient G/P is elementary abelian and hence an Fp vector space.
2. If g is not in P, a linear functional on G/P can be chosen nonzero on gP. The preimage of its kernel is a maximal index-p subgroup missing g. Thus g is not in Phi(G), proving the reverse inclusion Phi(G)&lt;=P.
3. If a set's images span G/Phi(G), its generated subgroup H satisfies H\*Phi(G)=G. A proper H would lie in a maximal subgroup M, and then H\*Phi(G) would lie in M, a contradiction. The converse is immediate under projection. Therefore the minimum generator number is dim\_Fp G/Phi(G). For the trivial group set Phi(G)=G, equivalently the intersection of the empty family within G; the dimension and minimum number are both zero.
