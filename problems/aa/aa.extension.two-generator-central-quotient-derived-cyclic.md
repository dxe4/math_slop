# Determine all commutators from a two-generator central quotient

*Hard · [Abstract algebra problems](index.md)*

Suppose \[G,G\] is central and $G/Z(G)$ is generated by aZ(G), bZ(G). Prove \[G,G\] is generated by c=\[a,b\], and derive \[a^m b^n z,a^r b^s w\]=c^(m\*s-n\*r) for integers m,n,r,s and central z,w.

<details>
<summary>Model proof</summary>

A bilinear alternating commutator on a two-generator quotient has one generator.

1. The hypothesis on the central quotient gives every element a form a^m\*b^n\*z with z central. With central commutators, direct expansion gives \[uv,t\]=\[u,t\]\*\[v,t\] and \[u,vt\]=\[u,v\]\*\[u,t\], including inverse-power versions.
2. Central factors contribute no commutator, so bilinearity yields \[a^m\*b^n\*z,a^r\*b^s\*w\]=\[a,b\]^(m\*s)\*\[b,a\]^(n\*r)=c^(m\*s-n\*r).
3. Thus every commutator is a power of c, so the subgroup they generate is contained in &lt;c&gt;. The reverse inclusion holds because c itself is a commutator. Therefore \[G,G\]=&lt;c&gt;.

**Self-assessment rubric**

- Use the central quotient to obtain forms for all elements.
- Derive the power formula with the correct sign and inverse cases.
- Prove both inclusions for the derived subgroup.

</details>
