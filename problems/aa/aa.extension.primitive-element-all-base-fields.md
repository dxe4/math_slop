# Prove the primitive-element theorem with the finite-field case separated

*Hard · [Abstract algebra problems](index.md)*

Prove every finite separable extension $L/K$ is simple. For infinite K use finitely many embeddings to choose a linear combination of two generators; for finite K you may use cyclicity of the finite field multiplicative group.

<summary>Hint 2</summary>

Avoid finitely many bad c, then compare the number of distinct conjugate images with the extension degree.

<details>
<summary>Model proof</summary>

A single generic combination separates every embedding in the infinite case.

1. If K is finite, then L is a finite field. A generator of the cyclic L multiplicative group belongs to no proper subfield containing all its powers, so it generates L as a field over K.
2. Now let K be infinite and first write L=K(alpha,beta). There are n=\[L:K\] distinct K-embeddings sigma\_i into an algebraic closure. If two have different beta images, at most one value of c makes their alpha+c\*beta images equal. If their beta images coincide but they are distinct, their alpha images differ and no c is bad.
3. Choose c outside the finite set of bad values. Then theta=alpha+c\*beta has at least n distinct conjugate images, so\[K(theta):K\]&gt;=n; the opposite bound comes from inclusion in L. Equality forces K(theta)=L.
4. A finite extension is generated by finitely many elements. Apply the two-generator argument repeatedly; every intermediate extension remains separable. This proves simplicity in all cases.

- Turn distinct images into full degree and reduce finitely many generators.

</details>
