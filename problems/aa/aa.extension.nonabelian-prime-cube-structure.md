# Derive central, derived and index-prime structure at order p cubed

*Hard · [Abstract algebra problems](index.md)*

Let G be nonabelian of order p^3, with p prime. Prove $|Z(G)|=p$, \[G,G\]=Z(G), G/Z(G) is C\_p times C\_p, and G has exactly p+1 subgroups of index p. You may use the nontrivial-center theorem for finite p-groups and normality of an index-p subgroup when p is the smallest prime divisor.

<details>
<summary>Model proof</summary>

The small order forces both central structure and an exact subgroup count.

1. The center is nontrivial. It cannot have order p^3 because G is nonabelian, or p^2 because then G/Z(G) is cyclic: if cosets are generated by gZ, all elements g^i\*z commute. Hence \|Z(G)\|=p.
2. The quotient has order p^2 and is noncyclic, so it is C\_p times C\_p. It is abelian, giving \[G,G\] contained in Z(G); nonabelianness makes the derived subgroup nontrivial, so it equals the order-p center.
3. Every index-p subgroup H is normal by the stated theorem and has cyclic quotient G/H. Thus \[G,G\]=Z(G) is contained in H. Such H correspond exactly to order-p subgroups of C\_p^2, whose nonzero vectors split into (p^2-1)/(p-1)=p+1 lines.

- Identify the elementary abelian quotient and force the derived subgroup equality.
- Show all index-p subgroups enter the correspondence before counting lines.
