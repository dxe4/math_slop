# Every subgroup of a cyclic group is cyclic

*Hard · [Abstract algebra problems](index.md)*

Let $G=\langle g\rangle$ be a cyclic group, finite or infinite. Prove that every subgroup $H\le G$ is cyclic.

<summary>Hint 1</summary>

Separate the trivial subgroup before choosing an exponent.

<summary>Hint 2</summary>

For a nontrivial subgroup, use the least positive exponent whose power lies in it and apply integer division to any other exponent.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Model proof</summary>

A least positive exponent generates every exponent occurring in the subgroup.

1. If $H=\{e\}$, it is generated by $e$. Otherwise some nonidentity power of $g$ belongs to $H$; taking its inverse if necessary gives a positive such exponent.
2. Let $d$ be the least positive integer with $g^d\in H$. Then every power of $g^d$ lies in $H$.
3. For any $g^k\in H$, write $k=qd+r$ with $0\le r<d$. Since $H$ is a subgroup, $g^r=g^k(g^d)^{-q}\in H$.
4. Minimality of $d$ forces $r=0$. Thus every element of $H$ is a power of $g^d$.
5. Hence $H=\langle g^d\rangle$, proving the claim in both finite and infinite cases.

**Self-assessment rubric**

- Handle the trivial subgroup and justify the existence of a positive exponent otherwise.
- Choose a least positive exponent using well-ordering.
- Apply Euclidean division and show the remainder power stays in the subgroup.
- Use minimality to prove both inclusions in the claimed cyclic description.

</details>
