# Remove a vanishing obstruction by adding one auxiliary variable

*Hard · [Abstract algebra problems](index.md)*

Assume the weak Nullstellensatz for every finite-variable polynomial ring over an algebraically closed field k. Prove the strong form $I(V(I))=\sqrt I$ for any ideal I in k\[x\_1,...,x\_n\], using the ideal generated by I and1-t\*f. Explain the denominator-clearing step.

<summary>Model proof</summary>

An auxiliary inverse variable converts geometric vanishing into an algebraic power identity.

1. If f^N belongs to I, then f vanishes on every common zero of I, so the radical is contained in I(V(I)). For the reverse take f vanishing on V(I); if f is the zero polynomial the assertion is immediate.
2. In k\[x\_1,...,x\_n,t\], let J=(I,1-t\*f). A common zero would have its x coordinates in V(I), hence f(x)=0, incompatible with1-t\*f=0. The assumed weak theorem forces J to be the whole ring.
3. Thus1 is a finite sum of polynomial multiples of elements a\_i in I plus a multiple of1-t\*f. Substitute t=1/f in the rational-function localization where f is invertible. The last term vanishes and each other coefficient has a denominator which is a power of f. Multiply by a sufficiently large common power f^N to get f^N=sum\_i b\_i\*a\_i in I. Hence f lies in the radical, proving equality.
