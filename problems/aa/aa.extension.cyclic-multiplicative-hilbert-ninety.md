# Construct a cyclic norm-one coboundary

*Hard · [Abstract algebra problems](index.md)*

Let $L/K$ be a finite cyclic Galois extension of degree $n$, generated by $\sigma$. Prove that $a\in L^\times$ has norm $1$ if and only if $a=x/\sigma(x)$ for some $x\in L^\times$. You may use linear independence of distinct field automorphisms.

<details>
<summary>Model proof</summary>

A nonzero automorphism sum constructs the required multiplicative coboundary.

1. If $a=x/\sigma(x)$, its conjugate product telescopes to $1$. Conversely assume $N_{L/K}(a)=1$ and define the partial products $b_i$, so $b_n=1$.
2. Independence of the distinct automorphisms shows $T=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}b_i\sigma^i$ is not the zero operator, since its coefficient of the identity is $1$. Choose $c$ with $x=T(c)\ne0$.
3. The identities $a\sigma(b_i)=b_{i+1}$ and $\sigma^n=1$ give $a\sigma(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}b_{i+1}\sigma^{i+1}(c)=x$. Hence $a=x/\sigma(x)$.

**Self-assessment rubric**

- Show the norm of a multiplicative coboundary telescopes to one.
- Use automorphism independence to choose a nonzero weighted conjugate sum.
- Reindex the partial products to prove the required coboundary equation.

</details>
