# Abelianize the square symmetry group

*Hard · [Abstract algebra problems](index.md)*

Let $D$ be the symmetry group of a square, of order $8$, generated by $r,s$ with $r^4=s^2=e$ and $srs=r^{-1}$. Find $|D/[D,D]|$, where $[D,D]$ is the commutator subgroup.

Compare the subgroup it generates with a quotient in which the generator images commute.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

Find a lower bound on the commutator subgroup and an abelian quotient giving the matching upper bound.

1. Using $[r,s]=rsr^{-1}s^{-1}$ and $s^{-1}=s$, the defining relation gives $[r,s]=r^2$. Thus $\langle r^2\rangle\subseteq[D,D]$.
2. The subgroup $\langle r^2\rangle$ is normal and has order $2$. In its quotient, the rotation satisfies $r^2=e$, so $r^{-1}=r$.
3. The relation $srs=r^{-1}$ then says the quotient generators commute, making that quotient abelian.
4. Hence every commutator lies in $\langle r^2\rangle$, so equality holds.
5. The abelianization has order $8/2=4$.

</details>
