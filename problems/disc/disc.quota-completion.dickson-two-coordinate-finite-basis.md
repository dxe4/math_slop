# Prove a finite basis theorem for upward-closed integer pairs

*Hard · [Discrete mathematics](index.md)*

Order N² coordinatewise, with N={0,1,2,…}. Prove every infinite sequence has i&lt;j with $(a_i,b_i)\le(a_j,b_j)$. Deduce every upward-closed U⊂N² is generated by finitely many minimal pairs: U is the union of the upper quadrants based at those pairs. Include empty U.

<details>
<summary>Model proof</summary>

One coordinate can be made nondecreasing before the other is controlled, ruling out infinite antichains.

1. If the first coordinates are bounded, an infinite constant-first-coordinate subsequence exists by pigeonhole. If they are unbounded, every tail remains unbounded, so successively select later terms with strictly increasing first coordinate. In both cases obtain an infinite subsequence whose first coordinates are nondecreasing.
2. The set of second coordinates on this subsequence has a minimum in N. Choose an index where that minimum occurs and any later subsequence index. Its second coordinate cannot be smaller, while its first coordinate is no smaller by construction. This gives the required i&lt;j.
3. Distinct minimal elements of any subset form an antichain. An infinite such set could be enumerated as a sequence because N² is countable, contradicting the just-proved pair property. Thus U has finitely many minimal elements.
4. For each u∈U, the finite rectangle of pairs coordinatewise below u contains at least u. Choose a member of U in it minimizing the coordinate sum. It is minimal in all of U, since any strictly smaller member would lie in that same rectangle and have smaller sum. Therefore every u lies above a minimal member. Upward closure gives the reverse containment. Empty U has no generators and equals the empty union.

**Self-assessment rubric**

- Justifies both bounded and unbounded cases with indices kept increasing.
- Uses a second-coordinate minimum to obtain the required ordered subsequence pair.
- Rules out an infinite antichain using countability of N².
- Finds a minimal member below every point by a finite-rectangle argument and proves the union description, including U empty.

</details>
