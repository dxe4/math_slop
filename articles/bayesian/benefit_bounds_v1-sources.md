# Sources for bounds on personal benefit

1. [Jin Tian and Judea Pearl, Probabilities of Causation Bounds and Identification, 2000](https://ftp.cs.ucla.edu/pub/stat_ser/R271.pdf), definition 3 and section 3.2.2, equation 14. These give the binary probability of necessity and sufficiency and its sharp bounds from the two causal marginal risks. The lesson uses desirable success as its outcome and proves the bound directly by constructing all compatible four-cell distributions. Its numeric populations, coupling-prior comparisons and finite-data exercises are original calculations.
2. [Scott Allen Mueller, Estimating Individualized Causes of Effects by Leveraging Population Data, 2021](https://ftp.cs.ucla.edu/pub/stat_ser/r506.pdf), section 3.2.2, printed pages 21–22, equations 3.20–3.24. These develop bounds conditional on covariates under conditional ignorability and aggregate them with population weights. The lesson's equally weighted two-stratum example is derived separately, with endpoint tables proving attainability under its declared baseline assumptions.

The existing [causal bridge](../../books/bayesian/adv-04_causal.md) already explains randomization, adjustment, confounding and overlap. It also distinguishes the average effect from the unobserved joint distribution. The existing [decision chapter](../../books/bayesian/12_decisions.md) distinguishes marginal action predictions from paired counterfactual outcomes. This additive investigation develops constructive personal-benefit bounds and coupling-prior sensitivity without changing those chapters or repeating their confounding analysis.
