# Sources for matched capture and unseen populations

1. [Allen B. Downey, Think Bayes second edition, chapter 15, Mark and Recapture](https://allendowney.github.io/ThinkBayes2/chap15.html), sections 15.1–15.2. These derive the hypergeometric overlap likelihood and update a finite population-size prior. The small population, exact tables, changed overlaps, independent audit and prior-support comparison here are separate teaching calculations.
2. [SciPy hypergeom documentation, Notes](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.stats.hypergeom.html). This defines the ordinary hypergeometric PMF, feasible support and its parameter convention. SciPy's total-population, marked-count, draw-count order differs from Wolfram's order.
3. [SciPy nchypergeom_fisher documentation, Notes](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.stats.nchypergeom_fisher.html) and [nchypergeom_wallenius documentation, Notes](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.stats.nchypergeom_wallenius.html). These distinguish Fisher's normalized odds-weighted PMF from Wallenius's sequential weighted draws without replacement. The accepted-batch rejection experiment is derived explicitly in the lesson, with the same physical population retained through rejected proposals.
4. [Agner Fog, Biased Urn Theory, June 16 2024](https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/BiasedUrn/vignettes/UrnTheory.pdf), sections 2–6. This primary author account defines both biased urn laws and explains why their shared informal name does not specify a sampling mechanism. Our fixed-size accepted-batch construction realizes Fisher's conditional law; it is not the sequential remaining-weight experiment.
5. [Wolfram FisherHypergeometricDistribution](https://reference.wolfram.com/language/ref/FisherHypergeometricDistribution.html) and [WalleniusHypergeometricDistribution](https://reference.wolfram.com/language/ref/WalleniusHypergeometricDistribution.html), Details and Background. Their signatures order sample size, marked population count, total population and odds. The lesson maps those arguments to its own symbols and keeps the two mechanisms distinct.
6. [William A. Link, Individual heterogeneity and identifiability in capture–recapture models, 2004](https://museucienciesjournals.cat/en/abc/issue/27-1-2004-abc/individual-heterogeneity-and-identifiability-in-capture-recapture-models.pdf), printed page 88 and Example 1 on pages 88–89. The closed-population heterogeneous model allows individual capture probabilities to follow an unknown distribution. Its examples compare identical positive capture-frequency distributions with different never-seen probabilities. This supports caution about restrictions on detection heterogeneity, not a blanket claim that every capture–recapture design is invalid or that all complete finite-population likelihoods are identical.

Existing E01 knows the population register and predicts unobserved outcomes. The existing [detection module](./detection_model_v1-lesson.md) studies a Poisson event rate and its recording mechanism. The present investigation instead learns unknown closed-population size from identity matches, and predicts existing members not in the recorded union.

Primary passages inspected 2026-10-20. Both prior supports are illustrative proper beliefs declared before capture, not empirically established capacities. The equations and exact comparisons concern synthetic protocols and do not establish practical field assumptions or learning gains for this particular lesson.
