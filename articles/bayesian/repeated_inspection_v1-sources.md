# Sources for repeated binary inspection

1. [Margaret Sullivan Pepe and Holly Janes, Insights into latent class analysis of diagnostic test performance, 2007](https://academic.oup.com/biostatistics/article/8/2/474/232752), section 2, printed pages 475–476; section 3, page 476; Appendix A, pages 481–482. This develops a conditional-independence latent-class framework with separate operating characteristics for three tests. The lesson's identical-repeat moment recurrence, pair-compatible family and sharp feasibility proof are derived separately. The original paper's analytic formulas must be read with the published correction below.
2. [Anton K. Formann and Dankmar Böhning, Letter to the editor, 2008](https://academic.oup.com/biostatistics/article/9/4/777/259221), printed pages 777–778. This corrects the prevalence formula's sign and the operating-characteristic factor in Pepe–Janes, and explains that direct finite-data inversion can produce parameters outside $[0,1]$ while a constrained likelihood fit gives a different boundary solution. The lesson uses its own polynomial derivation and distinguishes exact population feasibility from finite empirical frequencies; it does not present an arbitrary empirical inverse as a maximum-likelihood estimate.
3. [S. L. Hui and S. D. Walter, Estimating the Error Rates of Diagnostic Tests, 1980](https://jhanley.biostat.mcgill.ca/bios601/CandH-ch0102/HuiWalter1980.pdf), section 2, printed pages 168–169. Their design uses two tests in populations with different prevalences, invariant test performance across populations and conditional independence given the latent state. It is a different source of information from three identical repeats in one population; the lesson does not claim that every pair of tests or repeats identifies unknown calibration.

The existing [Bayes chapter](../../books/bayesian/01_bayes.md) states its known inspection-performance assumptions and already distinguishes conditional independence from copied or dependent reports. The [observation-process module](../../books/bayesian/adv-03_observation_process.md) discusses unknown Gaussian calibration and the role of independent replication. This additive investigation develops binary latent-class ambiguity and exact population triple identification without changing those correct chapters.

The two paired-data-equivalent systems, their full compatible family, fresh-item versus same-item updates, sharp moment region, irrational-root example and finite-count counterexample are original teaching calculations under the stated stable-state, identical-repeat and conditional-independence model. Its uniqueness result concerns the nondegenerate two-class model, not arbitrary latent propensity distributions. A single observed triple does not provide exact population moments or establish the model assumptions.

Primary passages inspected 2026-10-20. The references support the statistical framework and its limitations; they do not provide engineering calibration evidence or establish learning gains for this particular lesson.
