# Sources for competing first failures

1. [Terry Therneau, Cynthia Crowson and Elizabeth Atkinson, Multi-state models and competing risks, September 9 2026](https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/survival/vignettes/compete.pdf), section 2.1, equation 3, printed page 3. The cumulative-incidence formula uses overall event-free survival and the particular cause hazard. Printed pages 8–9 distinguish actual competing-event incidence from curves formed by treating the other event as censored in a separate Kaplan–Meier calculation. The lesson derives its constant-hazard state equations directly and treats intervention interpretation as an additional assumption.
2. [Terry Therneau, Cynthia Crowson and Elizabeth Atkinson, Competing Risks and Multistate Models, May 2 2024](https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/survivalVignettes/vignettes/tutorial.html), sections 1.1 and 1.2. These show that cause-specific hazards and cumulative hazards can be estimated separately, including representations that code the competing exit as censored, while absolute state probabilities require the joint transition system. This distinction prevents the overbroad claim that coding the other exit zero is inherently an invalid cause-hazard analysis.

The [existing survival module A12](../../books/bayesian/adv-12_survival.md) already develops the single-event exponential exposure likelihood, administrative censoring, Gamma updating and posterior prediction. It explicitly places competing risks outside its bounded scope. This extension develops the next first-exit case without changing its original model or evidence.

The bearing/controller state model, four synthetic records, independent proper Gamma priors, common-rate Gamma–Beta proof, unequal-rate incidence integrals, prior sensitivity, zero-event transfer and six practice tasks are derived for this investigation. They are mathematical teaching examples, not engineering evidence about real machines. Actual first-exit incidence, a cause-1-only exponential net risk, a fitted Kaplan–Meier estimate and an intervention effect are kept distinct.

Primary passages inspected 2026-10-20. The references support the mathematical and statistical distinctions. They do not establish learning gains for this particular lesson or identify the effect of physically removing a failure cause.
