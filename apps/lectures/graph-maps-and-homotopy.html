Optional written capstone · After Homotopy 4

Words through
a map.

Signed counts do not remember the order of a journey. What happens when a function replaces one journey by another? Construct an inverse up to homotopy, then make the entire deformation continuous.

This is an untimed extension to the four-lecture route. Start with a written attempt; open the hints and full answer when useful. The page works without JavaScript.

Set up the map · Try the task · Staged hints · Return to the lectures

Keep the common vertex fixed

Let W = S¹ ∨ S¹ be the figure eight: two circles joined at their common vertex v. Model each source circle as [0,1] with 0 and 1 identified. Let α and β run once positively around the two circles. Thus α(0)=α(1)=β(0)=β(1)=v; the interiors of these parameter intervals run through their respective nonvertex points.

Preparation. Use the figure-eight alphabet and basepoint convention, retracing and unique reduced words, and endpoint-fixed input reparametrization. Joining continuous pieces uses finite closed pasting. The circle quotient argument will let a continuous square family descend to a circle cylinder. These facts are prerequisites, not conclusions from a picture.

Write a and b for the two positive circuits. Capitals reverse direction: A means the reverse of a, and B the reverse of b. Read words left to right. The empty word is the constant loop. Signed counts are the number of a letters minus A letters and the number of b letters minus B letters. In W, equality of the complete reduced words classifies based loop homotopy; equal counts alone do not.

Use half-time concatenation for any composable paths p and r:

(p∗r)(s)={p(2⁢s)0≤s≤12r(2⁢s−1)12≤s≤1

A bar over a path means reversal: λ̄(s)=λ(1−s). Substitution produces an ordered word, but its actual composite path need not give equal time to all letters. Do not silently replace a parametrized map by its reduced word.

Define the based map F by its restrictions to the two circles:

F∘α=α∗β,F∘β=β

F(v)=v. The two displayed image paths are continuous loops at v, so they define continuous maps on their source circles. Those circles are closed subspaces of the finite Hausdorff graph W and agree at v; the finite closed pasting lemma gives a continuous map F:W→W.

Relative condition for this task. Every requested homotopy fixes v at every time. This asks for a based homotopy inverse. A free loop homotopy may move its starting point, and is a different requirement.

Attempt the whole construction

  1. Find F’s complete image words for A and B. Transport u=aB and w=Ba through F, then reduce. Record the input and output signed-count pairs. A learner says equal input counts identify the based classes of the transported loops. Decide with these two examples.
  2. Construct a continuous based map G:W→W such that both G∘F and F∘G are homotopic to idW relative to {v}. Specify G on both circle edges and check both composites. Give actual jointly continuous whole-graph homotopies: a list of cancellations or an inverse integer matrix is incomplete.
  3. Find two distinct source points with the same F-image. Explain why an inverse up to homotopy does not make F a homeomorphism.

Hint 1 · Reverse the whole image

To image a reversed edge, reverse its complete image path: reverse the letter order and each direction. Applying F to a concatenation applies it to each piece in the same left-to-right order.

Hint 2 · Undo a trailing excursion

F(a)=ab. Try making G’s a-edge follow a and then reverse b, while leaving its b-edge alone. Check G∘F and F∘G separately, on both edges.

Hint 3 · Turn cancellation into a map homotopy

First reassociate the actual composite path by interpolating its parameter input. Contract the retracing with the existing endpoint-fixed formula. Remove the remaining constant pause. Keep β fixed on the other circle. The two circle-cylinder families must agree along the entire vertex cylinder before pasting.

Check your argument

Complete worked answer · Words, both homotopies, and the quotient check

1. Transport order, not just counts

F(A)=BA, because the reverse of ab traverses b backward before a backward. Also F(B)=B.

Same counts, different transported based classes

aB(1, −1)abBa(1, 0)

The reduced words a and Bab differ. The covering-tree classification therefore gives different based classes in W. Equal counts, even before and after transport, do not identify them. They are conjugate and freely homotopic; this does not contradict the existing based-versus-free task. The fixed-vertex condition matters.

2. Define G as an actual graph map

G∘α=α∗β¯,G∘β=β

Set G(v)=v. In words G(a)=aB, G(b)=b, G(A)=bA and G(B)=B. As for F, its continuous image loops descend to the two source circles and agree at their vertex. Closed pasting defines a continuous based graph map.

G∘F on a has word (aB)b, reducing to a; F∘G on a has word (ab)B, also reducing to a. Both composites send b to b. Their exact a-circle paths are (α∗β̄)∗β and (α∗β)∗β̄, respectively. These paths have successive letter durations ¼, ¼ and ½, so cancellation has not yet made either parametrized map literally the identity.

3. Reassociate the exact composite

For G∘F put λ=β̄; for F∘G put λ=β. In either case the actual a-circle path is

Pλ=(α∗λ)∗λ¯

Its letter breakpoints are 0, ¼, ½, 1. Define the following continuous input change:

h(s)={s20≤s≤12s−1412≤s≤342⁢s−134≤s≤1
Jλ(s,t)=Pλ((1−t)⁢s+t⁢h(s))

The pieces of h meet at h(½)=¼ and h(¾)=½, remain in [0,1], and fix 0 and 1. Interpolating s and h(s) stays in [0,1], so J is jointly continuous by composition and keeps both path endpoints v. At time 0 it is Pλ. At time 1 it is exactly α∗(λ∗λ̄), now with letter breakpoints 0, ½, ¾, 1.

4. Retrace, then remove the pause

The existing contraction of a path followed by its reverse gives

Rλ(u,t)={λ(2⁢u⁢(1−t))0≤u≤12λ(2⁢(1−u)⁢(1−t))12≤u≤1
Kλ(s,t)={α(2⁢s)0≤s≤12Rλ(2⁢s−1,t)12≤s≤1

The R pieces agree at u=½, where both equal λ(1−t). Its endpoint values are λ(0)=v. The K pieces agree at s=½ because α(1)=Rλ(0,t)=v. Each is continuous on a closed half-square, so closed pasting proves joint continuity into W. At time 0, K is α∗(λ∗λ̄). At time 1 it is α followed by a constant pause, namely α(min(2s,1)). Both parameter endpoints remain v.

Remove that pause by another input interpolation:

L(s,t)=α((1−t)⁢min(2⁢s,1)+t⁢s)

The input remains in [0,1] and fixes 0 and 1. Thus L is jointly continuous, starts at α(min(2s,1)), and ends at α, with endpoints fixed. Points of W were never added together.

5. Descend the full family, then paste the graph

Join the three stages on thirds of deformation time:

Hλ(s,t)={Jλ(s,3⁢t)0≤t≤13Kλ(s,3⁢t−1)13≤t≤23L(s,3⁢t−2)23≤t≤1

At t=⅓, Jλ(s,1)=Kλ(s,0). At t=⅔, Kλ(s,1)=L(s,0). The time pieces are jointly continuous and agree on their closed seams, so Hλ is a continuous map of the full square I×I into W, where I=[0,1]. Its s=0 and s=1 edges both equal v for every t.

Let q:I→S¹ identify the endpoints. The product map

q×idI:I×I→S1×I

is a continuous surjection from a compact square onto a Hausdorff circle cylinder. It is therefore closed and a quotient map. The already jointly continuous Hλ is constant on each of its fibers, since the identified endpoint-edge values agree for every time. Hence the full square family factors through a jointly continuous circle-cylinder homotopy. Checking only separate time slices would not supply this conclusion.

On the b-circle use β(s) for every time. The two closed circle cylinders cover W×I and meet exactly on {v}×I. Both homotopies there equal v for every time. Finite closed pasting now gives a jointly continuous map W×I→W, fixing v. With λ=β̄ it runs from G∘F to idW; with λ=β it runs from F∘G to idW. This constructs both required based homotopies directly. No general converse from a fundamental-group isomorphism to homotopy equivalence is being used.

6. A homotopy inverse need not undo every point

α(½) is not v in the source a-circle, yet

F(α(12))=(α∗β)(12)=v=F(v)

Thus F is not injective and cannot be a homeomorphism. G supplies inverses up to the two based homotopies above; it is not a pointwise inverse function.

One moving vertex, two circles

Optional, untimed follow-up. The capstone above kept the vertex fixed. Now allow it to move during a homotopy, while both endpoint maps still send v to v. This uses the same figure eight, left-to-right word convention, endpoint-fixed reparametrization and closed-pasting arguments. Try it on paper or in your notebook, then open a hint or worked answer when useful.

Keep the existing distinction between based classes and free homotopy of one loop. In this path-connected graph, two loops based at v are freely homotopic exactly when their based classes are conjugate. A homotopy of a whole graph map has one vertex path shared by every restriction.

Attempt: can separate free homotopies be assembled?

Define a new map K:W→W by K(v)=v, K∘α=α, and let K∘β traverse babAB, giving each of its five letters one fifth of the parameter interval. At every join and at both endpoints the value is v. The circle quotient and finite closed pasting therefore define an actual continuous graph map. Its induced based classes satisfy K∗(a)=a and K∗(b)=babAB.

  1. Show that idW and K have freely homotopic restrictions on each source circle. Does this justify pasting independently chosen restriction homotopies?
  2. Decide whether an ordinary homotopy H:W×[0,1]→W from idW to K exists. Use the source loop γ=α∗β, with the existing half-time concatenation. Write its complete K-image word and transport it from each of three starting sheets.
  3. Explain the simultaneous requirement imposed by h(t)=H(v,t). Why would displaying one mismatched pair of chosen tracks fail to rule out every possible H?

For the exact finite calculation, a and A swap sheets 1 and 2; b and B swap sheets 2 and 3. Read each word from left to right. Inverse letters act by inverse permutations, which here happen to be the same swaps. You may use that conjugate permutations have equal cycle types, hence equal numbers of fixed sheets.

Choose a hint

Hint 1 · Compare the two individual restrictions

Write babAB as (ba)b(ba)−1. The a-restrictions agree, and the b-restrictions have conjugate based classes.

Hint 2 · Precompose the proposed whole homotopy

The composite loop ab has K-image a·babAB. If H existed, (s,t)↦H(γ(s),t) would give a free homotopy between those two whole image loops. Keep all six letters in their order.

Hint 3 · Use an obstruction that rules out every track

Compute both endpoint permutations. One is a three-cycle and the other is the identity. Conjugating by any permutation preserves fixed-sheet count. This excludes every possible common track, rather than just the two tracks you first selected.

Complete answer · Individual conjugacies and a simultaneous obstruction

1. Both separate free comparisons succeed

The a-restrictions are identical. Put d=ba; then d−1=AB and K∗(b)=d b d−1. The one-loop free-homotopy/conjugacy theorem gives a free homotopy between β and K∘β. Thus both individual conclusions are true.

This does not supply a whole-map homotopy. Two continuous circle-cylinder families can be pasted on W×[0,1] only if they agree at the common vertex for every time. The constant track chosen for the a-circle and a track of class AB chosen for the b-circle do not agree. That observation rules out this chosen pair only; a different pair might conceivably agree. We need an obstruction to all choices.

2. A whole homotopy would provide one conjugating class

If H0=idW and H1=K, its vertex path h(t)=H(v,t) starts and ends at v. Precomposing H with any based source loop gives a jointly continuous loop family whose two parameter endpoints both follow h. Traversing the boundary of its parameter square gives the necessary relation

x=[h]K∗(x)[h]−1,x∈π1(W,v)

The square boundary, read from its lower left corner, follows x, then h, then the reverse K-image loop, then h backward. This fixes the conjugation direction. The individual restrictions only give “for each generator, there is a conjugator”; a whole H requires “there is one conjugator for both generators”:

∀x∈{a,b}∃cx:x=cxK∗(x)cx−1⇏∃c∀x∈{a,b}:x=cK∗(x)c−1

Here c and cx are based loop classes at v. If the same c worked on a and b, it would also work on their product: the adjacent c−1c cancels. A whole-map homotopy therefore cannot choose a new vertex track for each generator or each source loop. We use only this necessary condition, not a general converse for arbitrary spaces.

3. Transport one composite source loop exactly

For γ=α∗β, the id-image has word ab and the K-image has complete word ababAB. As an actual path, K∘γ gives α half of the interval and each of the five later letters one tenth. It is not literally an equal-six-time path. Ordered word transport is unaffected by endpoint-fixed changes of speed.

Let Tw be the permutation obtained by following w. Left-to-right travel means

Tuw=Tw∘Tu,Tuwu−1=Tu−1∘Tw∘Tu

Deleting adjacent inverse letters does not change transport, so this finite action is well-defined on based classes. The second formula shows that conjugate words must give conjugate permutations. It is an exact algebraic test, not a sampled drawing of a homotopy.

Thus Tab sends (1,2,3) to (3,1,2), a three-cycle with no fixed sheet. TababAB is the identity and fixes all three sheets. Conjugating any permutation preserves its fixed-sheet count: a bijection simply relabels the fixed sheets. Hence these permutations cannot be conjugate, and the original words cannot be conjugate.

But precomposing the supposed H with γ would freely homotope these two loops. Their nonconjugacy contradicts that necessary consequence. Therefore no ordinary homotopy from idW to K exists, even though both separate generator restrictions are freely homotopic.

Limit of the finite test. Identity transport does not mean a word is null-homotopic. The word ababAB is already nonempty and reduced. It has the same finite transport as (ab)3, because the assigned swaps are involutions, but those are different reduced free-group words. Equal finite actions, or equal cycle types, do not establish free-group equality or conjugacy. Differing cycle types provide the obstruction used here.

Complete changed-task answer · A compatible whole-graph homotopy

1. One class works simultaneously

K̂∗(a)=a=a a A and K̂∗(b)=abA as based classes. The same c=A satisfies a=A K̂∗(a) a and b=A K̂∗(b) a after adjacent inverse cancellation. This gives the required common conjugating class. It does not by itself construct the map homotopy; the next two stages do.

The composite source word ab now has image aabA. Its endpoint permutation sends (1,2,3) to (2,3,1), another three-cycle. The earlier obstruction therefore disappears, but this matching cycle type is not our proof of existence.

2. Insert pauses without moving the vertex

Let I=[0,1]. Define a continuous input map ρ:I→I by

ρ(s)={00≤s≤133s−113≤s≤23123≤s≤1

For stage time u∈I, leave the a-circle fixed and put P(β(s),u)=β((1−u)s+uρ(s)). The input stays in I, fixes 0 and 1, and is jointly continuous. Hence P starts at idW and ends with β traversed on the middle third and constant v on the outer thirds. Both circle restrictions have vertex value v throughout, so quotient descent and closed pasting give a based whole-graph homotopy P.

3. Move both circle endpoints along the same path

Extend α periodically to a continuous map α̃:ℝ→W: α̃(s+n)=α(s) for s∈I and integer n. This is the circle parametrization, not a discontinuous choice of a coordinate representative. Put h(t)=α(1−t) and define the tail κt from h(t) to v by

κt(r)=α(1−t+tr),r,t∈I

On the a-circle use the rotation M(α(s),t)=α̃(s−t). On the b-circle define

M(β(s),t)={κt(3s)0≤s≤13β(3s−1)13≤s≤23κt(3−3s)23≤s≤1

At both interior seams κt(1)=α(1)=v matches β(0)=β(1)=v. At both parameter endpoints, the b-family equals κt(0)=h(t). The a-family has endpoints α̃(−t)=α̃(1−t)=h(t), so the two families agree on the entire moving vertex cylinder.

Each formula is jointly continuous on its closed parameter region. The interior seams agree, and each circle's identified endpoints agree at every time. The compact-to-Hausdorff quotient argument used above descends the full families to circle cylinders; finite closed pasting then gives a jointly continuous M:W×I→W. No numerical samples are needed for these continuity and compatibility claims.

At t=0 the tail is constant v: M starts exactly at the paused map P(·,1). At t=1 the a-rotation is α̃(s−1)=α(s), while the b-path traverses a, b and A on three equal intervals. Thus M(·,1)=K̂ as an actual parametrized map, not only as a word class.

4. Join the stages

Use P(x,2τ) for 0≤τ≤½ and M(x,2τ−1) for ½≤τ≤1. Their values agree for every x at τ=½ because P(x,1)=M(x,0). Closed time-pasting yields the required homotopy from idW to K̂. Its vertex is fixed during the first stage and follows h during the second, so its track class is A, exactly as the simultaneous equations required.
