Using the anchors

Use the same supplied reference card in each phase. It permits the definition of endpoint-fixed homotopy, straight interpolation with a codomain check, winding invariance in the punctured plane, and the closed-loop obstruction formed from two endpoint-fixed paths. Applying a supplied fact is assessed here; proving the winding or lifting theorem is not applicable to these tasks.

Throughout this key, I = [0, 1], X = ℂ ∖ {0}, u(s) = s(1 − s), and ωk(s) = e2πik s. A based homotopy at 1 has both vertical edges fixed at 1. A free loop homotopy requires its two vertical edges to agree, but permits their common value to move.

For each task, record Missing/incorrect, Partial, or Clear separately for each of the four dimensions below. Copy a short piece of response evidence and note the precise gap. An unanswered item or a missed follow-up is missing data, not an incorrect mathematical response. Keep an original attempt separate from later helped or revised work.

  • Definition/theorem: Does the response choose and apply the relevant mathematical relation or fact?
  • Hypotheses/scope: Does it identify the domain, target, relative conditions, and restrictions needed here?
  • Witness/construction: Does it supply an exact valid formula, counterexample, or invariant calculation?
  • Proof/quantifiers: Does its argument cover every permitted input, and support the stated conclusion?

All four dimensions apply to every core task as written. Use “not applicable” only for an obligation genuinely removed in a documented adaptation, with the reason recorded; it must not replace an omitted required check. In particular, not reproducing a theorem proof from the supplied card is not a deficiency. A theorem name is also not compulsory if the response states and correctly uses the fact.

Accept every mathematically valid method, including a different formula, witness, or proof. The displayed anchors guide judgment; they are not an exhaustive answer matcher. Where reviewers disagree, retain both judgments and their reasons before agreeing on a description. Do not add the dimensions into a pass, mastery, or phase total. These forms are not difficulty-equated, and uncontrolled changes between phases do not establish a learning effect.

Form A · baseline keys

A1 · A closed family with a moving basepoint

Prompt: Assess K(s, t) = e2πi(s+t) as a based homotopy at 1 from ω1 to itself. Explain exactly what passes and what fails, and give a valid based replacement if needed.

Key: K is jointly continuous and lies on the unit circle, hence in X. At t = 0 and 1 it equals ω1. For every t,

K(0, t) = K(1, t) = e2πit.

Thus every slice is a closed loop, but the common endpoint is −1 at t = ½. It is a free loop homotopy, not a based homotopy at 1. The stationary family J(s, t) = e2πis is a valid based replacement: it is jointly continuous, nonzero, has the required time slices, and both endpoints are 1 for all t.

Alternatives: Any time where e2πit ≠ 1 is a valid failure witness. Any fully verified based self-homotopy is an acceptable replacement. Different winding numbers are not involved; rejecting this K cannot exclude all replacements.

Definition/theorem
Missing/incorrect: Treats closed slices as sufficient for based homotopy. Partial: Mentions fixed endpoints without distinguishing the moving common endpoint. Clear: Correctly distinguishes free loop homotopy from based homotopy.
Hypotheses/scope
Missing/incorrect: Claims a zero or wrong time slice without a valid check. Partial: Checks only the failed edge. Clear: Identifies joint continuity, nonzero values, both time slices, closure, and the fixed-basepoint failure; verifies these for the replacement as relevant.
Witness/construction
Missing/incorrect: Gives no valid witness or replacement. Partial: Supplies an exact failed edge value or a valid replacement, but not both. Clear: Supplies both, for example the value −1 at t = ½ and the stationary family.
Proof/quantifiers
Missing/incorrect: Uses a picture or concludes no based homotopy can exist. Partial: Reaches the right verdict with incomplete universal checks. Clear: Uses an exact counterexample for this formula and verifies the replacement for every pair (s, t).

A2 · Construct inside a positive half-plane

Prompt: Join α(s) = 1 + iu(s) to β(s) = 1 + u(s) − iu(s) by an endpoint-fixed homotopy in X. Verify the construction.

Key: Both endpoints of both paths are 1, since u(0) = u(1) = 0. Straight interpolation gives

H(s, t) = (1 − t)α(s) + tβ(s)
= 1 + tu(s) + i(1 − 2t)u(s).

This is jointly continuous by sums and products of continuous functions. Its time-zero and time-one slices are α and β, and H(0, t) = H(1, t) = 1 for every t. Since u(s) ≥ 0 on I, its real part is 1 + t u(s) ≥ 1. Hence it never equals 0 anywhere on I × I.

Alternatives: Interpolation within the convex right half-plane is a valid argument if the response verifies both paths lie there. A two-stage homotopy through the constant path at 1 also works, provided its seam, time slices, relative endpoints, and avoidance are checked. Convexity of all of X is false and is not needed.

Definition/theorem
Missing/incorrect: Gives only another path or invokes false convexity of X. Partial: Chooses interpolation without identifying the endpoint-fixed requirement. Clear: Constructs a two-parameter endpoint-fixed homotopy, using a valid safe-region argument.
Hypotheses/scope
Missing/incorrect: Omits the puncture or changes the requested endpoints. Partial: Checks some boundary conditions or avoidance only at selected times. Clear: Checks joint continuity, the two time slices, both fixed endpoints, and membership in X on the whole parameter square.
Witness/construction
Missing/incorrect: Gives an invalid or undefined family. Partial: Gives a correct candidate without an exact avoidance estimate. Clear: Gives a valid family and an explicit estimate such as Re H ≥ 1.
Proof/quantifiers
Missing/incorrect: Relies on a plot or a few values. Partial: Has a valid strategy with an omitted boundary or continuity argument. Clear: Justifies every required property for all s, t ∈ I.

A3 · Different winding, different target

Prompt: Compare γ(s) = (1 + u(s))e2πi(s+s²) and δ(s) = e−2πis. Can they be based homotopic in X? How does the answer change in all of ℂ? Justify both conclusions.

Key: Both are continuous based loops at 1. The radius 1 + u(s) is positive, so the normalized γ has angle lift θ(s) = s + s2, starting at 0 and ending at 2. The normalized δ has lift −s, ending at −1. Their windings are 2 and −1. Winding is invariant under a based homotopy in X, so none exists there.

In ℂ, use H(s, t) = (1 − t)γ(s) + tδ(s). This is jointly continuous, has the required time slices, and fixes the common endpoint 1 because both paths have that endpoint. All complex values are allowed, so no puncture-avoidance proof is required. Winding about 0 cannot obstruct a homotopy allowed to pass through 0.

Alternatives: A correctly stated degree calculation after normalization or an equivalent lifted-angle obstruction is valid. Any verified endpoint-fixed construction in ℂ is acceptable. Failure of straight interpolation in X, by itself, would not prove impossibility there.

Definition/theorem
Missing/incorrect: Infers equivalence from the common circle image or applies winding invariance in all of ℂ. Partial: Names winding without connecting unequal values to nonexistence. Clear: Correctly applies winding invariance in X and interpolation in ℂ.
Hypotheses/scope
Missing/incorrect: Ignores closure, basepoint, or the changed codomain. Partial: Assumes these conditions without checking them. Clear: Establishes continuous nonzero based loops at 1, notes the positive radius, and scopes the obstruction to homotopies avoiding 0.
Witness/construction
Missing/incorrect: Has incorrect windings and no valid whole-plane construction. Partial: Gives correct windings or a valid whole-plane family, with the other component incomplete. Clear: Gives exact lifts or equivalent calculations yielding 2 and −1, and a valid ℂ-valued family.
Proof/quantifiers
Missing/incorrect: Merely rejects one candidate or trusts the drawing. Partial: Gives the correct two verdicts with an incomplete obstruction or construction check. Clear: Excludes every based homotopy in X by the invariant and verifies a whole-plane homotopy for all parameters.

Form B · immediate keys

B1 · The basepoint returns, but moves in between

Prompt: Assess K(s, t) = e2πi(−s+t(1−t)) as a based homotopy at 1 from ω−1 to itself. Explain what passes and what fails, and give a valid based replacement if needed.

Key: The formula is jointly continuous and nonzero. Since t(1 − t) = 0 at times 0 and 1, those slices equal ω−1. For each time,

K(0, t) = K(1, t) = e2πit(1−t).

It is a free loop homotopy. At t = ½ the common endpoint is eπi/2 = i, so it is not based at 1. Use the stationary replacement J(s, t) = e−2πis; it is jointly continuous and nonzero, has the required time slices, and fixes both endpoints at 1 for every time.

Alternatives: Any exact time with a common endpoint different from 1 suffices. For 0 < t < 1, the quantity t(1 − t) lies in (0, ¼], so every such time gives a failure. Other fully verified based self-homotopies are acceptable.

Definition/theorem
Missing/incorrect: Takes returning to the basepoint at times 0 and 1 as sufficient. Partial: Notices an endpoint issue but does not distinguish free and based homotopy. Clear: Correctly identifies a free loop homotopy with a moving common basepoint.
Hypotheses/scope
Missing/incorrect: Incorrectly claims nonclosure or a zero value. Partial: Checks only a failed boundary value. Clear: Checks continuity, nonzero values, the two time slices, closed slices, and the failed fixed-basepoint condition, then verifies the replacement.
Witness/construction
Missing/incorrect: Gives neither an exact failed edge value nor a valid replacement. Partial: Supplies one of them. Clear: Gives both, such as the endpoint i at time ½ and the stationary family.
Proof/quantifiers
Missing/incorrect: Infers general impossibility from this failure. Partial: Gives correct conclusions with a missing universal replacement check. Clear: Rejects this formula by one exact witness and validates a replacement for every parameter pair.

B2 · A second safe interpolation

Prompt: Join α(s) = 1 − 2iu(s) to β(s) = 1 + 2u(s) + iu(s) by an endpoint-fixed homotopy in X. Verify the construction.

Key: Both paths have both endpoints equal to 1. Straight interpolation gives

H(s, t) = 1 + 2tu(s) + i(−2 + 3t)u(s).

The formula is jointly continuous, equals α at time 0 and β at time 1, and equals 1 at s = 0 and 1 for every time. Because u(s) ≥ 0 and t ≥ 0, Re H(s, t) = 1 + 2t u(s) ≥ 1. It is therefore X-valued on the entire square.

Alternatives: A convex right-half-plane argument or a verified two-stage route through the constant path at 1 is valid. A plot of intermediate paths is evidence about a drawing, not the required proof of avoidance.

Definition/theorem
Missing/incorrect: Supplies only a path or claims X is convex. Partial: Selects interpolation without its endpoint-fixed interpretation. Clear: Uses the homotopy definition and a valid safe-region argument.
Hypotheses/scope
Missing/incorrect: Allows a zero or changes the prescribed endpoints. Partial: Checks only some time slices, relative edges, or membership conditions. Clear: Checks joint continuity, both time slices, both fixed endpoints, and avoidance on all of I × I.
Witness/construction
Missing/incorrect: Gives an invalid family. Partial: Gives a correct formula without a valid uniform avoidance argument. Clear: Gives a valid family and an exact estimate such as Re H ≥ 1.
Proof/quantifiers
Missing/incorrect: Relies on samples or appearance. Partial: Gives a sound strategy with an omitted verification. Clear: Justifies the construction for every s, t ∈ I.

B3 · A clockwise nonlinear angle

Prompt: Compare γ(s) = (1 + 2u(s))e−2πi(s+s²) and δ(s) = e2πis. Can they be based homotopic in X? How does the answer change in all of ℂ? Justify both conclusions.

Key: The positive radius 1 + 2u(s) and the endpoint values show that both are continuous nonzero based loops at 1. A normalized angle lift for γ is −s − s2, giving winding −2. The lift s for δ gives winding 1. Since winding is invariant under based homotopies in X, no such homotopy exists there.

In ℂ, the family H(s, t) = (1 − t)γ(s) + tδ(s) is jointly continuous, has the correct time slices, and fixes both endpoints at 1. It is allowed to take the value 0. The whole-plane claim therefore has a valid construction.

Alternatives: A correct normalized degree or lifted-angle argument can replace the displayed lift calculation. Accept a different verified whole-plane homotopy. A wrong sign in a winding is a calculation error; whether the response otherwise states and uses the obstruction correctly should be recorded separately.

Definition/theorem
Missing/incorrect: Uses equality of images as a homotopy test or extends winding invariance across 0. Partial: Names the invariant without its obstruction implication. Clear: Uses unequal winding in X and a valid construction in ℂ.
Hypotheses/scope
Missing/incorrect: Ignores the based-loop conditions or changed target. Partial: Assumes positive radius or endpoint values without checking them. Clear: Checks the loops at 1 and positivity, and explains why the obstruction applies only while avoiding 0.
Witness/construction
Missing/incorrect: Gives no correct invariant calculation or whole-plane family. Partial: Completes only one, or makes a winding calculation error within an otherwise relevant method. Clear: Obtains exact windings −2 and 1 and gives a valid ℂ-valued family.
Proof/quantifiers
Missing/incorrect: Rejects just one candidate as if it excluded all. Partial: Has the right conclusions with a gap in their justification. Clear: Excludes every based homotopy in X and verifies a construction in ℂ for the whole square.

Form C · delayed keys

C1 · An affirmative audit in a nonconvex target

Prompt: Someone rejects U(s, t) = (cos 2πs, [1 + t(1 − t)] sin 2πs) as a based self-homotopy of the unit loop because the punctured plane is nonconvex. Assess that rejection and verify your verdict.

Key: The rejection is invalid. Nonconvexity prevents a general interpolation guarantee; it does not invalidate an arbitrary particular family. This U is jointly continuous. At times 0 and 1 it is the unit loop, and at s = 0 and 1 it is (1, 0) at every time. Let a = 1 + t(1 − t). For t ∈ I, a ≥ 1, so

‖U(s, t)‖² = cos²(2πs) + a² sin²(2πs)
≥ cos²(2πs) + sin²(2πs) = 1.

Consequently U is a valid based homotopy in ℝ² ∖ {0} from the unit loop to itself.

Alternatives: A direct zero test works: if the first coordinate vanishes, the sine is ±1 and its coefficient a is positive, so the second coordinate is nonzero. This covers all pairs without requiring the norm bound. Equality of the two endpoint loops alone would not validate this particular family.

Definition/theorem
Missing/incorrect: Accepts the blanket nonconvexity rejection or uses same winding as sufficient to validate this formula. Partial: Says nonconvexity is inconclusive without assessing U. Clear: Separates a general sufficient condition from verification of a particular based homotopy.
Hypotheses/scope
Missing/incorrect: Misses a time or basepoint condition. Partial: Checks the four edges but omits joint continuity or global avoidance, or vice versa. Clear: Checks joint continuity, both prescribed time slices, the fixed basepoint, and avoidance for the full parameter square.
Witness/construction
Missing/incorrect: Gives no valid avoidance calculation. Partial: Observes the positive coefficient without completing the zero test or norm estimate. Clear: Gives an exact bound ‖U‖² ≥ 1 or a complete zero-exclusion argument.
Proof/quantifiers
Missing/incorrect: Uses finitely many plotted ellipses as proof. Partial: Gives an affirmative verdict with an unfinished universal check. Clear: Demonstrates validity for every s, t ∈ I and explains why the stated rejection does not follow.

C2 · Use a bound for arbitrary paths

Prompt: Continuous paths α, β : I → ℂ share endpoints 1 + i and 2 − i, and satisfy Re α(s), Re β(s) ≥ ⅓ for every s. Construct and verify an endpoint-fixed homotopy between them in X.

Key: Define H(s, t) = (1 − t)α(s) + tβ(s). Continuity of the paths and of scalar multiplication and addition implies joint continuity on I × I. The time slices are α and β. Because both paths have the same prescribed endpoints,

H(0, t) = 1 + i,   H(1, t) = 2 − i,
Re H(s, t) ≥ (1 − t)/3 + t/3 = 1/3.

The real-part bound holds for every pair and excludes 0, proving membership in X. These are paths between distinct endpoints, not based loops at 1; there is no need to assign each a loop winding.

Alternatives: It is enough to invoke and check convexity of the closed half-plane {z : Re z ≥ ⅓}, which is contained in X. A verified two-stage construction through a common path in that half-plane with the same prescribed endpoints also works. The stated assumptions supply a universal bound; inventing formulas for α and β would not address arbitrary given paths.

Definition/theorem
Missing/incorrect: Requires based loops or claims X is convex. Partial: Names interpolation without relating it to endpoint-fixed homotopy. Clear: Uses endpoint-fixed interpolation with the supplied safe half-plane.
Hypotheses/scope
Missing/incorrect: Assumes new path formulas or changes either endpoint to 1. Partial: Uses the bound but omits continuity or an endpoint check. Clear: Uses only the given continuity, shared endpoints, universal bound, and nonnegative interpolation weights on I.
Witness/construction
Missing/incorrect: Gives no valid general family. Partial: Gives interpolation without a complete bound. Clear: Gives a valid family and proves Re H ≥ ⅓, or gives an equivalent exact safe-region proof.
Proof/quantifiers
Missing/incorrect: Checks chosen example paths or finitely many parameters. Partial: Has a correct construction but leaves a required general step implicit. Clear: Proves the claim for every pair of paths satisfying the assumptions and every parameter pair.

C3 · Form a loop before using winding

Prompt: Compare α(s) = eπis and β(s) = e−πis, both from 1 to −1. Are they homotopic relative to endpoints in X? How does the answer change in ℂ? You may use the reference-card fact that endpoint-fixed homotopic paths make α ∗ β̄ nullhomotopic, where β̄(r) = β(1 − r).

Key: Neither α nor β is a closed loop, so the supplied integer loop winding should not be assigned separately to them. Concatenate α with the reverse of β, traveling each on half the interval:

(α ∗ β̄)(s) = α(2s) = e2πis for 0 ≤ s ≤ ½,
(α ∗ β̄)(s) = β̄(2s − 1) = e−πi(2−2s) = e2πis for ½ ≤ s ≤ 1.

The two formulas meet at −1. This is the unit loop based at 1 with winding 1. If α and β were endpoint-fixed homotopic in X, the supplied implication would make this loop nullhomotopic there. Winding invariance would then force winding 0, a contradiction. Thus no such path homotopy exists in X.

In ℂ, straight interpolation gives

H(s, t) = cos(πs) + i(1 − 2t)sin(πs).

It is jointly continuous, has time slices α and β, and fixes 1 and −1 at the two endpoints. At (s, t) = (½, ½) it is 0, which is permitted in ℂ. That last observation rejects this particular formula in X; the winding argument above excludes all endpoint-fixed formulas there.

Alternatives: A lift of an assumed path homotopy to real angles also works: the initial α lift runs from 0 to ½, whereas the β lift starting at 0 ends at −½; a fixed endpoint at −1 cannot change the lifted terminal angle continuously between distinct elements of ½ + ℤ. The lifting fact must be stated if used beyond the card. An equivalent degree argument for the concatenated loop is acceptable.

Definition/theorem
Missing/incorrect: Assigns integer loop windings to these nonclosed paths without a justified extension. Partial: Suggests joining or reversing paths without the nullhomotopy implication. Clear: Forms the appropriate closed loop and applies the permitted path-to-loop obstruction, or gives a correct lifted-angle argument.
Hypotheses/scope
Missing/incorrect: Treats the paths as loops at 1 or ignores the fixed terminal point −1. Partial: Recognizes the endpoints but incompletely checks the chosen obstruction’s conditions or the whole-plane family. Clear: Checks continuous paths in X and the relative endpoints in both target settings; checks the concatenation’s seam and basepoint when using that method, or the fixed initial lift and discrete terminal lift when using lifted angles.
Witness/construction
Missing/incorrect: Gives an incorrect concatenation or only a picture. Partial: Gives the correct loop winding or the correct ℂ-valued construction, with the other incomplete. Clear: Computes a valid obstruction such as α ∗ β̄ = ω1, and gives a valid whole-plane homotopy.
Proof/quantifiers
Missing/incorrect: Uses the one zero of straight interpolation as the sole nonexistence proof. Partial: Reaches both correct conclusions but leaves the contradiction or universal construction verification incomplete. Clear: Excludes every endpoint-fixed homotopy in X and verifies a construction for all parameters in ℂ.

C-S · optional construction, reported separately

Contract a nonconvex hypograph

Prompt: Let Y = {(x, y) ∈ ℝ² : y < x2} and p = (0, −1). Show that straight interpolation to p can fail, then construct a contraction of Y to p that fixes p. You may use the supplied continuous mutually inverse maps T(x, y) = (x, x2 − y) and T−1(a, b) = (a, a2 − b), which identify Y with ℝ × (0, ∞).

Failure witness: The point (2, 3) lies in Y because 3 < 4. Its straight path to p passes through (1, 1) at time ½, where y = x2. This boundary point is excluded. The witness invalidates that contraction formula; it does not prove Y noncontractible.

Construction through coordinates: T and its displayed inverse are continuous polynomial maps, and direct substitution verifies both inverse identities. It takes Y onto the upper half-plane b > 0 and takes p to (0, 1). Contract that convex half-plane to (0, 1), then return by T−1:

H((x, y), t) = T−1((1 − t)T(x, y) + t(0, 1))
= ((1 − t)x, (1 − t)y − t − t(1 − t)x²).

Direct verification: The resulting map is jointly continuous on Y × I. At time 0 it is the identity and at time 1 it is the constant p. For p, the second coordinate is −(1 − t) − t = −1, so p stays fixed. Writing (Hx, Hy) for its coordinates gives

Hx² − Hy = (1 − t)(x² − y) + t > 0.

The strict inequality holds at time 0 because x2 − y > 0, at time 1 because the expression is 1, and at intermediate times because it is a convex combination of positive numbers. Thus every intermediate point lies in Y. This contracts Y to p, fixing p throughout.

Alternatives: The composed coordinate construction is sufficient without expanding its second coordinate. The learner may use the supplied inverse, continuity, and half-plane identification without proving those facts again; they must justify the transported family’s membership, continuity, boundary conditions, and fixed point. Accept any other valid contraction and any exact failure witness for the naive interpolation. Neither proving the entire target convex nor invoking winding is needed.

Definition/theorem
Missing/incorrect: Infers noncontractibility from a failed formula or confuses retraction onto a larger set with contraction to p. Partial: Suggests changing coordinates without identifying the resulting contraction. Clear: Uses the contraction definition and a valid coordinate or direct construction, distinguishing one failure from general impossibility.
Hypotheses/scope
Missing/incorrect: Allows the boundary y = x2 or changes Y. Partial: Checks only some required properties of the transported or direct family. Clear: Establishes joint continuity, both time maps, the fixed point, and strict membership in Y, correctly using the supplied coordinate facts if that method is chosen.
Witness/construction
Missing/incorrect: Gives neither an exact failure nor a valid family. Partial: Completes the witness or the contraction, but not both. Clear: Gives both an exact failure witness and a valid contraction with an exact membership argument.
Proof/quantifiers
Missing/incorrect: Checks sampled points or treats a proposed formula as self-validating. Partial: Gives the right strategy with an omitted universal or strict-boundary step. Clear: Rejects the naive construction with one witness and proves the replacement for every (x, y) ∈ Y and every t ∈ I.
