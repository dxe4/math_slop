Permitted reference card · identical on A, B and C

Notation. I = [0,1]; X = ℂ ∖ {0}; u(s) = s(1−s); ωk(s) = exp(2πiks). The real part of z is Re z. Write complex numbers as pairs if preferred.

Path homotopy. For paths α, β : I → Y with common endpoints a, b, a homotopy relative to endpoints is a jointly continuous H : I × I → Y with H(s,0)=α(s), H(s,1)=β(s), H(0,t)=a and H(1,t)=b for every s,t. A based loop has a=b=p. A free loop homotopy may move the common endpoint, while keeping every slice closed.

Construction facts. Sums, products and compositions of continuous functions are continuous; division requires a nonzero denominator. A target is convex if it contains the whole segment between each pair of its points. Straight interpolation of two continuous maps into a convex subset stays there. In a nonconvex target, check the particular formula directly.

Winding facts allowed without proof. If γ : I → X is a loop based at 1, normalize it to γ/|γ| and choose a continuous real angle θ with γ/|γ|=exp(2πiθ) and θ(0)=0. Its winding is the integer θ(1). Based homotopies in X preserve winding. Positive radial factors do not affect this normalized angle.

Paths before loops. If α and β have the same endpoints and are homotopic relative to them, then α ∗ β̄ is nullhomotopic, where β̄(s)=β(1−s). The concatenation follows α on the first half of I, then β backward on the second. A nullhomotopic based loop in X has winding 0.

What is being assessed. Apply these facts and justify each required condition. A numerical sample or one failed formula does not decide every possible homotopy. Other correct methods are welcome.
