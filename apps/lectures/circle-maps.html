Lecture 04 · Functions, deformations, and composition

Degree belongs
to a map.

A circle can fold over itself and still define a homotopy equivalence. Another map can cover every point and still deform to a constant. The domain and target are the same; the maps do different things.

01 / Identify the object

Three preimages, one net turn

A loop is a map from an interval whose endpoints agree. Here the object is a function f:S¹→S¹. Parametrize its domain by z=exp(2πis), where 0≤s≤1. An angle lift F records f(exp(2πis))=exp(2πiF(s)). Values of F are measured in turns.

Begin with the folded angle

F(s)=2s for 0≤s≤¼;
F(s)=1−2s for ¼≤s≤½;
F(s)=2s−1 for ½≤s≤1.

The formulas agree at the joins. Since F(0)=0 and F(1)=1, their exponential has matching values at the identified endpoints. This defines a continuous circle map. Yet s=0 and s=½ give the same output 1 at different domain points 1 and −1.

Preparation. Use continuity, complex exponentials, the circle angle-lifting theorem, and winding from the preceding lectures. Piecewise linear means straight segments between the stated vertices. No differentiation or homology is required for this circle argument.

02 / A classification with a proof

The net change survives; the folds can disappear

For any continuous f:S¹→S¹, angle lifting supplies a continuous F:[0,1]→ℝ. Since the endpoint outputs agree, F(1)−F(0) is an integer. Define

deg(f)=F(1)−F(0)=m.

Choosing another angle lift adds a constant integer and leaves this difference unchanged. A homotopy of circle maps cannot change the degree: use a joint angle lift on the parameter square. Its endpoint difference varies continuously in deformation time and takes integer values, so it is constant. Choosing unrelated lifts for individual frames would not prove this.

Conversely, a lift with endpoint difference m can be deformed to the standard angle ms:

H(exp(2πis),t)=exp(2πi[(1−t)F(s)+tms]).

Check the identified seam, not equality of real angles

At s=1 and s=0, the angles in brackets differ by (1−t)m+tm=m. Their exponentials therefore agree for every t. The formula is continuous on the whole square. The map [0,1]×[0,1]→S¹×[0,1] identifying those edges is a continuous surjection from a compact space to a Hausdorff space, so it is a quotient map. The continuous formula with matching edges descends to a jointly continuous H on S¹×[0,1].

At t=0 it gives f; at t=1 it gives pₘ(z)=zᵐ. Thus two circle maps are homotopic exactly when their degrees agree. This is an ordinary homotopy. If f(1)=1 and F is normalized by F(0)=0, the construction also fixes that basepoint. The quarter-turn rotation instead moves its image of 1 during this homotopy.

For the folded map, m=1, so this gives a homotopy to the identity despite its three preimages of i. For the triangular angle T(s)=2s on the first half and 2−2s on the second, m=0. It covers the entire circle and then returns, yet exp(2πi(1−t)T(s)) contracts the map to the constant 1. Surjectivity alone does not determine a homotopy class.

03 / Change the operation

Following one map by another multiplies degrees

Composition means apply f to an input and then apply g to its output. Concatenation of their parameter loops means spend the first half of the interval following f and the second half following g. In this panel both authored maps fix 1, so those loops join at the common basepoint.

deg(g∘f)=deg(g)·deg(f),
wind(loop of f followed by loop of g)=deg(f)+deg(g).

To justify multiplication, deform f to pₘ and g to pₙ. Composition preserves homotopies: if F and G are their jointly continuous families, G(F(z,t),t) is a jointly continuous family of composites. The final composite is pₙ∘pₘ=pₙₘ. Addition for concatenation follows from unwrapped angles: the second path starts at the first path’s ending angle.

04 / Invert the homotopy class

Degree ±1 gives an inverse up to deformation

If g is a homotopy inverse of f and their degrees are n and m, then g∘f≃id implies nm=1. Since both are integers, m must be 1 or −1. Conversely, if deg(f)=m∈{1,−1}, let g=pₘ. Since f≃pₘ, both g∘f and f∘g are homotopic to pₘ∘pₘ=id. This constructs a homotopy inverse.

f:S¹→S¹ is a homotopy equivalence ⇔ deg(f)∈{1,−1}.

The folded map has degree 1, and the identity itself is a homotopy inverse. It is not a homeomorphism, since it is not injective. The map p₂ is not a homotopy equivalence, even though its domain and target are the very same circle. Failure of one chosen map does not prevent the spaces from being equivalent through another map.

05 / Change the domain

When can the boundary map fill a disk?

Given f:S¹→S¹, ask for a continuous E:D²→S¹ whose boundary values equal f. The target remains the circle. If E exists, H(z,t)=E((1−t)z) deforms f to the constant E(0); hence deg(f)=0.

Conversely, degree zero gives an angle lift F with F(1)=F(0). Define

E(r exp(2πis))=exp(2πirF(s)) for 0<r≤1,
E(0)=1.

For every fixed r, the endpoint angles agree, so this is well-defined on each circle. Away from the center it is continuous by the same quotient seam argument. At the center, the continuous F is bounded on [0,1]; if |F(s)|≤M then |E(r exp(2πis))−1|≤2πrM, uniformly in s. This proves continuity at 0. At r=1 the boundary values are f.

f extends to E:D²→S¹ ⇔ deg(f)=0.

The triangular map therefore fills a disk even though it is onto. The folded degree-one map cannot. A map into D² would ask a different question: the straight extension x↦x already fills the identity boundary map when the target includes the interior.

06 / Independent practice

Change the question, keep the objects explicit

Write an attempt before revealing a hint or explanation. Your writing is saved separately from the exact calculations and is not automatically proof-checked.

1. Count three preimages without changing the degree

For the folded F from Section 1, find all circle inputs mapping to i. Compute the degree from the lifted endpoints, and record the direction of travel at each preimage. Does the unsigned count equal the degree?

Hint

Solve F(s)=¼ separately on its three segments. Its values lie between 0 and 1.

Worked explanation

The parameters are ⅛, ⅜ and ⅝, giving three different circle inputs. The directions are forward, backward and forward. The degree is F(1)−F(0)=1; the unsigned preimage count is 3. Here the signed count +1−1+1 agrees with the endpoint calculation, but the endpoint argument establishes the degree without assuming a general signed-count theorem.

2. Construct the folded map’s inverse up to homotopy

Write a homotopy from the folded circle map to the identity. Check joint continuity, both endpoint maps and the identified domain seam. Give a homotopy inverse and explain why this does not give a homeomorphism.

Hint

Interpolate F(s) with s in the real angle line, then exponentiate. Which composite appears if the proposed inverse is id?

Worked explanation

Set H(exp(2πis),t)=exp(2πi((1−t)F(s)+ts)). The angle formula is jointly continuous and has endpoint difference 1 for all t, so its exponential descends continuously through the quotient square. It starts at the folded map and ends at id. Since F(0)=0, the image of domain point 1 remains fixed. Taking g=id makes both composites equal to the folded map, hence homotopic to id by this H. The folded map sends distinct inputs 1 and −1 to 1, so it has no genuine inverse function and is not a homeomorphism.

3. Surjective and null-homotopic

Let T(s)=2s for 0≤s≤½ and T(s)=2−2s for ½≤s≤1. Prove its circle map is onto and null-homotopic. State exactly why this does not contract the space S¹ itself.

Hint

The first half already reaches every angle modulo one. Contract the map using its continuous real lift, rather than trying to contract the identity map.

Worked explanation

On [0,½], T covers [0,1], so its exponential reaches all of S¹. Its endpoint difference is 0, and H(exp(2πis),t)=exp(2πi(1−t)T(s)) is a based homotopy to the constant 1. The initial map is this out-and-back map, rather than id:S¹→S¹. Contractibility of the space would require a homotopy of the identity, whose degree is 1 and cannot become 0.

4. Compose or concatenate?

Let f(z)=z−3 and g(z)=z². Compare g∘f with the based loop that follows the parameter loop of f and then that of g. Give a lift for each and compare their invariants.

Hint

The second concatenated segment begins at the first segment’s ending angle −3. Preserve that accumulated turn.

Worked explanation

The composite is z−6, with lift −6s and degree −6. For concatenation, use C(s)=−6s on the first half and C(s)=−3+2(2s−1)=4s−5 on the second half. Both pieces give −3 at s=½; C(0)=0 and C(1)=−1. Its winding is −1. Applying a map to another map’s output multiplies these degrees, whereas following the two loops consecutively adds them.

5. Test the chosen map, not just its two spaces

Which are homotopy equivalences: p₂, complex conjugation, and the folded map? Give an inverse up to homotopy when one exists, and prove the obstruction otherwise. Does the answer for p₂ mean two circles have different homotopy types?

Hint

Integer degrees of an inverse pair must multiply to 1. Conjugation on S¹ is z↦z−1.

Worked explanation

p₂ has degree 2, so an inverse up to homotopy would need an integer degree n with 2n=1, which is impossible. Conjugation has degree −1 and is its own genuine inverse. The folded map has degree 1, with id as a homotopy inverse by Task 2. Both domain and target of p₂ are S¹ and are homeomorphic through id; the obstruction concerns the selected map p₂.

6. Fill in two different boundary prescriptions

Decide whether the folded map and the triangular map extend continuously from S¹ to D² while keeping target S¹. Give a formula and check continuity at the center for each extension you claim. Then change the target to D² and reconsider the identity boundary map.

Hint

An extension into S¹ would provide a null-homotopy by restricting it to shrinking circles. The triangular lift is bounded by 1.

Worked explanation

The folded map has degree 1 and cannot extend into S¹, since an extension would make it null-homotopic. For the triangular map set E(r exp(2πis))=exp(2πirT(s)), with E(0)=1. The angular seam agrees because T(0)=T(1)=0. Since 0≤T≤1, the bound |E−1|≤2πr proves continuity at the center; the boundary values at r=1 are correct. With target D², the identity boundary map extends by E(x)=x. Keeping the target circle in the first question is essential.

Optional untimed extension. Transport words through a graph map, then construct both based inverse homotopies on the whole figure eight.

Return later: name the map and the operation
