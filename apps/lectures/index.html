Loops, covers and degree

Nine laboratories connect moving curves with mathematical arguments. Predict an outcome, use the experiment, then explain which hypotheses support the conclusion.

Preparation: continuous functions and curves, elementary proofs and introductory topology. The later word and covering arguments use groups. Each laboratory states its further preparation.

Follow the short course

  1. When does a loop remember a hole?

    Deform a loop while stating continuity and endpoint conditions.

  2. Graph maps and homotopy

    Follow vertices and edges through a homotopy and distinguish a formula from a continuous construction.

  3. Loops, words, and the torus

    Reduce words and compare the order of turns on a graph and a torus.

  4. A disk must lift from every sheet

    Track relators and explain which sheet conditions permit a disk attachment.

  5. Three sheets, how many symmetries?

    Predict lifted paths, endpoints and covering transformations.

  6. Lift, return, choose a root

    Relate winding and sheet permutations to continuous roots and logarithms.

  7. A matrix forgets word order

    Compare cellular boundary calculations with noncommutative words.

  8. Degree belongs to a map

    Compare explicit maps using lifts, degree and homotopy.

  9. Proof pathways

    Write a changed argument with its domain and hypotheses before comparing the worked model.

Keep an argument of your own

Attempt the written questions before opening hints or worked answers. Notes and study marks record your work; they do not grade a proof. Each laboratory retains its own examples, controls and source qualifications.
