01 / Reuse the earlier distinctions

Three checks before adding a face

This workshop continues covering graphs and words and cellular boundaries. It uses the covering-space classification theorem and the cellular homology theorem. Try each check before opening its repair.

Can a word fix one sheet while moving the others?

Yes. For a=(12), b=(23), the word b fixes sheet 1 and exchanges 2 and 3. The subgroup of loops closing at one chosen sheet is a stabilizer. The kernel of the whole sheet action consists of words fixing every sheet. The earlier stabilizer task proves why these differ.

If a lifted path begins at 1 and ends at 2, what is its chain boundary?

It is v₂−v₁. Give each positive edge its declared orientation; reverse traversal contributes its negative. Interior vertices cancel when the edge boundaries are summed. An open path is a 1-chain, but it is not a cycle or a possible cellular boundary of a 2-cell.

Does attaching a disk kill only one word in the free group?

It kills the normal subgroup generated by that word: its conjugates, inverses and products become trivial too. Thus π₁(W∪ᵣD²)=F(a,b)/⟨⟨r⟩⟩. The disk-attachment theorem supplies this presentation. Killing a word in one stabilizer does not show that its normal closure lies in the action kernel.

Core route: complete the sheet test and Tasks 1–3. The integer homology calculation and Tasks 4–6 also use a graph cycle basis and integer quotients; their prerequisites are stated below.

02 / Fix the composition convention

Read a path from left to right

Let W be two oriented circles a,b meeting at a vertex. In a covering graph, a positive edge aᵢ leaves sheet i and ends at a(i); similarly for bᵢ. A means traverse an a-edge backwards, and B means traverse a b-edge backwards. Distinct positive edges can share their endpoints.

Lw(s) = endpoint after reading w from sheet s.
Luv = Lv ∘ Lu, so s·(uv)=(s·u)·v.

Here ∘ is ordinary function composition: the function on the right acts first. The endpoint maps L therefore form a right action. If a usual homomorphism into permutations is required, set ρ(w)=Lw⁻¹; then ρ(uv)=ρ(u)∘ρ(v). A word fixes every sheet under L exactly when it does under ρ. Keep the convention when using a cycle such as (123), which sends 1→2→3→1.

For A from sheet j, use the positive a-edge ending at j in reverse. It need not be the positive a-edge leaving j. The workbench records that edge’s name and sign.

03 / State the new question

Which covering graphs survive an attachment?

Attach one disk to W along either boundary word:

T=W∪abABD², with r=aba⁻¹b⁻¹.
X=W∪aabAABD², with r=a²ba⁻²b⁻¹.

These are the two CW complexes from the word-order workshop. Their base cellular boundary columns are both zero. Their covering graphs can respond differently to the attachments.

Start with a specified three-sheet cover of W. We seek a three-sheet covering space of T or X whose restriction over W is that same graph cover. The attaching loop contracts across the disk in the new base, so its lift from every sheet must close. Each lifted open face must map homeomorphically onto the base open face. Checking just the selected row is insufficient.

Written explanations are personal attempts. The calculator checks finite paths and matrices; it does not grade a proof.

05 / Connect the calculation to topology

The all-sheet extension criterion

For these CW attachments

A graph cover of W extends to a cover of Y=W∪ᵣD² precisely when Lr(s)=s for every sheet s. When it extends, the number of sheets is unchanged and there is one lifted open face for every sheet.

Necessity: lift a null-homotopy

In Y, the attaching loop contracts across its disk. Lift that null-homotopy to a putative cover, starting at any chosen point over the vertex. A lift of a null-homotopic based loop is closed. Hence r must fix every sheet, including sheets other than the highlighted one.

Sufficiency: factor the whole action

The disk-attachment theorem gives π₁(Y)=F(a,b)/⟨⟨r⟩⟩. If every sheet is fixed, ρ(r) is the identity permutation. The kernel of the homomorphism ρ is normal, so it contains every conjugate of r and therefore its normal closure. The sheet action factors through π₁(Y).

The covering-space classification theorem for connected CW complexes realizes this factored action as a covering space of Y. Restricting it to W recovers the given graph cover because the F(a,b) action on the fiber is unchanged. Both selected graph actions are transitive, so their covers are connected. This imported theorem supplies the topological cover; the finite endpoint table verifies the needed relation.

The test is stronger than “r closes from the root.” It is also different from counting the base word’s signed exponents: both base columns are zero, while one of the four cover-and-word pairs fails.

Repeating the boundary changes the base

A nonclosing r might close after r² or r³. That does not repair an ordinary cover of Y. The preimage of the attached open disk must consist of copies mapping homeomorphically, with each lifted characteristic boundary following one traversal of r. Using rᵏ changes the attached base word. The map z↦zᵏ on a disk has a branch point at 0 for k>1 and is not a covering there. Task 3 makes this distinction explicit.

06 / Use the lifted cells

A zero base column can lift to nonzero columns

Fix the first cover a=(12), b=(23) and the relation X. In the edge order a1,b1,a2,b2,a3,b3, the three lifted faces have boundaries

d₂σ1=0,   d₂σ2=v,   d₂σ3=−v,
where v=a1+a2−2a3.

The chain v is nonzero upstairs: a1,a2,a3 are distinct positive edges. Under projection all three become a, so v maps to a+a−2a=0 downstairs. There is no conflict with the zero base column. The lifted cellular boundary formula counts signed occurrences of each upstairs edge.

Optional integer proof: why no 2-torsion appears here

A spanning tree uses a1 and b2. Its four non-tree edges give the integer graph cycle basis c1=b1, c2=a1+a2, c3=a3, c4=b2+b3. One can prove completeness directly: for any cycle with coefficients (x1,y1,x2,y2,x3,y3), the vertex equations give x1=x2 and y2=y3. It is therefore uniquely y1c1+x1c2+x3c3+y2c4.

The face image is generated by v=c2−2c3. Replace c2 by u=c2−2c3. Then (c1,u,c3,c4) is still an integer basis, since c2=u+2c3 recovers the old basis with integer coefficients. Quotienting by ℤu leaves a free group on [c1],[c3],[c4]. A coefficient 2 inside a primitive vector does not create torsion.

H₁≅ℤ³,   basis [c1],[c3],[c4].
ker(d₂)={xσ1+y(σ2+σ3): x,y∈ℤ}, so H₂≅ℤ².
H₀≅ℤ because the graph is connected; Hₙ=0 for n≥3.

The kernel statement follows from d₂(xσ1+yσ2+zσ3)=(y−z)v and v≠0. There is no d₃ image. The cellular homology theorem identifies these groups with the homology of the extended space. The calculation does not classify its fundamental group or its homotopy type.

For Tasks 5–6, use ker(d₁) as the integer cycle group and divide by im(d₂). A real rank computation alone cannot detect integer torsion. A change of integer basis must have an integer inverse.

07 / Work beyond the selected row

Six tasks, with separate hints and answers

Write an attempt before opening a hint. The first three use only lifts and the extension criterion; the last three add cellular chains. Tasks 2, 3 and 6 change to four sheets, beyond the controls.

1. Complete the four-case decision

For each selected cover, compute the endpoint of every starting sheet under T and X. State which pairs extend and how many lifted faces an extension has. Explain why the two zero base boundary columns do not decide this question.

Hint

With a=(12), a² is identity. With a=(123) and b=identity, both words have total signed a-count zero. Read the commutator in the declared left-to-right order.

Worked explanation

For a=(12), b=(23), T sends (1,2,3) to (2,3,1), so it fails. X fixes (1,2,3), so it extends. For a=(123), b=identity, both T and X fix all three sheets and both extend. Each successful extension has three lifted faces, one per sheet. The base exponent sums discard order and the distinctions between the lifted positive edges; they do not test the full sheet action.

2. A closed root that does not suffice

Change to four sheets with a=(12), b=(234), fixing all unlisted sheets. Let r=abAB. Find Lᵣ on all four sheets. Although r closes from sheet 3, calculate the endpoint of brB from sheet 3. Decide whether this graph cover extends over T and over X.

Hint

Compute the four-letter paths separately. For brB, start by sending 3 along b to 4; then apply r; then traverse B.

Worked explanation

The endpoint list for r is (2,4,3,1), so Lᵣ=(124) and only sheet 3 is fixed. Starting at 3, brB runs 3→4 under b, then 4→1 under r, then 1→1 under B. Thus r lies in the root-3 stabilizer while its conjugate brB does not. The action does not kill the normal closure of r, so there is no extension over T. Since a² is identity, X=aabAAB fixes every sheet and the graph cover does extend over X, with four lifted faces. The graph is connected because a reaches 2 from 1 and b connects 2,3,4.

3. Why returning after three turns changes the problem

Keep the four-sheet graph and r from Task 2. Does r³ fix every sheet? What base CW complex does this permit a cover of? Explain why attaching disks along the lifted r³ paths does not give an ordinary cover of T. Include the disk center in your explanation.

Hint

Lᵣ is a three-cycle with one fixed point. Compare attaching a disk along r³ with mapping the interior of one disk three times onto the original disk.

Worked explanation

Lᵣ³ is identity, so the same graph cover extends over the different base W∪ᵣ³D². This has the relation r³=1 instead of r=1. For T, each lifted open face must map homeomorphically onto the base open face, with its characteristic boundary lifting one traversal of r. The three-turn boundary cannot provide such a face over a moved sheet. A proposed local model z↦z³ fails to be locally one-to-one at 0, hence is branched there and is not an ordinary covering. The unchanged obstruction for r still rules out a cover of T.

4. Count the upstairs edges

In the three-sheet a=(12), b=(23) extension over X, compute each lifted face boundary from its signed edge traversals. Prove that their chain boundaries vanish. Project the nonzero boundary v to the base graph and explain why it becomes zero.

Hint

From sheet 2 the successive positive-edge names are a2,a1,b2,a3,a3,b2, with signs +,+,+,−,−,−. The first and last b2 contributions cancel.

Worked explanation

From sheet 1 the chain is a1+a2+b1−a2−a1−b1=0. From sheet 2 it is a2+a1+b2−a3−a3−b2=v. From sheet 3 it is a3+a3+b3−a1−a2−b3=−v. Now d₁v=(v₂−v₁)+(v₁−v₂)−2(v₃−v₃)=0. Projection sends v to a+a−2a=0, although v is a nonzero upstairs chain. Counting the distinct lifted edges gives the cellular columns; counting only base labels loses those distinctions.

5. Prove that a coefficient 2 does not force torsion

For the same extension, prove that c1=b1, c2=a1+a2, c3=a3, c4=b2+b3 is an integer basis of ker(d₁). Compute H₁ and H₂. Show why interpreting the coefficient −2 in v as a ℤ/2 summand is wrong.

Hint

Use the vertex equations to express any cycle uniquely in the four proposed generators. Then replace c2 by c2−2c3 using an invertible integer substitution.

Worked explanation

The vertex equations force the coefficients of a1,a2 to agree and those of b2,b3 to agree; b1 and a3 are unrestricted. This gives the proposed unique integer expansion. Write u=c2−2c3. The basis (c1,u,c3,c4) has integer inverse c2=u+2c3. The image of d₂ is exactly ℤu, so H₁ is free ℤ³ on [c1],[c3],[c4]. For a face chain (x,y,z), its boundary is (y−z)u; hence ker(d₂) has basis σ1,σ2+σ3. Since C₃=0, H₂=ℤ². Torsion depends on the whole integer image: u is primitive because its c2 coefficient is 1.

6. Change the cover and make torsion appear

Use the four-sheet graph a=(12), b=(234) over X. Prove that an integer graph cycle basis is c1=b1, c2=a1+a2, c3=a3, c4=a4, c5=b2+b3+b4. Compute the four face columns in this basis. Find H₁ and H₂ using an explicitly invertible integer change of basis.

Hint

The face columns are 0, c2−2c3, 2c3−2c4, 2c4−c2. Try u=c2−2c3, t=c3−c4 and d=c4. Express c2,c3,c4 back in terms of u,t,d.

Worked explanation

The vertex equations give equal a1,a2 coefficients and equal b2,b3,b4 coefficients, with b1,a3,a4 free; this proves the stated basis. Direct lifting gives columns 0, c2−2c3, 2c3−2c4, 2c4−c2. In the integer basis (c1,u,t,d,c5), the inverse is c4=d, c3=t+d, c2=u+2t+2d. The three nonzero columns become u, 2t, −u−2t. Their image is ℤu⊕2ℤt. Therefore H₁≅ℤ³⊕ℤ/2, with free generators [c1],[d],[c5] and order-two generator [t]. A face chain (x1,x2,x3,x4) has zero boundary exactly when x2=x3=x4, so H₂≅ℤ² on σ1 and σ2+σ3+σ4. Connectedness gives H₀=ℤ; all higher homology vanishes. Unlike Task 5, the remaining relation is twice a primitive generator after eliminating u.
