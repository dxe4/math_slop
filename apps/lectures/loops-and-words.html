When do turns
lose information?

On a circle, signed winding classifies based loops. On a figure eight, the order of the turns matters. On a torus, a filled square explains why that order can disappear.

01 / 10 minutes

Two circles share one point

Let W = S¹ ∨ S¹ be the figure eight: two circles identified at a single point v. A based loop starts and ends at v. Throughout this lecture, a homotopy of based loops keeps that point fixed at every time.

Choose a positive direction on each circle. Write a for one positive circuit of the left circle and b for one positive circuit of the right. Capital letters mean the reversed circuit: A = a⁻¹ and B = b⁻¹. Read concatenation from left to right. The empty word represents the constant loop; we write its group element as 1.

c = abAB = aba⁻¹b⁻¹.Travel left forward, right forward, left backward, right backward.

Predict before experimenting: c has net zero turns around each circle. Can it contract in W? Does knowing the two signed counts determine its based homotopy class?

The first lecture used π₁(S¹) ≅ ℤ. Two circles suggest two integers, but that guess discards the order of travel. We will prove exactly when discarding order is justified.

02 / 20 minutes

Cancel a retracing, preserve the order

Adjacent inverse pairs aA, Aa, bB, and Bb can disappear through a homotopy. The same edge is traversed and immediately retraced. A pair separated by other letters cannot be deleted by this rule. In particular, there is no adjacent inverse pair in abAB.

Why a retracing may disappear

For any path λ starting at v, the loop that follows λ and then reverses it has the endpoint-fixed contraction

R(s,t) = λ(2s(1−t))   for 0 ≤ s ≤ ½,
R(s,t) = λ(2(1−s)(1−t))   for ½ ≤ s ≤ 1.

Both formulas agree at s = ½. At t = 0 we traverse and retrace all of λ; at t = 1 every input is λ(0). Both endpoints stay at v. The pasting lemma establishes joint continuity. Insert this construction into a longer word to justify each adjacent cancellation.

This proves that reducing a word preserves its based class. The converse—that two distinct reduced words cannot become homotopic through more complicated loops—needs a further argument. The covering-tree extension supplies it.

03 / 20 minutes

A filled square changes the answer

Define the torus as T² = ℝ²/ℤ². Its quotient map q identifies (x,y) with (x+m,y+n) for integers m,n. Equivalently, q(x,y) = (exp(2πix), exp(2πiy)) in S¹ × S¹. The basepoint is q(0,0). Opposite sides of the unit square are identified in matching directions.

The torus versions of a and b are q(s,0) and q(0,s). The lift ℓ of abAB, starting at (0,0), follows the square boundary through (1,0), (1,1), (0,1), and back to (0,0). Now the whole plane, including square interiors, is available upstairs.

H(s,t) = q((1−t)ℓ(s)).For the commutator’s closed lift ℓ, this is a valid based contraction.

Audit every homotopy condition

ℓ is continuous on its four closed edge intervals; the formulas agree at the corners. Multiplication by 1−t and composition with the continuous quotient q establish joint continuity. At t = 0 we obtain abAB on the torus. At t = 1 every input is q(0,0). Since ℓ(0) = ℓ(1) = (0,0), both loop endpoints remain the basepoint for every t.

The same formula does not contract a. Its lift starts at (0,0) and ends at (1,0). Scaling changes the endpoint to (1−t,0). For 0 < t < 1 this is not an integer pair, so its image differs from q(0,0). The selected t = 0 and t = 1 slices close; the proposed whole family fails to be a homotopy of based loops.

A closed lift alone does not prove contractibility. We supplied a contraction in the convex plane. A graph made only of grid edges has unfilled square holes; a path around one of those holes cannot use this plane contraction while staying in that graph. The cover and its allowed points matter.

Thus π₁(W,v) is the free group F(a,b), while π₁(T²,q(0,0)) ≅ ℤ². In the first group ab ≠ ba; in the second they agree. The natural map from W to the two coordinate circles of T² induces the map sending each word to its exponent pair. It is onto and loses information: abAB lies in its kernel.

Why the torus test is complete

Use the path- and homotopy-lifting theorem for q: ℝ² → T². A loop starting at the basepoint has a unique lift starting at (0,0). Its endpoint is an integer pair (m,n). In an endpoint-fixed homotopy, the lifted endpoint varies continuously in the discrete set ℤ², so it cannot change.

Conversely, suppose two lifted paths L₀ and L₁ have the same endpoints, (0,0) and (m,n). The interpolation (1−t)L₀(s)+tL₁(s) stays in ℝ² and fixes both lifted endpoints. Its projection is a based homotopy of loops. Every integer pair is reached by a straight lifted path. Concatenation adds displacements. This proves the group classification, conditional on the named lifting theorem. For edge words, each a contributes (1,0), A contributes (−1,0), and similarly for b and B.

A consequence for spaces: W and T² cannot be homotopy equivalent. Homotopy equivalences between path-connected spaces induce isomorphic fundamental groups, with basepoint change along a path when needed. An abelian group cannot be isomorphic to a nonabelian one. This uses functoriality of π₁; different drawings alone would not establish the conclusion.

05 / Optional proof extension

Why distinct reduced words stay distinct

Build a graph whose vertices are all reduced words, including the empty word. From a vertex w, following an edge labelled a, A, b, or B appends that letter and cancels the last letter if they are inverse. Each edge is traversable in both directions with inverse labels.

This graph is a tree. Every nonempty vertex has exactly one neighbor of smaller length: remove its final letter. Repeated removal reaches the root. A finite cycle would have a maximum-length vertex with two distinct neighbors of smaller length, which is impossible. A loop has finite combinatorial length when it is an edge loop; the topological tree has no additional cycle hidden in its edges.

Map each labelled edge to the corresponding oriented circle edge of W. At every vertex the four incident half-edges map bijectively to the four half-edges at v. Small edge and vertex neighborhoods therefore map as a covering. Lifting a word from the root ends at precisely its reduced word.

Homotopy lifting keeps that endpoint invariant in the discrete fiber. Different reduced words therefore give nonhomotopic based loops. The cancellation contraction already proves the other direction for edge words. More generally, a lifted continuous path in this unit-edge, locally finite tree has compact image inside a finite subtree. It is homotopic relative to endpoints to the unique simple arc there, so arbitrary continuous loops also admit the classification.

The lifting theorem is a substantive input. Continue to the covering-space chapter for its proof, or use this section as a preview after the first lecture’s circle-lifting argument.

Keep the basepoint fixed. Based classes use equality of group elements. Allowing the basepoint to move changes the question: free loop classes correspond to conjugacy classes for path-connected spaces. The word baB is conjugate to a, but its reduced based word is different. Do not silently apply cyclic reduction to a based-loop comparison.

06 / Independent practice

Attempt before revealing an explanation

Use the formula and the stated theorems, rather than the appearance of a plot. Tasks 1–3 consolidate the core route. Tasks 4–6 ask for constructions and transfer; some can be assigned after class.

1. Explain each cancellation

Reduce a b B A b. For every deletion, identify the retraced path. Does a zero exponent sum in one generator mean that generator’s letters can be deleted wherever they occur?

Hint

Start with an adjacent inverse pair, then inspect the new adjacency.

Worked explanation

a b B A b → a A b → b. The bB pair retraces the right circle; the newly adjacent aA retraces the left. Arbitrarily separated inverse letters cannot be deleted. For instance abAB has zero exponent sums but no adjacent cancellation and is nontrivial in W.

2. Compare order without changing the counts

Are ab and ba based homotopic in W? On T²? Explain using a complete invariant for each space.

Hint

Compare reduced words first, then the two lifted displacements.

Worked explanation

In W the reduced words ab and ba differ, so the based classes differ by the covering-tree classification. On T² both have displacement (1,1), so interpolation of their lifts gives a based homotopy. Alternatively, ab(ba)⁻¹ = abAB is the null-homotopic torus commutator.

3. A whole family of invisible counts

For c=abAB, prove cᵏ is nontrivial in W for every nonzero integer k. What are its torus exponent sums?

Hint

Check the seam between consecutive copies. For negative k, write c⁻¹ explicitly.

Worked explanation

No cancellation occurs within c or across its seam Ba, so for k>0 the reduced length is 4k. The inverse is baBA; its seam Ab also has no inverse pair, giving length 4|k| for k<0. A nonempty reduced word is nontrivial. Every copy has exponent sums (0,0), so all these loops are null-homotopic on T². Zero counts lose infinitely many different based classes in W.

4. Check a proposed general contraction

A torus loop has lift L with L(0)=(0,0), L(1)=(2,1). Does q((1−t)L(s)) define a based contraction? Find an exact failure. Then construct a based homotopy between this loop and s ↦ q(2s,s).

Hint

Compare the scaled endpoint at t=½ with the integer lattice. For the repair, interpolate between lifts that share both endpoints.

Worked explanation

The proposed endpoint at t=½ is (1,½), which does not project to q(0,0), so the family fails the based-loop condition. It cannot contract this loop because displacement (2,1) is nonzero. Instead use J(s,t)=q((1−t)L(s)+t(2s,s)). The lifted endpoints remain (0,0) and (2,1), so both projected endpoints stay at the basepoint. Continuity follows from interpolation and q. This is a homotopy to the specified nonconstant representative, not a contraction.

5. Move the basepoint or keep it?

Compare baB with a as based loops in W. Explain why their being conjugate does not contradict your answer. Which statement in the lecture changes when homotopies may move the basepoint?

Hint

There is no adjacent inverse pair in baB. Separate equality from conjugacy.

Worked explanation

The reduced words baB and a are unequal, so the based classes differ. They are conjugate, baB=bab⁻¹. The stated free-loop classification identifies conjugacy classes rather than based group elements, so they become freely homotopic. This relies on the free-loop/conjugacy theorem; deleting the first and last letters in a based word would be an invalid substitution of questions.

6. Test a proposed homotopy equivalence

A learner says W and T² have “two independent directions,” so they should have the same homotopy type. Give an obstruction, stating the theorem that makes it an obstruction. Consider the inclusion of the two coordinate circles W → T² described above. Does failure of this particular map to induce an isomorphism alone rule out every possible equivalence?

Hint

Compare a group property preserved under every isomorphism.

Worked explanation

π₁(W) is nonabelian because ab≠ba; π₁(T²)≅ℤ² is abelian. Homotopy equivalence of path-connected spaces would give isomorphic fundamental groups, after any needed basepoint change. An isomorphism preserves commutativity, so no equivalence exists. Showing that one chosen map is not an isomorphism would only rule out that map as a homotopy equivalence; the nonisomorphism of the two groups rules out all such maps.

07 / Return later

Reconstruct the argument without the page

On a later visit, explain why a net count classifies loops on the torus but fails on the figure eight. Draw the commutator lift, identify the points allowed during its contraction, and state which lifting theorem your proof uses. Then invent two new words with equal exponent pairs and unequal reduced words.
