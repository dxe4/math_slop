01 / Prepare the objects

Three distinctions to keep

Does aa freely cancel when a exchanges sheets 1 and 2?

No. A denotes the reverse of a; only aA or Aa cancels. The permutation of sheets can have order two while a is an infinite-order element of the figure eight's free fundamental group. Review free words.

Does a closed lift have to contract?

No. Closure detects which loops belong to the subgroup represented by the cover. Contractibility is a different condition. The finite-circle-cover task already supplies an example; here aa will supply another.

Must every permutation of a fiber extend to a deck transformation?

No. A deck transformation is a homeomorphism d of the total space with p∘d=p. It must respect the lift of every labelled edge, not just permute the vertices. The exact compatibility test is derived below.

This workshop uses the free-group classification of loops on the figure eight, path and homotopy lifting, and the theorem that collapsing a spanning tree of a finite connected graph gives a wedge of circles. The covering-space chapter proves lifting; the covering-tree extension explains the free-group classification. The finite calculations below do not replace those topological theorems.

02 / Define a graph and a map

Six edges, even when endpoints coincide

The base W has one vertex v and two oriented circle edges a,b. Choose two permutations Pₐ,Pᵦ of the sheets {1,2,3}. For every sheet i, create a separate positive edge aᵢ from i to Pₐ(i), and a separate positive edge bᵢ from i to Pᵦ(i). Map each open edge homeomorphically onto the corresponding open base edge, preserving its direction; both endpoints map to v.

Our first cover: Pₐ=(12), Pᵦ=(23).
Positive edges: a₁:1→2, a₂:2→1, a₃:3→3;
b₁:1→1, b₂:2→3, b₃:3→2.

a₂ is not the reverse of a₁. They are different edges with the same two endpoints. The reverse of a₁ goes from 2 to 1 and is labelled A, whereas a₂ is labelled a. The same distinction applies to b₂ and b₃. No two-cells are filled in.

Why this really is a covering

At each sheet there is one outgoing a-edge and one incoming a-edge, because Pₐ is a bijection. Likewise for b. Thus the four edge germs at each vertex map bijectively to the four germs a,A,b,B at v, including both germs of a loop edge. Small star neighborhoods give three disjoint copies of a neighborhood of v. Interior edge neighborhoods also lift to disjoint copies. This proves the evenly covered condition.

Read words in travel order, left to right. For a word uv, first travel u, then v: i·uv=(i·u)·v. As ordinary functions on sheets, Lᵤᵥ=Lᵥ∘Lᵤ. Uppercase A,B travel along the appropriate inverse edge. The earlier cellular permutation test used ordinary rightmost-first function composition; do not carry its convention into this path trace.

The word belongs to the subgroup for sheet 1. Its closed lift is not null-homotopic: its projection is the nonempty reduced word abba.

What the endpoint proves

Let H=p∗π₁(E,1)⊆π₁(W,v)=F(a,b), where p∗ is the induced fundamental-group map. By the lifting criterion, H consists exactly of words whose lift from 1 closes. The connected component of 1 is its orbit under the letters and inverses. In our first cover, 1→2→3 along ab, so the cover is connected.

A right coset Ht is the set {ht:h∈H}; the index [F(a,b):H] counts distinct right cosets. Choose paths t₁=empty, t₂=a, t₃=ab. The cosets Htᵢ correspond to the three sheets: Htᵢ maps to 1·tᵢ. Indeed 1·u=1·v exactly when uv⁻¹∈H, or Hu=Hv. Thus [F(a,b):H]=3. This is an index, not the order of H. It also does not say every closed lift contracts. If a closed lift contracted, projecting its contraction would contract the original word in W.

04 / Collapse a tree

Four generators, with exact words

Use the spanning tree consisting of positive edges a₁ and b₂. Its paths from 1 are t₁=empty, t₂=a, t₃=ab. For a positive edge e:i→j outside the tree, travel out along tᵢ, follow e, and return along tⱼ⁻¹. Collapsing the tree makes these four remaining edges into four circles, so their loops form a free basis upstairs. A covering induces an injective fundamental-group map; their projected words therefore form a free basis of H.

Positive paths reach the whole component because inverse permutations are positive powers. A different tree changes the displayed basis; it does not change the subgroup. Changing the root can change the subgroup to a conjugate.

Rewrite the committed closed word

abba = (abbA)(aa) = g4 g2 after free cancellation.

Expanding the basis words and freely reducing gives abba again. This is an exact certificate for this word, not a proof by itself that these words generate every subgroup element.

Why rewriting works for every closed edge word

For each lifted step i→j labelled x, insert the tree loop tᵢxtⱼ⁻¹. Multiplying these factors cancels each adjacent tⱼ⁻¹tⱼ. The first and final tree paths are empty for a lift closed at its root, so the result is the original word. A tree-edge factor cancels to the identity; a non-tree factor is its basis generator or inverse. Thus every closed word rewrites in this basis. Freeness comes from the tree-collapse theorem, rather than from a finite list of examples.

Connected cover of the two-circle rose:
V=n, E=2n, rank π₁(E)=E−V+1=n+1.
Here n=3, so rank H=4, although its index is 3.
05 / Check a whole-space symmetry

Commuting with every lift

A deck map induces a permutation D of the sheets. The a-edge leaving D(i) must be the image of the a-edge leaving i. Its endpoint can be calculated in two ways, forcing D(Pₐ(i))=Pₐ(D(i)). Likewise D must commute with Pᵦ. Conversely, send each edge to the corresponding edge at D(i), with its base-edge coordinate unchanged. Commutation makes the endpoints agree, and D⁻¹ supplies the inverse homeomorphism. Thus a commuting permutation extends to a deck map.

Deck maps ↔ permutations D with DPₐ=PₐD and DPᵦ=PᵦD.

D=(12) fails: for b at sheet 1, D(Pᵦ(1))=2 while Pᵦ(D(1))=3.

Only the identity commutes with both permutations: 1 deck transformation, with sheet orbits {1},{2},{3}. The cover is not regular.

Why one fixed sheet forces the identity here

In a connected cover, every point can be reached from a chosen vertex by a path. A deck map fixing that vertex takes each such path to a lift with the same projection and starting point. Uniqueness of lifting forces the path, hence every endpoint, to stay fixed. Thus a nonidentity deck map has no fixed point in a connected cover.

In the first cover Pᵦ fixes only sheet 1. Any D commuting with Pᵦ must send 1 to a Pᵦ-fixed sheet, so D(1)=1. Connectedness now forces D to be the identity. A connected cover is called regular when its deck group acts transitively on a fiber. This cover is not regular. Its subgroup is not normal either: b∈H, but abA lifts 1→2→3→3 and is not in H.

Compare a regular cover with the same sheet count

Set Pₐ=(123), Pᵦ=identity. The cover is connected, and a word closes exactly when its signed a-count is divisible by three. This is the kernel of the homomorphism F(a,b)→ℤ/3 sending a to 1 and b to 0, so it is normal. Its deck maps are exactly identity, (123) and (132): any permutation commuting with a three-cycle is a power of that cycle. They act transitively, so this cover is regular. Its rank is still four.

In the first cover, Pₐ and Pᵦ generate six sheet permutations by word transport, while only one is a deck map. In the cyclic cover the two sets happen to agree. Transport around a based loop and a homeomorphism over the base are different constructions.

06 / Independent work

Use the argument beyond the controls

Write an attempt before revealing a hint. Written explanations are not automatically proof-checked. Tasks 5–6 change the sheet set beyond the workbench.

Optional preparation for Task 6: for a finite graph with E edges, V vertices and c components, H₁ over the integers is free abelian of rank E−V+c. Compute componentwise using tree collapse and H₁ as the abelianization of π₁. The task uses this graph-homology fact in addition to the core lifting prerequisites.

1. Same endpoints, different edges

In the first cover, lift aA and aa from sheet 1. Name the positive edge used by each letter and its direction. Explain why only one path backtracks, even though both end at 1.

Hint

At sheet 2, A uses the inverse of the positive a-edge ending there, not the positive a-edge leaving there.

Worked explanation

aA uses a1 forwards then a1 backwards, so the two traversals backtrack and cancel. aa uses a1 forwards then the different edge a2 forwards. It is a nonempty reduced edge loop. Projecting a contraction would contract the nonempty reduced word aa in W, which is impossible by the free-group classification.

2. A stabilizer is not the kernel

In the first cover, compare b, abA and b². Which lift closed from sheet 1? Which induce the identity permutation on all three sheets? Use a conjugate to decide whether H is normal.

Hint

b fixes sheet 1 but exchanges 2 and 3. A kernel requires trivial action at every sheet.

Worked explanation

b closes at 1 but acts as (23), so it is in the stabilizer H and outside the action kernel. abA sends 1 to 3 and is outside H; its sheet action exchanges 1 and 3. b² acts identically on every sheet and lies in the kernel, hence H. Since b∈H and abA∉H, H is not normal. None of the three nonempty reduced words is the identity in the free group; the action kernel is also larger than the trivial group.

3. Rewrite before simplifying the picture

Using the first cover's root-1 tree, rewrite abba and abaBA in the four displayed basis generators. Explain why both are nontrivial subgroup elements. How does the tree-collapse theorem strengthen the telescoping identity?

Hint

The first two steps of abba use tree edges. Its final two steps use b3 and a2.

Worked explanation

abba=(abbA)(aa)=g4 g2 after cancelling Aa. The loop abaBA is g3 itself: its a3 traversal is the only non-tree step. These are nonempty reduced words in the free basis, so neither is trivial. The telescoping identity proves generation; collapsing the tree additionally proves there are no nonempty reduced relations between these generators. Projection also shows their original words are nontrivial in F(a,b).

4. Keep the sheet count, change the cover

For Pₐ=(123), Pᵦ=identity, use the tree a1,a2. Find a free basis at sheet 1 and all deck maps. Compare its subgroup with the first cover using b and abA. Do equal sheet count and subgroup rank determine a cover?

Hint

The tree paths are empty,a,aa. Use each remaining positive edge, including the three b-loops.

Worked explanation

A basis is b,abA,aaa,aabAA (the table's order), equivalently {a³,b,abA,a²bA²}. The deck maps are the three powers of (123). Both b and abA have signed a-count zero, so both belong to this kernel subgroup. In the first cover only b belongs. Both subgroups have index three and rank four, but one is normal and the other is not. A cover isomorphism over W would conjugate the fiber actions and preserve its deck group; their different deck counts exclude such an isomorphism. They are nevertheless homotopy equivalent as abstract connected graphs of rank four.

5. Four sheets and an intermediate symmetry count

Change to sheets {1,2,3,4}, Pₐ=(1234), Pᵦ=(13), with 2 and 4 fixed by Pᵦ. Prove connectedness; calculate the free rank; use the forward tree a1,a2,a3 to find a basis. Find every deck permutation and decide regularity.

Hint

Anything commuting with a four-cycle is a power of it. Which powers preserve the transposition (13)? For the basis use paths empty,a,aa,aaa.

Worked explanation

Pₐ alone reaches all four sheets, so the cover is connected. There are eight positive edges and four vertices, giving rank 8−4+1=5. The five non-tree edges give {aaaa,bAA,abA,aab,aaabAAA}. A deck permutation commuting with Pₐ must equal Pₐᵏ. Conjugating (13) by an odd power gives (24), while an even power gives (13). Thus the only deck maps are identity and Pₐ²=(13)(24). Their sheet orbits are {1,3} and {2,4}, so this connected four-sheet cover is not regular. A nonregular cover can have more than one deck map. The Math Atlas group-computation chapter already develops the related dihedral coset action; this problem asks for its covering graph, subgroup basis and deck maps.

6. Remove connectedness

On four sheets set Pₐ=(12), Pᵦ=(34). Find the connected components, their free ranks, and the index of the subgroup based at sheet 1. Compute the total rank of H₁ of the graph. Does the deck map D=(12) contradict the fixed-point argument?

Hint

The orbit of 1 contains only 1 and 2. Count four positive edges in each component. D fixes an entire different component.

Worked explanation

The components have vertex sets {1,2} and {3,4}, each a two-sheet cover with four edges, so each fundamental group has rank three. The subgroup based at 1 has index two, the degree of that component, rather than four. The whole graph has H₁ rank 3+3=6=E−V+c, with c=2 components; it has no single based fundamental group accounting for both components at once. D=(12) commutes with both permutations, so it is a deck map; it fixes sheets 3 and 4. The fixed-point proof required connectedness: fixing a point determines a deck map only on the component reachable from that point.
