Method selection and verification

Look first for a chain-rule pattern f(g(x))g′(x)f(g(x))g^{\prime}(x), then for a product suited to integration by parts, a rational function suited to partial fractions, or a trigonometric identity. For a definite integral with a singular endpoint, replace that endpoint by a variable and take a one-sided limit. A numerical quadrature is appropriate when no usable elementary antiderivative appears or when the function is given by data. It needs a convergence or error argument.

A derivative check confirms an indefinite answer up to a constant. A definite answer can also be checked by estimating sign and size: if m≤f(x)≤Mm\leq f(x)\leq M on [a,b][a,b] with a≤ba\leq b, then m(b−a)≤∫abf(x) dx≤M(b−a)m(b-a)\leq\int_a^b f(x)\,dx\leq M(b-a). A result outside that bound is wrong regardless of how plausible the algebra looks. Units provide another check: integrating a rate multiplies by the input unit.

Hyperbolic derivatives and branches

For a differentiable inner function, multiply the applicable outer derivative by its derivative and check the inner range first. The value arcosh⁡1=0\operatorname{arcosh}1=0 exists, but its right slope is unbounded, so its displayed derivative does not extend to 11. A composition at an inner value of 11 needs its own one-sided analysis.
