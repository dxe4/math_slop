Rectangular channel circulation survey

Use Green’s theorem and four directed edges to cross-check circulation and orientation.

A planar calibration field is prescribed over a rectangular channel survey. Determine circulation around its boundary, verify the orientation and theorem conditions, then repeat the answer by direct edge integration before interpreting what a field measurement would require.

  • Theorem choice and hypotheses
  • Reproducible reporting

Working assumptions

  • The prescribed planar velocity field is steady and smooth on an open neighborhood of the rectangle.
  • The four directed edges form the complete simple survey boundary; values are a synthetic calibration model, not flow measurements.

Stages

1. Map the boundary and hypotheses

Draw and parameterize all four directed edges from bottom through right, top, and left. Before applying Green’s theorem, check the bounded region, complete simple piecewise-smooth boundary, counterclockwise orientation, and C1C^1 field on an open neighborhood.

Evidence to produce. Labeled route, four edge parametrizations, orientation explanation, and explicit theorem-hypothesis checklist.

Guidance. The region must remain on your left as you walk the route. The polynomial field is continuously differentiable around and throughout this rectangle.

2. Integrate over the interior

Calculate Qx−PyQ_x-P_y with coefficient units and integrate it over the entire 3 m3\,\mathrm m by 2 m2\,\mathrm m rectangle. Show both physical length bounds and explain why the result has circulation units m2/s\mathrm m^2/\mathrm s.

Evidence to produce. Both partial derivatives, area integral, evaluated result 3.0 m2/s3.0\,\mathrm m^2/\mathrm s, and dimensional check.

Guidance. Differentiate Q=bx2Q=bx^2 in xx and P=−ayP=-ay in yy; Green’s positively oriented formula integrates Qx−PyQ_x-P_y.

3. Verify every edge

Compute the directed line integral on each of the four edges independently, tabulate the contributions, and compare their sum with Green’s value. Reverse the traversal for a sign check and evaluate the width-shrinking limit as a separate boundary check.

Evidence to produce. Four-edge table with units m2/s\mathrm m^2/\mathrm s, matching sum, reversed sign, and width-limit calculation.

Guidance. On horizontal edges integrate P dxP\,dx; on vertical edges integrate Q dyQ\,dy. A single midpoint on each edge happens to integrate the nonzero constant edge components exactly here.

4. Explain the survey result

Write a field-survey note explaining the sign of circulation, what the integral measures, and why an incomplete route or an unmodeled interior singularity would invalidate the Green calculation. Separate the smooth calibration model from measured flow.

Evidence to produce. Short interpretation stating orientation, units, regularity, boundary completeness, and model limits.

Guidance. Reversing direction changes the sign but not the geometry. Green’s theorem needs regularity across the enclosed region, not just along the sampled edges.

Self-assessment criteria

Judge each criterion as not yet evidenced, developing, or supported by your work.

  • Oriented boundary. I parameterized every edge in a complete counterclockwise route and justified the region-on-the-left orientation.
  • Theorem conditions. I checked the simple piecewise-smooth boundary, bounded enclosed region, and C1C^1 field on an open neighborhood before applying Green’s theorem.
  • Interior calculation. I derived Qx−PyQ_x-P_y from the dimensioned field, used the full rectangle, and obtained circulation in m2/s\mathrm m^2/\mathrm s.
  • Independent checks. I reproduced each directed edge contribution and the total, then checked the reversed sign and zero-width limit without applying Green to a degenerate region.
  • Physical interpretation. I explained the circulation sign and distinguished a smooth prescribed field from measured flow, including the effects of a missing edge or interior singularity.

Limitations to address

  • The prescribed field may differ from measured channel velocities.
  • The model is planar and steady, so three-dimensional or changing flow is outside its scope.
  • Green’s theorem requires suitable regularity throughout the enclosed region and a complete oriented boundary.
