  1. Problem 1. Fluency Simplify (x2+3x)/(x2−9)(x^2+3x)/(x^2-9) and state every excluded input.

    Hint. Factor out xx in the numerator and use a difference of squares below.

    Solution. Factor the quotient as x(x+3)(x−3)(x+3)\frac{x(x+3)}{(x-3)(x+3)}. Cancellation gives xx−3\frac{x}{x-3} only for x≠−3,3x\ne-3,3. Both exclusions come from the original denominator; dropping −3-3 changes the function.

  2. Problem 2. Fluency Simplify x2−9x−3\frac{x^2-9}{x-3} and keep its domain.

    Hint. Factor the numerator.

    Solution. Factor x2−9=(x−3)(x+3)x^2-9=(x-3)(x+3) and cancel the nonzero factor x−3x-3. The result is x+3x+3 for x≠3x\ne3; the original input x=3x=3 remains excluded.

  3. Problem 3. Fluency Simplify x2+3xx2−9\frac{x^2+3x}{x^2-9} and state exclusions.

    Hint. Factor numerator and denominator first.

    Solution. Factor x2+3x=x(x+3)x^2+3x=x(x+3) and x2−9=(x−3)(x+3)x^2-9=(x-3)(x+3). Canceling gives x/(x−3)x/(x-3), but the original exclusions remain x≠−3,3x\ne-3,3.

  4. Problem 4. Interpretation In x2+3xx2−9\frac{x^2+3x}{x^2-9}, is x=−3x=-3 allowed after cancelling x+3x+3? Explain using the original denominator.

    Hint. Check original denominator.

    Solution. No. In the original quotient the denominator x2−9x^2-9 is zero at x=−3x=-3. The simplified formula has a value there, but cancellation does not fill the hole in the original domain.

  5. Problem 5. Fluency Rationalize 1+h−1h\frac{\sqrt{1+h}-1}{h} for h≠0h\ne0.

    Hint. Multiply by conjugate.

    Solution. Multiplying by the conjugate gives 1+h−1h=11+h+1\frac{\sqrt{1+h}-1}{h}=\frac1{\sqrt{1+h}+1} for h≥−1h\ge-1 and h≠0h\ne0. Cancellation is valid only because the original denominator is nonzero.

  6. Problem 6. Fluency Solve ln⁡(x−1)+ln⁡(x+1)=ln⁡8\ln(x-1)+\ln(x+1)=\ln 8 over the real numbers.

    Hint. Require x>1x>1 before combining logarithms.

    Solution. Both logarithm arguments must be positive, so x>1x>1. Then ln⁡(x−1)+ln⁡(x+1)=ln⁡(x2−1)=ln⁡8\ln(x-1)+\ln(x+1)=\ln(x^2-1)=\ln8 gives x2−1=8x^2-1=8 and x=±3x=\pm3. Only x=3x=3 is in the domain; its arguments are 22 and 44, with product 88.

  7. Problem 7. Fluency Solve ln⁡(x−1)+ln⁡(x+1)=ln⁡8\ln(x-1)+\ln(x+1)=\ln 8.

    Hint. Require x>1x>1 before combining logarithms.

    Solution. The domain is x>1x>1. Combining the logarithms gives ln⁡(x2−1)=ln⁡8\ln(x^2-1)=\ln8, hence x2=9x^2=9. Only x=3x=3 satisfies the original domain condition.

  8. Problem 8. Interpretation Is ln⁡(u+v)=ln⁡u+ln⁡v\ln(u+v)=\ln u+\ln v for positive u,vu,v?

    Hint. Test u=v=1u=v=1.

    Solution. No. With u=v=1u=v=1, the left side is ln⁡2\ln2 while the proposed right side is ln⁡1+ln⁡1=0\ln1+\ln1=0. The valid law is ln⁡(uv)=ln⁡u+ln⁡v\ln(uv)=\ln u+\ln v for u,v>0u,v>0; it applies to products.

  9. Problem 9. Fluency Find log⁡28\log_2 8 and log⁡2(1/4)\log_2(1/4).

  10. Problem 10. Proof Explain why x2=∣x∣\sqrt{x^2}=|x| for real xx.

    Hint. Square roots are nonnegative.

    Solution. The principal square root is nonnegative. Both x2\sqrt{x^2} and ∣x∣|x| are nonnegative and square to x2x^2, so x2=∣x∣\sqrt{x^2}=|x| for every real xx.

  11. Problem 11. Fluency If cos⁡θ=−5/13\cos\theta=-5/13 and θ\theta lies in quadrant II, find sin⁡θ\sin\theta and tan⁡θ\tan\theta.

  15. Problem 15. Interpretation At what real angles is tan⁡θ\tan\theta undefined?

    Hint. Require cos⁡θ≠0\cos\theta\ne0.

    Solution. Because tan⁡θ=sin⁡θ/cos⁡θ\tan\theta=\sin\theta/\cos\theta, it is undefined exactly where cos⁡θ=0\cos\theta=0: θ=π/2+kπ\theta=\pi/2+k\pi for integers kk. At these angles the denominator is zero and the numerator is ±1\pm1.

  19. Problem 19. Interpretation Where is 1+tan⁡2θ=sec⁡2θ1+\tan^2\theta=\sec^2\theta valid?

  20. Problem 20. Proof Derive sin⁡2θ\sin 2\theta from sine addition.

    Hint. Set α=β=θ\alpha=\beta=\theta in the addition formula.

    Solution. The addition formula is sin⁡(α+β)=sin⁡αcos⁡β+cos⁡αsin⁡β\sin(\alpha+\beta)=\sin\alpha\cos\beta+\cos\alpha\sin\beta. Setting α=β=θ\alpha=\beta=\theta gives sin⁡(2θ)=2sin⁡θcos⁡θ\sin(2\theta)=2\sin\theta\cos\theta for every real θ\theta.

  21. Problem 21. Fluency Convert the Cartesian point (−3,−3)(-3,-3) to polar coordinates with r≥0r\ge0 and 0≤θ<2π0\le\theta<2\pi. Then give one different polar representation without the angle-range restriction.

  22. Problem 22. Modeling A proposed position formula is x(t)=Asin⁡(t)+vt2x(t)=A\sin(t)+vt^2, with tt in seconds, AA in metres, and vv in metres per second. Diagnose and repair its dimensions.

  24. Problem 24. Interpretation Does the origin have a unique polar angle?

    Hint. Every θ\theta with r=0r=0 maps to the origin.

    Solution. No. The coordinate equations are x=rcos⁡θx=r\cos\theta and y=rsin⁡θy=r\sin\theta. At r=0r=0, both coordinates vanish for every angle, so (0,θ)(0,\theta) represents the origin for any θ\theta. No direction is distinguished.

  25. Problem 25. Interpretation Check dimensions of Asin⁡(ωt+ϕ)A\sin(\omega t+\phi) for position.

    Hint. The argument must be dimensionless, and AA must have length units.

    Solution. For Asin⁡(ωt+ϕ)A\sin(\omega t+\phi) to be a position, ω\omega must have inverse-time units, ϕ\phi must be an angle in radians, and AA must have length units. The sine factor itself is dimensionless.

  26. Problem 26. Challenge Prepare a four-entry reference covering cancellation, logarithms, angles, and polar coordinates. Include a failure case for each.

Chapter synthesis

  • Algebraic equivalence must preserve domain restrictions, especially after cancellation or logarithmic transformations.
  • Radians connect arc length with trigonometric function values; small-angle approximations have measurable error.
  • Coordinate conversions require quadrant and origin checks, while dimensional consistency checks the kind of quantity in an equation.

Common mistakes to check

  • “Cancelling a factor restores excluded inputs.” Cancellation preserves values only where the cancelled factor was nonzero; it does not enlarge the original domain. Test with problem 2
  • “x2\sqrt{x^2} is always xx.” The principal square root is nonnegative, so x2=∣x∣\sqrt{x^2}=|x| for real xx. Test with problem 10
  • “An identity and an approximation are interchangeable.” sin⁡2θ+cos⁡2θ=1\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1 is exact for every real θ\theta; sin⁡θ≈θ\sin\theta\approx\theta has useful accuracy only near zero when θ\theta is measured in radians. Test with problem 35
