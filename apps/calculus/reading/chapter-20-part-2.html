Concept checks

  1. Can two disagreeing path limits disprove a multivariable limit?

    • Yes
    • No
    • Only for lines

    Answer. Yes. A full limit must give the same value on every approach path.

  2. Can agreement along all tested lines prove a limit?

    • Yes
    • No
    • Only with a graph

    Answer. No. Unexamined curved paths remain; a uniform neighborhood argument is needed.

  3. What is fxf_x for f=x2+y2f=x^2+y^2?

    • 2x2x
    • 2y2y
    • 2x+2y2x+2y

    Answer. 2x2x. Hold yy constant and differentiate with respect to xx.

    Answer. f(x,y)=constantf(x,y)=\text{constant}. A level curve collects inputs with one fixed output value.

Chapter synthesis

  • Domains specify allowed approaches; graphs and level sets display different aspects of the same scalar function.
  • Conflicting paths disprove a limit; a distance bound can prove it uniformly over all paths.
  • Partial derivatives describe coordinate slices and alone do not guarantee continuity or differentiability.
  • Continuous mixed second partials on an open neighborhood guarantee equality of their two orders.

Common mistakes to check

  • “Agreement along the coordinate axes proves a limit.” Axes inspect only two paths; for q(x,y)=x2yx4+y2q(x,y)=\frac{x^2y}{x^4+y^2} away from the origin, the limit along every straight line through the origin is zero, while q(x,x2)=12q(x,x^2)=\frac12 for x≠0x\ne0. Test with problem 18
  • “A partial derivative follows the actual motion of an object.” It holds the other coordinates fixed; motion changing several coordinates requires a chain rule. Test with problem 23
  • “Mixed partial derivatives always commute.” Continuous mixed partials on a neighborhood guarantee equality; existence at the point alone does not. Test with problem 33
