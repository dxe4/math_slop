Written practice and solutions

  1. Problem 1. Fluency Differentiate (x2+1)(x3−2)(x^2+1)(x^3-2), showing the product rule before simplifying.

    Hint. Differentiate one factor at a time.

    Solution. The product rule gives 2x(x3−2)+(x2+1)3x22x(x^3-2)+(x^2+1)3x^2. Expanding yields 5x4+3x2−4x5x^4+3x^2-4x. Both the polynomial and its derivative are defined for every real xx.

  2. Problem 2. Fluency Derive the derivative of x2+1x−1\frac{x^2+1}{x-1} for x≠1x\ne1 and simplify it.

    Hint. Use the quotient rule with u=x2+1u=x^2+1 and v=x−1v=x-1.

    Solution. The quotient rule gives 2x(x−1)−(x2+1)(x−1)2=x2−2x−1(x−1)2\frac{2x(x-1)-(x^2+1)}{(x-1)^2}=\frac{x^2-2x-1}{(x-1)^2} for x≠1x\ne1. The original function is undefined at x=1x=1, so no derivative is asserted there.

  3. Problem 3. Fluency Differentiate (x2+3)(x3−1)(x^2+3)(x^3-1) using the product rule before simplifying.

    Hint. Differentiate one factor at a time.

    Solution. The product rule gives 2x(x3−1)+3x2(x2+3)2x(x^3-1)+3x^2(x^2+3). Expanding yields 5x4+9x2−2x5x^4+9x^2-2x. The unsimplified form shows both factor derivatives.

  4. Problem 4. Fluency Differentiate xx+2\frac{x}{x+2}, and state the excluded input.

    Hint. Use the quotient rule with u=xu=x and v=x+2v=x+2.

    Solution. The quotient rule gives (x+2)−x(x+2)2=2(x+2)2\frac{(x+2)-x}{(x+2)^2}=\frac2{(x+2)^2}. The original function and its derivative both require x≠−2x\ne-2.

  5. Problem 5. Fluency Find the derivative of 1x2\frac{1}{x^2} and state its real domain.

    Hint. Write the function as x−2x^{-2} for x≠0x\ne0.

    Solution. The power rule gives −2x−3=−2/x3-2x^{-3}=-2/x^3 for x≠0x\ne0. The original expression is undefined at zero, so its derivative has no value there.

  6. Problem 6. Fluency For y=(x−1)(x+2)(x2+1)y=(x-1)(x+2)(x^2+1), find y′y^{\prime} at x=1x=1 without expanding the full product.

    Hint. At a zero of one factor, most product-rule terms vanish.

    Solution. In the three-factor product rule, the terms that retain x−1x-1 vanish at x=1x=1. Differentiating that factor leaves 1(1+2)(12+1)=61(1+2)(1^2+1)=6, so y′(1)=6y^{\prime}(1)=6.

  7. Problem 7. Fluency Differentiate ln⁡(1+x2)/(1+x)\ln(1+x^2)/(1+x), and state its domain.

    Hint. The logarithm’s input is always positive, but the quotient has a restriction.

    Solution. The domain is x≠−1x\ne-1 because 1+x2>01+x^2>0 for all real xx. With u′=2x/(1+x2)u^{\prime}=2x/(1+x^2) and v′=1v^{\prime}=1, the quotient rule gives 2x(1+x)/(1+x2)−ln⁡(1+x2)(1+x)2\frac{2x(1+x)/(1+x^2)-\ln(1+x^2)}{(1+x)^2} for x≠−1x\ne-1.

  8. Problem 8. Fluency For x>0x>0, differentiate x1/xx^{1/x} and find where its derivative is zero.

    Hint. Take logarithms and differentiate (ln⁡x)/x(\ln x)/x.

    Solution. For x>0x>0, set y=x1/xy=x^{1/x}. Then ln⁡y=(ln⁡x)/x\ln y=(\ln x)/x, so y′/y=(1−ln⁡x)/x2y^{\prime}/y=(1-\ln x)/x^2. Hence y′=x1/x(1−ln⁡x)/x2y^{\prime}=x^{1/x}(1-\ln x)/x^2. The other factors are positive, so the derivative vanishes exactly at ln⁡x=1\ln x=1, or x=ex=e.

  9. Problem 9. Fluency Differentiate sin⁡(3x2)\sin(3x^2) and identify both chain-rule factors.

    Hint. The outside derivative is cosine; the inside derivative is 6x6x.

    Solution. By the chain rule, ddxsin⁡(3x2)=cos⁡(3x2)⋅6x\frac{d}{dx}\sin(3x^2)=\cos(3x^2)\cdot6x. The factor 6x6x comes from differentiating the angle 3x23x^2.

  10. Problem 10. Fluency Find the derivative and domain of ln⁡(1+x2)\ln(1+x^2).

    Hint. The logarithm's input is always at least one.

    Solution. Since 1+x2>01+x^2>0 for every real xx, the function has domain R\mathbb R. The chain rule gives derivative 2x/(1+x2)2x/(1+x^2), also defined everywhere.

  11. Problem 11. Fluency Differentiate ln⁡∣x−1∣\ln|x-1| and describe its domain.

    Hint. The absolute-value logarithm rule needs a nonzero inside.

    Solution. The derivative of ln⁡∣x−1∣\ln|x-1| is 1/(x−1)1/(x-1) on (−∞,1)∪(1,∞)(-\infty,1)\cup(1,\infty). At x=1x=1 the logarithm argument is zero, so the function and derivative are undefined.

  12. Problem 12. Fluency Differentiate xxx^x as a real function, and find where its derivative is zero.

    Hint. Take logarithms on the positive domain.

    Solution. On the positive domain x>0x>0, logarithmic differentiation gives y′=xx(ln⁡x+1)y^{\prime}=x^x(\ln x+1). Since xx>0x^x>0, the derivative vanishes exactly when ln⁡x=−1\ln x=-1, or x=e−1x=e^{-1}.

  13. Problem 13. Fluency Differentiate (x2+1)x(x^2+1)^x and give the full real domain.

    Hint. Write y=exp⁡[xln⁡(x2+1)]y=\exp[x\ln(x^2+1)].

    Solution. The base x2+1x^2+1 is positive for every real xx, so the domain is R\mathbb R. Logarithmic differentiation gives ddx(x2+1)x=(x2+1)x[ln⁡(x2+1)+2x2x2+1]\frac{d}{dx}(x^2+1)^x=(x^2+1)^x\left[\ln(x^2+1)+\frac{2x^2}{x^2+1}\right].

  14. Problem 14. Fluency Differentiate ln⁡(x2)\ln(x^2) and explain why the result cannot be assigned at zero.

    Hint. The logarithm argument is positive only for x≠0x\ne0.

    Solution. For x≠0x\ne0, the chain rule gives ddxln⁡(x2)=2xx2=2/x\frac{d}{dx}\ln(x^2)=\frac{2x}{x^2}=2/x. At x=0x=0 the logarithm is undefined, so no derivative can be assigned there.

  15. Problem 15. Fluency Find the derivative of arcsin⁡(2x)\arcsin(2x), including all domain and endpoint restrictions.

    Hint. Separate where the function exists from where its derivative is finite.

    Solution. The function exists where ∣2x∣≤1|2x|\le1, so its domain is [−1/2,1/2][-1/2,1/2]. The chain rule gives derivative 2/1−4x22/\sqrt{1-4x^2} on (−1/2,1/2)(-1/2,1/2). At the endpoints the one-sided quotient is unbounded, so there is no finite endpoint derivative.

  16. Problem 16. Fluency A quantity obeys Q(t)=Q0e−ktcos⁡(ωt)Q(t)=Q_0e^{-kt}\cos(\omega t), with Q0Q_0 measured in coulombs and tt in seconds. Find Q′Q^{\prime} and its units.

    Hint. Use the product rule and require dimensionless function inputs.

    Solution. Dimensionless arguments require kk and ω\omega to have inverse-second units, with ω\omega in radians per second. The product and chain rules give Q′(t)=Q0e−kt[−kcos⁡(ωt)−ωsin⁡(ωt)]Q^{\prime}(t)=Q_0e^{-kt}[-k\cos(\omega t)-\omega\sin(\omega t)]. Each bracketed term has inverse-second units, so the derivative has units coulombs per second.

  17. Problem 17. Interpretation Let f(x)=x3f(x)=x^3. Explain why the reciprocal inverse-derivative formula fails at zero, even though ff is one-to-one.

    Hint. Examine the difference quotient of the cube-root inverse.

    Solution. The inverse is g(y)=y1/3g(y)=y^{1/3}, but f′(0)=0f^{\prime}(0)=0 violates the reciprocal formula’s nonzero-derivative condition. For h≠0h\ne0, [g(h)−g(0)]/h=1/∣h∣2/3→+∞[g(h)-g(0)]/h=1/|h|^{2/3}\to+\infty. Thus gg has no finite derivative at zero even though ff is one-to-one.

  18. Problem 18. Fluency Differentiate e−2tcos⁡(3t)e^{-2t}\cos(3t), assuming tt is in seconds and 22 and 33 have inverse-second units.

    Hint. Apply product and chain rules separately.

    Solution. The product and chain rules give ddt[e−2tcos⁡(3t)]=e−2t[−2cos⁡(3t)−3sin⁡(3t)]\frac{d}{dt}[e^{-2t}\cos(3t)]=e^{-2t}[-2\cos(3t)-3\sin(3t)]. With the coefficients measured in inverse seconds, the arguments are dimensionless; if the output is dimensionless, the derivative has inverse-second units.

  19. Problem 19. Fluency Find the derivative of tan⁡x\tan x and state where the original function is defined.

    Hint. Write tan⁡x=sin⁡x/cos⁡x\tan x=\sin x/\cos x.

    Solution. The quotient rule gives cos⁡2x+sin⁡2xcos⁡2x=sec⁡2x\frac{\cos^2x+\sin^2x}{\cos^2x}=\sec^2x. This holds where cos⁡x≠0\cos x\ne0, namely x≠π/2+kπx\ne\pi/2+k\pi for every integer kk.

  20. Problem 20. Interpretation If dd is measured in degrees, differentiate sin⁡(πd/180)\sin(\pi d/180) with respect to dd.

    Hint. Convert degrees to radians inside the sine before applying the chain rule.

    Solution. The chain rule gives dddsin⁡(πd/180)=(π/180)cos⁡(πd/180)\frac{d}{dd}\sin(\pi d/180)=(\pi/180)\cos(\pi d/180) per degree. The factor π/180\pi/180 converts a change in degrees to a change in radians.

  21. Problem 21. Fluency Differentiate arcsin⁡(3x)\arcsin(3x) and state its derivative domain and function domain.

    Hint. Apply the inverse-sine derivative and an inner factor three.

    Solution. The function domain follows from ∣3x∣≤1|3x|\le1: it is [−1/3,1/3][-1/3,1/3]. On the interior (−1/3,1/3)(-1/3,1/3), the chain rule gives derivative 3/1−9x23/\sqrt{1-9x^2}. The endpoint quotients are unbounded, so finite derivatives do not exist there.

  22. Problem 22. Fluency Differentiate arctan⁡(2x)\arctan(2x) for real xx.

    Hint. Use u=2xu=2x and u′=2u^{\prime}=2.

    Solution. With u=2xu=2x, the chain rule gives ddxarctan⁡(2x)=u′1+u2=21+4x2\frac{d}{dx}\arctan(2x)=\frac{u^{\prime}}{1+u^2}=\frac2{1+4x^2}. The denominator is positive for every real xx.

  23. Problem 23. Fluency For f(x)=x3+2xf(x)=x^3+2x, find (f−1)′(3)(f^{-1})^{\prime}(3).

    Hint. Find aa with f(a)=3f(a)=3 before applying the inverse formula.

    Solution. Since f(1)=3f(1)=3 and f′(1)=3(1)2+2=5f^{\prime}(1)=3(1)^2+2=5, the inverse-derivative formula gives (f−1)′(3)=1/5(f^{-1})^{\prime}(3)=1/5. Because f′(x)=3x2+2>0f^{\prime}(x)=3x^2+2>0 everywhere, the inverse is well defined.

  24. Problem 24. Fluency Differentiate xy=1xy=1 implicitly and state where the graph derivative exists.

    Hint. Apply the product rule to x y(x)x\,y(x).

    Solution. Implicit differentiation gives y+xy′=0y+xy^{\prime}=0, so y′=−y/x=−1/x2y^{\prime}=-y/x=-1/x^2 using y=1/xy=1/x. The relation has x≠0x\ne0, and the derivative exists on both branches.

  25. Problem 25. Fluency Implicitly differentiate x2+xy+y2=7x^2+xy+y^2=7 and find the slope at (1,2)(1,2).

    Hint. Differentiate xyxy as y+xy′y+xy^{\prime} and verify the point.

    Solution. Differentiation gives 2x+y+(x+2y)y′=02x+y+(x+2y)y^{\prime}=0, hence y′=−(2x+y)/(x+2y)y^{\prime}=-(2x+y)/(x+2y) where x+2y≠0x+2y\ne0. At (1,2)(1,2), 1+2+4=71+2+4=7 and the denominator is 55, so the slope is −4/5-4/5.

  26. Problem 26. Fluency For x>0x>0, differentiate xsin⁡xx^{\sin x}.

    Hint. Take logarithms: ln⁡y=(sin⁡x)ln⁡x\ln y=(\sin x)\ln x.

    Solution. For x>0x>0, logarithmic differentiation gives y′/y=(cos⁡x)ln⁡x+(sin⁡x)/xy^{\prime}/y=(\cos x)\ln x+(\sin x)/x. Thus y′=xsin⁡x[cos⁡xln⁡x+(sin⁡x)/x]y^{\prime}=x^{\sin x}[\cos x\ln x+(\sin x)/x]. The positive domain makes the real logarithm valid.

  27. Problem 27. Proof Prove that the central difference f(a+h)−f(a−h)2h\frac{f(a+h)-f(a-h)}{2h} equals f′(a)f^{\prime}(a) exactly for every quadratic ff and every h≠0h\ne0.

    Hint. Write f(x)=Ax2+Bx+Cf(x)=Ax^2+Bx+C and expand at a±ha\pm h.

    Solution. For f(x)=Ax2+Bx+Cf(x)=Ax^2+Bx+C, the difference f(a+h)−f(a−h)f(a+h)-f(a-h) is A[(a+h)2−(a−h)2]+2Bh=4Aah+2BhA[(a+h)^2-(a-h)^2]+2Bh=4Aah+2Bh. Dividing by 2h2h for h≠0h\ne0 gives 2Aa+B=f′(a)2Aa+B=f^{\prime}(a) exactly. Floating-point evaluation may differ slightly because of rounding.

  28. Problem 28. Fluency Find the tangent line to x2+y2=25x^2+y^2=25 at (−3,4)(-3,4).

    Hint. Use y′=−x/yy^{\prime}=-x/y and point-slope form.

    Solution. From x2+y2=25x^2+y^2=25, implicit differentiation gives y′=−x/yy^{\prime}=-x/y where y≠0y\ne0. At (−3,4)(-3,4) the slope is 3/43/4, so the tangent is y−4=34(x+3)y-4=\frac34(x+3). The radius is perpendicular to this line.

  29. Problem 29. Fluency Find the tangent line to x2+y2=25x^2+y^2=25 at (3,4)(3,4).

    Hint. Differentiate the relation where y≠0y\ne0.

    Solution. Implicit differentiation gives 2x+2yy′=02x+2yy^{\prime}=0, hence y′=−x/yy^{\prime}=-x/y. At (3,4)(3,4) the slope is −3/4-3/4, so the tangent is y−4=−34(x−3)y-4=-\frac34(x-3).

  30. Problem 30. Interpretation Let F(x,y)=y2−x3F(x,y)=y^2-x^3. At (0,0)(0,0) on the relation F(x,y)=0F(x,y)=0, explain why the implicit-derivative formula −FxFy-\frac{F_x}{F_y} is unavailable and describe the real local branches.

    Hint. Factor the relation for x≥0x\ge0 instead of dividing by a zero partial derivative.

    Solution. With F=y2−x3F=y^2-x^3, Fy=2y=0F_y=2y=0 at the origin, so the formula −Fx/Fy-F_x/F_y is unavailable. For x≥0x\ge0 the real branches are y=±x3/2y=\pm x^{3/2}, each with a horizontal one-sided tangent at zero. The full relation is not a single-valued y(x)y(x) near the origin.

  31. Problem 31. Fluency Differentiate esin⁡(3x)e^{\sin(3x)}.

    Hint. Use three nested maps.

    Solution. Apply the chain rule from the outside inward: the derivative is 3esin⁡(3x)cos⁡(3x)3e^{\sin(3x)}\cos(3x), including the inner factor 33.

  32. Problem 32. Interpretation Why does the inverse-derivative formula fail for f(x)=x3f(x)=x^3 at x=0x=0?

    Hint. Inspect f′(0)f^{\prime}(0).

    Solution. f′(0)=0f^{\prime}(0)=0, so the reciprocal formula would divide by zero. The inverse x1/3x^{1/3} has an unbounded difference quotient at zero.

  33. Problem 33. Interpretation Restrict f(x)=sin⁡xf(x)=\sin x to [−π/2,π/2][-\pi/2,\pi/2]. Compare the value and derivative of f−1f^{-1} at input zero with those of the reciprocal function 1/f1/f at zero.

    Hint. An inverse reverses input and output; a reciprocal divides by the original output.

    Solution. On the stated interval sine is one-to-one, so f−1=arcsin⁡f^{-1}=\arcsin and f−1(0)=0f^{-1}(0)=0. Its derivative at zero is 1/cos⁡(0)=11/\cos(0)=1. The reciprocal is 1/sin⁡x1/\sin x, which is undefined at zero because sin⁡0=0\sin0=0; it has neither a value nor a derivative there. The superscript −1-1 does not mean reciprocal.

  34. Problem 34. Interpretation At zero, compute the symmetric difference [∣h∣−∣−h∣]/(2h)[|h|-|-h|]/(2h) for h≠0h\ne0. Does its limit prove that ∣x∣|x| is differentiable at zero? Check both one-sided derivatives.

    Hint. The central numerator vanishes even if the left and right slopes disagree.

    Solution. Since ∣h∣=∣−h∣|h|=|-h|, every central quotient is zero. But the forward quotient ∣h∣/h|h|/h tends to 11 from the right and −1-1 from the left. The two-sided derivative therefore does not exist. Symmetric cancellation can conceal a corner.

  35. Problem 35. Interpretation Differentiate sin⁡x\sin x at zero symbolically, compare central differences at h=0.1h=0.1 and 0.010.01, then give a function whose central differences agree but whose derivative at zero fails.

The central differences [sin⁡h−sin⁡(−h)]/(2h)=sin⁡h/h[\sin h-\sin(-h)]/(2h)=\sin h/h are about 0.9983340.998334 and 0.9999830.999983.   36. Problem 36. Fluency Differentiate tanh⁡(2x−1)\tanh(2x-1).

    Hint. Name the inner derivative.

    Solution. The inner rate is 22 and (tanh⁡u)′=1/cosh⁡2u(\tanh u)'=1/\cosh^2u, so the derivative is 2/cosh⁡2(2x−1)2/\cosh^2(2x-1) for every real xx.

  38. Problem 38. Interpretation Is arcosh⁡(1+x2)\operatorname{arcosh}(1+x^2) differentiable at zero?

    Hint. Use the nonnegative inverse branch in cosh⁡(2u)=1+2sinh⁡2u\cosh(2u)=1+2\sinh^2u to express the function using arsinh⁡(∣x∣/2)\operatorname{arsinh}(|x|/\sqrt2). Then form the point difference quotient.

Concept checks

  1. What is d(sin⁡(x2))/dxd(\sin(x^2))/dx?

    • cos⁡(x2)\cos(x^2)
    • 2xcos⁡(x2)2x\cos(x^2)
    • 2sin⁡x2\sin x

    Answer. 2xcos⁡(x2)2x\cos(x^2). The chain rule multiplies the outer cosine derivative by the inner derivative 2x2x.

  2. What is d(ln⁡x)/dxd(\ln x)/dx for x>0x>0?

    • xx
    • 1/x1/x
    • (ln⁡x)/x(\ln x)/x

    Answer. 1/x1/x. The logarithm derivative is 1/x1/x on the positive domain.

  3. In the product rule, d(uv)/dxd(uv)/dx is…

    • u′v+uv′u^{\prime}v+uv^{\prime}
    • u′v′u^{\prime}v^{\prime}
    • u′/v′u^{\prime}/v^{\prime}

    Answer. u′v+uv′u^{\prime}v+uv^{\prime}. Differentiate one factor at a time, retaining the other, then add.

  4. What is d(e3x)/dxd(e^{3x})/dx?

    • e3xe^{3x}
    • 3e3x3e^{3x}
    • ex3e^{x^3}

    Answer. 3e3x3e^{3x}. The outer exponential derivative is itself; the inner derivative is 33.

  5. At y=0y=0 on x2+y2=25x^2+y^2=25, why does y′=−x/yy^{\prime}=-x/y fail?

    • The curve disappears
    • The tangent is vertical there
    • The circle has a corner

    Answer. The tangent is vertical there. Division by yy is invalid; at (±5,0)(\pm5,0) the circle has vertical tangents.

  6. At x=1x=1, which statement about real arcosh⁡x\operatorname{arcosh}x is correct?

    • It is defined, but has no finite derivative there
    • It is undefined there
    • Its derivative there is zero

    Answer. It is defined, but has no finite derivative there. The value is zero, while 1/x2−11/\sqrt{x^2-1} grows without bound as x↓1x\downarrow1.

    Why the other choices fail.

    • It is undefined there — The principal inverse includes its endpoint.
    • Its derivative there is zero — A denominator tending to zero does not give zero slope.

Chapter synthesis

  • Choose rules from the expression’s structure and keep the original domain restrictions.
  • The chain rule combines local rates; inverse and implicit formulas require hypotheses that justify solving locally.
  • Logarithmic differentiation handles variable exponents, while numerical differences offer checks with both domain and precision limitations.

Common mistakes to check

  • “The derivative of a product is the product of the derivatives.” For x⋅xx\cdot x, multiplying derivatives gives 1, whereas the derivative of x2x^2 is 2x2x; both changing factors must contribute. Test with problem 1
  • “An inverse function and a reciprocal are the same.” arcsin⁡x\arcsin x is the inverse of sine restricted to [−π/2,π/2][-\pi/2,\pi/2], with domain [−1,1][-1,1]; csc⁡x=1/sin⁡x\csc x=1/\sin x where sin⁡x≠0\sin x\ne0. Their derivatives also differ where defined. Test with problem 33
  • “A central difference always detects differentiability.” For f(x)=∣x∣f(x)=|x| at zero, every symmetric difference f(h)−f(−h)2h\frac{f(h)-f(-h)}{2h} equals zero for h≠0h\ne0, although the left and right derivatives are −1 and 1. Test with problem 34
