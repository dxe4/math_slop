Appendix E · Units, models, and interpretation

Calculus is useful when the equation describes a real question. These checks keep an elegant calculation attached to a valid model.

From story to quantities

Name the independent variable, dependent quantity, domain, and units before differentiating or integrating. A rate of change has output units divided by input units; integrating that rate over the input unit recovers output units. The derivative of area with respect to radius has length units, while the derivative of volume with respect to radius has area units. If a proposed result has incompatible units, revisit the differential element or the model.

Constraints define feasible sets. A rectangle with fixed perimeter has nonnegative sides and a closed width interval; a physical tank has finite capacity; a probability density must be nonnegative and integrate to one. Solving a critical-point equation outside the feasible region gives no valid optimum. State whether a parameter is measured, fitted, assumed, or chosen for the experiment.

Accumulation and geometry

For continuous velocity, displacement over [a,b][a,b] is ∫abv(t) dt\int_a^b v(t)\,dt and distance traveled is ∫ab∣v(t)∣ dt\int_a^b |v(t)|\,dt. For motion along a line, work done by a varying force from x=ax=a to x=bx=b is ∫abF(x) dx\int_a^b F(x)\,dx, where FF is the signed force component along that line. For density, mass is an integral of density against the appropriate length, area, or volume element. A center of mass is first moment divided by total mass. In every case, identify the small piece—rate×time\text{rate}\times\text{time}, force×displacement\text{force}\times\text{displacement}, or density×volume\text{density}\times\text{volume}—before sending its width to zero.

For a solid sliced perpendicular to the xx-axis, volume is ∫abA(x) dx\int_a^b A(x)\,dx over the interval [a,b][a,b], where A(x)A(x) is the cross-sectional area. For a shell about an axis, its thin volume is approximately circumference×height×thickness\text{circumference}\times\text{height}\times\text{thickness}. For a path, arc length integrates speed. For flux, an oriented normal matters. Similar-looking integrals can answer different physical questions, so a labeled sketch and units are often as valuable as the antiderivative.

Evidence and limits

A model may be mathematically exact and physically approximate. Linear drag, constant acceleration, a smooth temperature curve, or a continuous probability density are assumptions about reality. Testing a computation against an exact special case checks implementation, not the truth of the physical assumptions. A conclusion should separate theorem, measured evidence, numerical approximation, and model choice.

A reproducible investigation records equations, parameter values and units, initial or boundary data, interval, algorithm, code, checks, and the conclusion supported by those checks. If a numerical result changes materially under finer steps, it is not yet settled. If a result depends on a field being smooth across a missing point or on a rate being continuous at an event, state that limitation instead of hiding it behind a plot.

World geometry and its projection

A spatial point is a world triple (x,y,z)(x,y,z). A top orthographic projection keeps (x,y)(x,y) and loses zz; other views can also overlap distinct world points. A trace at world height z=z0z=z_0 is an intersection with that plane, not the surface’s projected shadow. Compute speed, tangent, normal, curvature, and intersection claims from world coordinates and equations; a projected image alone cannot certify them.

In (Rcos⁡t,Rsin⁡t,ht)(R\cos t,R\sin t,ht), dimensionless tt gives hh units of length per radian and rise 2πh2\pi h per turn. Physical time requires a separate time law.
