Tracer removal from a mixed tank

Compare exact decay with Euler and Heun updates while checking conservation, stability, and model validity.

A well-mixed tank initially holds 100 mg100\,\mathrm{mg} of tracer. Its instantaneous fractional removal rate is the constant a=0.5 h−1a=0.5\,\mathrm{h}^{-1}. Predict the tracer remaining and removed after 4 h4\,\mathrm{h}, and assess two time-stepping methods.

Working assumptions

  • No tracer enters the tank.
  • Mixing is instantaneous and the instantaneous fractional removal rate aa remains constant.

Stages

1. Define the state and invariant

Derive differential equations for tracer remaining CC and amount removed AA. Give initial conditions, parameter units, assumptions, and a proof that C+A=100 mgC+A=100\,\mathrm{mg} stays constant.

Evidence to produce. State equations, parameter table, unit check, and conservation derivation.

Guidance. Removal decreases CC and increases AA by the same instantaneous rate. Differentiate C+AC+A.

2. Solve and implement

Solve the state equations exactly, with a dimensionless exponential argument. Implement simultaneous Euler and explicit trapezoidal Heun updates from the same old state for each step. Give both vector update formulas and runnable code or pseudocode.

Evidence to produce. Closed-form C(t)C(t) and A(t)A(t) with units, both vector update rules, and reproducible implementation.

Guidance. For Heun, evaluate both component rates at the old state, predict both components, then average the old and predicted rates.

3. Refine at one endpoint

For N=4,8,16N=4,8,16 steps, compare both methods at T=4 hT=4\,\mathrm{h}. Report hh, CNC_N, ANA_N, absolute endpoint error, vector-rate evaluations, and maximum ∣Cn+An−100 mg∣|C_n+A_n-100\,\mathrm{mg}| over each trajectory. Also run Euler with one 4 h4\,\mathrm{h} step and discuss its sign.

Evidence to produce. Reproducible table, exact remaining and removed benchmarks, invariant defects, and positivity failure case.

Guidance. Use h=(4/N) hh=(4/N)\,\mathrm{h}. Count one vector-rate evaluation per Euler step and two per Heun step; compare every run at t=4 ht=4\,\mathrm{h}.

4. Recommend a numerical report

Compare accuracy, work, asymptotic stability, positivity, and conservation as distinct properties. Use z=ahz=ah to state the step-size conditions; recommend a method and step size for this case. Explain why convergence cannot prove that a real tank is well mixed or has constant instantaneous fractional removal rate.

Evidence to produce. Reproducible report with source, output table, step-size recommendation, and model limits.

Guidance. Conservation can hold even when CC is inaccurate or negative. At z=2z=2, Euler does not decay in magnitude; Heun also does not decay.

Self-assessment criteria

Judge each criterion as not yet evidenced, developing, or supported by your work.

  • Setup and units. I stated both state variables, initial conditions, units, rate assumption, and conservation law.
  • Derivation. I derived the exact solution and simultaneous Euler and Heun updates from the differential equations.
  • Computation and validation. I compared all three step counts at 4 h4\,\mathrm{h} with exact remaining and removed amounts, maximum invariant defects, and vector-rate counts.
  • Interpretation. I distinguished accuracy, stability, positivity, conservation, and real-world model validity.
  • Communication. My source, inputs, output table, units, recommendation, and limits make the run reproducible.

Limitations to address

  • Instant mixing and a constant instantaneous fractional removal rate may fail in a real tank.
  • Numerical refinement assesses the discretization of this model, not the model assumptions.
  • Conservation alone does not certify positive or accurate component values.
