Mass of a nonuniform elliptical plate

Transform an elliptical density integral, cross-check its mass, and examine a smaller similar plate.

A flat elliptical plate is assigned a radially increasing areal density. Compute its ideal mass using a change of variables, verify the result by a separate Cartesian calculation and numerical midpoint sums, then assess a half-size similar interior plate.

Working assumptions

  • The plate is flat and the prescribed effective areal density applies exactly across its ideal elliptical domain.
  • No manufacturing variation, cut loss, or thickness change is included.

Stages

1. Map and cover the ellipse

Guidance. After dividing coordinates by their semiaxes, the domain is the unit disk. The Jacobian combines a 3 m3\,\mathrm m by 2 m2\,\mathrm m scaling with the polar factor rr.

Self-assessment criteria

Judge each criterion as not yet evidenced, developing, or supported by your work.

  • Density and units. I stated the ideal plate and physical areal-density law and kept dimensions of kg/m2\mathrm{kg}/\mathrm m^2, m2\mathrm m^2, and kg\mathrm{kg} consistent.
  • Map and coverage. I derived the disk bounds and ∣J∣=(6 m2)r|J|=(6\,\mathrm m^2)r Jacobian, explained the half-open seam convention, and identified the origin as the only measure-zero duplication.
  • Exact mass. I transformed both density and area, evaluated the full integral exactly as 15π kg15\pi\,\mathrm{kg}, and showed the radial algebra.
  • Independent validation. I supplied a Cartesian or moment derivation, reproduced both radial midpoint estimates and errors, and checked zero-radius and full-plate limits.
  • Scaled interpretation. I calculated the half-size mass from M(q)M(q) and explained why ideal density and ellipse assumptions do not quantify manufacturing variation.

Limitations to address

  • The boundary is an ideal ellipse, so cut variation can change its area and mass.
  • The prescribed areal density is assumed exact and may not describe a manufactured plate.
  • No material or thickness variation is included in the model.
