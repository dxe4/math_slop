Cooling-bath Taylor certificate

Choose a Taylor degree that certifies a cooling-law approximation across a full time interval.

A calibration bath cools toward a controlled ambient temperature. A technician needs a polynomial approximation to the disclosed cooling law for the first 2 min2\,\mathrm{min}, with a mathematical temperature error no greater than 0.02 ∘C0.02\,^{\circ}\mathrm C anywhere in that interval. Choose and justify a degree before recommending its use.

Working assumptions

  • Ambient temperature and cooling time constant remain fixed over the interval.
  • The ideal first-order cooling law describes the synthetic calibration case; sensor uncertainty is not supplied.

Stages

1. Derive the cooling model

Separate the cooling differential equation, apply the initial condition, define dimensionless time uu, and state its full domain. Show temperature and time units in a parameter table and state the physical assumptions.

Evidence to produce. Parameter table, separated solution, dimensionless variable uu and interval 0≤t≤2 min0\leq t\leq2\,\mathrm{min}, and explicit assumptions.

Guidance. Write the excess temperature T−20 ∘CT-20\,^{\circ}\mathrm C first. Its initial value is 80 ∘C80\,^{\circ}\mathrm C; dividing time by 4 min4\,\mathrm{min} makes the exponential argument unitless.

2. Certify a Taylor degree

Build the degree-NN Maclaurin polynomial for the bath temperature. Bound its Lagrange remainder uniformly for 0≤t≤2 min0\leq t\leq2\,\mathrm{min}, compare degrees 44 and 55 with the 0.02 ∘C0.02\,^{\circ}\mathrm C target, and explain precisely what a failed bound means.

Evidence to produce. Polynomial TNT_N, derivative maximum with units, uniform inequality, and degree decision.

Guidance. For a derivative of order N+1N+1 the exponential factor never exceeds one on this nonnegative interval. Use the largest uu, namely 1/21/2, to cover every time.

3. Reproduce comparison values

Evaluate the disclosed exponential law and degrees 44 and 55 at t=0,1,2 mint=0,1,2\,\mathrm{min}. Show a formula, spreadsheet, or short code; tabulate actual model differences and certified bounds with units. Check the initial-time boundary exactly.

Evidence to produce. Reproducible evaluation method and three-time comparison table including t=0t=0 and both bounds.

Guidance. Compute each polynomial from u=t/(4 min)u=t/(4\,\mathrm{min}). The observed error at a sample time is distinct from the guaranteed worst-case bound.

4. Recommend and qualify

Recommend the least degree certified by your comparison for the stated 0.02 ∘C0.02\,^{\circ}\mathrm C tolerance. Explain why the certificate cannot establish actual bath or sensor accuracy, and identify any floating-point effect your calculation omits.

Evidence to produce. Short recommendation with degree, domain 0≤t≤2 min0\leq t\leq2\,\mathrm{min}, tolerance 0.02 ∘C0.02\,^{\circ}\mathrm C, and separate approximation, model, measurement, and evaluation limitations.

Guidance. A mathematical truncation bound assumes the exponential cooling model and its parameters are exact. A small bound does not calibrate the sensor.

Final deliverables

  • Dimensioned cooling model and assumptions
  • Polynomial and uniform remainder derivation
  • Reproducible three-time comparison table
  • Qualified degree recommendation

Self-assessment criteria

Judge each criterion as not yet evidenced, developing, or supported by your work.

  • Dimensioned model. I derived the exponential law from the differential equation, kept u=t/(4 min)u=t/(4\,\mathrm{min}) dimensionless, and stated the full 0≤t≤2 min0\leq t\leq2\,\mathrm{min} interval and assumptions.
  • Taylor construction. I constructed the degree-NN temperature polynomial with the ambient offset, initial excess, correct alternating coefficients, and temperature units.
  • Uniform certificate. I justified the derivative maximum over 0≤t≤2 min0\leq t\leq2\,\mathrm{min} and compared the degree-44 and degree-55 bounds with the tolerance without treating a failed bound as failure of the approximation.
  • Reproducible checks. I supplied a repeatable formula or code and a t=0,1,2 mint=0,1,2\,\mathrm{min} table with model differences, certified bounds, units, and the exact initial-time check.
  • Interpretation. My recommendation distinguishes mathematical truncation from cooling-model, sensor, and floating-point errors and limits the claim to the stated interval.

Limitations to address

  • Constant ambient temperature and time constant may not describe a real bath.
  • The ideal exponential law and sensor readings may differ even when its polynomial is certified.
  • The stated remainder bound does not include floating-point evaluation error.
