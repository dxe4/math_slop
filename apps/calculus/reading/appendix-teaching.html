Appendix H · Learning, teaching, and assessment guide

The book supports self-study and a three-course sequence. These routines help readers and instructors turn its large collection of explanations, investigations, exercises, and laboratories into evidence of understanding.

A repeatable learner routine

For each lesson, first recall its prerequisite idea without notes. Read until you can state the new definition in your own words, then reproduce one calculation on paper. Predict the visual investigation before moving a control. Solve linked practice with hints closed, record the reason for any error, and return a day later to one problem without looking at the solution.

A chapter is ready to leave when you can state the main result with its hypotheses, solve a representative fluency problem, explain one boundary case, interpret units or geometry, and distinguish numerical evidence from proof. Marking a chapter as read records navigation progress only; the five concept checks and a fresh written problem provide better evidence of mastery.

Course use and assessment

The three fourteen-week routes identify a coherent sequence but leave room for local calendars. A typical meeting can begin with a prediction or misconception, develop the definition and one proof idea, compare two worked solutions, and end with a written problem whose setup matters more than arithmetic. Laboratories work best after the analytic structure is understood and before a final synthesis.

Balanced assessment samples fluency, interpretation, proof, modeling, and computation. Require theorem conditions and units in the grading criteria. For a computational submission, require source code, parameters, a benchmark, a refinement comparison, and a conclusion no stronger than the evidence. Oral explanation or a short error analysis can reveal understanding that a final numeric answer cannot.

Inclusive and accessible study

Every interactive chart has a text description of its current mathematical state, keyboard-operable controls, and a static counterpart in the complete book. The reader offers text-size, line-width, and theme controls. Exercise hints and solutions use native disclosure controls, while typeset expressions include MathML through the bundled renderer. Readers can use the complete-book HTML when a continuous document works better than paginated navigation.

An instructor should provide equivalent time and formats when a motor, visual, reading, or computational barrier changes the work required. Describe relevant visual structure aloud, permit symbolic or verbal explanations where they measure the same objective, and avoid making color the only carrier of meaning. Accessibility is an ongoing practice: invite precise reports about focus order, math speech, contrast, wording, and alternative representations.

New checks and prior reading records

Every chapter retains its original five concept checks; selected chapters now have additional unseen checks. A saved chapter visit records navigation, not mastery of lessons added later. Revisit the new written reasoning and compare your own solution before recording an outcome.
