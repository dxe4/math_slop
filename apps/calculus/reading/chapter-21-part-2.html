    Answer. ff and both partials at the point. The local linearization uses the value and both first partial derivatives.

  5. For a differentiable function, if ∇f⋅u=0\nabla f\cdot\mathbf u=0 for a unit u\mathbf u, what does that mean locally?

    • No first-order change in direction u\mathbf u
    • ff is globally constant
    • u\mathbf u is impossible

    Answer. No first-order change in direction u\mathbf u. The directional derivative vanishes along that direction at the point.

  6. When does the regular-level theorem supply ∇F(P)\nabla F(P) as a surface normal?

    • FF is C1C^1 near PP and ∇F(P)≠0\nabla F(P)\ne0
    • Whenever PP lies on the level
    • Whenever one partial exists

    Answer. FF is C1C^1 near PP and ∇F(P)≠0\nabla F(P)\ne0. The implicit function theorem then supplies a local graph and tangent plane.

    Why the other choices fail.

    • Whenever PP lies on the level — A zero-gradient point requires separate inspection.
    • Whenever one partial exists — One partial does not supply a full gradient or the needed regularity.

  7. For H=(z−x2−y2)3H=(z-x^2-y^2)^3 and K=z2−x2−y2K=z^2-x^2-y^2 at the origin, which tangent-plane conclusion is supported?

    • H=0H=0 has tangent plane z=0z=0; K=0K=0 has none. Their zero gradients alone settle neither result
    • Neither has a tangent plane because both gradients vanish.
    • Both have tangent plane z=0z=0 because both gradients vanish.

    Answer. H=0H=0 has tangent plane z=0z=0; K=0K=0 has none. Their zero gradients alone settle neither result. H=0H=0 is a paraboloid graph with quadratic height residual; K=0K=0 has three independent generator directions. Geometry decides each case.

    Why the other choices fail.

    • Neither has a tangent plane because both gradients vanish. — A sufficient regular-level-set hypothesis is being mistaken for a necessary geometric condition.
    • Both have tangent plane z=0z=0 because both gradients vanish. — A zero vector supplies no normal, and the cone generators cannot all lie in one plane.

Chapter synthesis

  • Differentiability provides one linear approximation whose error is negligible relative to the full input displacement.
  • The total differential predicts small changes, while the tangent plane represents that prediction geometrically.
  • Jacobians organize linear input-output relations; the chain rule composes them by matrix multiplication.
  • For differentiable functions in Euclidean coordinates, the gradient determines all directional rates and is normal to regular level sets.

Common mistakes to check

  • “Existing partial derivatives guarantee a tangent plane.” They identify its candidate coefficients, but differentiability requires a remainder estimate valid across all approaches; q(x,y)=x3x2+y2q(x,y)=\frac{x^3}{x^2+y^2} for (x,y)≠(0,0)(x,y)\ne(0,0), with q(0,0)=0q(0,0)=0, has both partials at the origin but is not differentiable there. Test with problem 24
  • “The gradient points along a level curve.” At a regular point it is perpendicular to the curve, since the function has zero rate along the curve. Test with problem 16
  • “A zero directional derivative means no change.” It means no first-order change at that point; f(x,y)=x2+y2f(x,y)=x^2+y^2 rises in every nonzero direction from the origin despite all its directional derivatives being zero there. Test with problem 18
