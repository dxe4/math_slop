Concept checks

  1. When x′(t)≠0x^{\prime}(t)\ne0, dy/dxdy/dx equals…

    • x′/y′x^{\prime}/y^{\prime}
    • y′/x′y^{\prime}/x^{\prime}
    • x′y′x^{\prime}y^{\prime}

    Answer. y′/x′y^{\prime}/x^{\prime}. The chain rule gives dy/dt=(dy/dx)(dx/dt)dy/dt=(dy/dx)(dx/dt).

  2. What is parametric speed?

    • x′+y′x^{\prime}+y^{\prime}
    • (x′)2+(y′)2\sqrt{(x^{\prime})^2+(y^{\prime})^2}
    • dy/dxdy/dx

    Answer. (x′)2+(y′)2\sqrt{(x^{\prime})^2+(y^{\prime})^2}. Speed is the magnitude of the velocity vector.

  3. Can the same geometric curve be traced twice by a parameter?

    • Yes
    • No
    • Only a straight line

    Answer. Yes. The image can repeat while the parameter interval records additional traversals.

  4. What is the area of a radius-RR polar disk?

    • πR2\pi R^2
    • 2πR2\pi R

    Answer. πR2\pi R^2. Integrating (1/2)R2(1/2)R^2 over 0≤θ≤2π0\le\theta\le2\pi gives πR2\pi R^2.

  5. What does a negative polar radius do?

    • Makes a point impossible
    • Points in the opposite angular direction
    • Squares the angle

    Answer. Points in the opposite angular direction. (−r,θ)(-r,\theta) represents the same point as (r,θ+π)(r,\theta+\pi) for positive rr.

  6. For e>1e>1, what does positive R=ed/(1+ecos⁡θ)R=ed/(1+e\cos\theta), used only where its denominator is positive, cover?

    • Both hyperbola branches

    Answer. The focus-side branch only. The second branch needs a second positive-radius formula or a signed-radius interpretation.

    Why the other choices fail.

    • Both hyperbola branches — Discarding the denominator restriction does not add valid positive-radius points.
    • The distant branch only — The displayed positive-radius derivation uses the focus side of the directrix.

Chapter synthesis

  • A parametrization includes its interval, direction, and tracing multiplicity.
  • Parametric slopes require division by x′, and second derivatives require that conversion again.
  • Integrating speed counts traveled length, including every repeated traversal.
  • Polar area and length formulas require geometric checks on bounds, intersections, and negative-radius representations.

Common mistakes to check

  • “The second parametric derivative is y′′(t)x′′(t)\frac{y^{\prime\prime}(t)}{x^{\prime\prime}(t)}.” Where the parametrization is twice continuously differentiable and x′(t)≠0x^{\prime}(t)\ne0, it is d2ydx2=1x′(t)ddt(y′(t)x′(t))\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}=\frac{1}{x^{\prime}(t)}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{y^{\prime}(t)}{x^{\prime}(t)}\right), because the derivative must be taken with respect to xx.   • “Every polar intersection solves r1(θ)=r2(θ)r_1(\theta)=r_2(\theta).” Negative-radius representations and the origin can require different parameter angles. Test with problem 22
