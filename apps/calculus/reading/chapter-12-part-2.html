  1. Problem 1. Fluency Find ∫x4+x2 dx\int \frac{x}{4+x^2}\,dx and verify it.

    Hint. Use the denominator as uu and compare dudu with the numerator.

    Solution. Let u=4+x2u=4+x^2, so du=2x dxdu=2x\,dx. Then ∫x/(4+x2) dx=12ln⁡(4+x2)+C\int x/(4+x^2)\,dx=\frac12\ln(4+x^2)+C. Differentiating gives 12(2x)/(4+x2)\frac12(2x)/(4+x^2); the logarithm's argument is positive for all real xx.

    Hint. Set u=1+x2u=1+x^2 and change both bounds.

  13. Problem 13. Fluency Evaluate ∫0πsin⁡2(x) dx\int_0^\pi \sin^2(x)\,dx.

    Hint. Use a half-angle identity rather than trying u=sin⁡xu=\sin x.

  14. Problem 14. Fluency Find ∫cos⁡3x dx\int\cos^3x\,dx.

  15. Problem 15. Fluency Find ∫sin⁡2x dx\int\sin^2x\,dx.

    Hint. Use the half-angle identity.

    Solution. The half-angle identity sin⁡2x=(1−cos⁡2x)/2\sin^2x=(1-\cos2x)/2 separates the integral into a constant term and a cosine term. Integrating each gives ∫sin⁡2x dx=x/2−sin⁡(2x)/4+C\int\sin^2x\,dx=x/2-\sin(2x)/4+C.

  16. Problem 16. Fluency Evaluate ∫0πcos⁡2x dx\int_0^\pi\cos^2x\,dx.

    Hint. Use half-angle or symmetry.

    Solution. Using cos⁡2x=(1+cos⁡2x)/2\cos^2x=(1+\cos2x)/2, the sine boundary term vanishes and ∫0πcos⁡2x dx=[x/2+sin⁡(2x)/4]0π=π/2\int_0^\pi\cos^2x\,dx=[x/2+\sin(2x)/4]_0^\pi=\pi/2.

Concept checks

  1. Which rule does substitution reverse?

    • Product rule
    • Chain rule
    • Quotient rule only

    Answer. Chain rule. The derivative of F(g(x))F(g(x)) is F′(g(x))g′(x)F^{\prime}(g(x))g^{\prime}(x).

  2. Which rule does integration by parts reverse?

    • Product rule
    • Chain rule
    • Power rule only

    Answer. Product rule. Rearranging (uv)′=u′v+uv′(uv)^{\prime}=u^{\prime}v+uv^{\prime} gives the integration-by-parts identity.

  3. What is ∫1/x dx\int 1/x\,dx on x<0x<0?

    • ln⁡x+C\ln x+C
    • ln⁡∣x∣+C\ln|x|+C
    • 1/x2+C1/x^2+C

    Answer. ln⁡∣x∣+C\ln|x|+C. ln⁡∣x∣\ln|x| differentiates to 1/x1/x on either connected side of zero.

  4. When substituting in a definite integral, what must also change?

    • Only the integrand
    • The bounds
    • The final answer sign always

    Answer. The bounds. The old xx-bounds must be converted to the new variable’s values.

  5. Does every continuous function have an elementary antiderivative?

    • Yes
    • No
    • Only on bounded intervals

    Answer. No. Some continuous functions, such as e−x2e^{-x^2}, have antiderivatives outside elementary closed forms.

  6. With x=sinh⁡tx=\sinh t for real tt, what is 1+x2\sqrt{1+x^2}?

    • cosh⁡t\cosh t
    • sinh⁡t\sinh t
    • ∣sinh⁡t∣|\sinh t|

    Answer. cosh⁡t\cosh t. The identity gives cosh⁡2t\cosh^2t, and cosh⁡t>0\cosh t>0.

    Why the other choices fail.

    • sinh⁡t\sinh t — Sinh can be negative and is not this square root.
    • ∣sinh⁡t∣|\sinh t| — ∣sinh⁡t∣|\sinh t| can be zero while the root is at least one.

Chapter synthesis

  • Choose methods from derivative structure: composition suggests substitution, products suggest parts, and rational structure suggests decomposition.
  • Trigonometric identities and completed squares expose familiar forms, while substitution branches control signs and domains.
  • Check every candidate by differentiation, keep bounds consistent, and treat numerical agreement as evidence rather than proof.
  • An accumulation function exists for a continuous integrand even when no elementary antiderivative exists.

Common mistakes to check

  • A substitution changes only the formula, not the bounds. In a definite integral, the bounds must be values of the current variable; keeping bounds in xx after switching the integral to uu generally changes the quantity being evaluated. Test with problem 2
  • A tiny sampled derivative residual proves an antiderivative is correct. Finite differences introduce approximation and rounding errors, and finite samples cannot establish an identity over an interval. Test with problem 33
  • Every continuous elementary function has an elementary antiderivative. The FTC guarantees an accumulation-function antiderivative, which need not have an elementary expression. Test with problem 24

Choose one mistake that tempted you. Give a counterexample or identify the missing condition, then solve the exact problem linked beside that mistake again without a hint.
