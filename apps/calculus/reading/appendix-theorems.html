Appendix C · Theorem conditions and proof patterns

Most mistakes in calculus are applications of a true theorem where one hypothesis is missing. This reference pairs each conclusion with the conditions that make it legitimate.

One-variable theorem checklist

An epsilon–delta limit proof chooses δ\delta after ε\varepsilon but before the arbitrary nearby input. To disprove a limit, two sequences or paths with different limiting outputs suffice; to prove it, control every eligible nearby input. A graph cannot check infinitely many points.

The mean value theorem gives a point where instantaneous and average slopes agree, but only when the function is continuous on the closed interval and differentiable throughout its open interior. The intermediate value theorem needs continuity on a closed interval but does not need differentiability. The extreme value theorem guarantees attained extrema for a continuous function on a compact interval. If an interval is open or unbounded, attainment can fail.

Series, approximation, and multivariable conditions

A convergent series requires terms tending to zero, but the converse fails. The alternating-series remainder bound requires decreasing nonnegative magnitudes tending to zero. Ratio and root tests are inconclusive at limiting value 11. A Taylor remainder bound needs a bound on the next derivative over the entire interval from center to evaluation point, not just at its endpoints.

For a multivariable function, existing partial derivatives at one point do not guarantee differentiability. The error after subtracting one linear map must be small compared with displacement length in every direction. Continuous partial derivatives near a point are a standard sufficient condition. To disprove a two-variable limit, two paths with different limiting values suffice; to prove one, use a uniform bound independent of path. Coordinate changes require both transformed domains and the absolute Jacobian determinant.

Proof moves that recur

For a limit, start from the required output error and factor it. Restrict the input to a convenient neighborhood to bound moving factors, then choose δ\delta as the minimum of the neighborhood radius and the tolerance-derived value. For an integral, trap arbitrary tagged sums between lower and upper sums whose gap tends to zero. For a series, compare tails rather than only individual terms.

For an optimization claim, state the feasible domain, list interior critical and nondifferentiable candidates, inspect boundaries, and compare values. For a vector theorem, name the region, boundary, orientation, and regularity before integrating. For a numerical claim, report the algorithm, step sizes, exact special case if available, and an error or invariance check. Each proof should identify what has been established and what remains an assumption.

Regular implicit surfaces

If F(P)=0F(P)=0, FF is continuously differentiable near PP, and ∇F(P)≠0\nabla F(P)\ne0, the level set has local surface structure and tangent plane ∇F(P)⋅(x−P)=0\nabla F(P)\cdot(\mathbf x-P)=0. The chain rule first proves only that velocities of curves on the level set are orthogonal to the gradient. A nonzero partial derivative and the implicit function theorem supply a local graph and two independent tangent directions, completing the plane argument.

If the gradient vanishes, this sufficient theorem gives no conclusion about the actual set: a cone apex has no unique tangent plane, while F=z3=0F=z^3=0 still describes the plane z=0z=0. Inspect the set separately.

Power series as ODE solutions

A power series with positive radius RR and its derivative series converge uniformly on compact subintervals of ∣t−a∣<R|t-a|<R; repeated differentiation is valid there and retains the same open radius. Test finite endpoints separately.

A recurrence obtained by matching ODE coefficients is initially a formal candidate: prove a positive convergence radius and convergence of every derivative series required by the equation, then substitute and check initial data. A truncated polynomial’s residual and its error from the exact solution are different quantities.
