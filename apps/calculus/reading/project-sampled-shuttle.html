Shuttle velocity trace

Estimate a velocity integral from samples, determine when it represents distance, and separate quadrature error from missing motion data.

A shuttle calibration run records velocity at fixed times. Estimate the integral from samples. A generating law is disclosed only for this synthetic calibration case so that you can benchmark the methods and interpret its distance.

Working assumptions

  • The supplied times and velocities are exact for the synthetic benchmark.
  • The disclosed law applies only to this calibration case.

Stages

1. Read the sensor record

Make a time–velocity table, specify units and interval, and distinguish signed displacement from total distance. Explain which sign claim follows from the disclosed generating law and why the samples alone identify neither integral.

Evidence to produce. Data table, assumptions, and a conditional distance and displacement statement.

Guidance. Positive sampled values do not rule out reverse motion between samples. Check that the disclosed quadratic is positive on the entire interval.

2. Estimate from coarse samples

Use the 2 s2\,\mathrm{s} grid to calculate composite trapezoidal and Simpson estimates of the velocity integral. Show the weighted sums and a reproducible spreadsheet formula or short program.

Evidence to produce. Both weighted sums, units, formula or code, and conditional integral estimates.

Guidance. There are four coarse subintervals, so composite Simpson applies. Write its 1,4,2,4,11,4,2,4,1 weights explicitly.

3. Refine and benchmark

Insert the additional 1 s1\,\mathrm{s} samples, repeat both rules, then integrate the disclosed quadratic exactly. Report a two-grid table and signed errors against that calibration benchmark at the common endpoint.

Evidence to produce. Refinement table with hh, method, estimate, exact benchmark, and signed error.

Guidance. For the 1 s1\,\mathrm{s} grid there are eight subintervals. Simpson exactness here is a polynomial property of the disclosed law.

4. Report the evidence

If only coarse samples existed, state a conditional estimate of the velocity integral and the interpolation or smoothness assumption it needs. Explain when it estimates distance. Discuss unsampled motion, sensor error, and timestamp error separately; do not claim a finite error bound from the samples alone.

Evidence to produce. Technical note with conditional estimate, stated assumption, uncertainty sources, and law-dependent claims.

Guidance. The disclosed law makes the benchmark a distance calculation because its velocity is positive. Coarse samples alone do not identify actual distance or displacement.

Final deliverables

  • Data and assumption table
  • Reproducible coarse and fine quadrature
  • Exact benchmark and error table
  • Technical note about uncertainty

Self-assessment criteria

Judge each criterion as not yet evidenced, developing, or supported by your work.

  • Setup and units. I distinguished velocity samples, displacement, and distance with correct time and length units.
  • Derivation. I showed correct composite trapezoidal and Simpson weights and integrated the quadratic benchmark.
  • Computation and validation. I reproduced both grid estimates, compared signed errors at 8 s8\,\mathrm{s} against the disclosed law, and explained why Simpson matches the quadratic.
  • Interpretation. I stated an interpolation or smoothness assumption for a coarse-only estimate and separated benchmark quadrature error from unsampled motion, sensor error, and timing error without claiming an actual distance or finite bound from samples alone.
  • Communication. My data table, formulas or code, results, units, and law-dependent claims can be audited.

Limitations to address

  • Unknown between-sample motion can alter actual distance arbitrarily without further assumptions.
  • The benchmark ignores sensor and clock error.
  • Polynomial exactness does not transfer to arbitrary velocity traces.
