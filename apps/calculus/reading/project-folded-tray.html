Folded-sheet specimen tray

Choose a manufacturable corner cut that maximizes the capacity of an open tray.

A workshop cuts equal square corners from a 30 cm30\,\mathrm{cm} by 20 cm20\,\mathrm{cm} sheet and folds the sides to make an open specimen tray. The cutting tool permits a corner cut from 2 cm2\,\mathrm{cm} through 4 cm4\,\mathrm{cm}. Recommend the cut that gives the greatest capacity within that permitted range.

Prepare with these lessons

  • Numerical error
  • Optimization objective and feasible set
  • Global maximum certificate
  • Boundary candidates
  • Reproducible reporting

Working assumptions

  • Cuts are equal squares; folds are perpendicular and lossless.
  • Ignore sheet thickness, seams, and variation in cut size.

Stages

1. Model the geometry

Draw the flat net and the folded tray. Label all three interior dimensions as functions of xx, a length measured in centimetres. Explain the manufacturing interval and derive a capacity model with units.

Evidence to produce. A labeled diagram, assumptions, feasible set, and derivation of V(x)V(x).

Guidance. The height is xx. Both sheet dimensions lose two corner widths. A critical point outside 2 cm≤x≤4 cm2\,\mathrm{cm}\leq x\leq4\,\mathrm{cm} cannot be selected.

2. Certify the choice

Differentiate the capacity, solve the stationary-point equation, and compare all feasible stationary points with both interval endpoints. Explain why your comparison proves a global maximum.

Evidence to produce. Derivative, candidate table, and closed-interval maximum argument.

Guidance. Use V′(x)=600 cm2−(200 cm)x+12x2V'(x)=600\,\mathrm{cm}^2-(200\,\mathrm{cm})x+12x^2. Include both endpoints.

3. Check the calculation

Reproduce endpoint capacities and the central-difference derivative at the unrounded stationary cut using h=0.05 cmh=0.05\,\mathrm{cm} and h=0.01 cmh=0.01\,\mathrm{cm}. Show formulas or short code, inputs, and outputs. Explain what this numerical check can and cannot establish.

Evidence to produce. Reproducible computation, values with units, and a distinction between a numerical check and proof.

Guidance. For a cubic, symmetric differencing leaves a term proportional to h2h^2. Check that both offsets around the unrounded cut stay in the permitted interval 2 cm≤x≤4 cm2\,\mathrm{cm}\leq x\leq4\,\mathrm{cm}.

4. Recommend a manufactured cut

Choose and state a cutting increment, round the optimal cut to a feasible cut at that increment, recompute its predicted capacity, and write a one-page decision memo. Discuss tool tolerance and material thickness without claiming a certified manufacturing advantage unless a tolerance is supplied.

Evidence to produce. Rounded recommendation, recomputed capacity, and explicit model limitations.

Guidance. The mathematical optimum exceeds the 4 cm4\,\mathrm{cm} endpoint by only about 0.305895461 cm30.305895461\,\mathrm{cm}^3. Recalculate capacity after rounding; a tolerance claim needs measured tool variation.

Final deliverables

  • Labeled net and assumptions
  • Candidate and finite-difference tables
  • Reproducible calculation

Self-assessment criteria

Judge each criterion as not yet evidenced, developing, or supported by your work.

  • Setup and units. I justified all three dimensions, the interval 2 cm≤x≤4 cm2\,\mathrm{cm}\leq x\leq4\,\mathrm{cm}, and capacity units.
  • Derivation. I derived V′(x)V'(x) and compared feasible critical points with both endpoints to certify the global choice.
  • Computation and validation. I showed reproducible capacities and two finite differences and stated that they check arithmetic, not global optimality.
  • Interpretation. My rounded recommendation has a recomputed capacity and identifies tolerance and thickness effects.
  • Communication. My diagram, table, memo, assumptions, and units let another reader repeat the decision.

Limitations to address

  • The idealized volume ignores wall thickness and seam losses.
  • Tool tolerance can dominate the small advantage over a 4 cm4\,\mathrm{cm} cut; no tolerance is supplied.
  • Finite differences check the derivative calculation at sampled cuts; they do not prove a global maximum.
