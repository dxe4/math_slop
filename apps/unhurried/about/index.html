The idea ↗
Mathematics belongs in your spare time, too

Serious ideas.
A lighter invitation.

You don’t need to be becoming a mathematician to enjoy mathematics.

For the pleasure of understanding.

This project is for curious adults, especially people who met mathematics in another field and would like to find their way back. Perhaps you enjoy an elegant argument. Perhaps you want to see what a formula is actually doing. There is room for both.

Each exploration has one clear destination, a local refresher, a worked example, the mathematics behind it, and a good place to stop. The article series connect ideas when you want more direction. Start here if you would like a short orientation.

Pictures that lead somewhere.

Our experiments invite you to predict and test. Beside the picture, you will find a formula, a table and an explanation. We distinguish numerical evidence, derivations, proof sketches and proofs, and say where an argument stops applying.

You can use the controls with a keyboard or by entering numbers. Static worked examples remain available without JavaScript. No streaks, compulsory accounts or timed quizzes stand between you and an idea.

What you can read.

There are currently 254 readable explorations, 26 subject series, two short taster series, and 52 dedicated catalog laboratories. Eight more experiments are embedded in explorations. The roadmap compares these counts with the researched catalog.

Reading times are estimates, and the lessons have not been studied with learners or screen-reader users.

Influences and original work.

The plan draws on research and open academic references, with design observations from 3Blue1Brown, Mathigon, Seeing Theory and other resources. The lessons, exercises and diagrams here are original. Each article links the mathematical references behind it.

Mathematics deserves careful checking.

We keep claims, assumptions and worked answers explicit so mistakes can be found and corrected. A question is enough; you do not need to supply a correction. Prepare a reader report with the page, section and what you noticed. Copy or download it to keep a record or share with the person helping you study. The report builder does not send reports.

If a calculation or explanation seems wrong, include the relevant line and the assumptions you used.

Your notebook stays yours.

Reading requires no account. Saved articles and notes live in your browser; you can export, import and clear them. The site makes no analytics, advertising, font-service or content-API requests. External reference links open the relevant source when you follow them.
