Abstract algebra

Symmetry, groups, rings and the role of structure-preserving maps. Verify groups through a square and a modular clock, then find inverses and first-return counts. A selected refresher in cosets, homomorphisms, polynomial rings, ideals and modular fields. A sustained proof route through Lagrange, quotients, integer ideals and modular fields, with Sylow stated and applied. Begin with functions; modular arithmetic; reading a short proof. This route uses a square and a modular clock to make one idea concrete: an operation can combine two objects, and the resulting system may have identity, inverses, associativity and closure. You will verify these laws in two finite settings, find a few return times, and build the square's full operation table, including a pair whose order matters. This selected core route is for someone who remembers arithmetic and basic proof moves but wants to reconnect group and ring language. It starts with symmetries, uses cosets and homomorphisms to understand group structure, and ends with ideals and the difference between a ring and a field. This route asks you to produce proofs, not just calculate examples. Its named targets are finite cyclic subgroups, Lagrange's theorem, the normal-subgroup criterion for quotient multiplication, classification of ideals of Z, and the prime-modulus field criterion.

About 150 minutes of reading and article exercises.
