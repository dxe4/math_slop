Optimization

Find good choices and learn when local reasoning can certify a global answer. Model a choice, compare candidate optima and use convex geometry to solve a small resource-allocation capstone. Refresh smooth and constrained optimization through calculations, dual certificates and a convex capstone. Prove selected convex optimality and algorithm claims, audit hypotheses and construct a certificate with a nonconvex contrast. Begin with Algebra; Graphs; Derivatives for bridge entries. This route begins with a sheet of card and ends with a small resource-allocation model. The thread is simple: name the quantity to optimize, state every feasible choice, find candidates, then say what proves that one candidate is best. This route is for someone who has already met derivatives, gradients and linear inequalities but wants their hypotheses back in focus. Work a diagnostic first: for f(x)=x³, explain why f′(0)=0 does not give a minimum; for f(x)=x² on x≥1, state the best feasible x; and for a proposed inequality multiplier under the convention h(x)≤0, state its required sign. This selected proof route studies convexity, quadratic descent, equality multipliers, weak duality, KKT sufficiency, subgradients and Newton's quadratic model. Its destination is an explicit certificate for a convex toy problem followed by an analysis of a stationary point where the same conclusion would be invalid.
