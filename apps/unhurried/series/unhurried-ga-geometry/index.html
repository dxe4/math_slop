Euclidean and non-Euclidean geometry

Constructions, invariants and models where the parallel postulate changes. Construct and justify a Euclidean angle bisector and circle-angle configuration, with the parallel-line triangle proof as support. Compare selected Euclidean theorems with spherical great circles and hyperbolic disk geodesics while tracking proof assumptions. Prove selected classical claims and compare exact lattice area with conditional hyperbolic angle-defect area. Begin with angle and triangle basics. Its destination is modest and concrete: make an exact angle bisector, explain why it works, and use parallel lines and circle arcs to reason about angles. Basic logarithms and integration help with the disk distance derivation; if they are new, use the exact distance formula supplied in that lesson and treat its integral as an optional extension. In the Euclidean part you will prove claims about vectors, triangle angles and circle angles, then use Pick's lattice formula with its exact hypotheses.

About 150 minutes of reading and article exercises.
