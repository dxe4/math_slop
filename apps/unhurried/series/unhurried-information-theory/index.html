Information theory

Bits, surprise, compression and noisy clues as mathematical measures of uncertainty. A four-lesson route through surprise, entropy, joint uncertainty and a readable prefix code. A seven-lesson route connecting noisy clues, binary coding and one-use channel calculations. A nine-lesson proof-based route through finite entropy, coding bounds, divergence and sample-estimation bias. Begin with probability, expected value and logarithms. This route takes a specified four-symbol source and asks two practical mathematical questions: how uncertain is its next symbol, and can its symbols be written as a binary stream that a reader can decode without separators? Work through four linked lessons and the short problems below. This route links two uses of probability. A noisy clue changes uncertainty about a hidden bit, while a prefix tree assigns codeword lengths to symbols with known weights. This route studies discrete sources with explicitly specified probabilities. Its central claims are the entropy chain rule, the nonnegativity of KL divergence, the binary prefix-code lower bound, and Huffman's expected-length optimality in its finite setting.
