Complex analysis

Rotation and stretching lead to unexpectedly rigid functions and useful contour integrals. Turn and square complex points, test directional derivatives, and explain a logarithm branch through a mapped-point capstone. Refresh holomorphy, branches, contour calculations, residues and convergence disks with a fully bounded real-integral capstone. Prove a restricted disk form of Cauchy theory with Green's theorem, audit consequences, and construct a failed disk estimate. Begin with Plane coordinates; Algebra; Trigonometry. This route starts with a number acting as a turn, follows the square map across a plane, and ends by asking why some apparently natural operations cannot be differentiated or given a continuous angle everywhere. It is a selected first look. This route is for someone who has seen complex arithmetic, differentiation and integrals before and wants a compact, demanding refresher. This route develops a restricted but complete chain from coordinate differentiation to a disk version of Cauchy's formula, then uses that formula for a derivative bound. It assumes real-line limits, Green's theorem for a disk or annulus, parametrized line integrals, uniform convergence on compact sets, and comfort writing proofs.
