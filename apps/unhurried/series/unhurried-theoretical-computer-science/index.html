Theoretical computer science

Finite machines, algorithmic cost, and rigorous limits of computation. Specify a finite procedure, compare exact growth counts, and prove a two-state reminder machine correct. Use invariants and recurrences to prove search behavior and compare exact cost models. Prove a universal halting decider impossible and distinguish that theorem from the open P-versus-NP question. Begin with basic functions and finite sets. This route asks three modest but useful questions. What must an algorithm promise? This route is for readers who know induction and logarithms but want a compact return to rigorous algorithm analysis. It joins three styles of argument: an invariant that makes a search correct, a recurrence that prices recursive work, and a state invariant that proves a string recognizer. This route asks a precise impossibility question and a separate efficiency question. Could one algorithm correctly decide whether every other program will halt on a given input?
