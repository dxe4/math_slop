Carry one modelling method across sound and networks

This accelerated route spends about nine hours on seven selected lessons. Its outcome is to derive a beat envelope, calculate a one-step network-spread probability, and test a chosen model on observations kept out of fitting. These are deliberately small, fully specified cases. The route does not teach acoustics, graph diffusion or statistical validation in general; it teaches how assumptions move with the formula.

Entry recap. A frequency in hertz is cycles per second. A sine wave sin(2πft) has period 1/f seconds. A graph has vertices joined by edges. For independent attempts, multiplying failure probabilities gives the chance all attempts fail. A residual is observed minus predicted. The lessons repair each notation locally, so trigonometry and probability can be refreshed while reading.

Budget roughly 175 minutes for seven lessons, 130 minutes for their exercises, 100 minutes for the mixed practice and derivations here, and 135 minutes for the capstone with a revision after checking it: 540 minutes. A good interim stop is after the first module, when you can show the units and sensitivity of a toy model.

Read the catalog modules in order

Refresh core ideas

  1. A model begins with a question. Write an assumption card before calculating anything. Use a fictional wall if you need a concrete output and check both the area ratio and a grid count.
  2. Build a miniature gallery that scales. Derive an area factor from two lengths and a volume factor from three. This prevents a plausible but false scaling shortcut when a sound or network parameter changes.
  3. Which assumption matters most? Compare a finite parameter change with a local derivative. Label the input range and the quantity held fixed. In the later network task, p is a probability rather than a geometric scale factor, but the same discipline applies.

The first bridge is from geometry to mechanism. Geometry allows exact algebra once a scaling rule is specified. The sound and network lessons likewise give exact consequences of stipulated equations, while the link to actual hearing or actual sharing needs evidence. Carry forward the habit of naming a model's scope.

Work across examples

  1. Two tones make a slow pulse. Prove the sine-sum identity by using the addition formulas or verify it algebraically. Distinguish the signed cosine period from the envelope magnitude's beat-peak period.
  2. Rules, randomness and repeatability in art. Save a seed and specify the algorithm; explain why a selected image no longer acts like a blind draw from the ideal iid model.
  3. A rumor on a small fictional network. Calculate one-step probabilities conditioned on the present active set. Check that no vertex activates through two edges in a single synchronous step.
  4. A curve can fit and still mislead. Keep an observation out of model selection, calculate its residual, and say exactly what that check supports. A low residual is neither a mechanism nor a causal effect.

The second bridge is from an exact identity to a measured check. A trigonometric identity can be verified for every real time under the signal definition. A network product is exact only given independent attempts. A held-out count evaluates a prediction on a chosen sample. They have different proof boundaries even when all three yield precise numbers.

Mixed practice before the project

Two ideal equal-amplitude tones have frequencies 300 and 304 Hz. Calculate their carrier frequency, beat-peak rate and first envelope zero after t=0. Separately, let A connect to B and C, with C connected to D. A starts active, attempts on every eligible edge are independent with p=1/2, and new activations occur at the next step. Find the chance D is active by the end of step 2. Explain why D cannot be active at step 1.

Worked mixed-practice answer

The average carrier frequency is (300+304)/2=302 Hz. The ideal sum is 2sin(2π·302t)cos(π·4t). Magnitude peaks every 1/4 s, giving 4 beats per second. First zero is when 4πt=π/2, so t=1/8=0.125 s. For the graph, C activates at step 1 with probability 1/2. Only if it does can it attempt D at step 2, succeeding with probability 1/2. Independence across these attempts gives P(D active by step 2)=1/4. D is two edges from A, and synchronous updates prevent a same-step cascade. None of this predicts a real friendship network.

For a boundary check, set p=0 and then p=1. The two-step D probability becomes 0 and 1 respectively, matching p². If both edge attempts were controlled by a common switch, the product p² would need reconsideration.

Capstone: a sound-and-marker exhibit

Create two panels and a validation note. Panel one uses ideal equal-amplitude 300 and 304 Hz sine waves; derive its envelope and calculate the effect of changing the second tone to 306 Hz. Panel two uses the A–C–D path above with independent p=1/2 attempts; derive the probability D is active by step 2 and recalculate at p=1/4. For a toy held-out check, suppose 20 separately seeded runs at p=1/2 were reserved before any change to the rule, and D activated by step 2 in 6. Report the observed proportion, prediction error and what this one check can and cannot establish.

Model solution

For the original tones, the identity gives 2sin(2π·302t)cos(4πt); the beat-peak rate is 4 Hz and interval 0.25 s. Changing to 306 Hz gives 2sin(2π·303t)cos(6πt), beat rate 6 Hz and interval 1/6 s. This is exact for the ideal signals. For the two-edge path, D needs both A→C at step 1 and C→D at step 2, so its probability is p²: 0.25 at p=1/2 and 0.0625 at p=1/4. The held-out proportion is 6/20=0.30, so observed minus predicted is +0.05, or one extra activation in 20 relative to the expected count of 5. A finite sample fluctuates; this difference alone does not falsify the rule. The runs check an implementation under the stipulated graph and seed procedure, not real social behaviour or a causal effect.

OutcomeEvidence

Give one point for each row and one for a limitation note about ideal tones or fictional connections. Four points complete this bounded route. If the algebra works but the conclusion overreaches, revise the assumption and validation note; that correction is a core modelling skill. Stop with the two panels and one-page memo, or continue to the deeper route for approximation and error analysis.
