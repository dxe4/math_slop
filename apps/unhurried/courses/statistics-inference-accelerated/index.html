Enter the route

This route begins after a quick refresher on fractions, averages, and simple probability. You should be comfortable interpreting 60 successes among 100 independent trials as a sample proportion of 0.60 and reading a scatterplot as pairs of measured values. Review the local capsules when notation is unfamiliar. The path is accelerated because it moves from design to inference and diagnostic checks in two modules; it still asks you to work exercises. Set aside about nine hours, including written calculations, the worked mixed practice, and the capstone.

Your bounded task is to read a model-conditional interval and p-value, then challenge a tempting trend story with subgroup rates and residuals. You will not learn a general causal-identification method or all interval constructions. Every numerical claim in this route must carry a population, sampling rule or fitted-model assumption. Write down the direction of a null-tail test before calculating it. Read an observed confidence interval as the product of a procedure whose long-run coverage depends on assumptions, not as a posterior probability for its fixed endpoints.

Worked mixed practice

Mixed practice: A spinner gives 60 blue outcomes in 100 independent trials. A Wald calculation gives p-hat=0.60 and estimated standard error sqrt(0.6×0.4/100)≈0.049. The approximate 95% interval is 0.60±1.96×0.049, or about [0.504,0.696]. This interval procedure has an approximate repeated-coverage interpretation under iid Bernoulli sampling, with known weaknesses near the boundaries. For an upper-tail null test of fairness, write P(X≥60|X~Binomial(100,0.5)), an exact finite sum; do not call it P(fairness is true). If the tail direction was chosen after the count, that exact sum no longer describes the full selection procedure.

Capstone and model solution

Capstone. Use the worked spinner count above for one interval and one predeclared upper-tail null question. Then audit a fictional puzzle-route comparison: A finishes 9/10 easy and 36/90 hard boards; B finishes 72/90 easy and 3/10 hard boards. Compute each subgroup and pooled rate, and state why the pooled result is misleading for a like-for-like comparison. Add a final diagnostic paragraph: for x=0,1,2,3,4 and y=2,1,2,5,10, a fitted least-squares line is y-hat=2x. List its residuals and say what pattern they show.

Show a complete model solution

Model solution: the Wald interval is about [0.504,0.696]. The upper-tail p-value is the sum from k=60 through 100 of C(100,k)/2^100, about 0.0284; it is conditional on the fair independent-spin null and the planned upper tail. A has easy and hard rates 90% and 40%, B has 80% and 30%. Pooled A is 45/100=45%, pooled B is 75/100=75%. B received ninety easy boards while A received ten, so different difficulty mixes explain the reversal. Equal half-easy weights give A 65% and B 55%, an arithmetic standardization, not a new experiment. The fitted values for the five x values are 0,2,4,6,8, giving residuals 2,−1,−2,−1,2. Their U shape warns against a straight-line description over that range; it does not identify a cause.

How to check your work

Outcome to practice: interval coverage and null tails are assessed in the spinner calculation; association limits are assessed by the residual diagnostic; pooled-versus-subgroup interpretation is assessed by the route table. The capstone intentionally asks for distinct checks because a single attractive aggregate statistic can fail for several different reasons.

Rubric, twelve points: two for the interval arithmetic and coverage interpretation; two for the prespecified null tail and its conditional meaning; four for accurate subgroup, pooled and standardized rates; two for the residual list and curvature interpretation; two for a causal-limit sentence. A satisfying stopping point is a short report where every percentage has its denominator and no probability is assigned to the fixed null hypothesis without an explicit probability model for hypotheses.
