From equations to a defensible model memo

This sixteen-hour route treats nine toy-model lessons as a sequence of proof and validation tasks. “Deep” means sustained work on the named results: dimensional scaling, local elasticity, a sine-sum identity, a conditional independence product, a pendulum approximation, and held-out error calculations. It does not confer broad expertise in differential equations, acoustics or empirical network science. The final product is a compact sound-and-network exhibit with derivations, numerical checks and a precise limitations memo.

Entry recap. A derivative describes local change: if y=f(x), dy/dx is the limit of output change divided by input change when the limit exists. Trigonometric angles here use radians. A sine wave's second derivative is minus its angular frequency squared times the wave. For independent events, joint probability is a product; correlated events can have the same marginal probabilities and a different joint probability. A residual is observed minus predicted, retaining output units. Each article defines the local notation again.

Plan about 225 minutes for nine lessons, 270 minutes for their exercises and independent re-derivations, 185 minutes for the proof practice below, and 280 minutes for the capstone, memo and self-review: 960 minutes. Split it over several sessions. A valid stopping point after the first module is an assumption ledger and sensitivity analysis; after the second, an exact-identity and network-rule comparison.

Three catalog modules and their bridges

Foundations

  1. A model begins with a question. State an answerable decision, variables, units and omissions. Prove that dividing panel area by tile area yields a dimensionless count, and identify when a grid count differs.
  2. Build a miniature gallery that scales. Re-derive the s² and s³ rules using products of two or three dimensions. Construct a one-direction stretch as a counterexample to careless use of similarity.
  3. Which assumption matters most? Derive the tile-side elasticity −2 and compare it with a finite 10% change. Explain why rounded pack counts have jumps and lack the same smooth local sensitivity.

These three lessons supply a disciplined vocabulary for the remaining cases. “Exact” always means exact within a specified mathematical model; an approximate physical description can still lead to exact algebra. A unit check catches some mistakes, but cannot certify that a parameter or mechanism represents reality.

Mechanisms and examples

  1. Two tones make a slow pulse. Derive the identity from sin(u+v) and sin(u−v). Prove that |cos(πΔft)| peaks every 1/Δf seconds while the signed factor repeats every 2/Δf.
  2. Rules, randomness and repeatability in art. Distinguish ideal iid sampling from a reproducible pseudorandom program. Derive a binomial region count and explain how preview selection changes the distribution of exhibited outputs.
  3. A rumor on a small fictional network. Condition on the active set before multiplying edge failures. Produce a correlated-attempt counterexample with the same marginals but a different no-transmission probability.

The bridge is to ask what repeats. In the beat identity, a signed mathematical factor repeats exactly. In random art, a seed repeats a particular sequence for one algorithm. In network simulation, independent repetitions produce variable outcomes. Those are three different claims, and none alone validates a physical or social interpretation.

Limits and deeper questions

  1. A swinging sketch and a linear model. Derive θ″+(g/L)sinθ=0 from tangential acceleration and force. Replace sinθ by θ only after stating small-angle scope; bound the pointwise restoring-term error.
  2. A curve can fit and still mislead. Construct another formula with the same training residuals and a different extrapolation, then check a truly held-out point. Explain why fit cannot establish mechanism.
  3. Stress-test a model before sharing it. Test units, boundary, parameter changes and held-out errors. Write a limitation note about transfer to a new setting and avoid describing a low MAE as proof of causality.

This final bridge separates approximation error from validation error. Taylor's bound controls the difference between θ and sinθ at an angle inside the ideal pendulum equation. A held-out residual compares a prediction with an observation under a particular data split. The former is mathematical; the latter includes data noise, measurement choices and possible model misspecification. Neither substitutes for the other.

Proof practice with checks

First task. Start with sin(u+v)=sin u cos v+cos u sin v and sin(u−v)=sin u cos v−cos u sin v. Add them, then choose u=(A+B)/2 and v=(A−B)/2. State the beat identity and show why peaks of its magnitude envelope occur every 1/|f₁−f₂| seconds for unequal frequencies.

Worked proof

Adding the two addition formulas cancels the cosine-sine terms and yields sin(u+v)+sin(u−v)=2sin u cos v. With the chosen u and v, u+v=A and u−v=B, giving sin A+sin B=2sin((A+B)/2)cos((A−B)/2). Set A=2πf₁t and B=2πf₂t. The slow factor's magnitude is |cos(π(f₁−f₂)t)|. Its peaks occur when its argument is an integer multiple of π, separated by Δt=1/|f₁−f₂|. This assumes ideal equal-amplitude zero-phase waves for the simple zero-trough envelope.

Second task. A target has two active neighbours, each with marginal probability 1/2 of sending a marker. Give one independent joint model and one perfectly correlated joint model. Calculate no-transmission probability for both and identify what the marginal chances cannot tell you.

Worked counterexample

Under independence, four send/fail pairs each have probability 1/4; both fail with probability 1/4. Under perfect correlation, both send with probability 1/2 and both fail with probability 1/2. Each neighbour still sends with marginal probability 1/2, but no-transmission probability is 1/2. Marginal probabilities alone do not determine a joint probability; the independence hypothesis is substantive.

Third task. Check the pointwise small-angle replacement at |θ|≤0.2 radians using |sinθ−θ|≤|θ|³/6. Explain exactly what the resulting relative bound does not prove.

Worked bound

For nonzero |θ|≤0.2, divide by |θ| to get |sinθ−θ|/|θ|≤θ²/6≤0.04/6≈0.00667, or 0.667%. At θ=0 the terms agree exactly. The bound concerns the restoring term at one angle; it does not establish that two full trajectories remain within 0.667% of each other for all time.

Capstone: miniature sound-and-network exhibit

Use two ideal equal-amplitude tones, 440 and 444 Hz, and a one-metre ideal pendulum with g=9.81 m/s² and initial angle 0.2 rad. Give the beat identity, beat-peak interval, linear pendulum period and a bound on the relative restoring-term error within |θ|≤0.2. Separately use the synchronous A–C–D path with independent edge chance p=0.5: derive the chance D activates by step 2, then raise p to 0.6 and report sensitivity as an exact difference. A predeclared held-out set of 20 toy runs at p=0.5 records D active in 7. Report the observed minus predicted rate and a short memo stating what each check supports and what it cannot establish.

Model solution and memo

The sound sum is 2sin(884πt)cos(4πt). Its magnitude envelope peaks every 1/4=0.25 s; the signed cosine repeats after 0.5 s. The linear pendulum has period 2π√(1/9.81)≈2.006 s. At |θ|≤0.2, |sinθ−θ|/|θ|≤0.2²/6≈0.00667 for nonzero θ; zero is exact. This bounds the restoring-term replacement, not full-path discrepancy. In the graph, D requires success on A→C and then C→D, so P(D by step 2)=p²=0.25. At p=0.6 it is 0.36, an absolute increase of 0.11 and a relative increase of 44% from 0.25. Seven of 20 held-out runs give 0.35, so observed minus predicted is +0.10; the count difference from expectation is 2. A binomial standard deviation for the count under independent runs is √(20×0.25×0.75)≈1.94, so a difference of 2 is unsurprising in scale, although this alone is not a formal fit test. The exact signal identity, force equation and network product follow from their ideal assumptions. The oscillator replacement is approximate, and finite held-out runs test this simulation under its rule. None demonstrates how real instruments sound to every listener, how actual people share information, or a causal effect.

Outcome to practice map and rubric

OutcomeEvidence
Derive an approximation and boundaryThe pendulum equation, linear period and pointwise small-angle bound are distinguished.
Test sensitivityThe two p² predictions and exact 0.11 change are reproducible.
Validate with a residualThe 7/20 holdout is compared with 0.25 and sampling variability is acknowledged.
Communicate scopeThe memo distinguishes mathematical identities, approximation and real-world claims.

Give one point per row, with a revision required for any missing assumption or unit. Four points complete this bounded route; merely obtaining the numerical answers is insufficient. The satisfying stopping point is a reader-checkable exhibit plus memo. A future empirical project would need real measurements and a design suited to its causal or predictive question.
