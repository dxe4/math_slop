A shrinking spike with stubborn area

Can pointwise convergence fail to control integrals or uniform error?

Make a prediction

The spike tends pointwise to zero but always integrates to 1. For xⁿ, the integral tends to zero, though convergence is not uniform.

A worked setting

Controls: Sequence: n·1_(0,1/n); Sequence index n: 10; Probe x: 0.05

At n=10, the spike has exact area 1 even though its pointwise limit is zero everywhere.

fₙ(x)=n·1_(0,1/n)(x); ∫₀¹fₙ=1; fₙ(x)→0 for each fixed x.

What the result means

The spike is n on 0<x<1/n and zero elsewhere. Its width times height is exactly one; sampling pixels could miss it. No one integrable function dominates every spike: for any δ>0, some spike fits inside (0,δ) with integral 1, contrary to absolute continuity of an integrable majorant’s integral.

Where the model stops

Finite n and a probe point illustrate formulas. Pointwise convergence and failure of domination require arguments over all n and x.
