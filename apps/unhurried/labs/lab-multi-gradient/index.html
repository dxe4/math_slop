A direction finder on a landscape

How well does a gradient predict a finite move?

Make a prediction

The directional derivative is ∇f·u; a quadratic finite move adds a second-order positive term.

A worked setting

Controls: Point x: 1; Point y: 1; Direction angle (degrees): 45; Positive x curvature A: 2; Positive y curvature B: 1; Move length: 0.5

Gradient (2, 1) gives directional derivative 2.1213203. The actual height change is 1.2481602.

f(x+hu)−f(x) = h(∇f·u) + h²(Auₓ²+Buᵧ²)/2; |u|=1.

What the result means

For f(x,y)=(Ax²+By²)/2 with A,B>0, ∇f=(Ax,By). The unit vector comes from the chosen angle.

Where the model stops

The derivative is a local prediction. A large step includes curvature and need not match it.
