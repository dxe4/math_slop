Sphere path measurer

How much longer can a meridian and latitude route be than the shorter great-circle arc?

Make a prediction

The shorter great-circle arc minimizes spherical path length; a latitude circle away from the equator is generally not a geodesic.

A worked setting

Controls: Sphere radius R: 1; Start latitude (degrees): 0; Start longitude (degrees): 0; End latitude (degrees): 45; End longitude (degrees): 90; Measured path: Shorter great-circle arc

What the result means

Latitude φ and longitude λ map to R(cosφ cosλ, cosφ sinλ, sinφ). The coordinate metric is ds² = R²dφ² + R²cos²φ dλ². The route preset follows a meridian to the target latitude, then the shorter longitude arc on that latitude.

Where the model stops

Metric longitude coordinates are singular at the poles, though the sphere and coordinate-free central-angle formula are smooth there. For antipodal endpoints the minimizing great circle is not unique. Decimal lengths are approximations.
