Eigenlines and ellipse

How do eigenlines and singular values describe different features of a linear map?

Make a prediction

The image of the unit circle has semiaxis lengths equal to the singular values; some real matrices have no real eigenlines.

A worked setting

Controls: Matrix a₁₁: 1; Matrix a₁₂: 0; Matrix a₂₁: 0; Matrix a₂₂: 1; Unit-vector angle (degrees): 30; Real eigenlines: Show

Every direction is an eigenline, eigenvalue 1. The image of the unit circle has semiaxis lengths 1 and 1.

AᵀA has eigenvalues σ₁² = 1 and σ₂² = 1; σ₁σ₂ = |det A| = 1 (up to rounding).

What the result means

The eigenvalues of AᵀA are the squared singular values. Real eigenlines are directions preserved by A, and need not coincide with the ellipse axes.

Where the model stops

Circle curves are sampled for display. Rounded singular values and eigenvalues are numerical; an eigenline is shown only when the real characteristic discriminant permits one.
