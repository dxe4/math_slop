Line and conic intersections

Where do the second intersection and its multiplicity go when an affine plot shows fewer than two points?

Make a prediction

Try a tangent and a vertical line, then compare real and complex charts.

A worked setting

Controls: Line form: Y = mX + bZ; Slope m (or vertical c): 0; Intercept b for ordinary line: 1; Show solutions in: Real chart

Line Y = 0X + (1)Z: discriminant Δ = 4. Two distinct real finite points, each multiplicity 1. Total complex projective multiplicity = 2. The line has infinity point [1:0:0], which is not on YZ=X².

In Z=1, solve x² − 0x − (1) = 0; y = 0x + (1). At [1:0:0], YZ−X² = −1, so there is no intersection at infinity for this line.

What the result means

For the smooth projective conic YZ=X², a nonvertical line Y=mX+bZ gives x²−mx−b=0 in Z=1. Its discriminant is m²+4b. A vertical line X=cZ meets the conic at (c,c²) and at [0:1:0]. Parameterizing the conic by [st:s²:t²] gives the vertical intersection equation t(s−ct), confirming multiplicity one at each point. Every selected line has total intersection multiplicity two over the complex projective plane.

Where the model stops

This checks one conic and a bounded family of lines. Negative discriminants have complex points invisible in a real affine plot. The model illustrates a degree-two case and does not prove the full Bézout theorem.
