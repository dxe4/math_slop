Euclid in rectangles

Why does each remainder step preserve the gcd?

Make a prediction

The last nonzero remainder is gcd(a,b), and back-substitution gives its integer linear combination.

A worked setting

Controls: First integer a: 84; Second integer b: 30; Division steps shown: 12; Show Bézout coefficients: Yes

gcd(84,30)=6; 3 division steps. Showing all.

a=qb+r, 0≤r<b; gcd(a,b)=gcd(b,r). 6=-1a+3b.

What the result means

If a=qb+r, a divisor divides both a and b exactly when it divides b and r. The ledger records this invariant at every division.

Where the model stops

Inputs are nonnegative and at most 144; (0,0) has no chosen gcd here. Rectangle tiles are an analogy, while the numeric ledger is exact.
