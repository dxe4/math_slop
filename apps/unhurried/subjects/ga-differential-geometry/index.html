Differential geometry

Curves, surfaces, geodesics and curvature from calculus. Measure length and turning for selected regular plane curves. Use curve and surface derivatives to calculate metrics and check great-circle geodesics. Compute selected surface curvatures and apply, with named hypotheses, Gauss–Bonnet and spherical holonomy. Begin with derivatives; vectors in the plane. This route studies three questions about a smooth plane path: how far does it travel, does changing its clock change that distance, and how fast does its direction turn per unit distance? You will leave with a complete calculation for circular arcs and a reliable test for when a curvature formula may be used. This route is for a reader who remembers multivariable calculus but wants to reconnect its derivatives to geometry. It first measures and frames regular curves, then uses two tangent directions to measure routes on a sphere. This proof-oriented route studies a narrow chain of results: regular surface patches supply metric and curvature calculations; a global theorem links the integral of curvature to topology; transport around one spherical triangle turns that integral into an arrow rotation. The course is a selected application dossier, not a comprehensive differential geometry qualification.
