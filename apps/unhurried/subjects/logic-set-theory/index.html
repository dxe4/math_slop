Logic and set theory

Precise statements, quantifiers, sets, functions, and infinity, with semantics kept distinct from proof syntax. A finite club-policy route through truth values, implication, sets, and quantifier order, ending with a complete relation-table countermodel. A proof-focused refresher on quantified scope, finite power sets, inverses, and modular equivalence classes. A sustained proof route from finite subset encodings to diagonal uncountability and Russell’s restriction on naive set formation. Begin with comfort with examples and counterexamples. A club organizer says, “Every member has read a book, and if a member recommends a book, the club discusses it.” There is plenty to unpack: which book can depend on the member, “and” combines two requirements, and an if–then rule has one specific kind of violation. This route is for someone who remembers elementary set notation but wants to recover exact scope and short proofs. Its bounded outcomes are to negate quantified claims correctly, prove a two-sided inverse criterion, and partition integers by an equivalence relation. Finite subsets can be encoded by binary strings. Infinite binary sequences also look like strings of choices, but no natural-numbered list catches them all.
