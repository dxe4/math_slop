Graph theory

Reason about networks, paths, colourings, trees, and planar structure. Build a simple graph from a small walking map, classify route sequences, and prove the handshaking identity by counting edge ends. Separate edge-covering routes, edge-count shortest paths, trees, and conflict colouring through worked certificates and a shared capstone. Prove selected structural results for trees, plane graphs, matching, and isomorphism, then analyse two relabelled drawings. Begin with sets of objects and pairs. You will translate a small map into vertices and edge pairs, distinguish three types of route, and prove one counting fact about degrees. This route is for a reader who already knows what vertices and edges are and can follow a short proof. It connects five graph techniques without treating them as interchangeable: an Euler trail covers edges once, BFS minimizes edge count from a source, a tree supplies unique paths, and a colouring separates conflicts. This sustained route studies three structural questions about finite graphs: what makes a tree sparse, what a crossing-free embedding does to the vertex–edge–face count, and how to recognise the same network after relabelling. A bipartite matching module adds a different kind of structure: alternating paths that improve an assignment.
