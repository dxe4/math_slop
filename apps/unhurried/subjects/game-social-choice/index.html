Game theory and social choice

Strategic choices and collective rankings explored through small transparent games and ballots. A bounded first route through payoff reading, pure best responses and one majority cycle. A selected route through pure and mixed games, ballot counting and one strategic vote. A proof-focused route through all nine lessons, including repeated incentives and Arrow’s exact scope. Begin with tables, fractions and ordered rankings. This course gives you two bounded skills: read a two-player payoff table and test whether either player would switch alone; count a three-option majority cycle from actual ballots. The game and election use different kinds of input, but both require a disciplined question about whose choice or comparison is being held fixed. It begins with payoff matrices and pure best responses, then solves a two-action mixed equilibrium and compares several ways to count ranked ballots. Its depth comes from stating hypotheses and testing implications on finite models.
