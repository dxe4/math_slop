Number theory

Divisibility, primes, congruences, and elementary cryptographic ideas through proof and play. Use witnesses, Euclidean remainders and a clock return theorem to justify a repeating rota. Reconnect Euclid, Bézout, prime infinitude and modular inverses through a reversible affine toy cipher. Prove selected modular theorems and use them to justify an exact small-key RSA round-trip. Begin with integer arithmetic. You need multiplication, division with remainder, and comfort with positive and negative whole numbers. This route is for a reader comfortable with algebra who wants to recover proof fluency. It connects two uses of Euclid: remainders calculate a gcd, while reversing those equations gives coefficients that can undo multiplication modulo a chosen number. This proof route studies a narrow chain of results: Bézout gives modular inverses; those inverses construct a solution for two coprime clock readings; prime moduli let us permute nonzero residues and reduce exponents; repeated squaring makes the resulting calculations short; and a tiny RSA example combines those ingredients. It is a sustained study of named elementary theorems, not a course in modern cryptography.
