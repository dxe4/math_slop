03
Article series · 10 readings · L1–L3

Single-variable calculus

Change, accumulation and approximation, one variable at a time. Connect secant rates, corners, area bounds and the fundamental theorem through a piecewise journey. Recover chain-rule, theorem-hypothesis, substitution and error-bound habits through mixed transfer work. Prove selected derivative, integral, mean-value and Taylor results and compare three boundary examples. Begin with Algebra with fractions; Graphs of functions. This route asks two connected questions: how can a changing graph have a rate at one instant, and how can a changing rate build a total? Algebra with fractions and the idea of a graph are enough to start. This route is for a reader who has previously differentiated and integrated but wants reliable transfer between tools. The goal is to rebuild three habits: check hypotheses before applying a theorem, carry units and endpoints through a calculation, and attach an error or limit argument to an approximation. This route studies selected proof mechanisms behind a derivative, an integral, an existence theorem, an improper limit, and a Taylor approximation.

04
Article series · 10 readings · L1–L3

Multivariable and vector calculus

Landscapes, flows, coordinates and the relationship between boundaries and interiors. A selected route through contours, gradients, polar area and work along paths. Recover core multivariable and vector calculus calculations with sign and domain checks. A proof workshop on differentiability, topology obstructions, and rectangular boundary identities. Begin with Single-variable derivatives and integrals; Vectors and dot products. This route develops four connected ideas: a contour describes equal height, a gradient predicts first-order change, a polar coordinate cell has a changing area, and a field's work is accumulated along an oriented path. These selected tools give a useful introduction to multivariable thinking. This route is for a reader who has studied multivariable calculus before and wants the core calculations back in working memory. This route is a selected proof workshop on differentiability, circulation, and boundary-to-interior identities.
