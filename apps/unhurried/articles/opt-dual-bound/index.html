Can a different problem prove your answer is good?

Finding a feasible choice gives an upper bound on a minimization problem's best value. A Lagrangian dual construction gives lower bounds. If the two meet, the candidate is optimal; if they nearly meet, the gap quantifies what remains unproved. This lesson proves weak duality directly and keeps strong duality separate.

Background capsule. Write a primal problem as minimizing f₀(x) subject to fᵢ(x)≤0. A feasible x obeys every inequality. The infimum p* is the greatest lower bound of feasible objective values; it may be unattained. For a real-valued function h, infₓh(x) is a number or −∞. Each inequality receives a multiplier λᵢ≥0. We use the convention L(x,λ)=f₀(x)+Σᵢλᵢfᵢ(x), and define the dual function g(λ)=infₓL(x,λ), with x ranging over the stated base domain.

A lower bound you can see

Worked example. Minimize x² subject to x≥1. Put the constraint in the agreed form f₁(x)=1−x≤0. For λ≥0, L(x,λ)=x²+λ(1−x). Complete the square: L=(x−λ/2)²+λ−λ²/4, so g(λ)=λ−λ²/4. At λ=1 this gives 3/4. Thus no feasible x can have objective below 3/4, even before we solve the primal.

At λ=2, g(2)=1. The feasible candidate x=1 also has objective 1, so lower and upper bounds match. Hence p*=1 and x=1 is optimal. Notice what was checked: λ=2 is nonnegative, x=1 is feasible, and g(2) really is the infimum over all real x, attained at x=1. If we had simply inserted x=1 into L without taking an infimum, we would not have constructed a valid lower bound.

Why weak duality never needs convexity

The core inequality is g(λ)=infxL(x,λ)≤p*forλ≥0

Proof with its domain. For any feasible x and nonnegative λ, each λᵢfᵢ(x)≤0. Therefore g(λ)≤L(x,λ)≤f₀(x). Taking the infimum of the rightmost quantity over feasible x yields g(λ)≤p*, provided the feasible set is nonempty and the value is interpreted in the extended-real sense. Convexity was not used. If a feasible candidate x̄ has objective U and a valid λ has finite dual value Lb=g(λ), then Lb≤p*≤U. The nonnegative difference U−Lb is a certified bound on the candidate's suboptimality.

The dual problem maximizes g(λ) over λ≥0. The best dual lower bound d* is at most p*. Equality d*=p* is strong duality, which requires additional conditions. For convex problems with suitable constraint qualification, such as a strictly feasible point under the usual Slater hypotheses, strong duality can hold. This article does not prove that theorem. It proves the elementary inequality that remains valid even for nonconvex objectives.

Two failures are easy to avoid. A negative multiplier reverses the direction of a constraint contribution and may make the claimed bound invalid. An infeasible primal candidate does not supply an upper bound on p*. For example, x=0 in the worked problem gives objective zero, but violates x≥1. Reporting a gap 0−g(2)=−1 would reveal the mistake rather than an astonishing improvement.

Build and audit a bound

1. Calculation. Minimize (x−1)² subject to x≥2. With constraint 2−x≤0, find g(λ), a dual multiplier matching the feasible candidate x=2, and the gap.

Hint for question 1

Complete the square in (x−1)²+λ(2−x), or set its derivative to zero.

Full solution for question 1

The minimizer of L over x is x=1+λ/2. Substitution gives g(λ)=λ−λ²/4: indeed the square term λ²/4 and constraint contribution λ(1−λ/2) combine to λ−λ²/4. At λ=2, g=1. Candidate x=2 is feasible and has objective (2−1)²=1. The gap is 1−1=0, certifying optimality by weak duality.

2. Certificate repair. In the original problem minimize x² subject to x≥1, a proposed report uses x=1/2 and λ=2 to claim a negative gap. Locate every relevant number and repair the report.

Hint for question 2

Check feasibility before calling an objective an upper bound.

Full solution for question 2

x=1/2 violates 1−x≤0 because 1−1/2=1/2>0. Its objective 1/4 is not a primal upper bound. λ=2 is dual feasible and g(2)=1, a valid lower bound. Replace the candidate by x=1, which satisfies the constraint and has objective 1. The corrected gap is 1−1=0. The apparent negative gap came from a nonfeasible candidate, not failure of weak duality.

A certificate with honest limits

For minimization, feasible choices give upper bounds and nonnegative inequality multipliers give Lagrangian lower bounds after taking the infimum. A zero gap certifies optimality without needing a general strong-duality theorem. A positive gap measures uncertainty about the particular candidate and bound, not necessarily an inherent gap between primal and dual optima. KKT conditions organize when the two sides meet.
