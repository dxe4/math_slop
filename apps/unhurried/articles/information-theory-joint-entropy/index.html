Do two clues double uncertainty?

Imagine two clue cards shown together. Each card may be red or blue. If the second is an exact copy of the first, observing two cards reveals no more than observing one. If their colors are chosen independently, the pair may carry twice as much uncertainty. Joint entropy records uncertainty about the complete pair, while conditional entropy records what remains unknown about one card after the other is revealed.

Background capsule. A joint probability p(x,y) is the chance of one particular pair. Summing a row gives the marginal chance p(x). If p(x) > 0, the conditional chance p(y|x) = p(x,y)/p(x). Entropy is the probability-weighted average of −log₂ probability, in bits. Conditional entropy H(Y|X) averages the entropy of Y separately within each positive-probability X group, weighted by the chance of that group. No conditional distribution is required for an X value that never occurs.

Two complete pair tables

Worked example. In a copying game, the pair RR has probability 1/2 and BB has probability 1/2; RB and BR are impossible. Each single card is fair, so H(X) = H(Y) = 1 bit. The pair has only two equally likely outcomes, hence H(X,Y) = 1 bit. Once X is known, Y is certain: H(Y|X) = 0. Now consider independent fair cards. All four pairs have probability 1/4, so the pair has entropy 2 bits. Knowing the first card leaves one full bit about the second.

A less extreme game makes X fair and copies it with probability 3/4, switching with probability 1/4. Its table is RR = BB = 3/8 and RB = BR = 1/8. Each marginal is still fair. Given either value of X, the conditional distribution of Y is (3/4, 1/4), so H(Y|X) = h₂(1/4), about 0.811 bit. The joint uncertainty is about 1.811 bits. The table sums to one and makes the calculation reproducible without a diagram.

Why the chain rule holds

H(X,Y)=H(X)+H(Y|X)

Exact claim and derivation. For any finite joint distribution, the identity holds with the convention that zero-probability pairs contribute zero. For every positive pair, p(x,y) = p(x)p(y|x), so −log₂ p(x,y) = −log₂ p(x) − log₂ p(y|x). Weight by p(x,y) and sum over positive pairs. The first sum collapses to H(X) because the joint row sums to p(x). The second becomes the weighted conditional entropy. A row with p(x) = 0 contributes nothing and needs no conditional division. Interchanging X and Y gives H(X,Y) = H(Y) + H(X|Y).

Conditional entropy is nonnegative, since each positive-probability surprise is nonnegative. Thus H(X,Y) ≥ H(X), but equality can hold when Y is determined by X. Separately adding H(X)+H(Y) equals joint entropy only when the variables are independent. For the copying game, that naive sum is 2 bits while the actual joint entropy is 1. The algebra proves the finite identity; it does not decide whether real clues follow the assumed table.

Use the table before the formula

Exercise 1. X is a fair bit. Y equals X with probability 3/4 and flips it with probability 1/4, independently of X. Write the four joint probabilities, then calculate H(X), H(Y|X), and H(X,Y).

Hint for exercise 1

Split each half-probability X row into a 3/4 copy branch and a 1/4 flip branch.

Full solution to exercise 1

RR and BB each have probability (1/2)(3/4) = 3/8; RB and BR each have (1/2)(1/4) = 1/8. H(X) = 1 bit. Each X row gives Y probabilities 3/4 and 1/4, hence H(Y|X) = h₂(1/4) ≈ 0.811 bit. The chain rule gives H(X,Y) ≈ 1.811 bits. Directly summing two 3/8 terms and two 1/8 terms confirms it.

Exercise 2. Let X always equal 0, and let Y be fair. Someone attempts to compute p(Y=1|X=1). Is this conditional probability needed for H(Y|X)? Find both H(Y|X) and H(X,Y).

Hint for exercise 2

Inspect the probability of the proposed conditioning event before dividing.

Full solution to exercise 2

P(X=1) = 0, so p(Y=1|X=1) is undefined under the ordinary ratio definition. The X=1 row has zero weight and is unnecessary. Given X=0, Y is fair, so H(Y|X) = 1 bit. H(X) = 0 because X is certain, and the chain rule gives H(X,Y) = 0 + 1 = 1 bit.

Know what the extra clue adds

Joint entropy counts uncertainty in a pair. The chain rule assigns the first variable its ordinary entropy and the second only its uncertainty after the first is known. In the copying game, the second card contributes zero; in the independent game, it contributes a full bit. This distinction leads to mutual information, the amount of uncertainty removed by seeing another variable. It also matters for compression of repeated or correlated symbols, where treating the pair as two unrelated letters may waste code length.

As a stopping check, ask whether the phrase “two bits from two binary cards” specifies enough to calculate joint entropy. It does not: a joint table or an independence assumption is needed. The MIT information and entropy text supports the entropy and conditional-information identities. Our card games provide finite tables that show exactly where the hypotheses enter.
