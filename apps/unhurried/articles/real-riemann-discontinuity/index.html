Can every bounded function be integrated by rectangles?

For many curves, narrower rectangles make upper and lower area estimates converge. Now imagine a function equal to one at rational inputs and zero at irrational inputs. Both kinds of input occur in every interval, however small. We will show that every upper rectangle is forced to height one and every lower rectangle to height zero. This is a structural obstruction, not a failure to sample enough points.

Background capsule. A partition of [0,1] is a finite chain 0=x₀<x₁<…<xₙ=1. On each cell, the lower sum uses the infimum of function values times cell width; the upper sum uses the supremum. A bounded function is Riemann integrable by the Darboux criterion exactly when upper and lower sums can be made arbitrarily close. We use two familiar density facts: every nonempty open interval contains a rational and an irrational. These facts can be proved from the Archimedean property and the irrationality of √2; their full derivation is beyond this local calculation.

A partition that changes nothing

Worked example. Define D(x)=1 if x is rational and D(x)=0 otherwise. Try the partition 0, 1/4, 1/2, 1. Each cell contains rationals and irrationals, so its supremum is 1 and infimum is 0. The three widths add to 1. Consequently U(P)=1 and L(P)=0. Cutting each cell into a million smaller intervals changes neither local extreme. A finite plot might draw only its rational sample values, or only its irrational ones, depending on how coordinates are represented. Either picture is misleading about the continuous interval.

The exact Darboux obstruction

Statement. For the rational indicator D on [0,1], every finite partition P has lower sum zero and upper sum one:

L(P)=0,U(P)=∑in(xi−xi−1)=1

Proof. Each partition cell has positive width. Its interior is a nonempty open interval. Rational density supplies a point where D=1, and irrational density supplies a point where D=0. Since D only takes those two values, the supremum on that cell is exactly 1 and the infimum exactly 0. The widths telescope to xₙ−x₀=1. Therefore U(P)−L(P)=1 for every P. In particular, no partition has a gap below 1/2, so the Darboux criterion fails. The function is not Riemann integrable.

The same observation shows D is discontinuous at every point. At a rational a, nearby irrational inputs give values zero while D(a)=1; at an irrational a, nearby rational inputs give one while D(a)=0. For ε=1/2, no δ can enforce continuity at either kind of point. This is a direct proof, independent of the more general theorem characterizing Riemann integrability by a null discontinuity set.

There is a surprising second integral notion. The rationals in [0,1] are countable and can be covered by intervals of arbitrarily small total length; see countable sets of zero length. Under Lebesgue measure, D equals zero almost everywhere, so its Lebesgue integral is zero. We are not treating “Riemann area” and “Lebesgue area” as interchangeable definitions: one fails to exist here while the other exists. The countable-cover argument supports the Lebesgue conclusion once measure and measurable-function integration are defined.

A different dense-set example warns against overgeneralization: a function that is one at only finitely many rational points and zero elsewhere is Riemann integrable with integral zero. Density of all rationals matters in D; merely having some discontinuities does not force failure.

Ask what every cell contains

1. Calculate. On [2,5], let E equal 3 at rational inputs and −1 at irrational inputs. Find U(P) and L(P) for every partition P.

Hint

Each cell has both values. The total width is now 3.

Worked solution

Every cell has supremum 3 and infimum −1. Thus U(P)=3(5−2)=9 and L(P)=−1(5−2)=−3, independent of the partition. The gap is 12, so E is not Riemann integrable on [2,5]. Changing the number of cells never shrinks that gap.

2. Compare. Let H(x)=1 at x=1/2 and zero elsewhere on [0,1]. Is H Riemann integrable?

Hint

Put 1/2 inside a very short pair of cells. What happens to the upper sum?

Worked solution

Yes. Every lower sum is zero. For any ε>0 choose a partition with a cell [1/2−η,1/2+η] of width 2η<ε, along with cells outside it. Only the central cell can have supremum one, so its upper sum is at most 2η<ε. The Darboux gap can therefore be made arbitrarily small and the integral is zero. A single isolated discontinuity is unlike the everywhere-discontinuous D.

Sampling cannot repair incompatible bounds

The obstruction is exact: every positive-width cell sees both values. A finite numerical trace cannot certify or refute this density property. The Riemann integral fails for D even though a later measure-based integral assigns it zero. This is a useful stopping point before investigating which sets have zero measure.
