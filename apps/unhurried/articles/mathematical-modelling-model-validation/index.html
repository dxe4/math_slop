What should be checked before sharing a supply estimate?

A fictional festival prints paper maps. A planner predicts how many maps each session will need from registered groups. A formula may be easy to calculate and still fail because its units, boundary behaviour or tested conditions are wrong. Validation is a set of question-specific checks; it is not a certificate that the model will always work.

Background capsule. A residual is observed minus predicted in the output's units. Absolute error removes the sign, so positive and negative mistakes cannot cancel. Mean absolute error (MAE) averages those magnitudes. A held-out set consists of observations excluded from choosing the formula and its parameters. Sensitivity asks how prediction changes when an input changes, holding others fixed. Sampling variation means a finite set of sessions need not represent all future sessions.

Suppose a simple rule predicts maps M(g)=3g+5 for g registered groups: three maps per group plus five spare maps. Its units are maps because 3 has units maps/group and 5 has units maps. We assume group sizes and map pickup habits are similar across sessions; we do not know that they are.

Run four different checks

Worked example. First, units: at g=10 groups, M=35 maps, not 35 groups. Second, boundary: at g=0, the rule predicts 5 maps. That can make sense if staff need spares; if no session runs without groups, the boundary is outside the intended domain and should not be treated as evidence. Third, sensitivity: increasing g by one always adds exactly three predicted maps. If observed large groups vary widely in size, this fixed slope is suspect.

Fourth, hold out two sessions before choosing the rule. Suppose the held-out observations are g=10 with 37 maps and g=20 with 61 maps. Predictions are 35 and 65. Residuals are +2 and −4 maps; absolute errors are 2 and 4. MAE is (2+4)/2=3 maps. The signed mean residual is −1 map, which hides some cancellation. The MAE is a more direct description of typical magnitude in these two cases, though two cases are too few for a reliable future-error estimate.

Define the score and its limits

For n>0 held-out cases, with observed yᵢ and a prediction ŷᵢ made without using case i to select the model, define eᵢ=yᵢ−ŷᵢ and

MAE=1n∑i=1n|yi−y^i|

This is an exact summary of those n errors. It is measured in the same unit as y, here maps. The qualification about model selection matters: if we tune 3 and 5 using the two “held-out” counts, their errors are training errors. The resulting score is optimistic as an independent check. An honest evaluation keeps the observations untouched until the rule is fixed, or explicitly labels a resampling design.

MAE also discards direction and distribution. A model that misses one large session badly can share the same MAE as one with several small misses. For planning stock, underprediction may cost more than overprediction, so a separate asymmetric decision rule may be needed. Even low held-out MAE shows association under a data setting, not causality. It does not prove registered groups cause map demand in the simple three-per-group way.

A useful validation memo gives domain (perhaps 1–30 groups), units, assumptions, boundary check, sensitivity, the held-out error table, and what changes would trigger revision. That is a reproducible claim about this model and evidence, not an all-purpose seal of approval.

Write a defensible check

1. Calculate. Another fixed model predicts 12, 17 and 22 maps for three held-out sessions. The observed counts are 10, 20 and 21. Find residuals, MAE and signed mean residual.

Hint for question 1

Subtract prediction from observation for each case, then average magnitudes separately from signed values.

Full solution to question 1

Residuals are 10−12=−2, 20−17=+3 and 21−22=−1 maps. Absolute errors are 2, 3 and 1, so MAE=(2+3+1)/3=2 maps. The signed mean is (−2+3−1)/3=0. Zero signed mean does not mean zero error; opposite signs cancel.

2. Find the boundary failure. A linear rule M(g)=3g+5 is used to predict maps for g=−2 and gives −1 map. What does this show, and how should the intended domain be stated?

Hint for question 2

Ask whether a negative number of registered groups is meaningful.

Full solution to question 2

g=−2 is not a meaningful group count, so −1 map is outside the model's intended input domain. State g as a nonnegative integer, and preferably the observed range, for example 1–30 registered groups. Within that range predictions are positive, though positivity alone does not validate the slope or intercept. New conditions require fresh checks.

Validation is a report of checks

Units, boundaries, sensitivity and held-out residuals test different parts of a model. The score MAE is exact for the cases used, but its usefulness elsewhere depends on how they were chosen and whether conditions transfer. A transparent limitation note is part of the answer.
