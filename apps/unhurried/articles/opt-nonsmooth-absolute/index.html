Can a sharp corner be the best point?

The graph of |x| has a clear minimum at zero, although it has no derivative there. A convex subgradient replaces the single tangent slope with every supporting slope. This idea also explains why medians, rather than means, minimize total absolute deviation.

Background capsule. For a convex function f:ℝ→ℝ, a number g is a subgradient at x if the line through (x,f(x)) with slope g lies at or below the graph everywhere. The set of all such g is written ∂f(x). The condition 0∈∂f(x) says the horizontal line at height f(x) supports the whole graph, which is exactly global minimality. A median of sorted data a₁≤⋯≤aₙ has at least half the observations on each side; when n is even, every point in [aₙ/₂,aₙ/₂₊₁] is a median.

Three observations and an interval of answers

Worked example. For data 1, 2, 10, let F(m)=|m−1|+|m−2|+|m−10|. At m=2, the cost is 1+0+8=9. At the mean 13/3, the cost is 10/3+7/3+17/3=34/3, larger than 9. Between 1 and 2, two observations lie to the right and one to the left, so moving right decreases F by one per unit. Between 2 and 10, two lie to the left and one to the right, so moving right increases F by one per unit. The minimum occurs at the median 2.

For even data 1, 2, 10, 12, the total absolute deviation is constant on [2,10]. At m=2 it is 1+0+8+10=19; at m=6 it is 5+4+4+6=19; at m=10 it is 9+8+0+2=19. Outside that interval, a move toward it reduces more distances than it increases. The minimizing set can thus be an interval, not a unique point.

Supporting slopes and the median proof

The defining inequality is g∈∂f(x)⇔f(y)≥f(x)+g(y−x)for ally

At x=0 for f(x)=|x|, the inequality becomes |y|≥gy for every real y. For y>0 this requires g≤1; for y<0, division by a negative reverses the sign and requires g≥−1. Conversely every g in [−1,1] satisfies |y|≥gy for both signs. Thus ∂|·|(0)=[−1,1]. Away from zero the graph is a straight line locally, and the unique subgradient is −1 for x<0 or 1 for x>0. This is a full derivation, not an appeal to a picture.

Median theorem. For sorted real data a₁≤⋯≤aₙ, minimizers of Σᵢ|m−aᵢ| are exactly the interval between the lower and upper middle order statistics. One proof pairs the smallest with the largest, the next smallest with the next largest, and so on. For a pair a≤b, |m−a|+|m−b|≥b−a, with equality exactly when m∈[a,b]. For even n, the intersection of the paired intervals is [an/2,an/2+1], the interval from the lower to upper median. For odd n, the paired intervals all contain the middle observation; the unpaired term |m−a(n+1)/2| forces m to that exact observation. This proves the characterization, including repeated values.

Absolute-loss minimizers need not be means. For 1,2,10 the mean is 13/3 and is pulled toward 10. The median result assumes ordinary equally weighted absolute deviations on the real line. Different weights or a restriction to a discrete feasible set require a modified check; do not transfer the answer unexamined.

Use slopes that still work at corners

1. Calculation. Find every minimizer of |m−1|+|m−4|+|m−9|+|m−12| and the minimum value.

Hint for question 1

Find the interval between the two middle observations and evaluate at one point.

Full solution for question 1

The middle observations are 4 and 9, so every m∈[4,9] minimizes the sum. At m=4 the value is 3+0+5+8=16. For any m in the interval, the outer pair contributes 12−1=11 and the inner pair contributes 9−4=5, totaling 16. Outside the interval, the inner-pair contribution increases, so no other point minimizes it.

2. Supporting line. Is g=3/2 a subgradient of |x| at zero? Prove the answer using one test input.

Hint for question 2

The support condition must hold for all y; choose a positive y.

Full solution for question 2

No. At x=0, g=3/2 would require |y|≥(3/2)y for every y. Taking y=2 gives 2≥3, false. The complete subgradient set is [−1,1], found by testing positive and negative y separately.

A derivative is optional for a convex minimum

Subgradients express global supporting inequalities. Zero belongs to the subdifferential exactly at a global minimizer of a convex function on the whole line. Pairing absolute distances proves the median result and exposes possible intervals of optima. For nonconvex nonsmooth functions, a local generalized slope of zero is not automatically a global certificate; the convex support inequality is the key.
