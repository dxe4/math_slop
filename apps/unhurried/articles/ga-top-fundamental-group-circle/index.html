Why circle loops add like integers

Start at 1 on the unit circle. Walking around once, twice, or in reverse produces loops that cannot all be continuously deformed into one another while keeping the starting point fixed. Their based-homotopy classes form a group, and the number of turns identifies that group with the integers. We will prove the group-law correspondence while stating the path-and-homotopy lifting lemma on which the classification rests.

Background capsule. A based loop γ:[0,1]→S¹ has γ(0)=γ(1)=1, where S¹={z∈ℂ:|z|=1}. Two loops represent the same class if a continuous homotopy between them keeps both endpoints at 1. Concatenation γ*η traverses γ during the first half of the time and η during the second. Reversing a loop gives an inverse class; the constant loop gives the identity. These assertions about the fundamental group follow from routine reparametrization homotopies, whose full formulas are outside this lesson.

Lift a walk to a line

Worked example. Use p:ℝ→S¹, p(u)=e2πiu. The real numbers 0, 1 and −1 all map to 1. The loop γn(t)=e2πint, for integer n, lifts to the straight real path un(t)=nt starting at 0. Its endpoint is n. For n=2 the lift climbs from 0 to 2 while the circle image goes around twice; for n=−1 it descends to −1 and traverses clockwise. The integer endpoint keeps information that the circle endpoint alone loses.

For two loops, first lift γ from 0 to m. To lift η after γ, begin its real lift at m rather than 0. Since adding an integer does not change p, the second portion ends at m+n. For example, one counterclockwise circuit followed by one clockwise circuit has lift endpoint 1+(−1)=0. Its class is the identity, even though the parametrized loop visibly moved.

The classification, conditional on lifting

Exact statement. With basepoint 1 and concatenation as group operation,

π1(S1,1)≅(ℤ,+)[γ]↦γ~(1)

Here γ̃ is the unique lift of the based loop γ with γ̃(0)=0. The required lifting lemma says that for any path α in S¹ and any chosen u₀ with p(u₀)=α(0), there is a unique continuous lift α̃ through p starting at u₀. It also says that a homotopy of paths lifts continuously once a lift of its initial path is specified; for our based homotopies the lift along the basepoint edge stays at 0. We use this lemma without proving it. It is a covering-space theorem, not a fact supplied by the appearance of a spiral.

Proof given that lemma. A based loop ends at 1, so its lift endpoint is an integer. A based homotopy lifts continuously over the square. As the homotopy parameter s varies, the lift endpoint is a continuous integer-valued function of s, hence constant. Therefore the integer depends only on the homotopy class. The concatenation calculation above proves that the map from classes to integers preserves the operation: endpoint(γ*η)=endpoint(γ)+endpoint(η).

Every integer occurs, since γn has lift un(t)=nt. To show injectivity, suppose γ and η have lifts u and v starting at 0 and ending at the same integer n. The formula H(s,t)=p((1−s)u(t)+s v(t)) is a continuous based homotopy between them: at t=0 both lifts are 0, and at t=1 both are n, which p sends to 1. Thus equal integers give equal classes. The map is bijective and operation-preserving, completing the conditional proof.

The lifting lemma is exactly the deferred part. Without it, we can classify the explicit γn examples but cannot assign a continuous lift to every possible circle loop or guarantee invariance under every based homotopy. The same integer is the winding number of the loop around 0, but the group statement concerns based classes and their concatenation.

Use the lift endpoint

1. Let α(t)=e6πit and β(t)=e−4πit. Find their classes as integers and the class of α*β. Give lift endpoints, not only a drawing.

Hint for question 1

Write each exponent as 2πi times an integer multiple of t.

Full solution for question 1

α(t)=e2πi(3t) lifts to 3t and represents 3. β(t)=e2πi(−2t) lifts to −2t and represents −2. The concatenation lift runs from 0 to 3, then from 3 to 3−2=1. Therefore [α*β] corresponds to 1. Its visible route may overlap itself, but the lift endpoint records the net turn.

2. Suppose two based loops γ and η have lifts u,v with u(0)=v(0)=0 and u(1)=v(1)=−2. Construct a based homotopy and state which assumption allowed you to speak of u and v.

Hint for question 2

Interpolate the real lifts, then apply p. Check both time endpoints.

Full solution for question 2

Set H(s,t)=e2πi((1−s)u(t)+s v(t)). At s=0 it is γ and at s=1 it is η. At t=0 the exponent is 0, and at t=1 it is −2, so both endpoints map to 1 for every s. Continuity follows from continuity of u and v. The path-lifting lemma for p supplied those real lifts; the interpolation itself then proves the homotopy.

State the theorem with its input

Lift endpoints turn loop concatenation into integer addition, and equal endpoints yield an explicit homotopy. The full classification of all circle loops depends on path and homotopy lifting, which this lesson assumes. This is a proof-based window into the fundamental group, not a computation of fundamental groups for arbitrary spaces. Revisit based homotopy for the boundary conditions or winding for polygonal calculations.
