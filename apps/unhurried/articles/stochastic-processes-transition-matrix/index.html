Vectors and small matrices

A vector in the coordinate plane can be represented by an ordered pair. Add vectors component by component: (1,2)+(3,−1)=(4,1). A scalar is an ordinary number used to scale a vector, so 2(1,2)=(2,4). These operations describe displacements using the chosen coordinate axes.

Worked example. A two-by-two matrix has two columns. Let its first column be (2,1) and its second be (0,3). Applied to a vector (x,y), it produces x times the first column plus y times the second.

(2013)(12)=(27)

Here (1,2) maps to 1(2,1)+2(0,3)=(2,7). The columns tell you what happens to the unit vectors (1,0) and (0,1); scaling and addition then determine every other input.

Try one. Where does the same matrix send (−1,1)?

Worked answer

Use −1(2,1)+1(0,3)=(−2,−1)+(0,3)=(−2,2). Equivalently the first output is 2x and the second is x+3y; substituting x=−1 and y=1 gives the same pair.

Watch the convention. We write input vectors as columns. Each output uses an entire row's products and sum, rather than multiplying corresponding entries independently.

Probability and conditioning

A probability model names the possible outcomes and their probabilities. An event is a collection of outcomes. In a finite model where every outcome is equally likely, an event's probability is its number of outcomes divided by the total number.

Worked example. Roll a fair six-sided die labelled 1 through 6. The event “greater than 3” has outcomes {4,5,6}, so its probability is 3/6=1/2. If you learn that the result is even, only {2,4,6} remain possible. These three outcomes are equally likely under the condition, and two of them are greater than 3.

P(greater than 3∣even)=23

The vertical bar reads “given.” Conditioning restricts the relevant outcomes and renormalizes their probabilities. The event you condition on must have positive probability for this ordinary ratio definition.

Try one. Given that the die result is odd, what is the probability it is less than 4?

Worked answer

The possible odd results are {1,3,5}. Two of them, 1 and 3, are less than 4, giving probability 2/3. The denominator counts the conditioned possibilities.

Watch the assumption. Counting outcomes equally works here because the die is fair. For unequal probabilities, add the assigned probabilities of the relevant outcomes.

Move a whole distribution forward

A generative playlist has three moods: Calm (C), Bright (B), Tense (T). The current mood affects the next one. We could follow one sampled playlist, but the more useful question is: if many copies start with a known distribution, what fraction is expected in each mood one step later? A transition matrix keeps the three conditional rules together.

Background capsule. A probability distribution is a list of nonnegative numbers totaling one. Conditional probability P(next mood j | current mood i) fills one row of a table. We use row vectors: πₜ = (chance of C, chance of B, chance of T) at time t. Multiplying a row distribution by a matrix means that the new chance of state j is the sum over all possible starting states i of πₜ(i)Pᵢⱼ. This is the law of total probability, not a new physical law.

Suppose the next mood depends only on the present mood and the same table is used each chime. Those are the time-homogeneous Markov assumptions for this example.

Worked example. Begin half Calm and half Tense: π₀ = (1/2,0,1/2). The next Calm fraction is (1/2)(1/2)+(0)(1/4)+(1/2)(0)=1/4. Bright gets (1/2)(1/2)+(0)(1/2)+(1/2)(1/2)=1/2. Tense gets 1/4. Thus π₁=(1/4,1/2,1/4). A playlist sampled once will land in exactly one mood. The row vector describes uncertainty over many possible samples, not a song that is partly in all moods.

Repeat once more: Calm becomes (1/4)(1/2)+(1/2)(1/4)=1/4; Bright remains 1/2 because every row sends half its chance there; Tense becomes (1/2)(1/4)+(1/4)(1/2)=1/4. The distribution is unchanged after the second chime. This is a particular stationary example, established by multiplication, not by a long animation.

The row rule and its boundary

πt+1=πtP,∑j=1rPij=1

The matrix P has row i equal to the conditional distribution of the next state from i. For any next state j, partition the possibilities by current state: P(Xₜ₊₁=j)=ΣᵢP(Xₜ=i)P(Xₜ₊₁=j|Xₜ=i). This is exactly the jth entry of πₜP. Each row sum equals one, so Σⱼπₜ₊₁(j)=Σᵢπₜ(i)ΣⱼPᵢⱼ=1. Nonnegative entries remain nonnegative. The matrix therefore preserves valid distributions.

Under a fixed P, two steps give π₂=π₀P², where P² means two successive uses of the same transition rule. If the rule changes by day, use P₀ then P₁: π₂=π₀P₀P₁. Reversing their order can change the answer. If the displayed mood omits a hidden phase that affects tomorrow, no matrix on these three visible moods may represent the conditional rule; state sufficiency must be checked first.

Counterexample to a common claim. Let a two-state matrix send A to B with certainty and B to A with certainty. Its rows normalize and π=(1/2,1/2) is unchanged by multiplication, yet starting at A produces A,B,A,B forever. The existence of a stationary row does not prove that every starting distribution approaches it. Periodicity matters.

Advance and audit

1. Calculation. Using the table, begin certainly in Bright. Find the distribution after one chime and the chance of Calm after two.

Hint for question 1

The first answer is the Bright row. Weight the second step by that row.

Full solution to question 1

Starting in Bright gives π₁=(1/4,1/2,1/4). For Calm on the next step, multiply by the Calm column: (1/4)(1/2)+(1/2)(1/4)+(1/4)(0)=1/8+1/8=1/4. The other second-step entries are 1/2 and 1/4, so normalization also checks the result.

2. Audit a proposed row. Someone writes a Calm row (0.6,0.5,0). Can it be a transition row? Explain and suggest a correction preserving the first entry.

Hint for question 2

Rows are conditional distributions and must total one.

Full solution to question 2

The entries sum to 1.1, so they cannot be exclusive next-mood probabilities. Keeping 0.6 for Calm and 0 for Tense leaves 0.4 for Bright, giving (0.6,0.4,0). That corrected row is nonnegative and sums to one. It is a different model, so later calculations must use its declared values.

Use the table for the question it answers

Multiplication advances uncertainty about a state under a fixed, sufficient Markov rule. Row normalization is an immediate arithmetic check. A stationary row is one special distribution that remains fixed; it is not automatically the destination of every start. The next lesson solves for such rows and states when convergence follows.
