Which fences actually hold the answer in place?

A constrained optimum may lie on one boundary while another constraint sits slack. Complementary slackness says a strictly slack inequality must receive zero multiplier. Together with stationarity and feasibility, this makes a compact certificate for many convex problems. The directions of the theorem matter: convexity gives sufficiency; necessity needs a constraint qualification.

Background capsule. Consider minimize f₀(x) subject to fᵢ(x)≤0, with x in an open Euclidean domain and differentiable functions. A constraint is active if fᵢ(x)=0 and slack if fᵢ(x)<0. The Lagrangian is L=f₀+Σᵢλᵢfᵢ with λᵢ≥0. For convex differentiable h, the supporting inequality h(y)≥h(x)+∇h(x)·(y−x) follows from convexity. A candidate must satisfy all original inequalities before a multiplier calculation can certify anything.

One active fence and one resting fence

Worked example. Minimize f₀(x)=(x−3)² subject to f₁(x)=x−1≤0 and f₂(x)=−x−2≤0. The feasible interval is [−2,1]. The nearest feasible point to 3 is x*=1, with objective 4. At x=1, the first constraint is active (f₁=0); the second is slack (f₂=−3). Stationarity is 2(x−3)+λ₁−λ₂=0. Taking λ₁=4 and λ₂=0 satisfies it. Both multipliers are nonnegative and λ₁f₁=λ₂f₂=0.

There is also an immediate direct proof: for any x≤1, |x−3|≥2, so (x−3)²≥4. The KKT check packages the same optimum into reusable conditions. If we tried λ₂=1, complementarity would fail because λ₂f₂=−3; the resting fence cannot carry a positive multiplier under this certificate.

Separate zero multiplier from slackness. Complementarity forces a slack constraint's multiplier to vanish, but the reverse implication fails. Minimize x² subject to x≤0. The optimum x=0 is active, yet stationarity 2x+λ=0 gives λ=0. The unconstrained minimum already lies on the fence, so no positive multiplier is needed. Thus active constraints may carry zero multipliers. A list of positive multipliers can identify supporting restrictions, but it need not list every active boundary.

Four conditions and two theorem directions

The conditions are fi(x*)≤0,λi≥0,λifi(x*)=0,∇xL(x*,λ)=0

Sufficiency theorem. Suppose f₀ and each fᵢ are convex and differentiable, and a feasible x* and λ satisfy the four conditions. Because λᵢ≥0, L(·,λ) is convex. Stationarity makes x* a global minimizer of L over the whole unconstrained domain, using the supporting inequality. Complementarity gives L(x*,λ)=f₀(x*). For any feasible y, L(y,λ)≤f₀(y). Combining these facts yields f₀(x*)=L(x*,λ)≤L(y,λ)≤f₀(y), so x* is globally optimal. This proof does not need strict feasibility once a KKT pair has been supplied.

Necessity boundary. A convex optimum need not admit multipliers without a qualification. A common sufficient condition for differentiable convex inequalities is Slater's condition: a point in the open domain exists that satisfies every inequality strictly; with affine equalities, it must also satisfy those exactly. Under the full convex-duality theorem, an attained optimum then admits suitable KKT multipliers. We cite that theorem rather than proving separation here. In the one-dimensional example minimize x subject to x²≤0, only x=0 is feasible and optimal, but stationarity would require 1+λ·0=0, impossible. Strict feasibility is absent.

Nonconvex stationarity is another trap. Minimize f(x)=−x² without constraints. At x=0 stationarity holds and the empty feasibility/complementarity conditions are automatic, yet 0 is a maximum, and the objective is unbounded below. The convexity hypothesis is what turns a KKT point into a global certificate.

Check every condition

1. Calculation. Minimize (x−4)² subject to x≤2 and x≥−1. Find a KKT pair and certify its value.

Hint for question 1

Use f₁=x−2 and f₂=−x−1. The closest feasible point to 4 is the right endpoint.

Full solution for question 1

Take x*=2, λ₁=4, λ₂=0. Primal residuals are f₁(2)=0 and f₂(2)=−3; dual multipliers are nonnegative. Stationarity is 2(2−4)+4−0=0. Both complementary products vanish. All functions are convex and differentiable, so sufficiency proves x*=2 globally optimal, with objective 4. Directly, x≤2 implies |x−4|≥2.

2. Faulty certificate. For the worked problem, someone offers x=0, λ₁=6, λ₂=0 because stationarity holds. What fails?

Hint for question 2

Evaluate both constraint values and their multiplier products at x=0.

Full solution for question 2

At x=0, both inequalities are feasible: f₁=−1, f₂=−2. Stationarity is 2(0−3)+6−0=0. But λ₁f₁=6(−1)=−6≠0, so complementarity fails. Indeed f₀(0)=9 exceeds f₀(1)=4. The multiplier was placed on a constraint that is slack at the proposed point.

A complete check is a useful proof

Primal feasibility, dual feasibility, complementary slackness and stationarity must all be stated under one sign convention. With convex differentiable data they suffice for a global minimum. Claiming they are necessary requires qualification, and claiming sufficiency for a nonconvex objective requires more work. Nonsmooth convex functions retain global certificates through subgradients rather than ordinary derivatives.
