Count what flows out of a box

A three-dimensional field can send material through a closed surface. Divergence measures local net source strength, and the divergence theorem equates its volume total with outward flux. For a rectangular box, the equality is a direct cancellation of opposite faces through the one-variable fundamental theorem.

Background capsule. F=(F₁,F₂,F₃) assigns a vector to each point. A face has an outward unit normal n: on the right x=b it is (1,0,0), on the left x=a it is (−1,0,0). The product F·n is signed flux per area. A triple integral sums over volume. Divergence is ∂F₁/∂x+∂F₂/∂y+∂F₃/∂z. A C¹ field has continuous first partial derivatives.

Six faces, one source count

Worked example. Choose the unit cube 0≤x,y,z≤1 and F=(x,y,z). On the right face x=1, F·n=1, so flux is 1. On the left face x=0, F·n=−x=0. The top y=1 and front z=1 faces each contribute 1, while their opposite zero-coordinate faces contribute 0. Total outward flux is 3.

The divergence is 1+1+1=3 throughout the cube. Volume is 1, so its integral is 3. This field expands away from the origin; arrows alone suggest outward flow, but the face-by-face signs and areas give the exact total. If F were constant, opposite faces would contribute equal and opposite flux, and divergence would be zero.

Prove the box identity

Let V=[a,b]×[c,d]×[e,f] and F be C¹ on a neighborhood. Pair the x faces. Their net contribution is ∫꜀ᵈ∫ₑᶠ[F₁(b,y,z)−F₁(a,y,z)] dz dy. By the one-variable fundamental theorem, the bracket equals ∫ₐᵇ ∂F₁/∂x dx. Hence the pair contributes ∭V∂F₁/∂x dV. The y pair similarly gives ∭∂F₂/∂y, and the z pair ∭∂F₃/∂z. Add the three pairs.

∯∂VF·ndS=∭V(∂xF1+∂yF2+∂zF3)dV

Proof boundary. This establishes the rectangular-box theorem. Subdividing a larger box cancels shared-face flux because the outward normals of neighboring cells are opposites. A standard sufficient general statement is: if V is a bounded solid with closed piecewise C¹ boundary and F is C¹ on an open neighborhood of its closure, then the outward flux through ∂V equals the volume integral of div F. Corners are allowed as lower-dimensional boundary pieces under this formulation. Passing from boxes to curved faces and proving this general statement requires a separate limiting argument; we state it rather than claim our box proof establishes it. The neighborhood condition ensures the derivatives used in the volume are defined right up to the boundary.

Sign matters. The theorem uses outward normals; inward normals would negate the left side. It also needs a closed boundary. Flux through only five faces of a cube can differ from the volume integral because the missing face may carry nonzero flow. A singular field inside a volume may defeat the smoothness hypothesis, even if its divergence vanishes wherever it is defined.

For a box with side lengths L, W, and H, a constant divergence c integrates to cLWH. That units check matters: divergence has flux per unit volume, so multiplying by volume returns flux. A single face flux instead depends on its area and on the field component normal to that face. Confusing these quantities can produce a numerically plausible answer with the wrong geometric meaning.

Balance a different box

1. Calculation. For F=(2x,0,−z) on V=[0,2]×[0,1]×[0,3], compute outward flux by faces and by divergence.

Hint for question 1

Only the x and z face pairs contribute; each face area differs.

Worked solution for question 1

At x=2, F·n=4 on area 1·3=3, giving 12; at x=0 it is zero. At z=3, F·n=−3 on area 2·1=2, giving −6; at z=0 it is zero. The y faces contribute zero. Total is 6. Divergence is 2−1=1, and the box volume is 2·1·3=6, so the volume integral is 6.

2. Explanation. Why can a constant field have nonzero flux through one face but zero net flux through a closed box?

Hint for question 2

Pair opposite faces and compare their normals.

Worked solution for question 2

The same constant component meets equal-area opposite faces, but their outward normals have opposite signs. Their signed contributions cancel pairwise. Divergence of a constant field is zero, consistent with zero total closed-surface flux. A single face does not include its cancelling partner.

Sources inside, flux outside

For a C¹ field on a box, opposite-face differences integrate the corresponding partial derivatives. Add all directions to obtain divergence and total outward flux. This is a proof for boxes and a template for understanding the general theorem, whose curved-surface passage needs more work. Keep the entire closed boundary and its outward orientation in the calculation.
