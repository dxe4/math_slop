A local test misses a global loop

A field can have zero curl at every point where it exists and still circulate around a missing point. This is the cleanest warning about the domain condition in the familiar “zero curl implies conservative” rule. We will calculate both sides exactly, then identify the hypothesis the shortcut omitted.

Background capsule. For F=(P,Q), scalar curl in the plane is Qx−Py. Circulation around a parametrized loop r(t) is ∫F(r(t))·r′(t)dt. A gradient field F=∇φ has zero circulation on every closed loop in its domain by the endpoint rule. A simply connected region has no hole that prevents a loop from being shrunk continuously to a point while staying inside the region.

Circle the excluded origin

Worked example. On U=ℝ²\{(0,0)}, define F(x,y)=(-y/(x²+y²),x/(x²+y²)). The denominator is positive at every point of U. Traverse the unit circle counterclockwise with r(t)=(cos t,sin t), 0≤t≤2π. Since x²+y²=1 there, F(r(t))=(-sin t,cos t)=r′(t). The dot product is exactly 1. Therefore the circulation is ∫₀²π1 dt=2π.

Reverse the orientation and the result is −2π. A circle of radius R>0 gives the same counterclockwise answer: F(R cos t,R sin t)=(-sin t/R,cos t/R), while r′=R(-sin t,cos t), so the dot product remains 1. A circle missing the origin cannot be continuously contracted through the origin while remaining in U. That missing point is where the field would become undefined, not an ordinary point with an unmeasured curl.

Inspect an annulus explicitly. Take 1≤√(x²+y²)≤2. The positive boundary of this region consists of the radius-two circle counterclockwise and the radius-one circle clockwise. Their circulations are 2π and −2π, adding to zero, exactly matching the integral of zero curl over the annulus. The inner boundary is essential. Keeping only the outer circle would describe a different boundary and would quietly fill the missing disk. This calculation distinguishes a legitimate use of Green's theorem on a region avoiding zero from an illegitimate use on the full disk.

Compute the curl and find the omitted premise

Write s=x²+y². Then Q=x/s and P=−y/s. Quotient differentiation gives Qx=(s−2x²)/s²=(y²−x²)/s². Similarly Py=(-s+2y²)/s²=(y²−x²)/s². Their difference is zero at every point of U. Thus zero curl and nonzero circulation coexist without contradiction.

∮|r|=1F·dr=∫02π1dt=2π

Exact boundary. On a simply connected open planar domain, a C¹ field with Qx−Py=0 has a potential; this is a standard theorem we state, not prove in full here. Our punctured plane is not simply connected. More directly, if our F had a globally single-valued C¹ potential on U, the endpoint rule would force the unit-circle integral to be zero, contradicting 2π. So no such potential exists.

Locally, away from a branch cut, the polar angle θ has gradient F. But θ jumps by 2π after one full turn; it cannot be a globally single-valued continuous potential on U. Do not apply Green's theorem to the filled unit disk: F is undefined at its center, violating the theorem's smoothness condition on a neighborhood of the region. On an annulus the inner and outer boundary circulations cancel with their induced opposite orientations.

Check the topology and sign

1. Calculation. Compute the circulation along r(t)=(2 cos t,−2 sin t), 0≤t≤2π.

Hint for question 1

This path runs clockwise; either compute the dot product or reverse a known circle.

Worked solution for question 1

F(r(t))=(sin t/2,cos t/2), while r′=(-2 sin t,−2 cos t). Their dot product is −sin²t−cos²t=−1. Integration from 0 to 2π gives −2π. The radius does not alter the magnitude.

2. Explanation. A student integrates the zero curl over the unit disk and concludes its boundary circulation is zero. Identify the exact invalid premise.

Hint for question 2

Check where the vector field is defined.

Worked solution for question 2

The disk contains (0,0), where x²+y²=0 and F is undefined. Green's theorem requires the field and relevant partial derivatives throughout an appropriate neighborhood of the filled region. Integrating a formula valid only off the origin over the disk does not meet that condition; the direct boundary integral is 2π.

Local curl is not the whole story

The computation establishes zero curl on U and circulation 2π around a counterclockwise loop. A global potential would contradict the latter. Stop with the domain-aware rule: inspect both derivatives and where the field exists. A loop avoiding a singularity can still enclose it, and enclosure can change the circulation even when every point on the loop is regular.
