When does changing size keep a triangle's shape?

A plan drawing and its built object can have corresponding angles equal while every length changes. Euclidean triangle similarity says the lengths then change by one common factor. We will derive that statement through a parallel segment, so the reason behind the ratio is visible.

Background capsule. A ratio a/b requires b≠0. Two Euclidean triangles are similar if their corresponding sides are proportional; corresponding angles are then equal. Conversely the AA criterion says two pairs of equal angles force similarity. The proof below supplies the proportionality step instead of assuming the criterion. It uses the Euclidean parallel-line angle theorem and elementary area: triangles with a shared altitude have areas in the ratio of their bases. All side lengths considered are positive.

A smaller triangle inside a larger one

Worked example. Take triangle ABC. Choose D on AB and E on AC so that DE is parallel to BC. Suppose AB=9 and AD=6, while AC=12. The scale from the large triangle to ADE is 6/9=2/3. The parallel-line theorem makes ∠ADE equal ∠ABC and ∠AED equal ∠ACB. We should therefore expect AE=(2/3)·12=8. It is tempting to accept the picture, but the intercept argument below makes that ratio exact.

One could take measurements from a printed diagram and get 7.9 instead of 8 because of line width. The exact result comes from parallelism, not the physical measurement. Likewise the last row is a mathematical consequence, even when the segment lengths are not labelled numerically.

Deriving the intercept ratio

Exact statement. If D lies strictly between A and B, E lies strictly between A and C, and DE∥BC in a Euclidean triangle, then

ADAB=AEAC=DEBC

Here is a local proof of the intercept relation AD/DB=AE/EC. Triangles ADE and BDE share altitude to line AB, so their areas have ratio AD/DB. Triangles ADE and CDE share altitude to line AC, so their areas have ratio AE/EC. Triangles BDE and CDE have the same base DE and equal altitudes because B and C lie on a line parallel to DE. Their areas are equal. Comparing the two ratios therefore gives AD/DB=AE/EC. Add 1 to both sides: (AD+DB)/DB=(AE+EC)/EC, or AB/DB=AC/EC. Equivalently AD/AB=AE/AC after simple cross multiplication.

For the DE/BC ratio, parallelism gives equal corresponding angles, so triangles ADE and ABC have matching angles. To avoid circularly invoking AA, use area once more or draw through E a parallel to AB meeting BC at F. DEFB is a parallelogram, hence DE=BF. The just-proved intercept relation applied along sides CA and CB in triangle CAB gives CE/CA=CF/CB. Thus BF/BC=1−CF/CB=1−CE/CA=AE/AC, and therefore DE/BC=AE/AC. This proves all three equalities under the stated assumptions.

For two arbitrary Euclidean triangles with two matching angles, use a rigid motion to place their matching angle vertices and rays together. Choose a pair of corresponding sides: if their lengths differ, place the shorter triangle's matching endpoint inside the longer side. Its third side must then run parallel to the longer triangle's third side by the converse of the parallel-angle relation. The nested result yields one common side factor. If the chosen sides are equal, the two triangles coincide by angle-side-angle congruence and the factor is one. This proves AA proportionality without assuming it in the placement step.

Find a scale, then challenge a false one

1. Calculation. In triangle ABC, DE∥BC, AD=4, DB=6 and AC=15. Find AE and CE.

Hint for question 1

AB=AD+DB. Apply the small-to-large ratio.

Full solution for question 1

AB=10, so AD/AB=4/10=2/5. Thus AE/AC=2/5 and AE=(2/5)·15=6. Since E lies on AC, CE=15−6=9. The intercept check is AD/DB=4/6=2/3 and AE/EC=6/9=2/3.

2. Failure test. Two triangles have side lengths (3,4,5) and (6,8,11). A student sees the first two side ratios are 1/2 and declares similarity. Explain.

Hint for question 2

Compare the third pair, and check whether the second triple even forms a triangle.

Full solution for question 2

The third ratio is 5/11, not 1/2, so there is no single scale. Both are valid triangles because 6+8>11, but matching two side ratios alone does not give AA or all-side proportionality. The included angle between those sides can differ.

The condition doing the work

Parallel segments in a Euclidean triangle yield equal angle pairs and one common ratio for all corresponding sides. The area proof also explains why merely “looking like” a scaled triangle is weak evidence. AA similarity is a Euclidean result: on a fixed-radius sphere, the angles of a standard convex triangle with shorter great-circle sides determine its side lengths, so there are no differently sized scaled copies with those same angles. Euclidean scaling does not preserve that sphere. The failure case with ratios (3,4,5) and (6,8,11) shows why a partial side match is insufficient. When using a map scale, identify matching vertices and verify the common factor rather than applying one ratio to an unrelated side.
