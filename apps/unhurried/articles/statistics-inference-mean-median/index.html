Which song length is typical when one track is very long?

One exceptionally long song changes the playlist average. The question is what the observed numbers allow us to say, and where the tempting shortcut fails.

Background capsule. The arithmetic mean adds values and divides by their count. The median is the middle ordered value for an odd number of observations, or the average of the two middle values for an even count. Both have the same units as the observations, but answer different questions.

Worked example

Five invented track lengths are 2, 3, 3, 4, and 8 minutes. The mean is (2+3+3+4+8)/5=20/5=4 minutes. The median is the third ordered value, 3 minutes. Replace the last track by a 28-minute soundscape: the mean becomes 40/5=8 minutes, while the median remains 3. The replacement is one observation, yet it doubles the mean. If the club must budget the total time for all five tracks, the mean is directly tied to total playback time. If it asks for the middle track length, the median says exactly that.

In the first playlist the 8-minute track contributes 8/20 of the total duration, or 40%, even though it is only one fifth of the tracks. When it becomes 28 minutes, that one track contributes 28/40=70% of total duration. The mean records that large change in aggregate listening time.

The claim and its conditions

x¯=∑i=1nxin

Changing one observation by an amount d changes the sum by d and the mean by d/n, provided n stays fixed. In our replacement, d=20 and n=5, so the mean rises by 4 minutes. The median is unchanged here because the changed observation remains the largest; its position does not cross the middle. This does not make medians immune to all changes. Replacing a middle value, changing enough values, or adding observations can move the median. Nor is the largest value necessarily an error: the 28-minute track may be genuine and important to total listening time.

Proof boundary and failure case. The formula and shift identity are exact for a finite numeric list. “Robust” is a comparison of sensitivity under specific changes, not a guarantee that the median is the best summary. A bimodal dataset such as 1,1,9,9 has median 5, a value no observation takes. Always look at the distribution as well as a single center.

The median’s invariance here is an order-statistic fact: the third value remains 3 because neither the first four values nor their ranks around the middle change. If the 28-minute track instead became 0 minutes, ordering would change to 0,2,3,3,4, and the median would still be 3 by coincidence; other changes can move it.

Try two checks

1. Calculation. For four track lengths 2, 2, 4, 12, calculate mean and median. Replace 12 by 20 and recalculate.

Hint for exercise 1

For four ordered values, average positions two and three for the median.

Full solution to exercise 1

Initially the sum is 20, so the mean is 5; the middle two values are 2 and 4, so the median is 3. After replacement the sum is 28, mean 7, and the middle two remain 2 and 4, so median 3. The 8-minute increase changes the mean by 8/4=2.

2. Interpretation or counterexample. A manager wants to estimate total minutes for 100 plays sampled from the same finite playlist. Which center is linked algebraically to total minutes, and why might the median still be reported?

Hint for exercise 2

Multiply each center by the number of observations and compare with the actual sum.

Full solution to exercise 2

The mean is linked exactly: sample sum = n times sample mean. The median generally does not reconstruct the sum. The median is still informative about the middle observed duration and can expose that a few long tracks pull the mean away from most tracks. Neither one alone describes the full spread.

For even sample sizes, check that the two middle observations are averaged before making a median claim. A list 2,2,4,12 has median 3, which is not itself an observed duration. This is still a meaningful midpoint of the central pair.

What you can carry forward

Choose a center after stating the task. Use the mean for arithmetic totals and the median for a middle rank; report both when asymmetry matters. An extreme legitimate value changes the mean by a predictable amount, while the median can stay still until ranks near the middle change.

The mean and median should be reported with units and sample size. “Typical” without those details can hide whether the question concerns total minutes, a middle track, or a prediction for a future selection from a different playlist.
