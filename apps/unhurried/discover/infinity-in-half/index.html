Infinity and its surprises · Short read

An infinity hiding inside its half

Match every positive whole number with an even one, without running out on either side.

About 6 minutes to read · Optional play and thinking at your pace

Bring: Doubling and halving positive whole numbers.

Do the even numbers run out first?

Put the positive whole numbers in one collection: 1,2,3,4,… . Put the positive even numbers in another: 2,4,6,8,… . The even numbers leave out every odd number, so it is tempting to say there must be fewer of them.

For finite collections, matching things one to one is a way to compare their sizes without knowing either count. Think of matching guests to chairs. Can we use that same idea here and pair every positive whole number with exactly one positive even number?

A small hint

Try matching each number with twice that number. Then ask whether any even number could be missed.

Check both possible failures

Reveal the argument

Every positive whole number n has the even partner 2n. Two distinct numbers cannot share a partner: if 2a=2b, dividing by two gives a=b. And every positive even number appears: for an even number m, its partner on the first side is the positive whole number m/2. Thus nothing is missed on either side and no partner is used twice.

n↦2nm↦m2

This matching is called a bijection. When two sets admit such a matching, they have the same cardinality, the mathematical notion of size used here. The positive evens therefore have the same cardinality as all positive whole numbers, despite forming a proper subset.

This does not say that half the numbers in a finite interval are all of them. Among 1 through 10, there are five evens. Nor does it prove that every infinite collection has this size. “Infinite” does not settle every comparison of cardinality.

What about the odd numbers?

Can you make a one-to-one matching from the positive whole numbers onto the positive odd numbers 1,3,5,7,… ? Check both uniqueness and coverage.

Worked answer

Match n with 2n−1. If 2a−1=2b−1, then a=b, so partners are unique. Every positive odd m has the positive whole-number preimage (m+1)/2, so all odd numbers are reached. The two collections have the same cardinality too.

A good place to stop

Infinity has not become a mysterious very large number. We have made one precise comparison by giving a rule and proving it works in both directions. That is enough to explain how an infinite collection can match a proper part of itself.
